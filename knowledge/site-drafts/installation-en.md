## Installing a composition

A composition is assembled for a specific room, so installation starts with three
decisions rather than with tools: how high it hangs, what it weighs, and how the load is
distributed. Everything below is settled at the design stage.

### Hanging height

**The lowest point sits no less than 2.1 m above the floor.** Below that people start
catching it with their heads, and it is the single most common mistake in finished
projects.

**Double-height rooms.** The lowest point aligns with the second-floor landing. The
composition then works on both levels at once: the silhouette reads from below, the
structure from above. Hung lower than the landing, the upper floor sees only cables.

**Stairwells.** Height is measured from the upper floor, not from the ground floor. The
composition must stay above head height on the upper landing.

**Over a table.** The lowest point sits 75–85 cm above the tabletop, so the light falls
on the table rather than into the eyes of the people seated at it.

**Clearance for the fixing.** Allow at least 5–7 cm between the structural ceiling and the
top of the composition for the suspension assembly. With a stretch or suspended ceiling,
add the depth of that construction.

**How the drop is calculated:** subtract the intended height of the lowest point above the
floor from the ceiling height. The difference is the working drop. On a composition it is
set during assembly, not adjusted on site.

### Load and fixing

The fixing is chosen by weight, not by size:

| Weight | Fixing |
|---|---|
| up to 3 kg | plug and screw |
| up to 5–7 kg | anchor hook |
| over 7 kg | expansion anchor, diameter and length by weight |
| over 25 kg | several suspension points, load distributed |

**The slab.** A standard concrete slab carries up to 100 kg at a point — for most
compositions the margin is many times over. The limit is in the fixing assembly, not in
the slab.

**Stretch and suspended ceilings.** The composition fixes to the structural slab through
a mounting plate installed before the membrane goes up. Nothing fixes to the lining:
plasterboard and PVC are not load-bearing.

**Multi-point suspension.** Compositions spread across an area hang from several points.
The point layout and the load on each are calculated in advance and form part of the
specification.

### Electrical

The power of a composition is the sum of its elements. The figure is needed to choose the
driver and the cable, and it is issued together with the specification.

A rule of thumb for the room: floor area in square metres × the lighting norm in W/m².
For a living room 15 W/m² is common — about 270 W for 18 m². This is a reference figure:
a composition is chosen for the task, not for the norm.

### What is issued before installation

The configurator calculates from the composition itself and shows in the specification:

- **calculated mass** — for choosing the fixing;
- **total power** — for the driver and the cable;
- **overall dimensions** — footprint, depth of the element cloud, distance to the ceiling;
- **the make-up** — element type and count, form, density.

The specification can be saved or printed. **The AutoCAD drawing is sent by the brand bot
on Telegram** — the button for it sits in the configurator itself.

A composition is assembled for the room, so these figures differ for each one and are
calculated before the order, not after.

## What is needed from whom

- **from the designer** — ceiling height, slab type, finished floor level, whether there
  is a suspended ceiling;
- **from the installer** — specification and DXF, point layout, load per point;
- **from us** — the calculation, the drawing, the suspension diagram, supervised
  installation by arrangement.
