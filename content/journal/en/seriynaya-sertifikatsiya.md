# Serial certification: what stands behind it and what it gives a project

Category: Production · Date: 9 September 2026 · Slug: seriynaya-sertifikatsiya

Vargov®Design products have passed serial certification under the requirements of the Eurasian Economic Union. For a private buyer that is one line. For an architect, a specifier and a tender department it is a set of verifiable documents, without which an author-designed piece never reaches the specification.

It would be easy to write a single sentence — "we are certified". But the sentence on its own means nothing: certificates differ, and the difference decides whether a composition passes into a project or is struck out at approval stage. So here is what was actually done.

A batch and a series are not the same thing

Certification comes in different shapes. The simplest is a batch: a particular shipment arrives, it is tested, a document is issued for those exact pieces. When the batch is gone, so is the document.

Serial certification works differently. It covers not boxes but production itself — everything made and everything that will be made while the certificate is valid. The scheme is named accordingly: 1с, serial production. Samples alone are not enough to obtain it: the certification body visits and examines the production itself, to establish whether it can repeat the result. That examination is recorded in a separate document, an act of production analysis.

This is precisely why tenders ask for a serial certificate, and almost never for a batch one.

What exactly is certified

Two certificates of conformity to the technical regulations of the Customs Union, issued on 7 and 8 September 2026 for five years. Together they cover 310 articles of lighting compositions.

The first covers 53 articles under two regulations at once: TR CU 004/2011 on the safety of low-voltage equipment, and TR CU 020/2011 on the electromagnetic compatibility of technical devices. The second covers 266 articles under the low-voltage regulation.

Registered separately are two declarations under TR EAEU 037/2016, on the restriction of hazardous substances in electrical and electronic products. This is the Eurasian counterpart of the European RoHS directive, and it examines composition rather than operational safety. A certificate does not replace that check, and the reverse is equally true: these are two different documents about two different things, and they should not be conflated.

What was actually tested

Behind the phrase "passed testing" stand specific standards. Eight of them fall within the scope, and three are worth naming, because they answer the questions projects ask most often.

IEC 60598-1 — general requirements for luminaires: insulation, heating, fixing, protection against electric shock. IEC 62471 — photobiological safety: whether the emission is harmful to eyes and skin in ordinary use. A non-obvious question, but a material one for public spaces. CISPR 15 — limits of radio disturbance: whether the fitting will interfere with other equipment in the building.

The remaining five concern electromagnetic compatibility, harmonic currents and voltage fluctuation. The dull part, on which it depends whether a composition will coexist with the dimmers, lifts and low-current systems of the building.

The body and the laboratory are different parties

A subtlety well known inside tender departments and almost unknown outside them: the certificate is issued by a certification body, while the testing is carried out by an independent testing laboratory. These are different organisations with different accreditations, and documentation asks for both.

Our certification body is Test-St. Petersburg LLC, accreditation certificate ROSS RU.0001.10SP28. Testing was carried out by the EurasEsTest joint testing centre, accreditation ROSS RU.0001.10TR01. The test reports carry numbers and dates, and they form part of the document package.

How to verify rather than believe

This is where the whole exercise earns its keep. A certificate is not a picture on a website but an entry in a state register. Every document has a registration number that opens the record in the register of the Federal Accreditation Service: who issued it, to whom, for what, until when, and whether it is in force or suspended.

We publish the full registration numbers on the certification page for exactly that reason — so that they can be checked without asking us. Scanned forms are not published: they carry personal and registry data that has no place in open access. The full package — certificates, annexes listing the articles, declarations and test reports — is provided on request to architects, specifiers and tender departments.

Why author-designed lighting needs this

There is a persistent notion that an author-designed object and a verifiable document belong to different worlds. That one-off work by definition has no paperwork, and serial work by definition has no face.

It is a convenient misconception, and it gets in the way. A composition assembled for one particular ceiling hangs above people in exactly the same way, warms up in the same way, connects to the same electrical system of the building, and must be exactly as safe. The difference between "beautiful" and "may be installed in a public space" is precisely the document package.

Serial certification is not a marketing line for us but the next stage of growing up: author-designed work should be not only expressive but fully ready for professional projects.
