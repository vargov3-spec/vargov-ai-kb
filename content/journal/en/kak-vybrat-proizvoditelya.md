# How to choose a maker of a bespoke light composition: eight questions to ask before you sign

Category: Production · Date: November 28, 2026 · Slug: kak-vybrat-proizvoditelya

A composition for a double-height living room, a stairwell or a hotel lobby is made once and for years. Ask any maker these questions — the answers show who will see the project through to installation.

1. Will you see the composition in your interior before it is made?

A sketch or a pretty render is not enough. You need a 3D model of your actual space — from the lowest point, from the gallery and from the entrance — to judge whether the composition reads as a whole and keeps the upper windows clear.

At Vargov®Design: a 3D model of the composition in your interior, before manufacture.

2. Are there files for the project?

Architects and engineers need more than a picture: a model for their BIM software, a specification and a tender document. Without them the composition is fitted into the design by hand, and mistakes surface on site.

At Vargov®Design: an IFC4 file for BIM, a PDF specification and a tender sheet, before manufacture. What is inside the IFC4 file is explained on its own page in the configurator section.

3. Who calculates the fixings — before finishing?

The number of mounting points, the weight and the position of the fixings must be known before the stretch ceiling or plasterboard goes up, not after.

At Vargov®Design: as a rule there are as many mounting points as elements — each hangs on its own line. The configurator counts them and puts them in the specification; details are in the guide to mounting points.

4. Will there be a full-size mounting template?

Marking out the ceiling on site is the main source of misalignment. A 1:1 template transfers the geometry of the composition to the ceiling without recalculation.

At Vargov®Design: the 1:1 mounting template is issued once the 3D model is approved, and a production engineer confirms it before the ceiling is marked out.

5. Is it the maker’s own production, and what is the real lead time?

A reseller quotes the supplier’s lead time; a maker quotes its own. Build it into the site schedule before finishing starts.

At Vargov®Design: own production, made to order, 60–90 working days; worldwide delivery.

6. What about warranty and installation?

Ask what exactly the warranty covers and whether it changes if a third-party crew installs.

At Vargov®Design: a 3-year manufacturer’s warranty on the light elements and drivers, whoever installs; a 1-year warranty on the work when we install. For a third-party crew there is supervised installation: a brand specialist advises and oversees the work.

7. Can you see the elements in person?

No render conveys the light, the texture and the scale of an element.

At Vargov®Design: a showroom in Moscow.

8. What confirms the brand’s work?

Awards with public winner lists, publications and completed projects can be checked by links — more reliable than claims on a website.

At Vargov®Design: awards with links to the organisers are collected in the awards section of the site.

In short

A good maker answers all eight questions before the contract and shows it with files, not promises. You can start in the online configurator: build a composition for your space and get a specification.
