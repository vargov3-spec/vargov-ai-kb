## LC0365 — Light composition

A composition about what has not yet opened. The bud is coiled into a spiral: a sharp point at the top, a soft shell below, the edge running inward on a smooth twist — an unopened tulip or a rolled calla leaf. The author takes the form at the moment before flowering, when everything is ready and nothing has been shown.

The field is dense with a sagging centre, so the composition reads equally well from below and from the side — from an upper floor or from the stairs. Volume is built not by the number of sources but by cross-reflections: every element is mirrored in the gloss of its neighbours.

By day it is a white cloud with cold highlights — pure plasticity, a quiet and graphic piece. In the evening the buds fill with warmth and the fine spires above them disappear, and the flowers seem to hang in the air by themselves.

There is a mirrored version of the same element: a smoky silver surface with gold spires. The same silhouette turns from a tender cloud into a graphite storm cloud with gold sparks — an entirely different temperament.

**Where it works.** Double-height living rooms, lobbies, stairwells, banqueting halls. The sagging centre sets the lower boundary — clearance is needed beneath the composition.

**Style.** Art Nouveau and contemporary classic: a botanical motif without ornament. The message is tender in the white version and dramatic in the smoky one — one piece for two different interiors.

The number of buds, the depth of the field and the finish of the buds and spires are all made to order.

## LC0364 — Light composition

A composition about a stroke of the pen. The ribbons are coiled into rings and loops — as though someone had written in the air in one continuous movement and left the trace. The form is not assembled, it is drawn.

The device is that the ribbon has two layers: a polished mirror surface outside, a ribbed glowing band within. The light is visible only from the inside of a coil, and the composition changes constantly as you walk around it — from one point you see the blind backs of the rings, from another their open glowing interiors.

By day it is metallic graphic work in the air: mirrored coils living off reflections, a dense dark piece. In the evening it acquires a second face — a fiery one: the warm bronze version reads as a melt, the cold silver one as a ribbon of magnesium burning in the dark. An object with two characters at once.

**Where it works.** A strictly vertical format several metres tall — stair flights, atriums, narrow tall entrance halls where the composition is visible from several levels at once. The change of faces reads only when the viewer moves.

**Style.** Contemporary sculpture and brutal opulence: one gesture instead of a composition. The message is artistic — an object-piece for a space that wants a statement.

The height, the number of coils, the finish and the temperature of the glow are all made to order.

## LC0363 — Light composition

A composition about a bush in bloom. Fine rods spread in every direction, interlacing into a volumetric sphere, and on each tip sits a pointed flower with a gold core. The branching is honest — into three or four shoots, as on a real bush, rather than into equal rays.

Close up it is a botanical study: you can see how the bud attaches to the branch, how the gold of the core is reflected in the whiteness. From a distance it is a glowing sphere woven out of sparks, like sea buckthorn in the sun.

By day the composition is graphic: white on silver, a clean line, no colour at all. In the evening hundreds of point sources turn it into a cloud of fireflies suspended above the table — the form disappears and the swarm remains.

**Where it works.** A spherical form has no front — the object is equally good in the centre of a room, above a round table, in an opening between floors. Point sources give sparkling rather than diffused light.

**Style.** Art Nouveau and natural modern: the branch as a classic motif. The message is alive and festive — a piece for an interior that loves detail.

The diameter of the sphere, the number of branches and buds and the finish of the rods are all made to order.

## LC0362 — Light composition

A composition about ripple. Thousands of narrow plates of varying length fold into a spiral that twists downward through several floors. Polished mirror, matte and clear are intermixed — three behaviours of light within one mass.

The device is separation: the light lives only in the ends of the clear strips, runs their whole length and breaks out below as a bright point. The mirrored ones do not transmit it at all — they only reflect their neighbours.

By day it is strict graphic work: vertical strokes of varying density, almost a draft drawing read through lines. In the evening the spiral comes alive — light runs from level to level, the composition shimmers like scales or like ripple under a lamp, and seems to be turning slowly.

**Where it works.** Very tall spaces — stair shafts, atriums, panoramic lobbies with double or triple height. The spiral is designed for a specific height and a specific route of ascent.

**Style.** Contemporary sculptural installation and kinetic art: movement with no mechanism. The message is monumental — a piece that sets the scale of a building.

The height of the spiral, the ratio of mirrored to clear plates and the density are all made to order.

## LC0361 — Light composition

A composition about a cluster. The branches hang down in a dense cone, narrowing toward the bottom and ending in a few solitary sprigs — like a real cluster pulled toward the ground by its own weight. The form is set by weight rather than by drawing.

Every leaf is elongated, slightly curved, with a sharp tip, veining and an uneven edge — taken from a real tree and gilded.

By day the object works as a sculpture in a bronze register: matte patina, deep shadows between the leaves, a heavy and calm piece. In the evening the light comes out of the depth of the canopy in warm rays and lifts the foliage from below — the gold begins to glow from inside, the shadows turn velvet. A burning bush.

**Where it works.** Double-height living rooms, stairwells, the zone above a low coffee table in a lounge — wherever the composition is looked at from below. The light goes downward: bright under the cluster, dim around it.

**Style.** Art Nouveau and natural opulence: gold foliage as a classic motif of abundance. The message is generous and warm — a piece for an interior that receives.

The height of the cluster, the density of the foliage and the finish of the patina are all made to order.

## LC0360 — Light composition

A composition about rain through foliage. Two tiers work toward one image: above, a dense ring of leaves with the sources hidden between them; below, dozens of faceted clear drops. Light from the canopy passes through them and ignites them from within — the effect of rain lit by a setting sun through branches.

The contrast is set firmly: warm matte foliage above, cold transparency below. Neither repeats the other; they complement each other in temperature.

By day it is a wreath with a clear fringe — gold and transparency, a piece read as silhouette. In the evening it becomes a glowing cloud with water pouring out of it; the drops sway with the movement of air and the highlights run about constantly.

**Where it works.** A round form — above a dining table, in an entrance hall, in the centre of a living room. The fringe sets the lower boundary: the composition hangs higher than its diameter would suggest.

**Style.** Art Nouveau and formal classicism: a wreath with pendants in a contemporary reading. The message is ceremonial and warm — a piece for the table people gather at.

The diameter of the wreath, the number of drops and the depth of the rain are all made to order.

## LC0359 — Light composition

A composition about vanishing. A thick form rolled into a shell with a wavy edge — an orchid petal, a rolled leaf, a sea shell. Entirely clear, without colour, with powerful optical distortions inside: a piece that exists by virtue of what it does to light rather than by its own appearance.

The sphere is dense at the centre and sparse at the edges, so it has no defined boundary — a cloud rather than a sphere.

By day the object all but dissolves, leaving only contours and flares along the edges: in a light room it is easy to overlook. In the evening light from the depth reflects off the inner surfaces of the petals, breaks up and comes out as soft highlights — the source cannot be located by eye. A glowing sphere with no edge.

**Where it works.** Light interiors with high ceilings — living rooms, lobbies, dining rooms, bedrooms. The composition adds volume without adding weight: it suits places that must not be made heavier.

**Style.** Contemporary classic and minimalism: transparency as the principal quality. The message is lightness — a piece for an interior that does not want a dominant.

The diameter of the sphere, the number of petals and the density of the cloud are all made to order.

## LC0358 — Light composition

A composition about frozen water. The surface of the discs looks fractured — not decorative hatching but a deep cleavage yielding dozens of facets. The author takes a state rather than a form: a leaf covered in hoarfrost, and ice cracked and seen against the light.

The branch reaches out horizontally, the discs tilted at different angles — like lily pads on the surface of a pond. No fracture repeats another.

By day it is a collection of ice sections on a curved golden branch — a piece read through texture, every facet catching light its own way. In the evening light enters the disc from the central core and runs out across the texture: each facet lights up as a separate spark and the leaf glows like frozen water against the light. From a distance, a chain of radiant circles.

**Where it works.** Long tables, bar counters, reception desks — wherever rhythmic light is needed along an axis. The tilts of the discs set a direction: the composition is oriented to the movement of the gaze.

**Style.** Art Nouveau and natural modern: the water lily as a classic motif, ice as a device. The message is cool and clean — a piece for an interior in a cold palette.

The length of the branch, the number of discs and their tilt are all made to order.

## LC0357 — Wall light

The same water lily taken out of the composition. A single disc with a fractured texture on a short curved bracket that comes out of the wall and holds the leaf at an angle, like a real stem. The piece is reduced to a minimum — and the texture reads more clearly for it than it does in the cluster.

Close up the essential thing is visible: the cleavage is deep, the facets number in the dozens, and no two lie in the same plane.

By day the wall light works as a wall sculpture: a relief catching daylight from the window, a quiet piece. In the evening light from the core goes into the mass and comes out around the whole perimeter, tracing the disc with a glowing contour — a soft background source that neither dazzles nor throws hard shadows.

**Where it works.** Solo beside a mirror, in a pair flanking a bedhead, in a series along a corridor — in a row the tilts of the discs can be alternated. The absence of hard shadows suits bedrooms and zones beside a mirror.

**Style.** Art Nouveau and natural modern: one leaf instead of a composition. The message is intimate — a piece for a wall that does not need an accent.

The diameter of the disc, the length of the bracket and the finish are all made to order.

## LC0356 — Floor lamp

A composition about scales. Cones are threaded in rows so that each is turned point outward and its neighbour points back: the result is dense scaling, like the skin of a pine cone or a coat of mail. The device is protective in origin — the author makes it decorative.

A smoky tea-brown tone mutes the light to amber. The floor lamp deliberately does not light the room — it tints it.

By day it is a dense dark column with a rich texture, almost an architectural detail: a piece read through relief and the shadow between the scales. In the evening the light pushes out between the cones — each tip catches fire, and the whole vertical is covered with a net of warm sparks. A column with a fire inside it.

**Where it works.** Corners, wall piers, the zone beside a staircase. In a pair the floor lamps work as a portal — flanking an entrance, a fireplace, a sofa. The muted light means a separate source is needed for reading.

**Style.** Dark luxe and Art Deco: texture and muted amber instead of shine. The message is intimate and evening-bound — a piece for an interior in a dark palette.

The height, the diameter of the column, the depth of the smoky tone and the finish of the frame are all made to order.

## LC0355 — Light composition

The same scaling gathered into a cascade. The cones are threaded in tiers on rings of differing diameter: the upper rows are wide, the lower ones narrow, and the composition ends in a sharp wedge. The form of a mountain rather than a cloud.

The density of the tiers gives the principal effect: the far elements show through the near ones, and the depth seems endless.

By day it is a mountain of honey with a rich geometric texture — a heavy piece read through silhouette and relief. In the evening each cone is lit at its base and the light runs down to the point; the multi-layered glow overlays itself. A glowing hive with a pulsing core.

**Where it works.** The composition is formal and substantial in image — an entrance hall, a dining room with a high ceiling, a stairwell. The wedge below sets the lowest point: clearance is needed under it.

**Style.** Art Deco and Eastern opulence: tiering as a symbol of wealth. The message is representative — a piece for a space that receives a guest.

The number of tiers, the diameter of the upper ring and the depth of the honey tone are all made to order.

## LC0354 — Light composition

A composition about a dandelion. Hundreds of tubular elements radiate in every direction from a central sphere, and at the end of each is a gold ball, like a seed on a parachute. The form is spherical but not rigid: the tubes vary slightly in length, so the silhouette is a little downy, alive.

The glow is split into two parts: a soft milky light along the whole length of the tubes and gold points of highlight on top of it. Neither gets in the way of the other.

By day it is a white sculpture with gold accents — clean, almost souvenir-clear in its image. In the evening the sphere becomes a source of even diffused light that throws no shadows and fills the room entirely, while the gold balls hold the eye.

**Where it works.** The compact format makes the piece universal — above a dining table, in a bedroom, in a child's room, in a meeting room. Shadowless light is convenient where people work and eat.

**Style.** Scandinavian minimalism and mid-century: the sphere as the purest of forms. The message is light and friendly — a piece for an interior with no pretension.

The diameter of the sphere, the number of tubes and the finish of the details are all made to order.

## LC0353 — Light composition

A composition with a plot of its own. The birds here are not decoration but participants in a scene: one dives, another hovers, a third is only approaching, and the beaks are turned toward the flowers. The wisteria cascade descends as a vertical cluster, the hummingbirds hang around it on separate cables — the composition tells a story rather than decorating a ceiling.

Every corolla carries a warm point of light, the branches are golden, the flowers a cold blue-grey.

By day the combination of dusty blue and matte gold looks noble and restrained: the piece reads through colour and silhouette. In the evening the flowers light up from within and the hummingbirds begin to throw highlights — the scene comes alive and the birds seem genuinely to move.

**Where it works.** A vertical format — stairwells and double-height living rooms where the scene is visible entire. The composition tells its story only in full view: cropped in height, it loses its point.

**Style.** Art Nouveau and the Japanese school: wisteria with a bird is a classic subject of the woodblock print. The message is narrative — a piece for someone who values a story and not only a form.

The height of the cascade, the number of flowers and birds and the depth of the blue-grey tone are all made to order.

## LC0352 — Light composition

The same scene in a green register. Flowers of two kinds — clear olive-green and milky white — open as wide bowls; between them hover chrome hummingbirds with white breasts.

The green bowls work as stained glass: light passes through and tints the air beneath the composition a grass tone, while the white ones stay neutral and balance the colour. The chrome birds reflect everything around them and seem almost liquid — the only elements with no appearance of their own.

By day the object reads as a botanical study in the transparent: green on chrome, a fresh and light piece. In the evening the light burns deep in each bowl, the air beneath the composition turns green, and the garden turns out to be lit from within with the birds held still inside it.

**Where it works.** Vertical and compact in footprint — a stairwell, a bay window, a narrow double-height space. Coloured light noticeably changes the tone of a room: neutral surfaces are needed nearby.

**Style.** Art Nouveau and botanical modern: a transparent garden. The message is fresh and natural — a piece for an interior that loves greenery.

The number of flowers and birds and the balance of green to white are all made to order.

## LC0351 — Light composition

The same story in white. The absence of colour shifts the accent onto plasticity: you can see how each flower is coiled along a spiral, how the edge turns, how light flows out of the depth of the bowl. Nothing distracts from form.

Dark birds against a white ground read graphically, like silhouettes on paper: they give the composition rhythm and keep it from turning sugary. That one contrast carries the whole piece.

By day the object looks refined, expensive, faintly museum-like — white gloss and dark metal, nothing surplus. In the evening the flowers fill with warm light and the birds begin to reflect that glow, and the scene comes alive: the white stops being cold.

**Where it works.** The most universal of the three versions — light interiors, bedrooms, living rooms in a neutral palette, stairwells. White works in any register, so the composition does not dictate a colour to the interior.

**Style.** Art Nouveau and contemporary classic: white with a graphic accent. The message is restrained and expensive — a piece for an interior that chooses quiet.

The height of the cascade, the number of flowers and birds and the finish of the birds are all made to order.
