## LC0320 — Light composition (Award Winning)

An object with no colour of its own. The composition shows nothing that belongs to it — it gathers up the interior around it and gives it back distorted, the way a drop of water gives back a room.

Because of that the piece never looks the same twice: in the morning it holds the reflection of the window, in the evening the warm light of the lamps; in winter it is grey, in summer green from the garden outside. The owner acquires not an object but a mirror of their own space.

The light is hidden in the joints and strikes the underside of the leaves — only reflected glow escapes, without a single direct beam. By day it is a sculpture in arrested metal; in the evening it is a glowing canopy with not one lamp in sight. The model holds an international award.

**Where it works.** A horizontal format — above a long table, in an entrance hall, over a reception desk, in a meeting room, in a showroom.

**Style.** High-tech and contemporary minimalism: clean planes, glazing, polished stone. The message is intellectual restraint — an object that declines to take the solo and is remembered for exactly that.

The spread of the branch, the number of leaves and the finish are all made to order.

## LC0319 — Light composition (Award Winning)

The same branch with the sign reversed. Where the neighbouring model reflects light, this one lets it through — and the result is the opposite character from identical geometry.

The light enters at the stalk and spreads through the body of the leaf, igniting it entire; highlights flare at the bends and a green shadow holds in the depth. The composition reads as underwater — weed in the body of the water, swaying with the current.

By day the branch is almost transparent and visible only as a contour; in the evening it fills with turquoise and becomes the principal patch of colour in the room. The composition holds an international award.

**Where it works.** A horizontal format for large spaces with a quiet background: above a table, in a lobby, in a spa area, beside a pool, in a winter garden.

**Style.** Eco-modern and resort luxury: pale walls, natural stone, water nearby. The message is coolness — the composition drops the temperature of a room by several degrees while changing nothing in it.

The spread, the number of leaves and the tone are all made to order.

## LC0318 — Light composition

A flowering with half the blooms still closed. Some corollas glow from within, others stay blind and merely reflect their neighbours — a garden caught halfway through the process.

The edge of every corolla is uneven, with folds, like a real flower after rain. One and the same form is rendered in two textures, and their alternation gives the composition a depth that neither one gives on its own.

The spiral of the cascade leads the eye downward and never lets it settle: however long you look, there is always a flower you have not yet made out.

By day it is a botanical sculpture — relief, shadow, two textures in dialogue. In the evening it is a garden where half the bush has come into bloom and the other half has gone into the dark.

**Where it works.** A vertical format made for stairwells, double-height living rooms, bay windows, tall entrance halls.

**Style.** Contemporary classic and soft Art Nouveau: pastel walls, rounded furniture, brass fittings. The message is generosity — the composition reads as an armful just carried into the house.

The height of the cascade, the number of flowers and the balance of lit to blind are all made to order.

## LC0317 — Light composition

The corolla of a giant flower seen from inside. The plates are angled toward the centre and downward — the composition opens upward while the viewer looks at it from below, standing inside the flower.

The thickness produces strong optics: each plate holds the refracted reflection of its neighbours, and the ring seems endless. The pink chosen is a cold one, amethyst — it keeps the piece in an adult register and stops it turning sweet.

The light strikes upward and only a glimmer along the edges escapes. The core stays dark — a device that makes you look inside.

By day it is a crown, faceted and cold. In the evening it is a flower glowing from deep within, never showing where it burns.

**Where it works.** A ring form for round tables, rotundas, the central zone of a living room, boudoirs, private dining rooms.

**Style.** Softened Art Deco and feminine Art Nouveau: velvet, mirrors, deep muted tones. The message is intimacy — the composition draws a closed circle of light you want to stay inside.

The diameter of the ring, the number of plates and the tone are all made to order.

## LC0316 — Wall light-sculpture

A leaf crumpled and smoothed out again. Every crease is left in place — that is the work: a piece about the trace of effort rather than about a smooth surface.

The gilding is applied unevenly, with darkening in the recesses, so the relief reads with contrast: the raised parts burn, the hollows go to shadow. The mirror at the core distorts the room like a fisheye lens, drawing the space into the flower.

The light escapes from under the petals around the perimeter and draws the contour. The result is a sun with a dark pupil — a form that radiates and absorbs at once.

By day it is relief: creases, shadow, the room reflected in the pupil. In the evening it is an eclipse stopped at the second when the corona is already visible and the disc is still black.

**Where it works.** An accent wall: an entrance hall, above a console, in a study, in a private living room, in a gallery space.

**Style.** Art Deco and contemporary art: large empty planes, dark walls, minimal furniture. The message is preciousness — the object works like a brooch scaled up to the size of a panel.

The diameter, the character of the crumpling and the finish of the mirror are all made to order.

## LC0315 — Light composition

A bird's tail at the moment of opening. The bands are angled and overlap one another — the movement is stopped halfway and the eye completes what it cannot see.

Every band catches the light at its own angle, so the wreath shimmers around its whole circumference without one static highlight. The composition changes with every step the viewer takes — it does not depict movement, it creates it.

The light lifts the reverse of the bands and only a radiant contour escapes. A dark core at the centre keeps the form from scattering.

By day it is metal: the room taken apart into shards of reflection. In the evening it is a ring of light around emptiness, with neither a source nor a centre.

**Where it works.** Works on the ceiling and on the wall: an entrance hall, above a round table, on an accent wall, in a reception room, in a showroom.

**Style.** Contemporary minimalism and gallery aesthetics: white walls, large planes, no small-scale decor. The message is pure plasticity — there is no subject here, only form, light and the pause between the elements.

The diameter, the number of bands and the finish are all made to order.

## LC0313 — Light composition (Award Winning)

A soap film stretched on a frame and never burst. The membranes sag and pull taut between the rods, forming concave surfaces — a form that in nature lives for seconds.

The films are all but invisible: they neither reflect nor obscure, they gather light along their edges and flare as fine ribs. By day the composition reads as a scientific model — a molecular lattice, a crystal structure; in the evening it disappears as mass and remains as a net of glowing lines.

The model holds an international award. The intent is to make lasting what is by nature instantaneous.

**Where it works.** Generous contemporary interiors: atriums, lobbies, galleries, meeting rooms, showrooms, offices.

**Style.** High-tech and scientific minimalism: concrete, glazing, metal, exposed engineering. The message is precision — the composition speaks the language of a formula and does not pretend to be an ornament.

The dimensions of the lattice, the number of membranes and the tone are all made to order.

## LC0312 — Light composition (Award Winning)

The same structure turned opaque — and by that turned from a diagram into a sculpture. Every bend, every tension in the surface is visible; what could only be guessed at in the transparent version is here declared at full voice.

The polish gathers reflections of the neighbouring elements, and the structure seems endless, folding back into itself. Cold and warm elements alternate, setting a rhythm of temperature inside a single geometry.

The light strikes the concave surfaces and only reflected glow escapes.

By day it is a net of mercury — heavy, dense, reflecting everything around it. In the evening it loses its weight: what remains is a glowing frame suspended with no support, like the diagram of a molecule drawn in the air. The composition holds an international award.

**Where it works.** Interiors with strong architecture: concrete, panoramic glazing, metal, double height. Lobbies, atriums, offices, galleries.

**Style.** Industrial luxury and constructivism: large volumes, exposed structure, a restrained palette. The message is the force of engineering — beauty derived from calculation rather than from ornament.

The dimensions, the ratio of cold to warm elements and the density of the lattice are all made to order.

## LC0311 — Light composition

A bonfire arrested at the moment of the flare. The tongues are twisted into spirals and drawn upward — the movement runs against gravity, and that is the only thing the composition asserts.

A mirrored coating on the inside changes the nature of the elements: they read as metallic rather than clear, and reflect one another, creating an endless gold echo inside the ring. The light lifts the tongues from below — from where a real fire burns.

By day the composition looks like a giant brooch; in the evening, like a flame that was not allowed to go out.

**Where it works.** A ring format for round tables, rotundas, the central zone of an entrance hall, banqueting halls, restaurants.

**Style.** Art Deco and grand opulence: gilding, mirrors, deep colours, symmetry. The message is celebration — the composition was made for occasions and does not hide it.

The diameter of the ring, the number of elements and the intensity of the gilding are all made to order.

## LC0310 — Light composition

The same fire, divided in two. Half the tongues burn evenly and softly, half flare with sharp highlights — one flame shown at two temperatures at once.

The light is hidden in the upper part of each element and runs down the spiral — against the usual logic of fire. Because of that the composition reads not as a bonfire but as a torch, inverted and hung.

The sheaf narrows toward the bottom and ends in a few solitary tongues, the way a real torch ends in sparks.

By day it is a sculptural group: two textures arguing over one form. In the evening the argument ends — every tongue burns separately, and the whole vertical becomes a single flame.

**Where it works.** A vertical format for staircases, bay windows, double-height living rooms, tall entrance halls.

**Style.** Contemporary classic and neoclassicism: pale walls, mouldings, brass. The message is festive restraint — the composition is dressed up exactly enough not to become excessive.

The height of the sheaf, the number of elements and the balance of lit to reflecting are all made to order.

## LC0309 — Light composition

Driftwood raised out of the forest and hung from the ceiling. The trunks are worked down to their cracks, knots and burls — the texture is real, found rather than invented.

Beneath the branches hangs rain: thousands of the finest threads with points of light at their ends, of varying length, answering the least movement of air. The light lives only at the tips — hundreds of white sparks in several layers.

By day it is graphic work: dark branches and silver fringe. In the evening the branches dissolve into darkness and what remains is glowing rain with no source — the effect the whole thing was built for.

**Where it works.** Very large spaces: atriums, hotel lobbies, panoramic halls, banqueting spaces, winter gardens.

**Style.** Eco-luxury and natural Art Nouveau: stone, wood, live plants, large volumes. The message is a forest after rain — the composition brings the state of nature into a room, not a picture of it.

The length of the branches, the density of the threads and the number of light points are all made to order.

## LC0308 — Light composition

Cold fire — a contradiction placed at the base of the piece. The colour is not a warm pink but a crimson with violet depth: the elements look lit even with the light switched off.

The form, meanwhile, is entirely of fire: twisted tongues reaching upward, a dense vertical group. The eye reads flame while the colour says the opposite, and the object stays inside that mismatch.

Fine gold rods between the elements provide a second light part — pointed, sharp, keeping the composition from going vague.

By day it is a sculpture of dense, almost jewel-like colour. In the evening it is a bonfire burning crimson: a fire whose warmth has been taken away and whose light has been left.

**Where it works.** A vertical format for stairwells, boudoirs, dressing rooms, private living rooms — interiors that are not afraid of colour.

**Style.** Glamour and contemporary Art Deco: velvet, deep shades, mirrors, bold combinations. The message is character — a composition for someone who chooses pieces rather than maintains neutrality.

The height, the number of elements and the depth of tone are all made to order.

## LC0307 — Light composition

Wind made visible. The ribbons are twisted helically and drawn out in one direction — the whole stream is moving, though no element will shift from its place.

The light enters at the tips and runs the length of the ribbon, flaring at the turns. Pink and gold alternate, setting a soft rhythm of colour — not a contrast but a gradation, as in a morning sky.

By day the composition is almost weightless: only highlights and contours are visible. In the evening it appears in full — warm air that has become noticeable.

**Where it works.** A horizontal format for long tables, bar counters, generous entrance halls, restaurant rooms, bedrooms with high ceilings.

**Style.** Soft Art Nouveau and romantic classicism: a pastel palette, light fabrics, pale wood. The message is lightness — the composition weighs nothing down, it lifts.

The length of the stream, the number of ribbons and the balance of tones are all made to order.

## LC0306 — Light composition

A net sagging under its own weight. The form is set not by a designer but by physics — the mesh falls the way any net stretched between fixed points will fall.

The light enters at a knot and runs along the tape to the next one, so what glows is not a point but the whole thread: the lattice turns out to be drawn entirely in light. A bluish cast in the mass adds to the likeness with water — a net raised while still wet.

By day the object throws diamond shadows on the floor and reads as graphic work; in the evening it becomes a glowing web beneath the ceiling.

**Where it works.** Large flat ceilings: lobbies, conference halls, panoramic living rooms, co-working spaces, exhibition spaces.

**Style.** Contemporary minimalism and a marine theme kept from the literal: pale tones, natural textures, plenty of air. The message is simplicity of solution — one idea carried through to the end, with not one surplus detail.

The dimensions of the mesh, the cell pitch and the depth of the sag are all made to order.

## LC0305 — Light composition

A thicket seen from below — the way a fish sees it. The rods vary in length and are slightly curved; they sway to the eye even in complete stillness.

Density is the point here: there are so many elements that the composition reads as a single body rather than a set of parts. Light gets inside and runs down the rod, so each tip glows as a separate drop — thousands of points within the mass of green.

By day the object looks like a dense green canopy; in the evening, like a thicket lit from a depth the eye cannot reach.

**Where it works.** Large spaces with high ceilings: atriums, spa areas, lobbies, pools, winter gardens.

**Style.** Eco-modern and biophilic design: living greenery, natural stone, water. The message is immersion — the composition places the viewer inside an environment rather than in front of it.

The diameter of the dome, the density of the rods and the tone are all made to order.
