## LC0380 — Floor lamp

A composition about a grove. Fine tubes of differing height rise from the floor — a stand of bamboo or a rank of organ pipes; between them, threaded on rods, hover large leaves twisted helically, with a wavy edge and a deep lens inside.

The thickness here behaves like water: the reflections of the neighbouring tubes gather in the leaf, and the object never looks the same from two positions.

By day the floor lamp reads as a transparent sculpture — all but invisible, only highlights and contours. In the evening the light enters at the end of the rod, travels its whole length and flares at the bends: the leaves fill with warmth and a soft radiance spreads around the base. The transparent becomes more noticeable at night than by day.

Two finishes change the character of the piece completely. Chrome — cold, technical, for glass and concrete. Gold — warm, enveloping, for wood, leather and muted tones.

**Where it works.** Solo beside an armchair, in a pair flanking a sofa or a portal. In a pair of differing heights the floor lamps read as a small grove — a device for generous living rooms and entrance halls.

**Style.** Natural Art Nouveau and contemporary classic: a plant taken apart into verticals. The message is calm — a piece for an interior that loves form without colour.

The height, the number of tubes and leaves and the finish of the rods are all made to order.

## LC0379 — Floor lamp

The same language, assembled more densely. The leaves are threaded one above another along a spiral and form a single twisted trunk; the tubes rise in a bundle around it, some above the foliage, some lost in its depth. Instead of a grove, one plant.

The thick form produces powerful optics: each leaf holds an inverted reflection of the neighbouring elements, and sharp highlights flare on the cut edges.

By day it is a transparent trunk read through highlights and distortions — a piece with no weight. In the evening light from the bases of the tubes rises and lights the trunk from within: a plant that glows from the root. Chrome gives an icy, almost aqueous reading; the gold finish turns the same form into a warm amber column — the leaves do not change, the temperature of the whole piece does.

**Where it works.** The corner of a living room, the zone beside a window, a pier between cabinets — wherever a vertical without mass is needed. Transparency requires a dark or saturated background: against a white wall the floor lamp loses its silhouette.

**Style.** Organic Art Nouveau and minimalism: the spiral as the single device. The message is lightness — a piece for a tight space that must not be made heavier.

The height, the density of the spiral and the finish are all made to order.

## LC0378 — Floor lamp

A composition about order breaking. Below, strict graphic work: the even verticals of a bundle, a clean line, nothing surplus. Above, the form explodes — clear "tongues" with ragged, seemingly fractured edges twist upward and fly apart, like flame or spray in a freeze frame.

The contrast of order and chaos is the model's principal device, and it reads instantly: one piece showing two states at once.

By day the floor lamp works as silhouette: a strict base, a dishevelled top, transparent fractures catching daylight. In the evening light travels from the ends of the tubes straight into the tongues and ignites every fracture — the gold version reads as a warm torch, the chrome one as a fountain of icy water. In both finishes the tongues themselves stay clear: the temperature is set by the rods.

**Where it works.** Entrance halls, the zone beside a staircase, meeting rooms — wherever a strong vertical accent is needed that takes up no floor area. The spread of the upper part requires clearance to the sides.

**Style.** Contemporary sculpture and brutal opulence: destruction as a form. The message is bold — a piece for an interior with character.

The height, the number of tubes and clear elements and the finish are all made to order.

## LC0377 — Floor lamp

A development of the theme: the ribbons are twisted upward and fly apart more freely, like tongues of a bonfire caught by the draught. Between them sit short glowing inserts, so the fire seems to burn inside the structure itself rather than to light it from outside.

The surface is deliberately uneven, slightly coarse: clear to the point of invisibility in one place, clouded and filled with warmth in another. There are no identical stretches.

By day it is a transparent sculpture on a stem — a piece to be examined close up, built entirely on texture. In the evening the upper part literally catches fire while the lower stays a dark strict vertical: a bonfire on a stand. The gold finish reinforces the association with fire; chrome takes it into another register — shards of ice thrown upward.

**Where it works.** Beside an armchair, in a winter garden, in a lounge — wherever the object is seen close up and the texture will be appreciated. The light is gathered at the top: the floor lamp gives an accent rather than general lighting.

**Style.** Organic Art Nouveau and contemporary art: irregularity as a virtue. The message is tactile — a piece you want to study.

The height, the number and spread of the elements and the finish are all made to order.

## LC0376 — Floor lamp

A composition about a bouquet in a narrow vase. From a massive cylindrical base rises a bundle of fine tubes, each finishing in a trumpet with a wavy rim. The flowers look in different directions and are raised to different heights — the way callas stand, not the way an artificial arrangement does.

It is a rare case of an art object delivering full functional lighting: light comes out of the depth of the trumpets in soft cones — up, sideways, onto the wall — and the direction of each flower is chosen at assembly.

By day the floor lamp reads as a silhouette: white gloss or mirrored trumpets on fine stems, the polished base reflecting the floor and making the piece visually lighter. In the evening the flowers throw light around the room in exactly the directions the client set — a bouquet that illuminates.

**Where it works.** Beside an armchair, in a bedroom, in an entrance hall — anywhere that needs light and silhouette at once. The aimed cones let you light reading, a wall or a picture.

**Style.** Art Nouveau and mid-century: a botanical motif with technical precision. The message is practical and elegant — a piece for those who want both beauty and function.

The height, the number of flowers and the finish of the stems and trumpets are all made to order.

## LC0375 — Floor lamp

A composition about a cob. The leaves sit in layers, overlapping, by the dozen — a dense vertical where every row covers the one before. The form reads as a cob, as a cone and as a cypress: everything that grows upward inside a tight wrapping.

The leaves work against the light: being thin, they do not block it, they diffuse it.

By day it is a sculptural column with rich plasticity — white on gold, a dense and calm piece. In the evening the glow pushes out of the depth of the column, tracing every leaf along its edge: the whole vertical shines with an even milky radiance without one dazzling spot, while the gold between the leaves gives warm vertical sparks. A glowing trunk around which a space gathers itself.

**Where it works.** In a pair flanking an entrance or a sofa, in a row along a long wall. Soft light without glare suits rest areas and reception rooms where people stay for a while.

**Style.** Organic Art Nouveau and contemporary classic: a natural form carried through to an architectural one. The message is calm and representative — a piece that is a column rather than an accent.

The height, the density of the foliage and the finish of the rods are all made to order.

## LC0374 — Light composition

A composition about a candle with no flame. Every clear petal is coiled along a spiral — a form somewhere between a calla leaf, a sail and a tongue of fire; below, a sharp point with a gold tip, above, a fine tube with a point of light. One form reads three ways, and the author does not choose between them.

The cascade is triangular: long elements at the centre, short ones at the edges, the lower edge converging into a sharp wedge.

By day the cascade reads as a cloud of transparent highlights — an almost invisible mass living off reflections. In the evening light enters from above and travels the whole length: a vertical glowing vein flares inside every petal while the form itself stays invisible. A swarm of candles suspended in the air; as you walk around, the elements overlap and the drawing changes constantly.

**Where it works.** Stairwells, double-height living rooms, the zone above a round table. The wedge below sets the lowest point — clearance in height is needed under the composition.

**Style.** Contemporary classic and minimalism: light as the only material. The message is ceremonial and quiet — a piece for a formal space without shine.

The number of petals, the height of the cascade and the depth of the wedge are all made to order.

## LC0373 — Light composition

A composition about a vortex laid on its side. Flat polished ribbons are twisted into spirals and threaded on a straight axis — movement fixed in the horizontal, where it does not usually occur.

Inside the coils sit faceted inserts lit along their whole length. The insert works as a prism: light travels inside it and breaks into sparks facet by facet, while the ribbon by contrast reflects the neighbouring coils — an endless multiplication of highlights arises inside the composition.

By day it is a metal spiral read through reflections: the drawing changes with every step the viewer takes. In the evening, from a distance, the composition reads as an unbroken ribbon of light twisted like a corkscrew. Gold gives a formal warm reading; black graphite with chrome a hard technical one.

**Where it works.** The linear format sits above a long table, a bar counter, a reception desk — wherever light is needed as a band rather than a pool. Even distribution along the length gives uniform light across the whole zone.

**Style.** Art Deco in gold and industrial modernism in graphite — one form for two different interiors. The message is dynamic — a piece for a space that moves.

The length, the number of coils and the finish are all made to order.

## LC0372 — Floor lamp

The structure reads as DNA or as the trace left by a spinning torch: the movement here is completely obvious although the object does not move.

By day the floor lamp works through silhouette and reflections — a clean line twisted in the air. In the evening the light is distributed evenly over the whole height: it lights the space softly, creating neither glare nor dark zones. The black version with cold light looks technological, almost museum-like; the gold one with warm light like a formal column.

**Where it works.** The corners of a living room, the zone beside a staircase, wall piers, reception rooms. Even light over the full height makes the floor lamp a genuine source rather than only an accent.

**Style.** Contemporary sculpture and parametric design: the spiral as pure mathematics. The message is intellectual — a piece for an interior with contemporary taste.

The height, the number of coils and the finish are all made to order.

## LC0371 — Light composition

A composition about a stone with a hole in it. The rings are thick, irregular, slightly skewed — as though turned by water rather than cast. There is not one true circle: the form belongs to the elements, not to a pair of compasses.

The rings are linked by gold pins with glowing ends and assembled into a dense field several layers deep. The mass is optically complex: through it you see the neighbouring rings, distorted as though through a lens.

By day it is a field of transparent sections — a piece read through distortions and overlays, the depth visible right through. In the evening light enters through the pins and spreads through the whole volume, and every ring glows along its inner contour. What remains is a net of glowing ovals hanging inside one another.

**Where it works.** Equally good in two formats — a compact circle above a table or an elongated band along a counter. It reads both close up and from a distance, so it suits intimate rooms and large halls alike.

**Style.** Natural minimalism and contemporary sculpture: the water-worn stone as a form. The message is calm and tactile — a piece for an interior that values substance.

The number of rings, the shape of the field and the density of the linking are all made to order.

## LC0370 — Light composition

A development of the ring theme at a formal scale. The rings are larger and thicker, with a scattering of bubbles inside: the mass looks aerated, alive, as though the movement in it had not yet finished.

They are held by broad bands of polished gold that curve around the rings the way a setting holds a stone. The device is a jeweller's, and it sets the whole logic of the piece: the transparent here is the gem, the metal only the setting.

By day it is a jeweller's work: gold, transparency, precision of joints — an object to be examined close up. In the evening light travels from the gold clasps into the ring, the bubbles flare, and each ring becomes a glowing hoop with a sparkling core. The separate elements become indistinguishable — what remains is a dense radiant cloud.

**Where it works.** The scale allows large spaces to be covered — lobbies, atriums, restaurant halls. The sagging centre demands height: the composition is built downward.

**Style.** Art Deco and jewelled Art Nouveau: the setting as a device rather than as a fixing. The message is formal and expensive — a piece for a representative space.

The number of rings, the dimensions of the cloud and the finish of the settings are all made to order.

## LC0369 — Light composition

A composition about a shoal going round an obstacle. The shoal moves diagonally, deeper and lower — not as a formation and not as a cloud but as a trajectory: the way large sea creatures pass an obstruction without breaking the group.

Every ray is cast whole: a thick body with a lens inside and a wing edge so fine it is almost a thread. The difference in thickness is the principal device — a dense body against a vanishing edge.

By day the object all but dissolves: only highlights and contours are visible, the shoal guessed at rather than read. In the evening light from the fixing points floods the wing from within — a wave of light runs along the clear body, the tip of the tail flares as a point — and the shoal appears entire, "swimming" slowly as the viewer moves.

**Where it works.** Long spaces — above a table, along a staircase, in a gallery. The diagonal is designed for a specific volume and a specific point of entry.

**Style.** Marine Art Nouveau and contemporary installation: movement as subject. The message is narrative — a piece that creates a scene.

The number of figures, the trajectory of the shoal and the spread are all made to order.

## LC0368 — Sculptural composition

The same image without light. Three white figures on fine rods on a shared base: one ahead, two following, all in different phases of movement. The author removes the electricity and leaves the plasticity alone — a test of the form's strength.

The smooth curve of a wing, a drawn-out tail, an edge lifted slightly — the moment of a turn in the body of water, caught in three positions at once.

By day the glossy surface gathers the light of the room and lights itself: the sculpture looks luminous with not a single lamp. In the evening, under directed light, it is chiaroscuro that works — a highlight along the edge of a wing, a deep shadow beneath it — and the figures acquire a volume invisible by day.

**Where it works.** A console in an entrance hall, a long chest of drawers, a meeting room — wherever an accent is needed without colour and without electricity. No wiring is required: it can be placed anywhere.

**Style.** Contemporary sculpture and minimalism: white, form, nothing more. The message is restrained — a piece for an interior for which one detail is enough.

The size, the number of figures and the finish of the base are all made to order.

## LC0367 — Light composition

The same shoal in a matte execution. Here the light does not pass right through as it does in the clear version — it comes out from under the wing as a soft halo, and every figure seems lit from below, like a fish above a sandy bottom. One substitution changes the whole character of the scene.

The shoal moves in a wave, rising and falling on cables of differing length, drawn out in a long arc.

By day white against a dark background reads with maximum clarity: the silhouette works as graphic art, without one highlight. In the evening the figures fill with a soft glow and gold points at the fixings — the calmest of the brand's shoals: no glare, no colour, only form and light.

**Where it works.** Bedrooms, spas, light living rooms, spaces beside water. The absence of glare makes the composition comfortable where people rest and where calm matters.

**Style.** Marine Art Nouveau and Scandinavian minimalism: movement without shine. The message is quiet — a piece for an interior that does not raise its voice.

The number of figures, the length of the arc and the drop heights are all made to order.

## LC0366 — Light composition

A composition about smoke caught and frozen. Inside every tube the finest fibrous texture is visible — arrested streams, like a feather in the body of ice. The texture is the protagonist here: it exists inside, it cannot be touched, only seen.

The field is gathered into a wave with a sagging centre — a stand of reeds in the wind. Every tube is caught by a gold collar.

By day, switched off, the composition remains a collection of clear rods with a silver drawing inside — a piece to be examined close up. In the evening light enters through the collar and runs down the fibres all the way to the end: the streams flare and turn into glowing threads. Smoke that has caught fire.

**Where it works.** In the compact version, four or five tubes above a table; at scale, hundreds of rods covering the whole ceiling of a stairwell. The texture is visible close up, so the composition is not hung higher than it needs to be.

**Style.** Natural minimalism and Japanese aesthetics: a drawing inside the substance instead of decor on the outside. The message is contemplative — a piece you look into.

The number of tubes, their lengths, the shape of the field and the finish of the collars are all made to order.
