## LC0106 — Light composition

A composition about a creature without a skeleton. The jellyfish is a body held together by water; taken out of its medium it stops being a form at all. The author gives it its medium back: a vertical rain of small pendants hangs around the figures, and the shoal exists inside it.

The dome is faceted, assembled from triangular plates with visible ribs — like a geodesic dome or a carapace. The tentacles do not hang straight down but stream sideways and below: the jellyfish is being carried by a current, and you can see it.

By day it is a shoal of creatures suspended in the air — amber bells with runs and patches of differing density, a fine clear fringe beneath them. In the evening the light passes through the amber, the domes glow from within with their net of ribs showing through, the tentacles pick it up and carry it their whole length, flaring at the tips, while the rain around catches the reflections and shimmers. An immersion into deep water.

**Where it works.** Generous volumes — atria, lobbies, pools and spas, double-height living rooms, children's spaces. The streaming tentacles need height: the space beneath must be clear.

**Style.** Marine Art Nouveau and contemporary sculptural installation. The message is narrative, almost theatrical — a piece that turns a room into a stage.

The number of figures, the calibre of the domes, the length of the tentacles and the density of the rain are all made to order.

## LC0105 — Light composition

A composition about a summer evening. The firefly is the only insect people find beautiful, and only because it glows. The author builds the whole piece on that: the sources are hidden in the rods, yet it seems the swarm itself is alight.

Rods of varying length hang in dense hatching and set the volume — some gold, some clear. Hundreds of small winged figures are set among them, unevenly distributed: denser below, sparser above, with individuals straying far from the mass.

By day the composition reads graphically — a rain of lines in which the figures are barely distinguishable. In the evening every rod ends in a point that flares as a star, and the thin amber wings transmit that light and fill with it. The hatching stays cool and almost invisible, and in the dark all you see is a swarm of fireflies over water.

**Where it works.** Generous rooms — lobbies, atria, stairwells, panoramic living rooms, winter gardens. Vertical hatching works with height: it establishes a volume that a low room does not have.

**Style.** Art Nouveau and natural romanticism: the insect as a precious thing, light as its property. The message is lyrical — a piece that brings back a memory.

The overall size, the number of figures, the length of the rods and the tone of the wings are all made to order.

## LC0104 — Light composition

A composition about dropped fabric. The panel is crushed and folded in large pleats — the form matter takes when you let it go. The author fixes chance: the pattern of the folds cannot be repeated twice, and that is the whole piece.

Close parallel ribbing runs across the entire surface, with a fine gold thread carrying point lights along the rim. The elements are large — each several times the size of an ordinary pendant — and hang at varied heights, in places running one into another.

By day it is fabric frozen in the air: the ribbing catches the light in bands, the folds give real chiaroscuro, and the surface reads as volume. In the evening the light runs along the gold rims and enters the ribbing, scattering across it in strokes — some passages burn brightly, others fall into shadow. The trace left by light rather than the fabric itself.

**Where it works.** Generous rooms — lobbies, atria, double-height living rooms, long dining halls. It exists in two formats: an elongated horizontal band and a multi-tier volumetric composition.

**Style.** Contemporary sculpture and Baroque drapery in a minimalist reading: the fold as a classic motif without classic decoration. The message is expressive — a piece for a space that wants a gesture.

The overall size, the number of panels, the pattern of the folds and the number of lights along the rim are all made to order.

## LC0103 — Light composition

The same spinning top, but in a dense array. Here the elements stand close together, layered, with a wavy lower edge: not a swarm but a mass. A flattened disc at the middle, a short cone above, a long sharp spike below — a top stopped in the air, multiplied by the hundred.

Above the mass runs a net of bare cables with point lights, receding to the ceiling: it adds volume and stops the composition reading as a flat band.

By day the object is all but invisible — entirely clear, it shows itself only in highlights. In the evening a double rhythm establishes itself: the discs diffuse the light and flare along their rims in a horizontal layer of reflections, while the spikes carry it downward and light up at their very tips in a vertical rain. A volume filled with hanging lights on two levels.

**Where it works.** An elongated horizontal format — long dining tables, bar counters, lobbies, meeting rooms. A dense mass gives more light than a sparse composition of the same length.

**Style.** Contemporary classicism and precise minimalism: geometry instead of ornament. The message is austere — a piece for an interior that holds its form.

The length, the number of elements, the length of the spikes and the drop heights are all made to order.

## LC0102 — Light composition

A composition about the berry and the thorn. Thousands of faceted spheres form a soft pale mass, among which narrow bronze petals with sharp tips are scattered. Berry and claw in a single thicket — the way any real bush is built.

The spheres hang densely, in layers, with a ragged lower edge; the metal elements are turned in different directions and work as dark accents in a pale field. Above, a net of fine cables with point lights holds the composition.

By day it is a cloud of berries — pale, sparkling, with a few dark strokes inside it. In the evening the light passes through the clusters and breaks on every facet: the mass sparkles and loses its source entirely, while the bronze petals reflect it directionally and give rare warm flares. The contrast is set by temperature — a cool white scatter with hot strokes inside it. A thicket full of sparks.

**Where it works.** Generous rooms — lobbies, banquet and wedding halls, double-height living rooms, atria. Light that breaks up gives plenty of glare: this is a ceremonial piece, not a working one.

**Style.** Grand classicism and Art Deco: the scatter as a symbol of abundance. The message is festive — a piece for a space where people celebrate.

The overall size, the number of spheres, the share of metal elements and the drop heights are all made to order.

## LC0101 — Light composition

A composition about a cave. A stalactite grows drop by drop over thousands of years — a form made entirely of time. The upper edge of the composition is level, the lower one wavy: a section through a cave where growth has been uneven.

Each faceted drop with its slight twist hangs on its own rod with a glowing point at the end, the rods varying in length. Bare rods are set between them — they work as hatching and give depth.

By day the composition reads graphically, through hatching alone: clear forms, fine lines, almost a technical drawing. In the evening the light travels from the point straight into the body, refracts on every rib and flares at the base of the drop, while the rod itself stays invisible. Because the array is dense, the light passes from drop to drop and the whole volume begins to glow — a row of icicles hung above the table.

**Where it works.** An elongated horizontal format — long dining tables, bar counters, meeting rooms, reception areas. The wavy lower edge sets varying heights above the table: the piece goes where people sit beneath it.

**Style.** Organic minimalism and contemporary classicism: a geological form without decoration. The message is restrained — a piece for an interior that avoids ornament.

The length, the number of drops, the length of the rods and the calibres are all made to order.

## LC0100 — Light composition

A composition about density. Hundreds of drops hang almost touching, in layers — a mass so thick that it stops reading as individual elements. The upper zone is left empty, only the hatching of cables; everything gathers in the lower half with a ragged edge.

Each drop is twisted on its axis: a sharp tip below, an open hollow at the middle. The thickness works as a lens, and once the light has entered the mass it never leaves in a straight line.

By day it is a wall of ice — clear, dense, read through highlights and the refraction of daylight. In the evening the light passes from element to element, refracting at every twist, and the source cannot be found: the volume itself glows. Because the mass is dense the illumination comes out even and diffuse, without a single hard highlight. A downpour frozen in the air.

**Where it works.** Generous rooms — lobbies, atria, double-height living rooms, banquet halls. Even glare-free light is comfortable where people spend time beneath it.

**Style.** Contemporary classicism and minimalism: mass instead of pattern. The message is monumental — a piece that works through volume, not detail.

The overall size, the number of elements, the density of the array and the drop heights are all made to order.

## LC0099 — Light composition

A composition about a ripple on metal. The line rises and falls in two crests with a trough between them — a wave set by the length of the suspensions. Each narrow curved tongue is turned on its own axis, so the mass never reads as flat: it has depth and shimmer.

Cool silver and warm gold are intermixed, the surface polished. The upper part of the composition stays almost dark — only the hatching of cables.

By day it is a ripple: the mirrored tongues catch daylight and invert it, and the pattern changes with every step the viewer takes. In the evening the light rolls out along the full length of each element and a glowing rib lights up — gold gives warm flares, silver cool ones. A wave full of sparks.

**Where it works.** An elongated horizontal format — long dining tables, bar counters, lobbies, meeting rooms. The wave pattern is calculated for the length of the table.

**Style.** Contemporary Art Deco and glamorous minimalism: two metals, no colour at all. The message is representative — a piece for a table where people are received.

The length, the wave pattern, the number of elements and the ratio of gold to silver are all made to order.

## LC0098 — Ceiling composition

A composition about a thicket growing downward. From a round mirrored base runs a dense fringe of fine rods, and monstera leaves sit along its lower edge, forming a wreath. The middle stays open: you can see the rods receding, and the thicket reads as having a root.

The leaf form is taken almost literally — a broad plate with deep splits, through-holes and drawn venation. Warm gold with patina is mixed with cool oxidised turquoise: one plant at two ages.

By day it is a metal thicket on the ceiling — dense, graphic, living on the reflections in the mirrored base. In the evening the light emerges through the splits in the plates and lights the neighbouring leaves from below: a warm glow stands inside the wreath, the rods reflect and multiply it upward toward the base, and the outer surfaces stay subdued. A bush lit from within.

**Where it works.** Living rooms, winter gardens, spas, restaurant floors, reception rooms. Ceiling mounting needs no height — the piece suits places where a pendant is impossible.

**Style.** Tropical modernism and mid-century: fifties botany without the kitsch. The message is buoyant — a piece for an interior that loves greenery.

The base diameter, the number of leaves and the ratio of gold to turquoise are all made to order.

## LC0097 — Ceiling composition

A composition about an armful. The stems spread from a round base outward and downward, each bending its own way: not a bouquet set in a vase but an armful thrown open in every direction. Between them runs a dense fringe of straight rods — what remains of the stems that have no flower.

The cups with their ragged wavy rims are open like a poppy, or like a flower that has just unfurled. The tone is pomegranate-crimson, almost opaque in places, lightening to pink in others.

By day the composition reads as a bouquet — saturated colour on gold, dense and painterly. In the evening the light passes through the body of the colour and the petals fill from within: the tone becomes deeper and hotter than by daylight, while the stems pick up the reflection and glow as fine lines. An armful of embers on gold legs.

**Where it works.** Living rooms, dining rooms, reception rooms, restaurant floors, boudoirs. The saturated tone needs a calm backdrop: against a coloured wall the flower is lost.

**Style.** Art Nouveau and romantic classicism: the flower as the principal subject of an interior. The message is passionate and warm — a piece for a space with character.

The base diameter, the number of flowers, the stem lengths and the depth of the pomegranate tone are all made to order.

## LC0096 — Light composition

A composition about a leaf that has already lain a while. Each element is slightly crumpled and rolled at the edge — not flat, but the shape a leaf takes after a day on the ground. The author takes not the moment of falling but what comes after: leaf-fall already settled.

The leaves hang at varied levels, turned at random, bunching in places and flying off alone far from the mass in others. Warm gold with patina is mixed with cool matte silver — a difference in age, not in species.

By day it is a leaf-fall caught in a frame: the crumpled surfaces give ragged highlights, and the composition shimmers as the viewer moves. In the evening the gold leaves reflect the light and flare warm, the silver ones diffuse it and stay subdued. A heap of leaves with light breaking out from beneath.

**Where it works.** Generous rooms — lobbies, stairwells, double-height living rooms, winter gardens. The scattered elements extend beyond the main envelope: allow clearance to the sides.

**Style.** Organic modernism and autumn classicism: fading as an aesthetic. The message is melancholy and warm — a piece for an interior that loves the season.

The overall size, the number of leaves and the ratio of gold to silver are all made to order.

## LC0095 — Light composition

A composition about tearing. The fragments are torn and crushed: ragged edges, deep creases, not one correct geometric line. The object must read as what is left after a tear, not as something assembled — and every decision serves that.

The fragments hang at varied levels, turned at random, arranged in no order at all. The calibres differ, from large down to fine crumbs: a scatter always sorts by mass.

By day it is an explosion of foil — gold mixed with matte silver, a random pattern, nothing predictable. In the evening not every fragment glows but one in five: the crumpled gold reflects the light in bands along the folds, the silver subdues it, and most of the mass stays dark. It is precisely that selectiveness that creates the impression of movement — a field of shards with occasional flares.

**Where it works.** Generous rooms with high ceilings — lobbies, atria, gallery spaces, double-height living rooms. A scatter needs air: pressed together, the composition turns into a heap.

**Style.** Contemporary art and brutalist luxury: the aesthetics of destruction, executed expensively. The message is bold — a piece for an interior unafraid of being abrupt.

The overall size, the number of fragments and the ratio of gold to silver are all made to order.

## LC0094 — Light composition

A composition about a split mineral. The facets meet at random angles, without symmetry — the way a stone looks when it has been broken rather than cut. The author deliberately refuses jewellers' regularity: what is valued here is the rock, not the workmanship.

The crystals hang at varied levels, bunching into a dense mass in places and falling away alone, far down or to the side, in others. The depth of colour depends on thickness: the more massive, the more saturated.

By day it is a scatter of minerals in the air — turquoise with a greenish cast, read through volume and refraction. In the evening the light enters the body and refracts on every facet, filling the colour from within: in the thick passages it deepens to emerald, in the thin ones it lightens almost to clear. Not one source is visible directly — a vein lit from inside the rock.

**Where it works.** Generous rooms — lobbies, atria, spas and pools, double-height living rooms. Saturated colour needs a neutral setting.

**Style.** Organic minimalism and geological aesthetics: the stone exactly as it is. The message is collector-minded — a piece for someone who values the material.

The overall size, the number of crystals, the calibres and the depth of the turquoise tone are all made to order.

## LC0093 — Light composition

A composition about fragility in quantity. One butterfly is a detail; a hundred butterflies are an event. The author uses that: the mass is denser below and at the centre, while at the edges individuals fly far off alone — and it is they that make the flock alive.

The wing form differs by species — some broad, some narrow with long tails; the veins are drawn. The figures are turned in different directions, some hanging flat, some edgewise.

By day it is a flock stilled below the ceiling — white, matte, faintly translucent, read through silhouette. In the evening the light passes through the thin wings and they begin to glow from within in a soft milky tone with the veins showing through. Not a single hard highlight: the glow reads as a property of the butterflies themselves, not as backlighting.

**Where it works.** Generous rooms — lobbies, double-height living rooms, winter gardens, wedding and banquet halls, children's spaces. Soft diffuse light suits zones where people spend time.

**Style.** Art Nouveau and romantic classicism: the butterfly as a classic motif of fragility. The message is light and bright — a piece for a space that wants to give pleasure.

The overall size, the number of figures, the range of wing forms and the tone of the glow are all made to order.

## LC0092 — Light composition

A composition about a dismantled stained-glass window. Every feather is assembled from separate coloured segments joined by silver soldering, and the seams form the pattern of the vane: they work as veins and as frame at once. Structure and drawing are one and the same thing here.

The feathers are turned in different directions and hang at varied levels; the main mass gathers at the centre, and below individual feathers break away and fall alone.

By day it is a flock of blue feathers — from cobalt and ultramarine to turquoise, read through colour against the light. In the evening the light passes through the segments and each fills with its own tone — blue beside turquoise, ultramarine beside near-black — while the silver seams flare as a graphic net over the colour. The pattern reads more clearly than by day: a stained-glass window taken apart and suspended in the air.

**Where it works.** Generous rooms — lobbies, atria, double-height living rooms, spas and pools, gallery spaces. The technique is made to be seen against the light: the piece is placed where people look up at it.

**Style.** Art Nouveau and the Tiffany stained-glass tradition in a contemporary reading. The message is collector-minded — a piece for an interior that values handwork.

The overall size, the number of feathers, the palette of the segments and the pattern of the soldering are all made to order.
