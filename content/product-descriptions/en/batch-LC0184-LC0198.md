# Product card texts — batch 35: LC0184–LC0198 (15 items)

Approved format: artistic image, day-to-evening shift, "Where it works", "Style", closing line on customisation. Element materials are never named.

## LC0198 — Floor lamp

A composition about light stacked like a wall. Links with an opening at their centre are laid in offset layers — the device is taken from masonry, where strength rests not on mortar but on order. The author pushes it to a paradox: the masonry comes out transparent.

Clear links alternate with mirror-gold ones, and there is no system to the alternation — the column reads as built by hand rather than cast. Gaps remain between the courses: the mass breathes.

By day it is an architectural fragment — a column lifted out of a portico and set down in a room. In the evening the masonry opens from within: light passes through the clear links, flares on their rounded ribs, and is intercepted by the gold ones and thrown outward in warm patches. The wall becomes a sieve of light.

**Where it works.** A vertical on a slender stem needs a free corner and a view from a distance — living rooms, halls, reception rooms, lounges, showrooms. It works well in pairs flanking an axis: a sofa, a fireplace, an entrance.

**Style.** Art Deco and contemporary classicism: the same language as the bronze floor lamps of the thirties, but with no ornament — only module and rhythm. The message is architectural: a piece for someone who values structure over decoration.

The column height, the number of links, the ratio of clear to gold and the metal finish are all made to order.

## LC0197 — Floor lamp

A composition about the unopened. A pine cone, a bud, a cocoon — a form in which everything already exists but nothing has yet been shown. The author takes the image literally: pointed petals with a soft twist are laid overlapping in a spiral, each course covering the one before, the rim breaking off below in sharp tips.

The lower half of the column is deliberately left empty — bare rods only. The mass gathers at the top, at eye level: the object does not grow from the floor, it hangs like fruit on a branch.

By day it is a dense milky sculpture — opaque, cool, without a single highlight. In the evening a core wakes inside: through the slots between the petals a warm orange burning is visible, while the petals themselves stay milky and cold. That difference is the whole character of the piece: calm outside, heat within.

**Where it works.** Soft diffuse light and a vertical silhouette — living rooms, bedrooms, reception rooms, lounges, hotel rooms. It suits places that need not general light but a point of presence.

**Style.** Scandinavian minimalism and organic modernism: a pure natural form without ornament. The message is quiet and intimate — a piece for an interior that values silence and material clarity.

The column height, the number of petals, the tone of the glow and the finish of the rods are all made to order.

## LC0196 — Floor lamp

A composition about the lining. Every element is a broad sickle with a deep hollow: white and opaque outside, polished gold within. The author turns each element on its axis its own way, and the column begins to play at openness: one shows a white back, the next reveals a gold interior.

The tiers run unevenly, with gatherings and gaps — you read a branch with large leaves, or an inflorescence opening upward. The stem and base are in the same gold: the structure is reduced to a single vertical, all attention on the crown.

By day it is graphics — white silhouettes on a gold shaft, sculpture without light. In the evening the gold within heats to a honeyed tone and throws reflected light outward: not one direct point, only a warm radiance out of the hollows. The white outside stays cool. The piece literally glows from inside.

**Where it works.** A large crown and soft reflected light — living rooms, halls, reception rooms, showrooms, restaurant areas. It needs height to spare and a clear wall behind it.

**Style.** Hollywood glamour and contemporary Art Deco: white with gold, the contrast of opaque and mirrored. The message is representative — a piece for an interior that receives guests.

The height, the number of elements, the gilding finish and the colour of the outer surface are all made to order.

## LC0195 — Floor lamp

A composition about coal. Black outside, heat within — a device as old as a campfire, brought here to a form. Crumpled discs of deep black with a mirrored gloss: each pressed in at the middle and rolled at the edge, as though a finger had been laid on it while the form was still soft. The load-bearing structure is polished gold, and it shows only in the gaps.

The discs are set in layers, turned in different directions, covering the gold with themselves. The column reads as a black mass with metal breaking through from beneath.

By day it is a dark monolith — a thing that absorbs light rather than giving it back. In the evening the mass opens: narrow hot bands emerge, lighting the gold linings. A pair of these lamps flanking a sofa in a dark room gives no general light — they set two vertical columns of glow with the scene held between them.

**Where it works.** A dark palette and dramatic light — living rooms in deep tones, private studies, bars, lounges, showrooms. They work better in pairs than singly.

**Style.** Dark luxury and brutalist modernism: black with gold, no lightness at all. The message is masculine and club-like — a piece for an interior that does not flirt.

The height, the number of discs, the gilding finish and the depth of the black are all made to order.

## LC0194 — Floor lamp

A composition about the depth of water. Elongated leaves of deep bottle green shading into transparency at the rim — a colour that belongs to a body of water, not to its surface. Large smooth curves catch and stretch reflections, so the object never looks the same from two positions.

The leaves are laid in overlapping layers in a spiral. The top is dense, below the rim breaks off cleanly and only bare rods continue — the thicket is lifted above the floor, like weed torn loose from the bed.

By day it is a clear sculpture: the green reads as dense, almost stone-like. In the evening light enters the body from the edge and spreads across the whole plane of the leaf — the green fills from within and turns emerald, while the gold of the rods shows through, adding a warm vein to a cold colour. The thicket comes alive.

**Where it works.** A saturated colour needs a calm ground — living rooms, winter gardens, spas, reception rooms, restaurant interiors. It works well by a window, where daylight is added to the artificial.

**Style.** Art Nouveau and organic modernism: the same line as the vases of Gallé and Daum. The message is collector-minded — a piece for someone who chooses colour as the principal argument.

The column height, the number of leaves, the depth of the green tone and the metal finish are all made to order.

## LC0193 — Light composition

A composition about weightlessness. The manta is the only large creature that does not swim but flies: its movement is closer to a bird's gliding than to a fish. The author strips the form of everything surplus and leaves a pure silhouette — a broad triangular wing, drawn-out tips, a curved tail.

The shoal runs in tiers, some rising, some sliding low, the figures turned at random. Glowing points hang between them — plankton, bubbles, suspended matter. It is they, not the figures, that carry the light: on one individual a wing edge flares, on another only a shadow is visible.

By day it is a sculptural shoal below the ceiling — white with occasional dark mirrored accents, graphics on the plane of the ceiling. In the evening immersion sets in: the figures recede into darkness, leaving the luminous suspension and the silhouettes passing through it. The room becomes a body of water.

**Where it works.** A format for large volumes — atria, lobbies, pools and spas, double-height living rooms, children's spaces. It needs height: the shoal reads only from above, at a distance.

**Style.** Contemporary sculptural installation, closer to a museum than to an interior piece. The message is narrative — a piece that creates a scene rather than complementing one.

The overall size, the number of figures, the ratio of pale to mirrored and the density of the light points are all made to order.

## LC0192 — Light composition

A composition about dusk. The magnolia flowers before its leaves — a bare branch on which large cups open straight away. The author takes that moment and carries it into evening: the palette is deliberately subdued, grey instead of white, and the crown reads as heavy, like a garden at the hour when colour has already left the petals but the form still holds.

Branches with a rough cast texture are woven into a sphere. Closed buds alternate with half-open and fully open ones — flowering is shown not as a state but as a process.

By day it is a bronze sculptural branch: heavy, dark, almost archaeological. In the evening a few flowers light up within the crown — the light emerges from the cups in warm patches, lights the neighbouring petals from within and flares on the gold of the branches. Most of the crown stays in darkness, and it is precisely that restraint that holds the drama.

**Where it works.** A voluminous crown asks for height — living rooms with high ceilings, stairwells, private studies, lobbies in a dark palette. It suits a position above a round table or in an opening where people walk around it.

**Style.** Dark luxury and Art Nouveau in an evening reading: a natural form presented as something precious. The message is intimate and adult — a piece for an interior with character.

The crown diameter, the number of flowers, the depth of the grey tone and the finish of the branches are all made to order.

## LC0191 — Light composition

A composition about morning. The same device as the dark version, read in full light: gold cast branches woven into a sphere, and on them large flowers with a delicate rose-cream shift from the base of the petal to its edge. The surface is velvety, without a single highlight.

The only difference from the twilight version is colour, but it turns the piece over entirely: instead of heavy bronze, a tree in full bloom. The crown looks light and airy, the petals almost translucent. Buds are set among the open flowers — the flowering is still going on.

By day it is a sculptural tree in bloom: rose on gold, a festive and open piece. In the evening the light passes through the thin petals, they light up from within, the rose tone fills and intensifies, and the gold branches pick up the reflection. The crown burns almost entirely — not in separate points but as a cloud.

**Where it works.** Living rooms with high ceilings, bedrooms, stairwells, wedding and ceremonial halls. It asks for a pale ground: against a white or cream wall it reads more precisely than against a dark one.

**Style.** Romantic Art Nouveau and contemporary classicism: flowering as the principal subject of an interior. The message is festive and feminine — a piece for a space that wants to give pleasure.

The crown diameter, the number of flowers, the depth of the rose tone and the finish of the branches are all made to order.

## LC0190 — Light composition

A composition about the upstroke. A narrow curved sickle with sharp ends and a slight twist on its axis — the form reads both as a wing at the moment of lift and as a leaf caught by wind. The author does not specify which: what matters is not the nature of the thing but the phase of the movement.

The composition is drawn out horizontally and built in tiers: the elements hang at varied heights, turned in different directions, overlapping in places. The mass is not flat — it has depth, and the flock reads as volumetric.

By day it is gold graphics on the ceiling — mirrored strokes living on reflected daylight. In the evening a glowing rib flares on every wing: the light rolls out along the full length of the sickle, reflections multiply from element to element, and the composition begins to shimmer at the slightest shift of the eye. A flock caught at the moment of lift.

**Where it works.** An elongated horizontal format — long dining tables, bar counters, meeting rooms, lobbies. It works where people pass beneath it and look along it, not across.

**Style.** Contemporary Art Deco and glamorous minimalism: pure form, mirrored finish, no ornament at all. The message is representative — a piece that sets the standing of a space from the doorway.

The length, the number of elements, the gilding finish and the drop heights are all made to order.

## LC0189 — Light composition

A composition about a flock that has already scattered. The same sickle form — a narrow wing with sharp ends and a twist — but read quietly: a white matte mass with gold elements strewn through it as rare flares.

The elements are turned at random, some rising above the main mass, others breaking away below. The flock reads as sparse and mobile rather than drawn up — the moment after take-off, before the formation has settled.

By day it is a cloud of leaves or birds below the ceiling — light, almost incorporeal. In the evening the white field gives a soft even glow without highlights, while the gold reflects sharply, in points. The difference between the two finishes creates the rhythm: a calm luminous ground with occasional hot sparks in it.

**Where it works.** An elongated horizontal format — long tables, meeting rooms, reception areas, panoramic living rooms. Soft light without direct glare is convenient where people work or eat beneath the composition.

**Style.** Scandinavian minimalism with a jewellery accent: a restrained base, pinpoint opulence. The message is calm — a piece for an interior that does not raise its voice.

The length, the number of elements, the share of gold and the drop heights are all made to order.

## LC0188 — Floor lamp

A composition about structure that does not hide. Chromed rods run right through the column, and the author does not mask them — on the contrary, broad gaps are left between the gold blades so the frame stays visible. The piece honestly shows how it is made.

The blades — a broad leaf with a sharp tip and a slight twist — run in tiers from bottom to top, overlapping. Each is turned on its axis its own way: the column comes out transparent despite the density of metal.

By day it is a sculpture in two metals: cool chrome inside, warm gold outside, and the contrast holds the object. In the evening the light comes from below, strikes the underside of every blade and reflects upward — the belly lights up while the top stays dark. Deep inside the column the chrome catches the gold reflections and multiplies them.

**Where it works.** Living rooms, halls, reception rooms, bars, showrooms. Light reflected upward lifts the ceiling — the lamp works as a source of volume, not only as an accent.

**Style.** Industrial luxury and Art Deco: exposed structure, expensive finish. The message is technical — a piece for someone who cares how a thing is made.

The column height, the number of blades, the metal finish and the height of the light unit are all made to order.

## LC0187 — Floor lamp

A composition about the body of water. Discs with a deep funnel at the centre — a section like a lens: they are not merely coloured a marine shade, they accumulate it. The rim betrays the thickness, and within it the colour moves from azure to deep blue, the way water moves away from a shore.

Discs of varying calibre are set in layers and at random: some face straight ahead, some are turned edgewise, some overlap their neighbours. At the centre of every funnel is a gold hemisphere, the only warm point in a cold palette.

By day it is a sculpture in colour: the discs work through reflection and the colour reads as flat. In the evening the light lives in the gold cores: it enters from the centre, spreads to the edges and refracts through the body — one disc reflects inside another, and not a single source reads directly. The column becomes a pillar of water lit from beneath.

**Where it works.** Living rooms, spas, reception rooms, restaurant interiors, spaces with a marine theme. Saturated colour needs a neutral ground — against a pale wall it reads more precisely.

**Style.** Art Nouveau and the Murano tradition of coloured work in a contemporary reading. The message is collector-minded — a piece bought for its colour and examined close to.

The column height, the number of discs, the calibres and the depth of the marine tone are all made to order.

## LC0186 — Floor lamp

A composition about recognition. A monstera cannot be mistaken for anything else: the deep splits and through-holes are its signature. The author takes the form almost literally, with the venation drawn, and does not stylise it — because all the force of the image lies in instant recognition.

White leaves alternate with gold ones of the same geometry, turned in different directions and laid in layers into a dense vertical thicket. The lower half is bare stem only: the plant is raised above the floor, as in a shot taken from below.

By day it is a sculpture of a plant — white gloss with gold insets, graphic and flat. In the evening the thicket begins to glow from within: the light emerges through the splits, lights the gold linings of the neighbouring leaves and draws patches on them that have passed through the holes. A tropical noon, folded into a room.

**Where it works.** Living rooms, winter gardens, spas, reception rooms, restaurant interiors. It suits a corner or a position beside a column, where the thicket reads as silhouette.

**Style.** Tropical modernism and mid-century: the same botany as in fifties interiors, but without the kitsch. The message is buoyant — a piece for a space that loves greenery and light.

The column height, the number of leaves and the ratio of white to gold are all made to order.

## LC0185 — Light composition

A composition about shedding. The garland sags at the centre and opens upward — the form of something stretched between two points, alive, with a sag from its own weight. Large flowers with sharp radiating petals sit on the arcs, with leaves scattered between them.

The essential thing here is that some of the leaves break away from the mass and hang lower, singly, on fine threads. A light train forms beneath the composition: flowering is shown not at its peak but at the moment it has already begun to fall. Beauty with a stated term.

By day it is a flowering branch lifted to the ceiling — white with gold, festive graphics. In the evening the petals light up entirely, from within, with no visible source; the gold leaves give pinpoint flares, the white ones stay soft patches. The garland becomes a cloud of light with sparks slowly falling from it.

**Where it works.** A format for formal spaces: lobbies, double-height living rooms, banquet and wedding halls, atria. It asks for length — hung along a table or across a volume, between two fixing points.

**Style.** Romantic Art Nouveau and ceremonial classicism. The message is ceremonial — a piece for a space where people gather for an occasion.

The span of the garland, the number of flowers, the length of the train and the ratio of white to gold are all made to order.

## LC0184 — Light composition

A composition about how light is caught. Here the sources are not hidden inside the elements — they stand separately, on fine gold rods, while clear caps with rolled rims hang around them and gather their light. The relation between light and form is inverted: the form does not glow, it catches.

The caps — broad, crumpled, with a wavy rim — hang at varied levels and are turned at random. The thickness of each wanders, so every one has its own runs and lenses: no two are alike.

By day the composition is all but invisible — read only through highlights, like dew on a web. In the evening the body works as a lens: a bright gold core appears deep inside every cap, and a glowing thread runs along the wavy rim. A cloud of fires, each one caught in its own bowl.

**Where it works.** Living rooms with high ceilings, stairwells, zones above a round table, lobbies. The layering reads only with height to spare — in a low room the composition flattens.

**Style.** Organic modernism and the Scandinavian school: a natural form, warm light, no symmetry. The message is contemplative — a piece looked at long and close.

The overall size, the number of elements, the density of the layers and the tone of the glow are all made to order.
