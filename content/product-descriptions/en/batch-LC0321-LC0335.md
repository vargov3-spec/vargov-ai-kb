## LC0335 — Light composition (Award Winning)

A polar sky carried into a room. The colour moves from deep emerald at the top to clear at the bottom — the way a real aurora melts toward the horizon, having no lower edge at all.

The composition puts itself out. By day it is a dense green-and-blue curtain; in the evening the mass disappears and only thousands of sparks remain, hanging in layers at different depths. An object that loses its body in the evening and leaves the light behind.

The model holds an international award. The intent lies in refusing an edge: the composition does not end, it dissolves.

**Where it works.** A format for large verticals — atriums, stair shafts, panoramic lobbies, double-height living rooms, wherever the height can be shown in full.

**Style.** Contemporary luxury and Nordic Art Nouveau: a cold palette, panoramic glazing, dark noble surfaces. The message is contemplative — the composition gives not light but the state of a northern night.

The height, the density of the threads and the depth of the gradient are all made to order.

## LC0334 — Light composition

Hoarfrost enlarged to the size of a palm. Inside each plate needles radiate from the centre — the drawing winter makes on a window overnight and erases by noon.

Here it is arrested. The light stands exactly at the centre of the pattern and ignites the whole of it, turning every element into a snowflake under a magnifier.

A crumpled edge and a gold border argue with the coldness of the drawing. The piece lives off that argument: ice graphics in a warm setting.

By day the thickness produces powerful distortion: through one plate you see the next, refracted and enlarged — a depth that should not exist in a flat object. In the evening the light stands exactly at the centre of the pattern and ignites the whole of it, turning every element into a snowflake under a magnifier.

**Where it works.** The horizontal format suits a long table, an entrance hall, a meeting room with a high ceiling, a living room with a fireplace.

**Style.** Contemporary classic and softened Art Deco: pale walls, gilded fittings, mirrors. The message is a winter marvel — the composition brings a festive note into an interior without descending to festive decor.

The number of plates, their calibres and the density of the cloud are all made to order.

## LC0333 — Light composition

A collection of vessels that never held anything. Bellied flasks, drawn-out decanters, squat retorts — the forms are taken from craft and released from function.

Light pours into each cavity from below, like a liquid, and stops at the neck. The vessel turns out to be full — not of a substance but of a glow, and that substitution is what the whole thing was conceived for.

No form repeats another: the asymmetry here is a signature rather than carelessness.

By day it is a workshop shelf lifted into the air: the forms read as silhouettes and no two are alike. In the evening light pours into each cavity from below, like a liquid, and stops at the neck — the vessels turn out full, not of a substance but of a glow.

**Where it works.** The cascade narrows downward — a format for stairwells, bay windows, double-height living rooms, wine rooms and restaurant halls.

**Style.** Eclecticism and artisan luxury: exposed brick, wood, antiques mixed with the contemporary. The message is collectorly — a piece for someone who gathers objects with a history and values the trace of a hand.

The number of vessels, their forms and the depth of the cascade are all made to order.

## LC0332 — Light composition

Ice from a river shallow, set on edge. The surface is rough, chipped, full of inclusions — the substance is deliberately left imperfect, because perfect ice does not exist.

The light enters from above and gets caught in the mass rather than passing through. The plate glows not against the light but from within, diffusely, like a real floe under a low sun.

The ring form closes the composition on itself: it has no front and no back, it is equally composed from any side. A crown with every point alight.

By day it is ice from the shallows: a rough chipped surface with inclusions, a piece read through texture and irregularity. In the evening the light enters from above and gets caught in the mass rather than passing through — the plate glows from within, diffusely, like a real floe under a low sun.

**Where it works.** The ring form is made for round tables, rotundas, the zone above a kitchen island, private dining rooms.

**Style.** Nordic minimalism and eco-modern: pale wood, linen, stone, a muted palette. The message is natural honesty — the composition does not pretend to be precious, it pretends to have been found.

## LC0331 — Decorative screen

A border the gaze passes through. The screen does not cut a space off, it separates it into layers: movement is visible behind it but not detail — exactly as much as is needed to keep the zones connected.

The modules are assembled into a rigid grid, but the surface of each is slightly convex and glossy, so the wall shimmers as you move along it. Alternating pale and dark links set a diagonal rhythm — a static structure reads as fabric.

By day the screen draws diamond shadows on the floor; in the evening, lit from the side, the gloss turns it into a wall of mother-of-pearl.

**Where it works.** Zoning without walls: between a living room and a dining room, in an entrance hall, in a restaurant, in a showroom, beside a staircase, behind a reception desk.

**Style.** Contemporary classic and softened Art Deco: the composition likes the company of velvet, brass and mirrors. The message is delicacy — dividing a space without cutting it apart.

The dimensions of the panel, the palette of the links and the density of the weave are all made to order.

The shade wanders from light to dark module by module, and that unevenness makes the surface alive, like old bronze that hands have touched. The gloss catches any source and scatters soft highlights across the wall.

The system can be built to any opening — from a full wall to a pier half a metre wide. This is not a product of fixed size but a language in which a border is constructed.

By day the shade wanders from light to dark module by module, and the surface lives like old bronze that hands have touched. In the evening the gloss catches any source and scatters soft highlights across the wall — the bronze lacework begins to shimmer.

**Style.** Art Deco and Eastern opulence: dark wood, deep colours, an abundance of metal. The message is restrained luxury — bronze lacework that works as a background rather than taking the solo.

The dimensions, the tone of the links and the configuration of the sections are all made to order.

## LC0329 — Light composition (Award Winning)

A gesture rather than an object. A broad band is thrown into the air, twisted and left in the position it was caught in — the whole composition is one movement carried through to the end.

The model holds an international award. The intent is concision: one form, one substance, one gesture — and nothing beyond that.

By day the ribbing works as an optical grating: from one angle the ribbon is almost transparent, from another it flares gold, and as the viewer moves waves run along it. In the evening the light travels the length of the band, emphasising the twist — the gesture becomes a glowing stroke of the pen.

**Where it works.** A horizontal format for large spaces: above a long table, in a lobby, in a meeting room, in a gallery, in a restaurant hall.

**Style.** Contemporary minimalism and gallery aesthetics: empty walls, large planes, no small-scale decor. The message is confidence — a piece that has nothing to prove in order to be the main thing in the room.

The length of the ribbon, the character of the bend and the tone are all made to order.

**Style.** Dark Art Nouveau and brutal opulence: wood, brass, leather, deep tones. The message is drama — the composition works like a shadow that has been given a form.

## LC0325 — Light sculpture

A backwater seen from above. The bowls are laid overlapping, like leaves on still water — with no order and no centre, arranged the way the current arranged them.

Traces of working are left deliberately: scratches, streaks, an uneven patina. The composition is meant to look touched by time rather than to look as though it came off a line.

By day the object stays dark and matte: darkened bronze with the marks of working, scratches and an uneven patina. In the evening the light strikes upward into the underside of the neighbouring leaves and only a glimmer escapes — bronze on the outside, hidden gold within.

**Where it works.** A ceiling or wall format; works as an independent art object: an entrance hall, a private living room, a spa area, a study, a restaurant.

**Style.** Art Nouveau and vintage luxury: antique furniture, dark wood, aged surfaces. The message is noble ageing — a piece that will only improve over the years.

The number of bowls, the character of the patina and the points of illumination are all made to order.

## LC0324 — Wall lights

A form caught at the moment of compression. The body looks crushed by hand — uneven facets, creases, the marks of pressure — and that is the only thing happening in it.

Three versions differ in the character of the crumpling, and no frame repeats another. Repeatability here would be a defeat — the piece is built on every example being the only one of its kind.

By day the body reads as crumpled: uneven facets, creases, the marks of pressure — the piece works through plasticity alone. In the evening the light escapes only around the perimeter, from under the edge, drawing the contour as a fine line, while the core stays dark: a window into a room where the light has already been switched off.

**Where it works.** Entrance halls, corridors, private studies, stair flights, gallery walls — wherever a wall light works as an accent rather than as a source of general light.

**Style.** Brutal minimalism and contemporary art: concrete, rough stone, large empty planes. The message is the force of the substance — an object about the trace of effort being worth more than a smooth surface.

The size, the depth of the relief and the finish are all made to order.

## LC0322 — Light composition

A tropical plant cast whole. The leaves are twisted helically, with a sharp tip and a deep lens in the body — a form taken from nature and carried through to an optical instrument.

Within the thickness of each leaf the reflections of its neighbours gather, and the canopy comes out layered: through the near leaf you see the far one, refracted and enlarged. The green is saturated, botanical, with a cold undertone.

By day it is the opposite: the graphic of the stems and leaves that are almost transparent. In the evening the foliage fills with light while the stems go into shadow — what remains is greenery hanging in the air with nothing to hold it.

**Where it works.** A vertical format made for stairwells, bay windows, narrow double-height spaces, winter gardens.

**Style.** Eco-modern and tropical luxury: live plants, natural textures, warm wood. The message is vitality — the composition brings into a house the sense of something growing rather than something arranged.

The height of the branch, the number of leaves and the tone are all made to order.

## LC0321 — Staircase balustrade

A rare format for the brand: not a luminaire but a piece of architecture. Here the light is built into a structure people run a hand along — and that changes the relationship with it.

The intent is to make an event of the passage rather than of a point. A staircase is usually what you walk through; here it is what you climb for.

By day the leaves work as stained glass: the cold blue reads against the light while the warm metal of the handrail keeps its own temperature. In the evening the light from the treads washes the whole flight in blue, and the contrast of the two lights carries the staircase entire.

**Where it works.** Private residences, boutique hotels, showrooms, galleries — anywhere a staircase should be an event rather than a transit.

**Style.** Contemporary luxury and Art Deco: marble, brass, deep colours, formal proportions. The message is ceremony — climbing a staircase like this becomes a staged gesture.

The length of the flight, the number of elements, the tone of the leaves and the metal finish are all made to order.
