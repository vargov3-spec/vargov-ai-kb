# Product card texts — batch 42: LC0077–LC0091 (15 items)

Approved format: artistic image, day-to-evening shift, "Where it works", "Style", closing line on customisation. Element materials are never named. LC0083–LC0080 are round convex mirrors, LC0079 is an art object.

## LC0091 — Light composition

A composition about a dismantled stained-glass window. Every leaf is assembled from coloured segments joined by gold soldering, and the seams work as veins and as load-bearing frame at once: pattern and structure are one and the same thing here, so the leaf reads even without light.

The forms vary — some broad and five-lobed, some narrow with sharp teeth. The leaves are turned at random and hang at varied levels; below, a few break away and fall alone.

By day it is a thicket of ivy — from grass green to dark olive and almost black, read through colour against the light. In the evening the light passes through the segments, each filling with its own tone — pale green beside bottle green — while the gold seams flare as a graphic net laid over the colour. A crown lit from within.

**Where it works.** Generous rooms — lobbies, atria, winter gardens, double-height living rooms, restaurant floors. Stained glass is made to be seen against the light: the piece is placed where people look up at it.

**Style.** Art Nouveau and the Tiffany stained-glass tradition: a plant motif in coloured mosaic. The message is collector-minded — a piece for an interior that values handwork.

The overall size, the number of leaves, the palette of the segments and the pattern of the soldering are all made to order.

## LC0090 — Light composition

The same technique in a hot palette. Scarlet, brick, orange and ochre — and among them a few single green leaves: the ones that have not yet turned. That single detail converts an ornament into a moment in time: autumn is shown as a process, not a state.

The forms are maple and oak, with deep lobes and sharp teeth. The mass is denser to left and right and thinner at the centre; below, individual leaves fall alone, drawn out into a chain.

By day it is a scattered armful of leaves — dense colour read against the light, a living composition without symmetry. In the evening the palette comes alive: the scarlet segments deepen to ruby, the orange to amber, the ochre lightens to gold, and the gold seams read as veins traced in light. A bonfire broken down into separate embers.

**Where it works.** Generous rooms — lobbies, atria, double-height living rooms, restaurant floors, winter gardens. The saturated palette needs a calm backdrop.

**Style.** Art Nouveau and autumn classicism: stained glass as a way of catching colour. The message is warm — a piece for an interior in a natural range.

The overall size, the number of leaves, the palette of the segments and the pattern of the soldering are all made to order.

## LC0089 — Light composition

A composition about the way soft coral grows. Long curved stems with a swelling at the tip are each turned differently: some hook over, some stay almost straight. This is not ornament but behaviour — it is how everything living reaches toward light, each shoot on its own.

The array is dense, level at the top and deeply sagging below. Among the white mass are a few coral-orange elements, scattered unevenly, like fish in a thicket.

By day it is a coral colony — matte, white, calm, read through silhouette and a few points of colour. In the evening the matte surface diffuses the light and every stem glows along its full length in an even milky tone without flare, while the orange ones fill with dense colour and work as accents. A thicket lit from the depths.

**Where it works.** Generous rooms — lobbies, pools and spas, double-height living rooms, atria. Soft light with no hard points suits rest zones and water.

**Style.** Marine modernism and organic minimalism: growth as the only motif. The message is calm — a piece for an interior where people unwind.

The overall size, the number of stems, the share of coloured elements and the drop heights are all made to order.

## LC0088 — Light composition

A composition about what is inside a flower. From outside the piece is closed — only the white matte surface of the petals, laid in overlapping layers. From within something quite different opens up: a dense net of fine spokes and rods radiating outward, with the light node at the centre. A flower head whose entire core structure is on view.

The form changes completely with the viewpoint: from the side it is a flat disc with a fringe, from below an open flower with a burning centre. One piece and two different objects.

By day the composition reads through form: a white half-open bowl, calm and graphic, concealing how it is built. In the evening the light emerges from the centre through the gaps between the petals, lighting them from within, while the frame casts fine vein-like shadows across them. The flower turns out to be transparent to its own light.

**Where it works.** Living rooms, dining rooms, lobbies, reception rooms, zones above a round table. The principal view is from below, so the piece is hung above an area where people stand or sit rather than pass through.

**Style.** Organic modernism and mid-century: a natural form with its structure exposed. The message is rational and warm — a piece for someone who values how a thing is made.

The disc diameter, the number of petals, the tone of the finish and the drop height are all made to order.

## LC0087 — Light composition

A composition about a current. The mass runs from the upper left corner to the lower right in a broad band, with every leaf turned along that direction. The diagonal is no accident: the horizontal reads as rest, the vertical as falling, and only the diagonal reads as movement.

The large elements gather in the upper part, the small ones below, where the current breaks into single leaves. The colour drifts from golden ochre to deep bottle green, with translucent passages here and there.

By day it is a current caught in a frame: a diagonal band living on colour and inclination. In the evening the light enters the body and spreads across the whole plane of the leaf, filling the green from within and flaring on the gold passages; the cables vanish into darkness and do not read, so the leaves seem carried by the current. A shoal crossing the room on the diagonal.

**Where it works.** Generous rooms and irregular geometry — stairwells, atria, curved halls, double-height living rooms. The diagonal is designed for the specific volume and entrance point.

**Style.** Organic modernism and contemporary installation: movement as the principal subject. The message is dynamic — a piece that leads you through a space.

The length and pitch of the current, the number of leaves, the palette and the drop heights are all made to order.

## LC0086 — Light composition

A composition about structure visible only against the light. Thin plates with a pronounced fibrous structure read by day as simply pale — all their complexity is hidden and opens up only when lit from behind.

Each plate is bowed in an arc, several overlapping to form a single sail. The tiers are laid over one another, overlapping in places. The palette drifts from cool milky grey to warm honey — the transition runs horizontally, left to right.

By day the composition reads as a heap of shells: soft, pale, working through form and overlap. In the evening the light passes through the material and reveals the whole fibrous structure — every plate becomes a map of veins. Outward the light emerges soft and diffuse, without a single bright point: a cloud in which every scale glows.

**Where it works.** Generous rooms with high ceilings — atria, lobbies, double-height living rooms, banquet halls. The tiering needs height: in a low room the layers merge.

**Style.** Organic minimalism and the Japanese aesthetics of paper: light through the material rather than light within it. The message is quiet — a piece for an interior that does not argue.

The overall size, the number of sails, the number of tiers and the palette are all made to order.

## LC0085 — Light composition

A composition about two halves that behave in opposite ways. The left is white, glossy, reflective, without a single pattern. The right is bronze with botanical etching, where branches and grass heads emerge against the light. Between them a mixing zone, where the sails alternate.

The device lies in the fact that the difference is not immediately apparent. By day it is a transition from pure geometry to ornament — the piece reads as a gradient, calm and decorative.

In the evening the halves separate for good: the white reflects the light and stays opaque, only the rims glowing; the bronze transmits it and the etched pattern lights up from within, revealing the whole botanical graphic. One half falls into shadow, the other burns — the composition literally splits in two.

**Where it works.** Generous rooms — lobbies, double-height living rooms, banquet and restaurant halls, atria. The asymmetry sets a direction: the burning half is oriented toward the principal position.

**Style.** Contemporary Art Deco and botanical modernism: minimalism and ornament in one object. The message is contrastive — a piece for an interior that likes surprise.

The overall size, the point of transition, the etching pattern and the number of tiers are all made to order.

## LC0084 — Light composition

A composition about hatching. Short clear rods hang horizontally, the rows running in tiers, curving as they rise and fall. Hundreds of lines oriented one way and layered over each other give the effect of pencil hatching — a volume built out of separate strokes.

Density is uneven: in places the strokes bunch into tight packs, elsewhere they hang alone far from the mass. That is how a hand hatches, not a machine.

By day the composition is all but invisible — the colourless rods read only through highlights, and the drawing in the air is barely guessed at. In the evening the light enters a rod at one end and travels its whole length, so every stroke lights up as an even line. What remains is a drawing suspended in the air — a sketch made in light.

**Where it works.** Generous rooms — lobbies, atria, double-height living rooms, stairwells, meeting rooms. Transparency needs a dark backdrop: against a white ceiling the piece disappears by day.

**Style.** Contemporary minimalism and the graphic school: the line as the only element. The message is intellectual — a piece for an interior that rests on an idea.

The overall size, the number of rods, the wave pattern and the drop heights are all made to order.

## LC0083 — Mirror

A composition about growth rings. The frame is built from concentric rings of varying thickness, and the rings are irregular — they wander in width and sit slightly off centre. This matters in principle: a tree section and ripples on water are never regular, and turned geometry would kill the image.

The mirror itself is convex, and that changes what it is for. It gathers a whole room into a small circle — window, ceiling, furniture, the view beyond — and the reflection comes out compressed, as through a fisheye lens. The piece is not for checking how you look; it is for seeing a space entire.

By day the frame reads through texture: antiqued gold with patina, almost white in places, elsewhere drifting into warm ochre, with fine bluish veining between the rings. In the evening, under side light, the rings throw deep shadows and the frame gains volume — turning from graphics into relief.

**Where it works.** Entrance halls, living rooms, studies, bedrooms, corridors — wherever an accent is wanted rather than a functional mirror. The convexity distorts the reflection: it does not serve for getting ready.

**Style.** Organic modernism and antique classicism: the Flemish convex mirror in a contemporary reading. The message is collector-minded — an object, not a household item.

The diameter, the number of rings, the gilding finish and the depth of the convexity are all made to order.

## LC0082 — Mirror

A composition about a whirlpool. Instead of even rings the frame is wound into a large spiral fold — the surface rotates around the centre like a funnel, or fabric twisted into a rope. The relief is deep, with pronounced crests and troughs.

The opening is not perfectly round but slightly faceted: the frame encroaches on it with uneven projections, so the boundary between relief and reflection is alive rather than drawn with a compass.

By day the piece reads through plasticity: leaf gold with heavy patina, pale and almost white on the crests, drifting into dark bronze in the hollows, with bluish veining between the folds. In the evening, under directional light, the spiral throws long twisting shadows and the frame begins to rotate before your eyes. The convex plate draws the whole room into itself — a whirlpool with a window at its centre.

**Where it works.** Entrance halls, living rooms, boudoirs, private studies, reception areas. The effect depends on side light: allow for a directional source.

**Style.** Baroque plasticity in a contemporary reading: movement set in the frame. The message is dramatic — a piece for an interior that likes strong forms.

The diameter, the depth of the relief, the gilding finish and the shape of the opening are all made to order.

## LC0081 — Mirror

A composition about a single fold. There is no fine detailing here: a broad band wraps the mirror in a circle and turns back on itself, leaving the cut visible. One gesture across the whole frame — and that is the entire intent.

The opening is strongly displaced toward the upper edge and has an uneven, multi-faceted contour. The symmetry is broken deliberately: the piece reads as a shell or a rolled leaf, not as a classical round frame.

By day the frame works through plasticity — pale leaf gold with wear and fine cracks, almost white on the raised sections, drifting into copper in the shadows. In the evening, under directional light, the single fold throws a deep dramatic shadow and the volume doubles in strength. The convex plate gathers the room into a compact reflection with strong distortion at the edges.

**Where it works.** Entrance halls, living rooms, private studies, boudoirs, gallery walls. The asymmetry needs clear space around it: the piece does not sit in a row with others.

**Style.** Contemporary sculpture and organic modernism: one form instead of ornament. The message is spare — a piece for an interior that chooses a single strong detail.

The diameter, the depth of the fold, the displacement of the opening and the gilding finish are all made to order.

## LC0080 — Mirror

A composition about a ripple on water. Soft undulating folds spread from the centre in uneven circles of varying depth and width, welling over one another and never repeating. The frame looks laid by hand rather than cast — this is how water spreads from a thrown stone.

The opening is irregular, with soft lobes along its contour: the frame approaches it as a wave, without a hard boundary. The transition between reflection and relief is blurred.

By day the palette is restrained, almost beige — pale antiqued gold with cool bluish veining deep in the folds and wear on the crests. The object does not read as gilding; more as weathered wood or plaster. In the evening side light brings out the full depth of the folds, and the frame spreads around the compressed convex frame of the reflection.

**Where it works.** Entrance halls, bedrooms, living rooms, boudoirs, corridors. The restrained palette suits bright interiors where gold would be too loud.

**Style.** Organic modernism and wabi-sabi: irregularity and fading as virtues. The message is quiet — a piece for an interior that avoids shine.

The diameter, the depth and number of folds and the gilding finish are all made to order.

## LC0079 — Art object

A composition about a shell enlarged many times over. The form curls in two opposing waves with a deep hollow between them, and close linear hatching runs across the whole surface — annual layers, the grooves on a mollusc valve. Scale turns the familiar into the unfamiliar: what once fit in a palm now fills a corner of a room.

The object gives no light — it is pure sculpture, made for viewing in the round: from every side a different form reads.

By day the colour does the work: from pale pink and cream on the crests to deep coral and near-burgundy in the shadows, with a warmed golden band at the very depth of the fold. The transition is gradual, without boundaries — and it brings out the volume better than any light could. In the evening, under side light, everything becomes a play of folds and shadows: the hatching intensifies, the hollow deepens, and the object reads almost as relief.

**Where it works.** Halls, gallery spaces, private living rooms, showrooms, spas. Viewing in the round means the object is placed at the centre or on a plinth, not against a wall.

**Style.** Contemporary sculpture and organic modernism: a natural form at the wrong scale. The message is artistic — a work of art rather than a furnishing.

The dimensions, the palette and the character of the hatching are all made to order.

## LC0078 — Light composition

A composition about a drawing carried into space. The frame of fine ties joined into an irregular polyhedral net is not concealed — it is the principal form. Triangles of varying size meet at the nodes: a structure that holds nothing but itself.

Glowing points sit at the nodes, and small polished discs are fixed along the ties, turned in different directions. There are exactly enough of them to stop the net being only geometry — and not one more.

By day the object looks like a molecular model or an architectural grid: a transparent structure read through lines. In the evening the light flares at the nodes as stars, the discs reflect and invert it, and a small cloud of secondary highlights forms around each point. The frame is transparent, so foreground and background glow at once — a patch of night sky with its connections drawn in.

**Where it works.** Generous rooms — lobbies, atria, double-height living rooms, meeting rooms, showrooms. The depth reads only when seen through: the piece is set within the volume, not up at the ceiling.

**Style.** Bauhaus and scientific minimalism: structure instead of decoration. The message is intellectual — a piece for a space that thinks.

The overall size, the density of the net, the number of lights and the palette of the discs are all made to order.

## LC0077 — Light composition

A composition about a piece of earth lifted into the air. The volume is entirely covered in preserved moss — dense, velvety, with an uneven living surface; the form is irregular, with projections and hollows. This is not an abstraction but a fragment: a part of forest taken out and hung up.

Openings are cut through the body, framed by metal craters with ragged rims, like a drilled bore. The device rests on a collision of textures: soft matte organic matter that absorbs light entirely, and hard mirrored metal that throws it out.

By day it is a fragment of forest suspended in the air — deep green, light-absorbing, with occasional metallic flashes at the craters. In the evening all the sources hidden inside the mass emerge only through the openings: craters of differing calibre face different ways, and the light spreads unevenly, in patches. Rock with holes burnt through it.

**Where it works.** Generous rooms — lobbies, restaurants, winter gardens, spas, showrooms, meeting rooms. Preserved moss needs no care or watering, but dislikes direct sun.

**Style.** Biodesign and brutalist modernism: living texture against metal. The message is ecological and hard at once — a piece for an interior with a contemporary agenda.

The overall size, the number of craters, their calibres and the metal finish are all made to order.
