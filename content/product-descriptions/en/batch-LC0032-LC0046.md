## LC0046 — Light composition

A composition about flame set in depth. Each drop is twisted on its vertical axis: the wall spirals around the core, the sharp tip staying above and below. The shape of fire with its movement taken away — something that does not occur in nature and can only be made this way.

The mass is packed in tiers with a wavy lower edge; the upper third is given over to the hatching of bare cables with point lights.

By day it is a cluster of amber drops — warm, translucent, with visible thickness, living on the refraction of daylight. In the evening the light runs along the spiral of the twist, reflecting off the walls, and emerges already broken into several bright bands; a glowing core appears within the body, and because the mass is dense the light passes from element to element. A field of small flames.

**Where it works.** An elongated horizontal format — long dining tables, bar counters, lobbies, meeting rooms. The dense mass gives plenty of warm light: the piece works as the main illumination of a zone.

**Style.** The Murano tradition in a contemporary reading: colour and torsion held in depth. The message is warm — a piece for an interior that values comfort.

The length, the number of elements, the depth of the amber tone and the drop heights are all made to order.

## LC0045 — Light composition

A composition about full bloom. The flowers sit close, in whole clusters, with gaps between them — the crown reads as grown rather than set out on a grid. This is a botanical principle: plants do not flower evenly but in sprays, and the author keeps to it.

The form is small, with three or four sharp reflexed petals and an open cup — closer to jasmine or lilac than to a large garden flower. The band sags at the centre and rises at the ends, like a real branch under its own weight.

By day it is a branch in full bloom: pearlescent on gold, bright and festive. In the evening the light passes through the thin petals and lights them from within — they fill with a warm milky tone and take on an iridescent cast at the rim, while the branches pick up the reflection and glow in fine lines through the mass of flowers. A cloud of luminous sprays.

**Where it works.** An elongated horizontal format — long dining tables, bar counters, banquet and wedding halls, reception areas. The sag sets the lower limit above a table.

**Style.** Art Nouveau and romantic classicism: the flowering branch as a classic motif of celebration. The message is ceremonial and bright — a piece for an occasion.

The length, the number of flowers, the density of the sprays and the finish of the branches are all made to order.

## LC0044 — Light composition

A composition about the snowflake. No two are ever alike, and the piece is built precisely on that: more rays here, longer ones there, an off-centre hub elsewhere. Not one element repeats another, and this is not decoration but a condition of truth.

The mass is sparse: there is plenty of air between the elements and the whole depth is visible. The stars hang at varied levels and orientations, some flat, some edgewise.

By day it is a set of crystals suspended below the ceiling: clear rays read through highlights at their ends. In the evening the light enters each ray at its inner end and travels outward, flaring at the tip as a bright point with a radiant halo. Every star gives its own glow, in proportion to its rays — a swarm of flashes at varied depths. Snowfall caught by a camera flash.

**Where it works.** Generous rooms — lobbies, atria, double-height living rooms, banquet halls. The sparseness needs volume: compressed, the composition loses its depth.

**Style.** Nordic modernism and contemporary classicism: winter as motif, the crystal as form. The message is festive and cool — a piece for a light palette.

The overall size, the number of elements, the ray lengths and the drop heights are all made to order.

## LC0043 — Light composition

The same twisted drop read in graphite. The device rests on a division of temperatures: outside everything is cold — grey smoke, steel highlights, white points above; inside each drop is a hot orange core, visible only through the openings of the twist.

The form is identical, the palette its opposite — and the piece changes in mood. Instead of warmth, a smouldering restraint.

By day it is a cluster of smoky drops, almost black against the light, with a mirrored surface: a dark, heavy, graphic mass. In the evening what you see from outside is a dark mass in which dozens of embers are smouldering — narrow orange bands breaking through the twist. A fire cooling under ash.

**Where it works.** An elongated horizontal format — long dining tables, bar counters, private living rooms, lounges in a dark palette. The dark mass gives little general light: the piece sets a mood rather than illuminating.

**Style.** Dark luxury and contemporary minimalism: smoke outside, heat within. The message is intimate and adult — a piece for an evening interior.

The length, the number of elements, the depth of the smoky tone and the colour of the cores are all made to order.

## LC0042 — Light composition

A composition about the vine. The branches curl freely and interweave into a vertical spiral — a vine grows by clinging, and its form always follows a support. Here there is no support, so the vine winds around itself.

Berries of differing shapes, from small and round to large and elongated, are set in dense clusters like real fruit. The composition begins broad at the top and narrows below, where single bunches hang alone.

By day it is a vine with ripened clusters: clear berries mixed with copper-rose, a mirrored surface with a metallic sheen. In the evening light enters each berry at its base and fills it from within — the clear ones flash white, the copper ones fill with a dense warm tone, and each reflects its neighbour in its depth. The clusters signal to one another: a vineyard lit from inside.

**Where it works.** A vertical format — stairwells, atria, double-height living rooms, shafts between floors. The spiral is calculated for the specific height and viewpoint.

**Style.** Art Nouveau and Mediterranean classicism: the vine as one of the oldest decorative motifs. The message is generous and warm — a piece for an interior that speaks of abundance.

The overall height, the number of clusters and the ratio of clear to copper are all made to order.

## LC0041 — Light composition

The same spiral, with flowers instead of fruit. White cups with two or three reflexed petals, opaque outside and glowing within — closer to bindweed or calla, simplified down to a few planes. The author simplifies deliberately: this flower needs no botanical accuracy, only silhouette.

The flowers sit in clusters, facing every direction: some down, some sideways, some up. The spiral narrows below and ends in single buds.

By day it is a thicket of white bindweed on a chromed branch — cool, clean, graphic. In the evening the light emerges through the open mouth as a warm patch and lights the petals from within, so they fill with cream while the outsides stay cool and matte; the chromed branches reflect that light and turn into glowing lines between the flowers. A garland of luminous cups wound into a spiral.

**Where it works.** A vertical format — stairwells, atria, double-height living rooms, winter gardens. The spiral reads as you walk around and climb: the piece is designed for a moving viewer.

**Style.** Art Nouveau and Scandinavian minimalism: a botanical motif pared back to planes. The message is light and bright — a piece for a cool palette.

The overall height, the number of flowers, the density of the clusters and the finish of the branches are all made to order.

## LC0040 — Light composition

A composition about a thicket. The rods are woven freely, with no geometry at all: they bend, cross and separate like branches in undergrowth. Density is uneven — in places the tubes bunch together, elsewhere the branch stays bare.

The tubes point in every direction and at every angle — up, sideways, down — so the composition has no clear silhouette: it reads as a cloud of glowing strokes rather than an object.

By day it is a thicket of budding branches — gold graphics read through lines and highlights on the ribbing. In the evening the light lives at the end of each tube: the ribbing gathers it into fine vertical hatching, while the holders throw sharp flares. Hundreds of points form an unbroken warm field with the gold frame showing through. A bush full of lights.

**Where it works.** An elongated horizontal format — long dining tables, bar counters, meeting rooms, lobbies. Having no silhouette, the piece does not argue with the interior — it works through light, not form.

**Style.** Organic modernism and contemporary classicism: a thicket instead of a chandelier. The message is alive and informal — a piece for an interior with no taste for ceremony.

The length, the number of tubes, the planting density and the metal finish are all made to order.

## LC0039 — Light composition

A composition about a wreath drawn out into a spiral. The laurel is a symbol whose form has always been closed: wreath, ring, circle. The author unwinds it into a vertical, and the sign turns back into a plant.

The leaves sit in pairs and groups: white beside gold, then two gold in a row, then white again — the alternation is free, with no pattern. The spiral narrows below, where single leaves descend alone.

By day it is a wreath drawn into a spiral: white gloss with drawn veining beside mirrored gold, graphic and ceremonial. In the evening the white leaves transmit the light and glow from within in soft milk, while the gold ones reflect and flare sharply; each leaf lights the one below it, and a constant exchange of warm and cool runs through the crown. A laurel that burns in every other leaf.

**Where it works.** A vertical format — stairwells, atria, double-height living rooms, reception rooms. The spiral needs height and a view from several levels.

**Style.** Classical antiquity in a contemporary reading: the wreath without the pathos. The message is ceremonial — a piece for a representative space.

The overall height, the number of leaves and the ratio of white to gold are all made to order.

## LC0038 — Light composition

A composition about what has fallen. The magnolia sheds its petals whole — they lie on the ground uncrumpled and stay beautiful for another day or two. The author takes exactly that moment: an armful of fallen petals lifted into the air.

Each petal is elongated, twisted on its axis and pointed at the tip, each with a slightly different bend — the mass looks alive. Hundreds of elements are laid in layers, level at the top and ragged below; the upper third holds a sparse hatching with small points.

By day it is an armful suspended in the air: milky white, calm, read through silhouette and soft shadow. In the evening the matte surface diffuses the light, each petal glows along its whole length in an even warm tone without flare, and because the layers are dense the glow passes onward. The composition gives soft light with not a single hard point — a cloud lit from within.

**Where it works.** An elongated horizontal format — long dining tables, banquet and wedding halls, lobbies, meeting rooms. Glare-free light is comfortable where people eat and work beneath it.

**Style.** Romantic modernism and contemporary classicism: bloom at the point of fading. The message is tender — a piece for a bright interior.

The length, the number of petals, the tone of the glow and the drop heights are all made to order.

## LC0037 — Light composition

A composition about a vortex. The mass begins broad at the top, twists into a spiral and descends, narrowing to single elements. The cables vanish into darkness and do not read, so the scales seem to hang unsupported, caught in a current.

The elements are small, no larger than a palm, and mean nothing individually — only the mass works. Clear, gold and dark bronze are intermixed: three ways of meeting light in one current.

By day it is the trace left by a whirlwind: a drawing in the air, living on reflections, all but imperceptible. In the evening not every element glows but one in ten — the gold ones give warm flares, the clear ones cold sparks, the bronze ones almost extinguish. Most of the mass stays dark, and that is exactly why the composition reads as movement rather than object: a current of sparks descending between floors.

**Where it works.** A vertical format for tall openings — stairwells, atria, shafts between floors, double-height living rooms. The spiral is designed for the specific height.

**Style.** Contemporary sculptural installation: the composition as an event within a volume. The message is dynamic — a piece that makes people look up.

The height of the vortex, the pitch of the spiral, the number of elements and the palette of finishes are all made to order.

## LC0036 — Light composition

A composition about a shaving. A narrow twisted ribbon with close transverse scoring along its length — a curl taken off by a blade. A form from the workshop, not from nature: it arises only as a by-product of work, and the author makes it the subject.

The ribbons hang vertically, slightly turned, in tiers, level at the top with a wavy lower edge. The upper third is taken up by the hatching of bare cables with cool point lights.

By day it is a shaving taken off a block of ice: clear, read through ribbing and highlights on the coils. In the evening the light enters from above and spreads along the ribbing, so each ribbon glows in strokes rather than as a patch; transparency opens all the layers at once, the front one and everything behind it. The composition reads right through and looks like a mass made entirely of light.

**Where it works.** An elongated horizontal format — long dining tables, bar counters, lobbies, meeting rooms. Transparency needs a dark backdrop: against a white ceiling the piece disappears by day.

**Style.** Industrial minimalism: a production form treated as an aesthetic object. The message is contemporary — a piece for an interior that values honesty of form.

The length, the number of elements, the depth of the ribbing and the drop heights are all made to order.

## LC0035 — Wall composition

A composition about a thrown handful. The elements are laid out in a free circle — denser at the centre, sparser toward the edge, each turned differently. There is no strict grid, only a general circular field: the piece reads as a handful cast onto a plane, not as a set pattern.

The form is an elongated curved grain with faceted sides, slightly twisted, its planes meeting at random angles. Warm polished gold is mixed with cool mirrored silver.

By day the object works through reflection: the polished facets catch the light of the room and give each grain its own band of highlight, and because the facets face different ways the composition shimmers and shifts as the viewer moves. Warm and cool reflect differently, and a temperature rhythm appears across the field. In the evening, under directional light, the wall turns into a field of sparks.

**Where it works.** Feature walls — halls, living rooms, stair landings, reception areas, dressing rooms. The effect depends on directional light: allow for a track or spots.

**Style.** Contemporary Art Deco and minimalism: a scattering rather than a composition. The message is light and expensive — a piece for a wall that needs no subject.

The area of the layout, the number of elements, the calibres and the ratio of gold to silver are all made to order.

## LC0034 — Light composition

A composition about a deep wave. The line drops sharply at the centre and lifts at the ends — not a gentle ripple but a full trough. The petals hang closer along the crests and sparser in the trough, so the wave is stated twice: by form and by density.

Each element is a broad crumpled petal with a rolled edge, each with a slightly different bend. Mirror-bright gold is mixed with matte white; above the mass runs a net of bare cables that sets the volume.

By day it is the crest of a wave: the mirrored petals catch daylight and invert it, the white ones stay calm, and the line reads as plasticity. In the evening the level of light travels along the length of the composition — brighter on the crests, subdued in the trough: this is real chiaroscuro, not an even wash. A current lit from within.

**Where it works.** An elongated horizontal format — long dining tables, bar counters, lobbies, meeting rooms. The trough at the centre sets the minimum height above a table.

**Style.** Contemporary Art Deco and organic modernism: the wave as a classic motif of movement. The message is dynamic and ceremonial — a piece for a table meant to impress.

The length, the depth of the wave, the number of petals and the ratio of gold to white are all made to order.

## LC0033 — Light composition

A composition about emptiness in a setting. Square and diamond frames are closed along their outlines while their centres stay empty — the piece consists of borders and of what is not inside them. Because the middles are hollow, the mass reads right through: behind the front layer you see the second and the third.

The frames are turned at varied angles, threaded through one another in places, overlapping in others; short gold rods with luminous ends are set between them.

By day it is a drawing carried into space: clear faceted outlines mixed with amber ones, read as lines. In the evening the light enters the frame at its fixing points and travels the outline like a lightguide, tracing every figure with a glowing line, while sharp sparks flare on the facets. The frames themselves disappear — what remains is an interlacing of luminous squares.

**Where it works.** An elongated horizontal format — long dining tables, meeting rooms, lobbies, reception areas. Transparency in depth works from the side: the piece is hung at eye level rather than at the ceiling.

**Style.** Bauhaus and contemporary minimalism: geometry without mass. The message is intellectual — a piece for an interior that rests on structure.

The length, the number of frames, the calibres and the share of amber are all made to order.

## LC0032 — Light composition

A composition about the broken link. No ring is closed — a section is missing from each, and it is precisely in that gap that the light bursts out as a bright point. A chain that holds nothing, because every one of its links has been cut.

The shapes vary: some rings are round, some drawn into ovals, some almost hexagonal. They hang at varied levels, threaded through one another to form couplings; the upper half is taken by a sparse hatching of bare cables, with the whole mass gathered below.

By day it is a set of clear links — thick outlines on gold holders, read through highlights. In the evening the light enters at the fixing point and travels the whole ring like a lightguide, tracing it with a fine glowing line, then bursts out as a point at the break. An interlacing of luminous loops suspended in emptiness — a chain assembled out of light.

**Where it works.** An elongated horizontal format — long dining tables, bar counters, meeting rooms, lobbies. The couplings read in the round: the piece is designed for a view from the side.

**Style.** Contemporary minimalism and jewellery modernism: the link as the unit of composition. The message is spare — a piece for an interior that chooses one strong idea.

The length, the number of rings, the calibres and the finish of the holders are all made to order.
