## LC0530 — Decorative composition

A composition about a creature that answers the air. Long trains of tentacles — fine threads strung with pearl beads of differing calibre — respond to any movement of air: the object lives even when there is nobody in the room.

The domes with their soft iridescent cast look as though they had only just risen from the deep. The composition is assembled as a shoal: two or three jellyfish at differing heights above a bedside zone, a family of domes above a staircase.

By day it is white with mother-of-pearl — a quiet, almost monochrome piece read through the plasticity of the domes and the rhythm of the beads. In the evening a warm light comes on inside the domes and the pearls shimmer along the whole length of the trains: the jellyfish glows not itself but through what it draws behind it.

**Where it works.** Bedrooms, bathrooms, spa areas, children's rooms, spaces beside water. The trains set the lower boundary and need somewhere they will not be brushed against.

**Style.** Marine modern and romantic classicism: pearl and iridescence as a palette. The message is tender and quiet — a piece for a private space.

The number of jellyfish, the length of the trains and the density of the beads are all made to order.

## LC0529 — Decorative composition

A composition about germination. Out of a faceted cocoon shoots break upward — twisted, with the texture of a stem just watered; around them, on invisible threads, clear bubbles rise, air released by the root. The moment when a seed stops being a seed.

The cocoon is dense and radiant, like a druse: something that opened rather than something that was opened.

By day it is green shoots above a faceted core with a scattering of bubbles — a piece read through the vertical and the colour, fresh and alive. In the evening light from the cocoon floods the shoots from below and the green catches fire — spring-like, almost edible — while the bubbles catch the highlights and dissolve the composition into the space.

**Where it works.** Staircases, double-height halls, full-wall niches, winter gardens. The vertical story requires height: the shoot has to have room to stretch.

**Style.** Bio-design and Art Nouveau: germination as subject. The message is fresh and optimistic — a piece for an interior that loves life.

The height of the shoots, the size of the cocoon and the density of the bubbles are all made to order.

## LC0528 — Decorative composition

A composition as a scene. One piece contains a whole underwater episode: a ribbed dome the colour of a sea wave, clear tentacles arrested like jets, and a shoal of silver fish scattering from the centre. Not an object but a moment.

The light is built into the dome itself: it descends the tentacles.

By day it is a turquoise dome with a clear fringe and mirrored flashes around it — a piece read through colour and the composition of the scene. In the evening the turquoise lights up as a lagoon, the tentacles carry the light downward, and the fish flare with reflected highlights: the scene comes alive entire.

**Where it works.** Children's rooms, bathrooms, poolside zones, spas. It works solo as an art object or in a group at differing heights — like a bloom of jellyfish in the body of the water.

**Style.** Marine modern and narrative sculpture: the underwater world kept from the literal. The message is playful and alive — a piece for a space that tells a story.

The size of the dome, the length of the tentacles and the number of fish are all made to order.

## LC0527 — Decorative composition

A composition about a slow pulse. Some domes are open, others contracted — the colony is shown in different phases of one movement, the very movement by which jellyfish swim.

Colour is the protagonist: a dense ultraviolet and inky blue, a matte velvet surface, full frills below with the light hidden in their folds.

By day it is a sculptural colony of matte domes: the deep blue-violet reads as volume, a dense and quiet piece. In the evening warm gold lights in the folds of the frills, and the colony turns into a deep-sea garden glowing from under its skirts — darkness above, light below.

**Where it works.** Interiors that are not afraid of the dark — a lounge, a bar, a bedroom with character, a private study. Against a pale ground the deep colour loses its drama.

**Style.** Dark luxe and marine modern: ultraviolet as a palette. The message is intimate and evening-bound — a piece for an interior with a mood.

The number of jellyfish, the shade and the density of the colony are all made to order.

## LC0526 — Decorative composition

A composition about the solid pretending to be silk. The plasticity is deliberately textile: folds, ruffles, softness — the ribs of the domes work as the spokes of a parasol, the frills of the tentacles as foam.

The layered cloud reassembles itself from every angle: now domes, now skirts, now glowing cores.

By day it is a dusty lilac mass with textile plasticity — a piece read through folds and soft colour. In the evening a warm peach light shows through the frills and paints the composition with a sunset gradient: lilac above passing into peach below, and the light quite literally embraces.

**Where it works.** Bedrooms, boudoirs, window displays, boutiques, private living rooms. Soft warm light without glare suits zones where people rest.

**Style.** Romantic modern and Art Nouveau: drapery as a form. The message is tender and feminine — a piece for an interior with a soft palette.

The number of parasols, the degree of pink in the light and the scale of the cloud are all made to order.

## LC0525 — Decorative composition

A composition about a bouquet with no front. The flowers face in different directions and the cluster is equally beautiful from any position — this is how you gather a bouquet meant to stand in the middle of a room, not one arranged for a photograph.

Large glossy flowers with live crimped edges are threaded on gold rods into a dense vertical.

By day it is white gloss: the composition reads through the plasticity of the crimped edge and the volume of the blooms. In the evening the light fills the petals the way the sun fills a fine shell, and the cluster glows with a peach interior — the white turns warm.

**Where it works.** Staircases, double-height spaces, corners that need a soft formal accent. The vertical format takes little floor area.

**Style.** Art Nouveau and romantic classicism: the hibiscus as a tropical motif. The message is dressy and warm — a piece for a light interior.

The number of flowers, the height of the cluster and the colour temperature of the light are all made to order.

## LC0524 — Light composition

A composition about miniature tornadoes. Cones with pleated skirts read as inverted glasses or as the funnels of a whirlwind — a form in which everything converges to a point below.

The composition is monochrome and architectural for it: only transparency, steel and light. The pleating breaks the rays into hundreds of facets.

By day it is a strict triangular swarm: the faceted surface catches light on all its planes, and the piece reads through brilliance and rhythm. In the evening the light inside every funnel is driven along the folds and comes out as a silver radiance — the swarm shimmers like frosty air under a street lamp.

**Where it works.** Above a long table, in a meeting room, in an entrance hall — wherever strict but living light is needed. The triangular swarm is calculated for the length of the zone.

**Style.** Contemporary minimalism and Art Deco: pleating instead of ornament. The message is strict — a piece for an interior that holds its form.

The number of funnels, their calibres and the format of the swarm are all made to order.

## LC0523 — Light composition

A composition in two tempos. The strict architecture of rings — and the weightless, melting dust of rain around it: the piece is a draft drawing and a cloud at once.

Bands of polished gold curve into wide rings, faceted rods hang from them as a dense fringe, and from above the finest beads descend on hundreds of threads.

By day, close up, the precision of the contour and the faceting is visible; from a distance a radiant cloud: two scales of perception in one piece. In the evening light comes from the gold bands and floods the fringe from within, the facets burn warm, and the rain of beads catches the reflections and scatters them — the architecture dissolves into dust.

**Where it works.** Formal spaces — an atrium, a lobby, a restaurant with a high ceiling, a banqueting hall. The rain of beads requires height: it is built downward from the rings.

**Style.** Art Deco and formal classicism: the ring and the pendant as a classic pairing. The message is a statement of status — a piece for a space that declares its level.

The diameters of the rings, the density of the fringe and the area of the rain are all made to order.

## LC0522 — Light composition

A composition about a dive. A wing with a bubbled mass flows into a long gold tail-spike — the form of a swallow at the moment it folds its wings and drops.

The composition is built on diagonals: the tails rule the space with swift lines. No element repeats the tilt of its neighbour — the flock is alive.

By day it is gold lines with clear wings: a piece read through diagonals and bubbles seen against the light, sharp and mobile. In the evening light from the gold body floods the wings from within — the flock flies glowing, and the tails remain dark strokes between the patches of light.

**Where it works.** Spaces with movement — staircases, entrance halls, rooms with high ceilings, galleries. The direction of flight is set along the route from the entrance.

**Style.** Art Nouveau and contemporary sculpture: the swallow as a classic motif of speed. The message is dynamic — a piece for a space people move through.

The number of birds, the direction of flight and the spread of the flock are all made to order.

## LC0521 — Light composition

A composition about foam and water. The matte tenderness of the opal rays and the cold shine of the chrome tail-spikes work as a pair: a soft body and a mirrored needle stitching the flight together.

The flock reassembles with every step the viewer takes — now a dense shoal, now sparse solitary silhouettes.

By day it is milky figures with a warm gradient inside and silver tails — a piece read through silhouette and soft transitions. In the evening the light inside the wings comes on evenly and calmly, without sharp highlights: the composition glows entire. The quietest of the brand's flying pieces.

**Where it works.** Bedrooms, living rooms in a light register, spas, spaces beside water. Even light without glare is comfortable where people stay a while.

**Style.** Marine modern and Scandinavian minimalism: flight without shine. The message is calm — a piece for an interior that does not raise its voice.

The number of figures, the density of the flock and the balance of white to chrome are all made to order.
