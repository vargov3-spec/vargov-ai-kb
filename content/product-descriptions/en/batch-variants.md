## LC0593-2 — Floor lamp

The same bouquet, gathered more quietly. The umbrellas stand closer together and almost at one level — not a scattered inflorescence but a dense head from which the light comes out in a single broad pool. The change in spacing between the discs changes the whole character of the piece: there it was a garden, here it is one flower.

The stems rise from the base in a strict bunch, and the vertical reads cleaner than in the loose version.

By day it is graphic work in pleated discs on golden stems: the relief is read through light and shade, the silhouette is compact and spare. In the evening light from the cores runs out along the folds and gathers into one shared glow — the lamp gives not a scatter of haloes but an even warm circle beside the armchair.

**Where it works.** Beside a reading chair, at a bedhead, in a study, in a private sitting room. The dense head throws its light in a single gather: the piece works not only as an image but as a working source.

**Style.** Art Nouveau and contemporary classic: botany in a restrained reading. The message is intimate — a piece for an interior that has no need to raise its voice.

The height of the stand, the number and tilt of the umbrellas and the finish of the stems are all made to order.

## LC0593-1 — Floor lamp

The same bouquet, opened wider. The umbrellas are spread across the height and turned in different directions — each catches its own angle, and the vertical stops being a stand with lights on top: it becomes a plant reaching several ways at once.

The spread of tilts gives the piece movement: from one point it is a dense crown, from another a sparse branch.

By day it is fine botany at full height: the pleating reads as hatching, the golden stems draw the vertical, the piece is light and tall. In the evening each disc lights up separately and gains a halo of its own — the lamp gives not one circle of light but several small ones, at different heights. A garden standing beside the armchair.

**Where it works.** The corner of a sitting room, the zone by a window, a bedroom, a reception room. Umbrellas facing different ways let the light be tuned to the task: some down for reading, some up for atmosphere.

**Style.** Art Nouveau and organic modernism: growth as the principle of form. The message is warm and alive — a piece for an interior that loves nature.

The height of the stand, the number of umbrellas and the spread of heights and tilts are all made to order.

## LC0545-1 — Light composition

The same bouquet, taken larger. The corollas are scaled up, the garland is loosened, and air appears between the flowers — the piece stops being a dense line and becomes a sparse branch where every flower reads on its own.

The contrast of two characters is kept: the milky white ones hold a matte calm, the smoky clear ones give depth and highlight. In the loosened arrangement that contrast is seen at once rather than discovered on closer looking.

By day it is large corollas in the air: matte beside clear, the piece read through the modelling of the petals and the gaps between them. In the evening the white ones glow evenly, the clear ones let the light through and flare, and the chain of flowers begins to shimmer every other one — a flowering that has a rhythm.

**Where it works.** Sitting rooms, dining rooms, bedrooms, reception rooms, boutiques. A loosened arrangement asks for length rather than height: the composition is built along the zone.

**Style.** Art Nouveau and romantic classicism: flowering as the subject. The message is tender and bright — a piece for an interior in a calm palette.

The number of corollas, the spacing between them and the balance of white and smoke are all made to order.

## LC0543-2 — Light composition

The same fracture, taken off the wall. The wings with their split-rock texture are released into the air and repeated — not one object but a mass: dozens of fractures at different heights, each with its own vertical slit of light.

The device stays the same — it is not the mass that glows but the crack — yet in the plural it reads differently: there it was a single pause, here it is their rhythm.

By day it is a dark sculptural field: the black texture reads as relief, the golden veins outline the breaks, the composition is dense and heavy to the eye. In the evening the slits light up through the whole depth of the mass, and the field turns into rock cracked in a hundred places — the light seeps from within while the volume itself stays mute.

**Where it works.** Lobbies, restaurants, bars, private sitting rooms, gallery spaces. A dark mass asks for dark surroundings: against a light background the contrast stops working.

**Style.** Brutal luxe and contemporary sculpture: the fracture as a module. The message is strong — a piece for an interior with character.

The number of elements, the depth of the mass and the colour temperature are all made to order.

## LC0543-1 — Wall light

The same fracture at intimate size. One element, taken from the mass and hung on the wall at eye level — where the texture is seen close to and the slit of light reads not as a device but as a detail.

The reduction changes the tone of voice: a large composition impresses, a small one invites you closer. The piece is made for short distance.

By day it is a graphic object on the wall: the black texture of the breaks, the golden veins along the edges, nothing more. In the evening the slit lights up as a thin vertical and lays a soft warm halo on the wall around it — the mass stays dark, only the crack glows.

**Where it works.** Entrance halls, corridors, bedrooms at the bedhead, studies. Solo as a single accent, or in a series — then a rhythm of fractures grows across the wall.

**Style.** Brutal luxe and minimalism: the break as form. The message is restrained — a piece for a wall that needs one detail only.

The size, the finish of the wings and the colour temperature are all made to order.

## LC0406-1 — Wall light

One turn of the spiral, taken off the staircase. An arc with its dense fringe of faceted rods is lifted out of the great vertical and hung on the wall — a fragment that was one step in the composition has become a piece in its own right.

The device is kept: the light is woven into the arc rather than hung from it. The structure itself burns, and the fringe only passes the light on.

By day it is a golden arc with a clear fringe: the piece reads through its line and the highlights on the facets, compact and precise. In the evening the arc lights up from within, the rods break the light into sparks, and a glowing step appears on the wall — a fragment of ascent with neither beginning nor end in sight.

**Where it works.** Staircase flights, halls, corridors, reception rooms. Especially apt beside composition LC0406: on a staircase the wall lights carry the spiral along the wall, repeating its pitch.

**Style.** Art Deco and formal classicism: the step as a module. The message is ceremonial — a piece for a formal wall.

The width of the arc, the density of the fringe and the finish are all made to order.

## LC0332-1 — Wall light

One floe, stood against the wall. A single plate is taken out of the ring — rough, chipped, full of inclusions in its depth — and hung vertically: what was a point of the crown in the composition has become a shard of its own here.

The device stays as it was: the light enters from above and lodges in the depth without passing through. The plate glows not against the light but from within, diffused.

By day it is texture and nothing besides: the uneven surface, the chipped edge, the inclusions read close to. In the evening the depth fills with diffused glow while the edge stays dark — a floe under a low sun, set upright.

**Where it works.** Entrance halls, bathrooms, bedrooms at the bedhead, corridors. Soft diffused light without highlights suits places where the light must not dazzle. It also works as a companion to composition LC0332 — the same ice on the walls around a round table.

**Style.** Nordic minimalism and eco-modernism: pale wood, linen, stone, a muted palette. The message is natural honesty — the piece does not pretend to be precious, it pretends to have been found.

The size of the plate, the depth of the texture and the colour temperature are all made to order.

## LC0217-1 — Light composition

The same line, released to any length. The composition is counted in running metres: the ribbon is extended section by section, and the pattern of the wave is composed for the particular room — a flourish with no set size.

The device of the line is kept whole: the ribbing changes density as it turns, and the lights along the edge are spaced unevenly, stressing the bends where the line turns. No section repeats its neighbour, because repetition would kill the gesture.

By day it is a smoky band frozen in the air — almost graphic work, almost a drawing, read through its trajectory and the hatching of the ribbing. In the evening light runs along the edge and scatters over the ribbing in small flecks: what remains is a trace left by light in the dark.

**Where it works.** Long spaces — dining halls, meeting rooms, lobbies, receptions, galleries, corridors. The length is chosen for the zone: the composition can run the whole length of a table or follow the perimeter of a room.

**Style.** Contemporary minimalism and gallery aesthetics: bare walls, large planes, no small decor. The message is a gesture — the composition is about the movement of a hand, not about an object.

The length of the ribbon, the pattern of the wave, the shade and the number of lights along the edge are all made to order.

## LC0192-1 — Light composition

The same magnolia at another hour. The crown is loosened, the branches are drawn apart, and the garden turns from a dense sphere into a spreading branch — the twilight is the same, but there is more air in it.

The proportion of open to closed has shifted: there are more open cups, and the crown stops being mute. There the drama rested on darkness, here it rests on the pauses between the flowers.

By day it is a sculptural branch with a rough cast texture: heavy, muted grey, read through silhouette and gaps. In the evening light comes out of the cups in warm patches, lights the neighbouring petals from within and flares on the gold of the branches — the flowering appears in turn rather than all at once.

**Where it works.** Sitting rooms with high ceilings, stair flights, lobbies, private studies. A spreading branch is built across: it needs the length of a wall or a zone, not height alone.

**Style.** Dark luxe and Art Nouveau in an evening reading: a natural form served as a jewel. The message is intimate and grown-up — a piece for an interior with character.

The spread of the branch, the number of flowers, the depth of the grey and the finish of the branches are all made to order.

## LC0166-2 — Wall light

A handful of seeds left on the wall. A few elements from the great scatter are gathered into a compact vertical bunch — what read as a crown in the composition has become a branch pressed to the plane here.

The ratio of finishes is kept: gold holds the main mass, clear and opaque green are set in as rare accents. At small size that proportion reads more precisely — every inclusion counts.

By day it is a warm graphic silhouette on the wall: elongated grains with sharp ends, read through contour and highlight. In the evening the three finishes part in character — gold flares sharply, clear gives white sparks, green fills with a mute tone and no highlight at all — and the bunch shimmers unevenly, like a branch in the wind.

**Where it works.** Entrance halls, corridors, bedrooms, staircase flights, zones by a mirror. It works solo, in a pair beside a doorway or in a series along a wall — then the wall lights carry on composition LC0166-1, repeating its rhythm on the vertical.

**Style.** Organic modernism with a jeweller's accent: a natural form, an expensive finish. The message is warm — a piece for an interior that loves gold but does not love ceremony.

The number of elements, the ratio of finishes and the dimensions are all made to order.

## LC0166-1 — Light composition

The same scatter, assembled from modules. The composition is counted in elements: each is a finished form, and any number of them makes up a field of the required size. There is no set arrangement — there is a principle from which both an intimate group above a table and a field across the whole ceiling can grow.

A free layout is essential: denser at the centre, sparser at the edges, with a wavy lower edge. Rank and file would kill the image — seeds are not laid out in rows.

By day it is a warm scatter in the air: the elements read through silhouette and overlap, the field calm and even. In the evening the finishes begin to behave differently — gold flares sharply, clear gives white sparks, green fills with a mute tone — and the mass shimmers unevenly, like a crown in the wind.

**Where it works.** Long dining tables, bar counters, meeting rooms, lobbies, retail galleries. The modularity allows the composition to be assembled for any geometry: a dense group, a drawn-out line or a continuous field.

**Style.** Organic modernism with a jeweller's accent: the module instead of ornament, natural form instead of decor. The message is warm — a piece for an interior that loves gold but does not love ceremony.

The number of elements, the area of the field, the ratio of finishes and the height of the suspensions are all made to order.
