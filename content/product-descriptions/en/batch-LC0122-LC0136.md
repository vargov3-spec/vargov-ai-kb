# Product card texts — batch 39: LC0122–LC0136 (15 items)

Approved format: artistic image, day-to-evening shift, "Where it works", "Style", closing line on customisation. Element materials are never named. LC0123 and LC0122 are wall panels, LC0126 is a curtain.

## LC0136 — Light composition

A composition about movement in a circle. The shoal runs in a closed loop around an empty centre — and that emptiness is the point: the fish are circling around something that is not there. The device comes from the behaviour of real shoals, which gather into a torus when they defend themselves.

The ring is not flat: on one stretch the shoal rises, on another it drops, and the loop turns out to be twisted through space. Density is uneven — in places the fish move in a wall, elsewhere they break away alone.

By day it is graphics on the ceiling: narrow forked-tail silhouettes, clear mixed with gold, read through outline. In the evening the level of light shifts around the circle — one arc burns bright, the opposite one is nearly in shadow: the clear bodies give white flares along their rims, the gold ones light up whole. A shoal circling an invisible centre.

**Where it works.** Generous rooms — lobbies, atria, double-height living rooms, pools and spas. The ring reads only from above at a distance: it needs volume, not a ceiling close overhead.

**Style.** Marine modernism and contemporary sculptural installation. The message is narrative — a piece that tells a story rather than decorating.

The ring diameter, the number of elements, the share of gold and the drop heights are all made to order.

## LC0135 — Light composition

A composition about a root. A long winding tube with smooth turns and swellings — a form with no geometry: neither a root nor a stem nor a weed ever has any. The author refuses repetition, and every element is bent its own way.

The mass descends from a round mirrored base and narrows below. The stems interweave and touch here and there: the thicket reads as continuous, yet every individual stem is visible.

By day it is a mass of white coral suspended below the ceiling — dense, calm, monochrome. In the evening the light enters a stem at its end and travels its whole length, and the milky surface diffuses it so that not a point but the entire stem glows, evenly and softly. A thicket lit underwater.

**Where it works.** Living rooms with high ceilings, lobbies, spas, stairwells. The composition builds downward, so what matters is not floor area but clear height beneath it.

**Style.** Organic modernism and marine aesthetics: a line without a single angle. The message is soft and enveloping — a piece for an interior where people rest.

The base diameter, the number of stems, the element lengths and the tone of the glow are all made to order.

## LC0134 — Light composition

A composition about a galaxy in your hand. Thousands of the finest bubbles are sealed within the body of each dish, gathered into a spiral around the centre. This is not decoration but structure: a spiral of air fixed in a clear mass, repeating the pattern by which star systems are built.

The dishes are irregular and asymmetrical, each with its own geometry. There are few of them, they hang at varied heights and are tilted at different angles. The voids between them are as much part of the composition as the elements.

By day it is a set of clear bowls: the spirals of bubbles are visible against the light and the piece reads through detail. In the evening a point lights up at the centre of each dish, the light scatters along the spiral and a luminous galaxy appears around it — denser toward the centre. The light emerges both upward and downward, so table and ceiling are lit at once. A cluster of nebulae.

**Where it works.** Living rooms, dining rooms, meeting rooms, lobbies, zones above a round table. The sparse arrangement does not overload the ceiling — it suits medium heights too.

**Style.** Scandinavian minimalism with a cosmic motif: a simple form with complex content. The message is contemplative — a piece you look into.

The number of elements, the calibres of the dishes, the density of the spiral and the drop heights are all made to order.

## LC0133 — Light composition

The same galaxy taken at scale. Here the dishes are gathered not into a sparse group but into a dense multi-tier mass — several dozen elements, broad at the top, narrowing below.

Density changes the piece in substance: where the smaller version left emptiness between dishes, here one overlaps another and the light passes through two or three layers in a row. The spirals of bubbles lay over one another and give a depth no single element possesses.

By day it is a cloud of clear forms — many-layered, read through highlights, almost colourless. In the evening a sky full of galaxies opens from below: hundreds of glowing spirals at varied distances, the near ones sharp, the far ones blurred. From above, only the net of cables receding to the ceiling. A star chart hung overhead.

**Where it works.** Generous rooms with high ceilings — atria, lobbies, double-height living rooms, stairwells. The layering exists only when seen from below at a distance.

**Style.** Contemporary sculptural installation with a scientific motif. The message is large-scale — a piece for a space that should astonish.

The overall size, the number of elements, the number of tiers and the density of the spirals are all made to order.

## LC0132 — Light composition

A composition about undergrowth. The pods are gathered in groups of three to five, in the manner of an inflorescence — neither singly nor as a solid mass. That is a botanical principle: a plant always grows in nodes, and the author keeps to it.

The column is uneven: it thickens in places and almost breaks off in others. Bare cables with small white beads run between the clusters, working as hatching. The vertical reads as grown rather than assembled.

By day it is a botanical study — milky white mixed with clear green from grass to olive, a clean graphic form. In the evening the sources sit in the nodes rather than in each element, and the glow is distributed in patches: the white pods diffuse it milkily, the green ones fill with a grassy tone. This is how sunlight breaks through real undergrowth — unevenly.

**Where it works.** A vertical format — stairwells, atria, double-height living rooms, winter gardens. It needs height rather than width: the composition builds downward in a single stream.

**Style.** Organic modernism and botanical aesthetics: the plant without stylisation. The message is fresh and alive — a piece for an interior that loves greenery.

The overall height, the number of clusters and the ratio of white to green are all made to order.

## LC0131 — Light composition

The same shoal, but moving in one direction. There is no ring here — a dense horizontal mass, hundreds of individuals in tiers, all heading the same way. The shoal is not defending itself; it is migrating.

The upper zone of the composition is left empty: only the net of cables. The device makes the shoal appear to be moving under its own power rather than hanging.

By day it is graphics on the ceiling — narrow silhouettes read through outline, a silvery subdued mass. In the evening it turns out there are markedly fewer gold fish than clear ones, and they read as individuals that have caught the sun: they light up whole while the rest give only a flare along the rim. This is exactly how a real shoal looks in a body of water — a handful flare, thousands stay shadow.

**Where it works.** Generous rooms — lobbies, atria, pools and spas, panoramic living rooms. The composition is drawn along one axis, so it is oriented along the main line of movement.

**Style.** Marine modernism and contemporary installation: natural behaviour reproduced precisely. The message is calm and narrative — a piece for a space people move through.

The overall size, the number of elements, the share of gold and the drop heights are all made to order.

## LC0130 — Light composition

A composition about a moment. Every element is inclined the same way at the same angle — like grass laid down by wind. That single device turns the object into an event: the composition reads not as a thing but as the instant in which it was caught.

The line sags at the centre and rises at the ends — the arc of a crest. Above it runs a net of bare cables with small glowing points: spray above the wave, broken away from the mass.

By day it is a crest caught in a frame: white feathers mixed with bronze, the pure graphics of the inclination. In the evening the light grazes along each feather and emphasises the angle — the white ones glow evenly, the bronze give warm highlights, the points above scatter as sparks. A wave lit from within.

**Where it works.** An elongated horizontal format — long tables, bar counters, meeting rooms, reception areas. The inclination sets a direction: the piece is oriented along the line of sight from the entrance.

**Style.** Organic modernism and contemporary classicism: movement as the only decoration. The message is dynamic — a piece for a space that does not stand still.

The length, the arc pattern, the number of elements and the ratio of white to bronze are all made to order.

## LC0129 — Light composition

A composition about rain. Fine rods of varying length, each with a large drop at its end, stand in a dense array — you see through them as through a downpour. Hundreds of elements almost touching: the composition does not depict rain, it reproduces it.

The top is level, the centre deeply sagging — the silhouette is funnel-like, as though the mass were drawn down by its own weight. The drops are elongated and heavy, with a perfectly smooth surface: the shape water takes as it falls.

By day it is pure hatching — vertical lines read graphically, almost a technical drawing. In the evening the rod works as a lightguide: it stays almost invisible while the drop at its end flares. The composition becomes a volume filled with hanging luminous points at varied depths — a downpour with its movement taken away.

**Where it works.** Generous volumes — atria, lobbies, stairwells, double-height living rooms, banquet halls. The depth of the array needs height: in a low room the funnel does not read.

**Style.** Contemporary classicism and minimalism: the vertical instead of ornament. The message is ceremonial and austere — a piece for a formal space without excess.

The overall size, the rod lengths, the calibre of the drops and the density of the array are all made to order.

## LC0128 — Light composition

A composition about two directions. Two forms work in opposite ways here: the bells open upward and give their light to the ceiling, the icicles hang down and carry it to the table. One object lights both up and down, and that is not a technical solution but a subject.

The bells are gathered into a dense group at the centre — a field of flowers or a rank of gramophone horns. Icicles with ragged fire-polished surfaces form a fringe at the edges. The tone is amber at varying densities: almost colourless in places, thickening to honey in others.

By day it is a thicket of clear flowers — warm, translucent, read against the light. In the evening the light from deep in the bells rises and settles on the ceiling as a soft warm patch, while the icicles pick it up and carry it down, flaring at their tips. A field from which sparks are falling.

**Where it works.** An elongated horizontal format — long dining tables, bar counters, lobbies, meeting rooms. Light reflected upward makes the composition a source of general illumination, not merely an accent.

**Style.** Art Nouveau and warm contemporary classicism: a botanical motif in an amber palette. The message is hospitable — a piece for a table where people gather.

The length, the number of bells, the length of the icicles and the depth of the amber tone are all made to order.

## LC0127 — Light composition

A composition about the surface of water seen from below. The plates hang almost horizontally, in layers, and the composition is gathered into a broad oval with a sparse centre — this is how water looks when you are beneath it.

The whole surface carries the texture of ripples: close undulating folds of the kind wind leaves behind. The form is oval with uneven edges — no plate repeats its neighbour, because ripples do not repeat.

By day it is a set of ice floes suspended below the ceiling: saturated turquoise, clear and opaque passages intermixed, the piece read through colour. In the evening light is fed into the rim and spreads along the ribbing, so the ripples begin to glow in strokes, while the thickness gathers the light at the edges and traces every plate with a bright line. The gold fixings stay warm points in a cold field. Water lit from the depths.

**Where it works.** Generous rooms — lobbies, pools and spas, panoramic living rooms, dining rooms. The horizontal set reads only from below: the piece is hung over an area where people stand or sit.

**Style.** Marine Art Nouveau and organic modernism: water as motif, colour as argument. The message is cool and refreshing — a piece for an interior in a cold palette.

The overall size, the number of plates, the depth of the turquoise tone and the drop heights are all made to order.

## LC0126 — Light curtain composition

A composition about living fabric. The spacing on every strand is different — in places the elements sit close, elsewhere long empty stretches remain. The irregularity is deliberate: the curtain must read as cloth, not as a grille, and cloth always has gatherings and gaps.

The elements are tumbled and smooth, without facets: copper-rose, clear, with small gold spheres between them. Not one repeated rhythm.

By day the curtain works as a translucent screen — it subdues the view without closing it, and lives on the reflections of daylight. In the evening the light runs down the strands from above and enters every element: the clear ones flare as white sparks, the rose ones fill with a honeyed tone, the gold spheres give sharp star-points. Moving air makes the plane shimmer and shift — a wall of hanging lights that never stands still.

**Where it works.** Zoning and decoration — dividing zones in a living room, a screen at a window, a backdrop in a restaurant, the treatment of a stairwell opening, wedding and banquet halls. The suspended structure is installed without interfering with the walls.

**Style.** Art Deco and Eastern opulence: strands with stones as the oldest way of dressing an opening. The message is festive — a piece for a space where things happen.

The curtain dimensions, the strand spacing, the set of elements and the palette are all made to order.

## LC0125 — Light composition

A composition about a shell that holds light. A broad bowl with a wavy rim and a deep dent at its centre: copper-rose and calm outside, mirrored within. One form, two opposite roles — restrained without, hot within.

The elements hang at varied levels, tilted at different angles, overlapping in places, but with enough air between them: the mass never congeals.

By day it is a cluster of copper forms — warm, quiet, working through soft reflections. In the evening a point lights up inside each shell: the light reflects off the mirrored layer, takes on copper and spreads across the whole inner surface, while the outer face stays rose and even. The mouths face different ways, and the composition looks different from every point.

**Where it works.** Living rooms, dining rooms, lobbies, meeting rooms, zones above a long table. The varied tilts mean the piece is best assembled for a specific plan.

**Style.** Warm Art Deco and contemporary classicism: copper and rose gold rather than yellow. The message is intimate and rich — a piece for a warm palette.

The overall size, the number of elements, the depth of the copper tone and the drop heights are all made to order.

## LC0124 — Light composition

A composition about a knot. Broad ribbons are rolled into loops and figures of eight, twisted freely, each in its own way: some form a closed ring, others break off in a sharp end. The incompleteness is deliberate — a knot still being tied.

The reverse of the ribbons is gold, the face white, with faceted clear fragments set between them. The pyramid is dense at the centre, thinned at the edges, with single elements descending below.

By day it is a tangle of ribbons — white with gold and cold sparks, plastic and volumetric. In the evening three finishes give three different glows within one mass: the light strikes the gold linings and emerges in warm patches, the faceted fragments pick it up and scatter it in hard white sparks, and the white face stays cool and matte. A knot with gold burning inside it.

**Where it works.** Living rooms with high ceilings, lobbies, stairwells, banquet halls. The pyramid needs height — pressed to the ceiling it loses its volume.

**Style.** Contemporary Art Deco and Hollywood glamour: ribbon, gold, shine. The message is dressy — a piece for a space that likes an effect.

The overall size, the number of elements, the ratio of finishes and the drop heights are all made to order.

## LC0123 — Wall panel

A composition about plumage. The feathers are laid overlapping in a ring, their tips facing outward and inward at once: the outer row sets a radiant contour, the inner one closes the middle, leaving a dark opening at the centre. The piece reads both as a wreath and as an open wing curled into a circle.

The palette is built on transition: one part of the wreath in deep blue-green with a metallic sheen, the opposite in warm ochre and gold, with a gradual flow between them. The colour changes around the circle exactly as it does in the plumage of a real bird — not in patches but by shift.

By day the panel works through pure graphics and colour: the finely worked barbs give a ribbed surface that catches the light in bands. In the evening the backlight emerges along the rims and traces every feather with a gold line — the mass gains volume, and you can see where the feathers lie two deep and where only one.

**Where it works.** Feature walls — halls, living rooms, private studies, reception areas. The round form asks for a clear wall with no neighbouring objects: the panel works as the sole object.

**Style.** Art Deco and jewellery modernism: the feather as a classic motif of opulence. The message is decorative and expensive — a picture-piece rather than a luminaire.

The wreath diameter, the number of feathers, the palette and the lighting scheme are all made to order.

## LC0122 — Wall panel

The same device, opened out. The feathers are laid not in a ring but in a spiral: a dense coil above unwinds downward and runs off to the right as a tail, shedding feathers along the way. The last ones hang alone, apart from the mass — movement shown together with its fading.

The palette is olive and gold: from subdued moss green above to warm ochre and old gold in the tail. The transition is gradual, without boundaries, so the spiral reads as a single gesture rather than two halves.

By day the panel works through silhouette and gradient: the ribbed surface of close barbs catches the light in bands and changes with the sun. In the evening gold lines light up along the rims and the spiral becomes volumetric — you can see the layers, the overlaps and the way the tail loses density toward its end.

**Where it works.** Feature walls — halls, stair landings, private living rooms, reception areas, gallery spaces. The open form sits well on a long wall and along a stair flight.

**Style.** Art Nouveau and contemporary decorative sculpture: movement inscribed into a plane. The message is dynamic — a piece for a wall that should lead the eye.

The overall size of the spiral, the number of feathers, the palette and the lighting scheme are all made to order.
