## LC0304 — Light composition

Light hidden inside something that absorbs light. The surface is matte, downy, almost textile — it kills highlights completely, and that is the whole calculation.

Hundreds of points scattered through the mass read against such a background with full contrast, like sparks deep in moss. The composition does not shine — it smoulders, and that is a rare state for a light object.

By day it is a warm patch of colour with a rich texture, a piece you sense by touch rather than by glow. In the evening the mass disappears and what remains is a constellation suspended in the air with nothing holding it.

**Where it works.** Generous interiors with high ceilings: lobbies, lounges, spas, private clubs, living rooms with a fireplace.

**Style.** Eco-luxury and comfortable minimalism: natural fabrics, wood, a muted palette. The message is warmth and shelter — the composition reads as something alive and soft that asks to be touched.

The diameter of the dome, the density of the pile, the tone and the number of light points are all made to order.

## LC0303 — Light composition (Award Winning)

A surface that flows into itself. The form folds and unfolds without a single corner — the tail of a fish, the wing of an insect, a wave at the moment it turns over: everything is recognised at once and nothing separately.

Every scale reflects light at its own angle, so moving along the composition sends waves of highlights running across it. The object does not move, yet it never looks frozen.

By day it is armour — dense, cold, blue-steel in cast, like blued metal. In the evening the light sets the scales alight from within, and the armour turns into a fish heading for deep water.

The composition holds an international award.

**Where it works.** Large spaces: atriums, lobbies, panoramic halls, pools, spa areas, showrooms.

**Style.** Contemporary luxury and biomimicry: large volumes, smooth surfaces, a dark palette. The message is natural mechanics — beauty derived from the way living things are built.

The dimensions, the number of scales and the tone are all made to order.

## LC0302 — Light composition

Stones split by hand rather than worked on a machine. The faceting is deliberately irregular — the piece is about chance being more precise than symmetry.

Inside each element its own lamp burns, and the facets work as prisms: the light comes out in bundles, breaks up, reflects in its neighbours. Varying the drop height gives the scattering volume — a cloud of jewels suspended in the air.

By day it is a collection of minerals laid out on invisible shelves. In the evening it becomes the heat of coals lifted to the ceiling and left there to burn down.

**Where it works.** A flexible format: a compact group above a table or a wide field in a hall, a lobby, a restaurant, a private living room.

**Style.** Contemporary luxury and brutal opulence: rough stone, dark metal, leather. The message is possession — the composition speaks of value directly, without circumlocution.

The number of stones, their calibres and the palette are all made to order.

## LC0301 — Light composition

An artefact raised from the sea floor. An irregular trunk, a porous rough surface with runs down it, gold trumpet-cups grown into it like polyps — the object looks found rather than made.

Hanging strands with hundreds of sparks at their ends give a second light part: the trumpets throw directed light, the strands shimmer diffusely. Two lights with different characters in one object.

By day it is a biological specimen scaled up to the size of a chandelier. In the evening it becomes a reef in a diver's torch beam: you see only what the light has caught, the rest goes off into blackness.

**Where it works.** A horizontal format for long tables, generous halls with character, restaurants, private clubs, galleries.

**Style.** Art Nouveau and natural surrealism: dark walls, antiques, collected objects. The message is discovery — a piece for someone who collects the unusual and is not put off by the strange.

The length of the trunk, the number of strands and the palette are all made to order.

## LC0300 — Light composition

The ripple from a drop that fell a few seconds ago. Rings run out from the centre of each disc and overlay the rings of their neighbours — a drawing that water holds for an instant.

The discs are layered at several levels, so the gaze passes through three or four layers at once and the ripple multiplies against itself. The composition reads through optics alone: there is almost no form, there is refraction.

By day the object is barely noticeable against the ceiling — a transparent cloud that is easier to miss than to see. In the evening it appears in full: the surface of water lit from below, suspended overhead.

**Where it works.** Large horizontal spaces: lobbies, dining rooms, panoramic living rooms, meeting rooms, spas.

**Style.** Minimalism and Japanese aesthetics: emptiness as a material, pale tones, natural textures. The message is contemplation — the composition does not demand attention, it rewards it.

The area of the composition, the number of discs and their calibres are all made to order.

## LC0299 — Light composition

A system of beacons, each with a beam of its own. The cups work as parabolic mirrors: light bounces off the concave surface and leaves as a directed cone — wherever the element is turned.

Because the tilts differ, the composition draws pools of light on the ceiling and walls while at the same time delivering working light downward. It is a rare case of an art object solving the artistic and the technical task with no compromise on either.

By day it is a scattering of gold mirrors, each reflecting the room in its own way. In the evening it becomes a constellation of directed beams that have ruled the space into sections.

**Where it works.** Generous halls and double-height spaces: lobbies, atriums, banqueting halls, galleries, showrooms.

**Style.** Art Deco and contemporary luxury: gilding, mirrors, large planes, formal proportions. The message is theatricality — the composition places accents in a space the way stage lighting places them on a set.

The number of cups, their diameters and the angles of tilt are all made to order.

## LC0298 — Light composition

Gold that lives in the depth rather than on the surface. Foil is fused into the mass — the leaf looks transparent and metallic at once, and the contradiction is never resolved from any viewpoint.

The veins are etched from the reverse and flare under light. Every leaf is turned its own way: some flat, some edge-on, some mid-turn — a fall recorded at a long exposure.

By day it is a herbarium lifted into the air: the dry graphic of veins seen against the light. In the evening the leaves catch fire from within, and the cascade becomes an autumn rain whose gold is not reflected but its own.

**Where it works.** A vertical format for stairwells, double-height living rooms, bay windows, tall entrance halls.

**Style.** Contemporary classic and soft Art Nouveau: warm wood, brass, quiet walls. The message is the generosity of autumn — the composition brings in the state of harvest and late light.

The number of leaves, the balance of clear to gold and the height of the cascade are all made to order.

## LC0297 — Light composition

A glacier at sunset — two states of water in one composition. The blue crystals read as ice, the amber ones as arrested sun, and together they give what the mountains give for ten minutes a day.

The faceting is large, with broad planes, as on an unworked mineral. Every crystal is lit from within, so the colour lives in the mass itself rather than lying on the surface.

By day it is a mineralogical display case — cold, precise, collectable. In the evening it becomes a wall of ice lit through its whole thickness, with something warm sensed behind it.

**Where it works.** A vertical, formal format: staircases, atriums, entrance groups, double-height living rooms, hotel lobbies.

**Style.** Contemporary luxury and alpine opulence: stone, wood, large windows, long views. The message is the grandeur of nature brought down to domestic scale.

The number of crystals, the ratio of the colours and the depth of the cascade are all made to order.

## LC0296 — Light composition

A garland that does not end. The composition is built like a line of text: it runs the length of the table and has neither a beginning nor a full stop — only rhythm.

The rhythm is set by alternation: some leaves glow evenly and softly, others flare with sharp highlights. The light is hidden at the base of each and lifts it from below, leaving the upper side in shadow — foliage as seen by someone standing under the canopy.

By day it is a botanical garland, fine and graphic. In the evening it becomes a ribbon of light drawn across the room with every second leaf alight.

**Where it works.** A linear format for very long tables, bar counters, galleries, banqueting halls, elongated meeting rooms.

**Style.** Contemporary classic with a classical note: laurel, symmetry, noble proportions. The message is celebration — the composition reads as a wreath stretched above the gathering.

The length of the garland, the number of leaves and the balance of the two finishes are all made to order.

## LC0295 — Light composition

A form that is barely there. The elements are so clear that what you see is not them but the pattern of scoring, hanging in the air.

The scoring works as a diffraction grating: light breaks on the ridges into rainbow highlights, and the feather begins to shift colour without a single drop of pigment in it. The rainbow here is not applied — it is computed.

By day the composition reads as a collection of transparent feathers laid out through the air. In the evening point sources ignite the scoring and the group shimmers the way a slick of oil shimmers on water.

**Where it works.** Generous interiors with a quiet background — pale walls, minimal decor around: living rooms, dining rooms, galleries, reception rooms.

**Style.** Minimalism and optical design: clean planes, no ornament, precise light. The message is physics instead of decor — beauty obtained from the properties of a substance rather than from a pattern applied to it.

The number of feathers, their calibres and the density of the group are all made to order.

## LC0294 — Light composition

A flock flying out of light into shadow. Half the feathers glow right through, half work as silhouette — and the transition between them sets the direction of travel.

Because of that the composition appears layered, although every element hangs in one plane. The depth here is optical rather than spatial — a deception that holds from any position.

By day it is a flock caught mid-turn: dark and pale figures intermixed. In the evening the clear feathers light up and the dark ones vanish altogether — the flock thins before your eyes, as though half of it had already gone.

**Where it works.** A horizontal format for long spaces: dining rooms, lobbies, meeting rooms, galleries, restaurant halls.

**Style.** Contemporary classic and graphic Art Nouveau: contrasting combinations, dark wood, brass. The message is movement and departure — the composition is about a flock always being half elsewhere already.

The number of feathers, the balance of lit to dark and the spread are all made to order.

## LC0293 — Light composition

An object with two faces. Outside, white modesty with not a single highlight; inside, solid gold. Walk around it and the piece changes completely, from matte restraint to baroque opulence.

Each cup is tilted so as to reveal its interior only from one particular position. The composition decides for itself to whom and when it will open — and that is built into the geometry, not left to chance.

By day it is a collection of white cups, calm and almost monastic. In the evening the gold flares from within, and the cascade becomes a cloud of warm light in which the white can no longer be seen.

**Where it works.** A vertical format for staircases, bay windows, double-height living rooms, tall entrance halls, private dining rooms.

**Style.** Contemporary classic and neo-baroque: pale walls, mouldings, gilded detail. The message is restraint with opulence behind it — the piece makes no announcement but rewards whoever comes closer.

The number of cups, the depth of the cascade and the gilding finish are all made to order.

## LC0292 — Light composition (Award Winning)

A composition made entirely of reflections. It has no colour of its own and almost no surface — only a mirror in which the interior, the light and the cluster itself converge.

Switched on, the object loses its form: the spheres turn into a gathering of bright patches and it becomes impossible to make out where one berry ends and the next begins. The piece disappears inside its own glow.

By day it is a jeweller's sculpture — precise, cold, every stem legible. In the evening it becomes a cluster of molten metal with neither boundaries nor detail.

The composition holds an international award.

**Where it works.** A vertical format for stairwells, tall entrance halls, bay windows, double-height living rooms, showrooms.

**Style.** High-tech and glamour at once: polished surfaces, glazing, no textiles. The message is shine without apology — the composition was made to impress and does it head-on.

The height of the cluster, the number of spheres and the finish are all made to order.

## LC0291 — Light composition

A spiral as a way of leading the eye. The elements turn around a shared axis and the gaze travels them from top to bottom as it would travel steps — the composition sets its own viewing route.

Every wing is lit along its curve, so the light also moves along a spiral. The piece is built so that standing in front of it is awkward: it wants to be walked around, or passed on the way down a staircase.

By day it is a stream of gold petals frozen mid-turn. In the evening it becomes a vortex with every coil alight and the centre left dark, like the throat of a funnel.

**Where it works.** A vertical format that requires height: stair shafts, atriums, double-height spaces, galleries with a mezzanine.

**Style.** Art Deco and contemporary luxury: gilding, deep tones, grand staircases, large scale. The message is ascent — the composition turns climbing between floors into a passage through light.

The height of the spiral, the number of elements and the intensity of the gilding are all made to order.

## LC0290 — Light composition

Spring taken as a colour. The lime green is cold and young — the shade that stays on foliage no more than a fortnight a year.

The half-transparency with its milky clouding spreads the light evenly along the full length of each element, with no bright patches. Every "leaf" glows entire — like a real one against the light, with the sun behind it rather than in front.

By day it is a stand of sedge lifted to the ceiling. In the evening it becomes a flock of glowing wings held in flight: the form is the same and the image turns into its opposite.

**Where it works.** A horizontal format for long tables, lobbies, wide-plan halls, winter gardens, spas.

**Style.** Eco-modern and Scandinavian freshness: pale wood, linen, live plants, plenty of air. The message is renewal — the composition brings in the beginning of the season rather than its height.

The spread of the flock, the number of elements and the tone are all made to order.
