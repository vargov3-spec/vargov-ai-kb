## LC0410 — Light composition

A composition about a crown. Thick hoops with an inner bubbled texture catch on one another through gold collars — dozens of rings entered into each other like the links of a mail coat. The form is assembled not by suspensions but by its own linkages: it holds itself up.

The bubbles in the mass make the substance alive — it looks arrested at a moment when the movement inside it had not yet finished.

By day it is a massive precious volume: close up you see the precision of the form and the joints, from a distance a dense transparent mass. In the evening light burns in the collars and spreads through the rings, tracing each with a radiant contour: what remains is a glowing crown with not one source in sight.

**Where it works.** Entrance halls, reception rooms, dining rooms, banqueting halls. The dense volume needs height and clear surroundings — other accents beside it are surplus.

**Style.** Jewelled Art Nouveau and Art Deco: the link as a unit of luxury. The message is representative — a piece that sets the level at first glance.

The number of rings, the diameter of the crown and the finish of the collars are all made to order.

## LC0409 — Light composition

A composition about the moment between botany and fireworks. Hundreds of fine stems break out from a single centre and fall away into a full sphere — the form can be read as a flower head, a firework or a shower of sparks arrested in the air. The author does not choose: it is exactly that duality that makes the piece universal.

The stems are bronze in tone, with a drop at the end of each.

By day it is a graphic sphere: fine lines radiating from a point, a piece read as silhouette with almost no mass. In the evening a warm drop lights at every end, the stems go into the dark, and what remains is a swarm of lights hanging as a cloud — an explosion stopped at its best phase.

**Where it works.** Living rooms, dining rooms, lobbies, meeting rooms, and covered outdoor spaces — verandas and terraces. A spherical form has no front: it is placed at the centre of a zone.

**Style.** Mid-century and natural Art Nouveau: the dandelion as a classic of twentieth-century design. The message is festive and light — a piece for an interior without heaviness.

The diameter of the sphere, the number of stems and the density of the flower head are all made to order.

## LC0408 — Light composition

A composition about a wave of sails. The cones, opening downward, are gathered into a relief: a dense crest at the centre, sparse elements at the edges. The composition does not simply cover a space — it draws its shape, setting the height with light.

Two characters in one mass: matte white and honey amber, intermixed without order.

By day it is a wave of pale cones — a piece read through silhouette and the changes in height, calm and plastic. In the evening the white ones glow evenly, the amber ones fill with a dense sunset tone, and the wave acquires a relief of temperature on top of its relief of form: where there is more amber, it is warmer.

**Where it works.** Lobbies, dining rooms, panoramic living rooms, restaurant halls. The cones are aimed downward — the light is soft and diffused, suitable for zones where people stay a while.

**Style.** Scandinavian minimalism and mid-century: the cone as the purest of forms. The message is warm and calm — a piece for an interior that does not argue.

The number of cones, the balance of white to amber and the area of the wave are all made to order.

## LC0407 — Light composition

A composition about a night garden. The flowers read as silhouettes and the light as their core: a dark register is rare in light compositions, and the piece looks found rather than made because of it. The branch might have been cut in a garden after sunset.

The trumpets are graphite-grey with a white glow inside, sitting on dark curved branches and spreading horizontally.

By day it is dark graphic work against a pale ground — a piece read through contour, almost like an ink drawing. In the evening light leaves the bowls as directed warm beams while the bodies stay dark: only the glowing mouths are visible, hanging in the air. Callas that opened at night.

**Where it works.** Interiors with dark walls, lofts, restaurants, bars, private studies. The directed beams let a table or a counter be lit precisely.

**Style.** Dark luxe and Art Nouveau: a botanical motif in a graphite register. The message is dramatic — a piece for an evening interior.

The number of flowers, the length of the branch and the depth of the graphite tone are all made to order.

## LC0406 — Light composition

A composition about a staircase of light. The arcs twist into a spiral from top to bottom, and on each hangs a dense fringe of faceted rods. The form quotes a spiral staircase outright: turns, treads, ascending movement.

The light is woven into the arcs rather than hung from them: the structure itself burns.

By day it is a gold spiral with a clear fringe — a piece read through its turns and the highlights on the facets, alive as you walk around it. In the evening every turn lights from within, the fringe breaks the light into sparks, and the spiral becomes a staircase leading up. Movement seen from below and from every floor differently.

**Where it works.** Formal verticals — atriums, stairwells several floors high, shafts between levels. The spiral reads whole only at full height: cropped, it loses its point.

**Style.** Art Deco and formal classicism: the spiral as a symbol of ascent. The message is ceremonial — a piece for a building rather than for a room.

The height, the number of turns and the density of the fringe are all made to order.

## LC0405 — Light composition

A composition about seeds filled with light. White valves open to reveal a clear core with a scattering of points shimmering inside it — a fruit shown in section, at the moment when everything inside is already ready. Above every bud is a comb of fine plates, like a crown.

Hundreds of buds at differing heights form a living relief — a wave rather than a plane.

By day it is white on white: the composition reads through the plasticity of the valves and combs, through light and shade, without one patch of colour. In the evening sparks flare inside the cores and the opened valves begin to glow from within — a fresh, clean register acquires a second layer.

**Where it works.** Light interiors — living rooms, dining rooms, spas, wedding and banqueting halls. The wave relief requires area: compressed, the composition loses its plasticity.

**Style.** Natural Art Nouveau and contemporary classic: the opened fruit as a motif of abundance. The message is bright and festive — a piece for an interior in a white register.

The number of buds, the shape of the wave and the density are all made to order.

## LC0404 — Light composition

A composition about an upturned basket. Large faceted fruits hang in clusters beneath gold capitals, from which bundles of fine rods project — ears of grain above the fruit. The composition reads as a basket of precious harvest tipped over above the table.

The monochrome of transparency and gold makes the piece formal without being gaudy: two substances, not one surplus colour.

By day it is clear fruit beneath gold capitals — a piece read through faceting and highlights, dense and generous. In the evening the light comes from the capitals and fills every fruit, breaking up in the facets: the cluster fills with light from within the way a real one fills with juice.

**Where it works.** Dining rooms, banqueting halls, lobbies, reception rooms. The dense cluster requires height — clearance is needed beneath it.

**Style.** Art Deco and formal classicism: the harvest as a symbol of abundance. The message is generous — a piece for the table people are received at.

The number of fruits, the tiering and the dimensions are all made to order.

## LC0403 — Light composition

A composition about an explosion stopped at its best moment. From a vertical axis, stem-rays break out in every direction, and at the end of each a bud opens with a sparkling core: a dense core above, elements flying apart at the edges. The firework is caught exactly in the phase of opening, before it began to fade.

The light is born inside every bud — hundreds of shimmering points in a clear centre.

By day it is a white vertical with a gold axis: a piece read through its rays and silhouette, graphic and clean. In the evening the buds light from within with a scattering of sparks, the axis goes into the dark, and what remains is a firework suspended in the air.

**Where it works.** A vertical format — staircases, entrance halls, double-height living rooms, atriums. The spread of the rays extends beyond the footprint of the axis: clearance is needed to the sides.

**Style.** Art Deco and formal classicism: the firework as the form of celebration. The message is ceremonial and bright — a piece for a space that greets.

The height, the number of buds and the density of the firework are all made to order.

## LC0402 — Light composition

A composition about a branch above a bench. The vine winds as a horizontal line and reaches out above the table exactly as a real branch reaches out above a garden bench — along it, not down onto it.

Leaves of two kinds, white gloss and gold, are intermixed; the points of light are hidden at the stalks. White-and-gold botany is the brand's most recognisable formula: the composition is formal without being heavy, rich without being loud.

By day it is clean graphic work: white on gold, read through the silhouette of a leaf. In the evening the light lifts the foliage from below in a warm tone — the gold leaves flare sharply, the white ones glow evenly, and the vine comes alive the way a real one comes alive under a lamp.

**Where it works.** Dining rooms, living rooms, lobbies, restaurant halls, reception desks. The horizontal line is calculated for the length of the table or the zone.

**Style.** Art Nouveau and contemporary classic: the vine as a classic motif. The message is hospitable — a piece for an interior that receives.

The length of the vine, the density of the foliage and the balance of gold are all made to order.

## LC0401 — Light composition

A composition about the plan of a city in three dimensions. The bars are fixed at right angles: horizontals and verticals fold into a volumetric grid in which there is not one accidental line. This is not a chandelier but a spatial frame of light.

Inside every bar runs a veined texture — the only living thing in a strict orthogonal system.

By day it is an architectural grid: clear bars on black rods, read through lines and inner drawing, a technical and precise piece. In the evening the light burns in the ends as cold points — the grid is marked out by its nodes and the volume becomes legible right through. A city plan lifted into the air.

**Where it works.** Lofts, offices, meeting rooms, contemporary interiors with character. The orthogonal geometry needs straight lines around it: in a classical interior the piece argues with the finishes.

**Style.** Bauhaus and industrial modernism: structure instead of decor. The message is rational — a piece for a space that values order.

The dimensions of the grid, the number of bars and the depth of the layers are all made to order.
