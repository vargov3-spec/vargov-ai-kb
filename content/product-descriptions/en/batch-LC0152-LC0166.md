# Product card texts — batch 37: LC0152–LC0166 (15 items)

Approved format: artistic image, day-to-evening shift, "Where it works", "Style", closing line on customisation. Element materials are never named. LC0163, LC0162, LC0160, LC0159, LC0158 and LC0157 are decorative screens; LC0161 is a floor lamp.

## LC0166 — Light composition

A composition about the seed. An elongated grain with pointed ends — a form in which everything is compressed to the limit: it is a pip, a spindle and a drop caught in flight all at once. The author does not specify which, because what all three share is concentration, a readiness to unfold.

The mass is arranged freely, without an array: denser at the centre, sparser at the edges, the lower edge wavy. Gold takes up most of it, with clear and opaque green set in as occasional accents — the way a crown carries leaves of a different age.

By day it is a scatter of seeds in the air: warm, calm, read through silhouette. In the evening the three finishes begin to behave differently — the gold flares sharply, the clear throws white sparks, the green fills with an opaque tone without a single highlight. The composition shimmers unevenly, like a crown in wind.

**Where it works.** An elongated horizontal format — long dining tables, bar counters, meeting rooms, lobbies. The free layout needs length to spare: compressed, it loses its randomness.

**Style.** Organic modernism with a jewellery accent: a natural form in an expensive finish. The message is warm — a piece for an interior that loves gold but dislikes ceremony.

The length, the number of elements, the ratio of finishes and the drop heights are all made to order.

## LC0165 — Light composition

A composition about the crack as ornament. Crazing is usually counted a defect: a net of fine cracks across a surface. Here it is rubbed with gold, and damage turns into drawing. The device is Japanese in spirit — kintsugi, where the break is not hidden but emphasised.

The branches are woven into a long horizontal band with side shoots. The leaves are turned at random: some show a white crazed back, others open a hot gold interior. Every lining is gilded in full.

By day it is a branch with whitened foliage — frost, old glaze, something time has touched. The crazing reads as graphics on white. In the evening the light strikes the gold linings and emerges reflected and warm; the white stays cool while the gold in the cracks catches the light and traces a glowing net across every leaf. A crown with gold burning between its leaves.

**Where it works.** An elongated horizontal format — long tables, kitchen islands, meeting rooms, reception areas. The crazing is seen close to, so the piece is hung lower than usual.

**Style.** Wabi-sabi and European classicism in one: the marks of time presented as value. The message is collector-minded — a piece for someone who values texture over shine.

The length of the branch, the number of leaves, the density of the crack pattern and the gilding finish are all made to order.

## LC0164 — Light composition

A composition about a paper bird. Two triangular sails meeting at an angle, sharp tips, a short keel — the form is folded, not modelled: what you read in it is a crease, not a volume. Origami lifted into the air and multiplied into a flock.

The top of the composition is left empty — only a net of cables with point lights. The device is simple and precise: with no support above, the whole mass appears to hover rather than hang.

By day it is a flock of paper birds below the ceiling — gold and clear intermixed, light graphics. In the evening the difference between the finishes sets the rhythm: gold rolls the light across the whole plane of the sail in warm patches, while the clear wings flare only along their ribs in cold sparks. A swarm whose wings are alight.

**Where it works.** Generous rooms — lobbies, panoramic living rooms, dining rooms, atria. The empty upper zone only works with a high ceiling: otherwise the hovering trick does not read.

**Style.** Japanese minimalism and contemporary sculpture: the fold instead of ornament. The message is light and poetic — a piece for an interior that refuses to look heavy.

The overall size, the number of elements and the ratio of gold to clear are all made to order.

## LC0163 — Decorative screen

A composition about an optical illusion. Every element is presented corner-forward, with three faces visible, and so reads now as a cube, now as a hexagon — depending on where you stand. This is the classic optical game known from parquet and mosaic: a volume that may turn out to be a plane.

The layout of finishes is not subordinated to any pattern — gold with patina, white and graphite are mixed freely. The surface looks alive rather than ornamental: there is no motif, only distribution.

By day the screen works through light, shade and reflection: the faces catch light differently and the plane shimmers of its own accord. In the evening the contour takes over — a fine glowing line traces every face, and the wall becomes a three-dimensional net hanging in the air. The gold cubes give a warm reflection, the white ones glow softly, the graphite ones stay voids.

**Where it works.** Zoning without a solid wall — separating a living room from a dining room, the entrance to a private area, a partition beside a staircase, a screen in a restaurant or hotel foyer. The gaps between elements pass air and light, so space is marked out rather than divided.

**Style.** Art Deco and optical modernism: geometry as the principal subject. The message is architectural — a piece that works with the structure of a space rather than its finishes.

The screen dimensions, the number of elements, the layout of finishes and the lighting scheme are all made to order.

## LC0162 — Decorative screen

A composition about masonry made of grain. Elongated forms with rounded sides — a grain, a pill, a river stone — are laid in dense rows offset by half a step. The author takes the logic of a brick wall and replaces the brick with an organic form: strict construction, soft element.

The gaps are narrow: the screen comes out almost continuous but not solid — air and light pass through the slots. The elements are turned slightly at random, and that stops the row from being machine-made.

By day the surface is calm and almost monochrome — white with occasional gold insets, a wall as a wall. In the evening the backlight emerges from behind the plane along the contour of every element: the white grains read as separate volumes with a halo, the gold ones catch the light across their whole surface and flare. The monolith breaks up into hundreds of outlined forms.

**Where it works.** Zoning generous rooms — a partition between living room and kitchen, a screen at an entrance, a wall in a reception area, a backdrop behind a bar counter. An almost continuous plane gives more privacy than an open-work screen.

**Style.** Organic modernism: a natural form in a strict grid. The message is calm and architectural — a background piece, not an accent.

The screen dimensions, the number of elements, the ratio of white to gold and the lighting scheme are all made to order.

## LC0161 — Floor lamp

A composition about a thicket. The calla is a flower that does not open but wraps itself around: a funnel with a turned rim and a drawn-out tip. The author repeats it dozens of times, sets each on its own rod and gathers the rods into a bundle — the result is not a bouquet but a thicket that has grown on its own.

The flowers run the full height of the column in tiers, turned in different directions and overlapping. The lower half is left bare: only rods on a round disc — stems without foliage, as real callas have.

By day it is a clear sculpture of a thicket — milky white on chrome, cool and graphic. In the evening the light passes through the thin walls of the funnels and lights every flower entire in a soft milky tone; the chromed rods reflect that light and multiply it deep inside the column. A field of flowers glowing from within.

**Where it works.** Living rooms, bedrooms, spas, reception rooms, winter gardens. Soft diffuse light without glare — the lamp works as a source of mood rather than as working light.

**Style.** Art Nouveau and Scandinavian minimalism: a botanical motif stripped of decorativeness. The message is lyrical — a piece for an interior that loves quiet.

The column height, the number of flowers, the tone of the glow and the finish of the rods are all made to order.

## LC0160 — Decorative screen

A composition about weaving. A four-pointed star whose tips converge toward the centre, with concave sails forming between them — joined at their points, the elements give a continuous lozenge net. The device is taken from textiles: the screen is not assembled but woven.

The gold elements are scattered across the white field not evenly but in groups, on the diagonal. The pattern reads as random flashes within the mass, and that enlivens the strict geometry — the net stops being mechanical.

By day the screen works through light and shade: each sail is slightly concave and catches the light its own way, so the plane never looks uniform. In the evening the light emerges through the openings of the net — the white sails diffuse it and glow softly, the gold ones intercept it and throw hard highlights. The fabric begins to shimmer from within.

**Where it works.** Zoning — separating a living room from a dining room, a screen at an entrance group, a partition in a spa, a backdrop behind reception. The open-work net steals almost no light, so it suits darker zones.

**Style.** A Moorish motif in a contemporary reading: the mashrabiya grille without ornamental overload. The message is decorative but restrained — a piece that adorns without dominating.

The screen dimensions, the number of elements, the share of gold and the lighting scheme are all made to order.

## LC0159 — Decorative screen

A composition about the reflected room. Triangular elements with an undulating polished surface are each bowed differently, so the reflections on them stretch and break up as they do on water. The screen does not show itself — it shows what stands before it, distorted.

The main mass is cool, grey with a greenish cast; the gold triangles are gathered not into a pattern but into free diagonal groups — they read as gleams breaking through the metal.

By day it is a mirror without a subject: the screen changes with every step the viewer takes, and it never holds a static reflection. In the evening the light passes through the slots between the faces and flares in the gold cells, while the grey ones stay cold mirrors and go on reflecting the room. The plane lives in two ways at once — by its own light and by borrowed light.

**Where it works.** Zoning — a partition in a lobby, a screen in a restaurant, a divider beside a staircase, a backdrop behind a bar or reception. A reflective surface visually expands a room: it works where volume is lacking.

**Style.** Industrial modernism and brutalist luxury: metal as the principal language. The message is contemporary and urban — a piece for an interior with character.

The screen dimensions, the number of elements, the share of gold and the metal finish are all made to order.

## LC0158 — Decorative screen

A composition about chainmail. The same device of four-pointed elements joined at their tips, but executed entirely in polished gold. Dropping the second colour changes the piece fundamentally: instead of graphics in white and gold you get a continuous metal fabric that works only through light and shade.

Every point is slightly concave, and a complex chiaroscuro appears across the surface — some faces burn, others fall into shadow. The screen reads as forged mail or as scales: armour hung on a wall.

By day it is a dense gold mass living on reflected daylight: the pattern changes with the sun. In the evening the light comes from deep in the net, emerges through the openings, strikes the sails and reflects repeatedly, element to element. The plane fills from within and looks red-hot.

**Where it works.** Formal spaces — a hotel lobby, a reception room, a private study, a backdrop behind a bar counter, a wall inset. Unbroken gold demands restrained surroundings: everything else nearby should stay silent.

**Style.** Art Deco and Eastern opulence: metal fabric as a symbol of wealth. The message is direct and generous — a piece for an interior unembarrassed by richness.

The screen dimensions, the number of elements, the gilding finish and the lighting scheme are all made to order.

## LC0156 — Light composition

A composition about two sides. The element is twisted a half-turn on its central axis, so it shows both faces at once: one white and opaque, the other gold with the texture of leaf. No element is ever wholly warm or wholly cool — the twist will not let you choose.

The bowl is broad and shallow, the mass thinned above and denser below. The elements hang at varied levels and overlap in places — a cloud with a solid floor.

By day it is a cloud of crumpled foil: uneven highlights, a random pattern, the piece read through texture. In the evening the light strikes the gold faces and reflects outward in warm patches, while the white ones stay cool and matte. On one sail you see white, on the next gold — the composition shimmers at the slightest shift of the eye, as though burning in every other element.

**Where it works.** Living rooms with high ceilings, stairwells, lobbies, double-height spaces. The bowl builds downward, so allow for height.

**Style.** Glamorous minimalism and contemporary Art Deco: gold and white, no geometry at all. The message is festive — a piece for an interior that loves shine.

The overall size, the number of elements and the ratio of white to gold are all made to order.

## LC0155 — Light composition

A composition about braking. An open fan with sharp tips and a deep hollow — the silhouette reads both as a bird's tail as it kills its speed before landing and as an opened bud. What the two readings share is opening: the form is shown at the moment it is most fully unfurled.

The elements are turned in different directions and hang in layers. The upper contour is uneven: individual figures rise above the main mass or drop below it, and the flock never looks drawn up.

By day the object reads as a flock stilled below the ceiling — white with gold, a dense sculptural mass. In the evening the light emerges through the open mouths in narrow warm patches: the white bowls are lit from within, the gold ones flare. From outside the elements stay opaque — only the opened sides glow, and the composition looks different from every point.

**Where it works.** Generous rooms — lobbies, double-height living rooms, dining rooms, atria. The directional glow means the piece should be oriented along the principal line of sight.

**Style.** Contemporary classicism with an organic motif: movement caught in sculpture. The message is lively and dynamic — a piece for a space where something is happening.

The overall size, the number of elements, the ratio of white to gold and the drop heights are all made to order.

## LC0154 — Light composition

A composition about a hidden core. An elongated lozenge is twisted on its long axis, and the twist creates pockets — inner folds you cannot see into from outside. It is these that glow, not the elements themselves: the composition is built so that the source always hides in the fold.

The pyramid is dense at the centre and thinned toward the edges, with single lozenges descending below. All are turned at random, and the mass reads as volumetric, with hollows and gatherings.

By day it is a swarm of twisted ribbons — white with gold, a form without light, living on plasticity alone. In the evening the outer surfaces stay opaque while the inner folds fill with light: what burns is not the mass but its core. A cloud with fire hidden inside it.

**Where it works.** Living rooms with high ceilings, stairwells, lobbies, banquet halls. The pyramidal build needs height: pressed to the ceiling, it loses its silhouette.

**Style.** Contemporary Art Deco: the twisted ribbon as the principal motif, white with gold as the palette. The message is ceremonial — a piece for a formal space.

The overall size, the number of elements and the ratio of white to gold are all made to order.

## LC0153 — Light composition

A composition about ash and heat. The outer face is opaque, dark grey and rough — as though covered in ash or oxide. The inner hollow is gilded and polished to a mirror. The contrast is set hard and holds the whole piece on a single device: from outside the object is almost invisible, from inside it is white-hot.

The elements are turned in different directions — some show a grey back, others open a burning interior. The bowl is dense, without gaps: the mass reads as something already burnt through on the surface.

By day the composition is restrained and graphic — a sculpture in unworked metal, a dark silhouette below the ceiling. In the evening the figures vanish into the dark entirely and what remains is a net of glowing gold triangles suspended in emptiness. You see not the form but its lining.

**Where it works.** Interiors in a dark palette — private living rooms, bars, lounges, lobbies, restaurants. The device only works against a dark ground: against a pale wall the grey face stops disappearing.

**Style.** Brutalist luxury and contemporary art: rough texture outside, something precious within. The message is strong and masculine — a piece for an interior built on contrast.

The overall size, the number of elements, the texture of the outer surface and the depth of the gilding are all made to order.

## LC0152 — Light composition

A composition about depth. The tinting is uneven: almost colourless in places, thickening to honey in others, with visible runs in the body. This is not a flaw but a condition — because of that unevenness every element changes the light passing through it in its own way.

Twisted ribbons with sharp ends hang in layers, turned at random. The mass reads right through: behind the front layer you see the second and the third, and the depth of the composition becomes part of its drawing.

By day it is a cloud of crumpled forms — clear, warm, working on the refraction of daylight from a window. In the evening the light passes through several layers and gathers temperature on the way: where it goes through the colourless it stays white, where it crosses dense amber it fills with copper. The source cannot be found — the whole mass glows, and every element works as a lens for its neighbour.

**Where it works.** Living rooms with high ceilings, stairwells, lobbies, double-height spaces. The layering is visible only from the side and below — the composition is built for a specific height.

**Style.** The Murano tradition in a contemporary reading: colour held in the body rather than on the surface. The message is warm and collector-minded — a piece chosen by feel rather than from a catalogue.

The overall size, the number of elements, the depth of the amber tone and the drop heights are all made to order.
