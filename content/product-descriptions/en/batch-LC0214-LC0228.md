# Product card texts — batch 33: LC0214–LC0228 (15 items)

Approved format: artistic image, day-to-evening shift, "Where it works", "Style", closing line on customisation. Element materials are never named. LC0228, LC0223 and LC0217 carry the Award Winning mark. LC0215 and LC0214 are floor lamps.

## LC0228 — Light composition · Award Winning

Marked Award Winning — the badge appears on the brand photography for this item.

A skirt in movement. The tiers are laid in a wave: the hems run not in a circle but along a free line, rising and falling — the same curve fabric takes from a step.

The colour chosen is one on its way out: tea rose caught at the moment of fading. Not a fresh pink but the kind that will turn brown by tomorrow — and that is the whole character of the piece.

By day it is smoky fabric folded in the air — dense, matte, almost textile. In the evening the light runs down into the tubes from above, every tier lights up as vertical hatching, and dark divisions stay between the tiers: a dress put on over light.

**Where it works.** A large format for formal spaces: double-height living rooms, lobbies, banquet halls, atria, theatre foyers.

**Style.** Theatrical classicism and neo-Baroque: velvet, drapery, mouldings, deep tones. The message is a stage gesture — a composition about costume rather than about a luminaire.

The number of tiers, the length and pattern of the wave, the tone and the finish of the hems are all made to order.

## LC0227 — Light composition

A spiral still unwinding. The top is almost empty, the whole mass below — the composition is caught at the beginning of a movement, not at its end.

Every petal is crumpled, like one that has fallen and dried at the edge. The inner face is gilded, the outer matte white: the light strikes the lining and emerges already reflected — warm, soft, without direct points.

By day it is a vortex of fallen petals — white, dry, almost graphic. In the evening gold flares deep within the white mass: two colours live on every element at once, and a cool surface turns out to be lit from inside.

**Where it works.** A vertical format for tall openings: a stairwell, an atrium, a double-height living room, a lift shaft, a bay window.

**Style.** Contemporary classicism and soft modernism: pale walls, gilded details, rounded furniture. The message is the moment of falling — a composition about beauty that has already begun to leave.

The height of the spiral, the number of petals, the pitch of the twist and the gilding finish are all made to order.

## LC0226 — Light composition

Shell and burning core. From outside the element stays white and opaque; inside it burns — the light emerges through a narrow warm slot at the fold.

The flock is not drawn up but scattered: rods of varying length, elements at varying angles, as though the wind had just caught them. The upper zone stays transparent — only rods running to the ceiling.

By day it is a cloud of white paper: folded sheets, wings, sails — the form reads as all of these at once. In the evening only the linings glow, and the swarm becomes a flock burning on the reverse of its wings.

**Where it works.** For large horizontal spaces: lobbies, dining rooms, meeting rooms, panoramic living rooms, banquet halls.

**Style.** Contemporary minimalism and Japanese aesthetics: paper, timber, clean planes, an absence of ornament. The message is origami — something complex obtained by one fold of something simple.

The area of the composition, the number of elements, the density of the flock and the gilding finish are all made to order.

## LC0225 — Light composition

Ripple instead of surface. The mosaic is set so that neighbouring facets face different ways: dense green here, almost white there, drifting into sea blue elsewhere — the colour is not fixed, it fluctuates.

The ribbon is rolled into a free loop and never repeats itself at any point. The only firm line in the whole form is the fine edging along the rim, which is also what holds it.

By day it is a sculpture in broken ice — cold, faceted, scattering highlights. In the evening the turquoise fills from within, the edging flares along the contour, and the ice becomes a wave lit from beneath.

**Where it works.** For living rooms with high ceilings, lobbies, zones above a round table, spas and pools, restaurant floors.

**Style.** Contemporary modernism and a marine theme without literalness: pale stone, water, panoramic glazing. The message is the movement of water — a composition about how the surface of the sea is never level.

The dimensions of the ribbon, the pattern of the curve, the tone of the turquoise and the finish of the edging are all made to order.

## LC0224 — Light composition

A wood in autumn sun. Some elements are almost invisible, others burn amber — the mass never reads as dense, it shimmers in patches, like a crown with low sunlight striking through it.

Inside every element clear and gold are mixed in uneven runs: on some the gold takes almost the whole area, on others it stays a thin vein. No two are alike.

By day it is a cloud of crumpled film — light, rustling, almost transparent. In the evening the light passes through several layers and changes temperature on the way: through the clear it stays white, through the amber it fills with copper — a swarm of burning autumn leaves.

**Where it works.** For living rooms with high ceilings, stairwells, lobbies, zones above a table, winter gardens.

**Style.** Eco-modernism and warm classicism: timber, brass, natural fabrics, a subdued palette. The message is an Indian summer — a composition about the short season when light turns golden.

## LC0223 — Light composition · Award Winning

Marked Award Winning — the badge appears on the brand photography for this item.

A crown that grew rather than being assembled. The petal form is deliberately irregular: rolled, broken, slightly twisted at the edge — not one repeats its neighbour.

Density is uneven: thick at the middle, sparse toward the ends of the branch. That is how real flowering behaves, where resources never stretch to everything at once.

By day it is a branch in bloom — white, light, with occasional gold accents. In the evening the light goes inside the buds: the white shows through in warm milk, the gold reflects in hard highlights, and from below the composition reads as an inflorescence lit from within.

**Where it works.** An elongated horizontal format — for long dining tables, bar counters, meeting rooms, reception areas, wedding venues.

**Style.** Contemporary classicism and romantic modernism: pale walls, brass, a pastel palette. The message is flowering — a composition about spring stopped at its best point.

The length of the branch, the number of flowers, the share of gold buds and the density of the crown are all made to order.

**Style.** Art Deco and contemporary opulence: gilding, vertical divisions, symmetry. The message is a waterfall indoors — the force of an element brought to strict geometry.

## LC0221 — Light composition

A column of bubbles in a glass. The composition looks as though it were rising rather than hanging: the top is held by an even net of cables while the spheres are grouped in clusters, as if pushed up from below.

Each sphere refracts in its own way, so no two are alike. The light hides in only a few and is passed onward: the neighbours pick it up, refract it and diffuse it — the whole mass glows and the sources never read.

By day it is a scatter of honey drops suspended in the air. In the evening the amber gives a warm aura that settles on ceiling and walls: the room ends up inside the glass rather than beneath it.

**Where it works.** An elongated horizontal format — for long tables, bar counters, lobbies, panoramic living rooms, wine rooms.

**Style.** Warm modernism and gastronomic luxury: timber, brass, subdued light, deep tones. The message is a celebration at the table — a composition about that point in an evening when the second round has been poured.

The overall size, the calibres of the spheres, the density of the clusters and the tone are all made to order.

## LC0219 — Light composition

The regular hatching of water and the free movement of living things inside it. The whole idea rests on that contrast: the rain is dense and even, the shoal sparse and unruly.

The fish give no light: the gold ones reflect, the white ones stay silhouettes. What glows is the medium, not its inhabitants — the reverse of the usual device, and memorable for it.

By day the composition reads as a shoal in clear water: you see both the fish and the hatching. In the evening the water lights up and the fish recede into shadow — the medium comes forward and crowds out those who live in it.

**Where it works.** For generous volumes with high ceilings: atria, lobbies, pools and spas, double-height living rooms, children's spaces.

**Style.** Marine modernism and resort luxury: water, white stone, pale timber. The message is that the medium matters more than the figure — a composition about how water defines everything within it.

The overall size, the number of fish, the ratio of gold to white and the density of the rain are all made to order.

## LC0215 — Floor lamp

An Eastern engraving hidden in a mirror. The ornament is matte on a mirrored ground, so it reads only at an angle and changes as you walk around — the pattern appears, then vanishes.

The shade is stepped: three tiers descending in steps, each smaller than the last. The silhouette comes out like a collar or a headdress — a thing with bearing rather than with a shade.

By day the lamp works as a sculptural vessel with a large crown, and the ornament is visible only close to. In the evening the lantern comes on: the lower tier lights the neck, the mirror kindles, and the pattern emerges as a contour — an engraving that appears when it gets dark.

**Where it works.** Living rooms, studies, bedrooms, private rest zones — wherever light is needed without a ceiling fixing.

**Style.** Eastern luxury and Art Deco: deep colours, brass, rugs, carving. The message is a cipher — a composition about a pattern that does not reveal itself at once.

The height, the etching pattern, the finish and the number of tiers are all made to order.

## LC0214 — Floor lamp

The same architecture, translated into geometry. Instead of a flowing botanical motif — a strict repeat: lozenges, meanders, broken bands running in a spiral around the body.

The only difference between the two versions is the graphic on the body, but it decides everything: the botanical drawing gives classicism, the geometric one gives modernity with an Eastern accent. One silhouette, two eras.

By day it is a decorative vessel with a large crown — dense, rhythmic, almost architectural. In the evening the light emerges as even hatching and settles on the shoulders of the vase: a column with engraved metal burning at its foot.

**Where it works.** Living rooms, studies, lounges, private reception rooms, showrooms, hotel rooms.

**Style.** Contemporary Art Deco and Eastern minimalism: sharp graphics, metal, monochrome. The message is order — a composition about ornament as a system rather than as decoration.

The height, the graphic of the repeat, the finish and the number of tiers are all made to order.
