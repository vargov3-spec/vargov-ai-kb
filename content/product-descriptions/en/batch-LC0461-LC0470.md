## LC0470 — Spatial sculpture — light composition

A composition about an explosion stopped in the moment. Faceted boulders — like uncut diamonds — hover among crumpled petals as thin as foil, with live creases. There is no symmetry at all: the piece lives by the laws of nature rather than of composition.

The cables dissolve into the air, leaving pure suspension: the structure is taken out of sight entirely.

By day it is a scattering of gold and silver with clear boulders between them — the mirrored surfaces catch the room and multiply it, and the composition never looks the same twice. In the evening light burns inside the faceting and reflects in the mirrored surfaces, flying apart in rainbows and sharp highlights: it seems the light was detonated from inside and then stopped.

**Where it works.** Lobbies, atriums, double-height living rooms, showrooms, gallery spaces. The chaotic assembly requires free volume — there should be no other accents nearby.

**Style.** Contemporary art and brutal opulence: destruction as a form. The message is bold — a piece for a space that wants to speak.

The number of boulders and petals, the balance of gold to silver and the dimensions are all made to order.

## LC0469 — Decorative composition

A composition about a setting for a firefly. In every drop a round opening is cut, and through it passes a cable with a point of light: the source hangs inside the form without touching it. Light and shell exist separately — the signature gesture of the series.

Hundreds of drops gather into a wave: matte white intermixed with clear green.

By day it is a calm wave of flat forms with windows — a piece read through silhouette and the depth of the bottle tone in the green ones. In the evening the white ones glow softly with borrowed light, the green ones fill with colour, and inside every opening a point of its own burns: the drop becomes a setting rather than a lamp.

**Where it works.** Living rooms, lobbies, meeting rooms, restaurants. The wave is built wide: the composition covers a zone rather than occupying a point.

**Style.** Contemporary minimalism and optical art: form and light as separate entities. The message is intellectual — a piece for an interior that values a device.

The number of drops, the balance of white to green and the shape of the wave are all made to order.

## LC0468 — Decorative composition

A composition about a meadow lifted into the air. Thousands of trefoils gather into a dense vertical cloud, uniform from a distance and breaking up close to into thousands of live leaves, each at its own angle.

Among the whiteness, as occasional sparks, are gold clovers: four-leaf charms hidden in the mass. They have to be found, and that is the whole point.

By day it is a dense white wall read as a texture — a monolithic and quiet piece. In the evening points of warm light come on between the leaves, the gold clovers flare, and rare accents appear in the uniform mass: a meadow with individual blades of grass alight.

**Where it works.** Lobbies, atriums, stairwells, double-height living rooms, reception desks. The vertical cloud is designed for a specific opening and reads from several levels.

**Style.** Bio-design and natural minimalism: mass instead of a bouquet. The message is alive and warm — a piece for a space that wants nature.

The area of the cloud, the density and the number of gold accents are all made to order.

## LC0467 — Light composition

A thinned-out version of the same meadow. The trefoils hover more freely, there is air between the elements, and the cloud breathes — instead of a wall there is a swarm.

The principal difference is in the light: it is now in the bowl of every flower rather than between the leaves. Each becomes a small lantern.

By day it is a light white cloud with occasional gold accents — the composition reads through the silhouettes of individual elements rather than as a mass. In the evening light from the cores ignites every flower from within, and the composition becomes a swarm of fireflies above a meadow.

**Where it works.** Living rooms, bedrooms, dining rooms, meeting rooms. The thinning gives soft diffused light: the composition is comfortable where people stay a while.

**Style.** Natural Art Nouveau and Scandinavian minimalism: the meadow in a thinned reading. The message is light and bright — a piece for an interior in a white register.

The number of trefoils, the density and the share of gold are all made to order.

## LC0466 — Light composition

A composition about a bundle of kindling. The rods are laid as a loose armful and cross at live angles — the way branches lie when caught by a cord: the chaos is judged but not drafted.

Fine ring engraving along every rod reads as threads of hoarfrost grown overnight.

By day it is a clear armful with gold ends: the engraving reads as hatching, and the composition looks wild and jewel-like at once. In the evening light enters at the ends and runs along the engraving in sparks — every rod acquires a dotted glowing line, and the bundle catches fire from within.

**Where it works.** Living rooms, lobbies, dining rooms, restaurant halls. The horizontal cloud is built wide and requires no height.

**Style.** Natural minimalism and jewelled Art Nouveau: kindling presented as treasure. The message is a contrast — a piece for an interior that loves paradoxes.

The number of rods, their lengths and the dimensions of the cloud are all made to order.

## LC0465 — Light composition

A composition about a nest woven out of gold. An oval wreath of interlaced branches carries clusters of drop-flowers — the form reads both as an upturned basket and as a nest: something plaited rather than assembled.

Every drop is tilted at its own angle, and a source looks downward out of each.

By day it is gold plaiting with white clusters — a piece read through the interlacing of the branches and the rhythm of the drops. In the evening light goes down from every flower: the composition delivers genuine illumination and not only an image, and a field of light pools of differing strength appears beneath it.

**Where it works.** Above a table in a dining room, in a meeting room, in a restaurant, in a reception room. The downward light makes the piece a formal chandelier rather than a decorative object.

**Style.** Art Nouveau and formal classicism: the nest and the basket as motifs of abundance. The message is hospitable — a piece for the table people gather at.

The diameter of the wreath, the number of drops and their tilts are all made to order.

## LC0464 — Light composition

A composition about a cut fruit. The scaled shell is built petal by petal, and in the cut a milky interior glows — the piece shows what on a real lychee is visible only once it has been opened.

The branches wind as a horizontal vine, the fruits facing in different directions; pomegranate red is intermixed with white.

By day it is a cluster of tropical fruit on gold — a dense red read through the scaled texture, a bold and rare register. In the evening the milky cuts light up, the red shell stays dark, and the composition burns from within: fruit filled with light.

**Where it works.** Dining rooms, restaurants, bars, private living rooms. Saturated red takes command of a palette — neutral surfaces are needed nearby.

**Style.** Eastern opulence and Art Deco: the exotic fruit as a symbol of abundance. The message is bright and generous — a piece for an interior that is not afraid of colour.

The number of fruits, the balance of red to white and the length of the vine are all made to order.

## LC0463 — Light composition

A composition about a whirlpool. Faceted plates of differing length descend in a spiral cascade along a frame — a vortex twisted from ceiling to floor. The spiral leads the eye downward and does not let go.

The light is woven into the spiral: the points hide between the prisms rather than hanging separately.

By day it is a gold frame with clear faceted pendants — a piece read through its twist and the highlights on the facets, alive as you walk around it. In the evening the whole whirlpool glitters with cold fire: the light breaks up in the facets and is passed from level to level, and the spiral begins to turn before your eyes.

**Where it works.** Staircases and double-height halls. The spiral is designed for a specific height and reads differently from every floor.

**Style.** Art Deco and formal classicism: the vortex as a formal motif. The message is ceremonial — a piece for a vertical that has to impress.

The height of the spiral, the number of prisms and the direction of the twist are all made to order.

## LC0462 — Light composition

A composition about a sunset cloud. The field is gathered into a relief: large veils at the centre, small ones at the edges — the way a real cloud is built, with the mass growing toward the middle.

Corrugated disc-flowers in an amber-gold tone read as mushroom caps or as opened silk parasols — a soft form despite the density.

By day it is a warm field overhead: the corrugation reads through folds and soft chiaroscuro, the composition calm and uniform. In the evening light comes on at the centre of every disc and the corrugation drives it out along the folds in warm rays: the cloud fills with a sunset tone from within.

**Where it works.** Living rooms, lobbies, restaurants, spa areas, banqueting halls. The field is built wide: the composition covers the ceiling of a zone.

**Style.** Art Deco and natural Art Nouveau: the parasol and the cloud as motifs. The message is warm — a piece for an interior in a gold register.

The number of veils, their calibres and the area of the field are all made to order.

## LC0461 — Light composition

A composition about weight that does not press. Voluminous folded forms drawn together at a gold knot read as pouches tied with a cord, or as buds an instant before opening — as something full and heavy. And they hover all the same.

The play of weight against weightlessness is the piece's principal trick, and it rests on one detail: the knot at the top looks like a gathering rather than a suspension.

By day it is dense folded volumes — clear and smoky, read through the plasticity of the folds, a piece that looks substantial. In the evening the light traces every fold along its edge, and the mass becomes outlined rather than filled: the heavy turns out to be empty.

**Where it works.** Living rooms, dining rooms, lobbies, reception rooms, boutiques. Voluminous forms require height — pressed to the ceiling, they lose their plasticity.

**Style.** Contemporary sculpture and Art Deco: drapery as a form. The message is expressive — a piece for an interior that needs a gesture.

The number of elements, the balance of clear to smoky and the dimensions are all made to order.
