## LC0420 — Floor lamp — light sculpture

A composition about a colony. White tees link into a porous vertical structure with voids, bends and inner passages — coral grows exactly like this: not to a plan but by addition. No section repeats another.

The light does not pour out directly — it pushes through in fragments from the depth, through openings and pores.

By day it is a snow-white sculpture read through relief and the shadows inside its voids — a quiet and complex piece. In the evening it begins to breathe: the light comes out unevenly, in patches, and the colony looks like a living organism glowing from within. The floor lamp works solo or as a family of differing heights — and then a whole reef grows up against the wall.

**Where it works.** Entrance halls, living rooms, spa areas, showrooms, winter gardens. Fragmented light gives atmosphere rather than illumination: a second source is needed nearby.

**Style.** Bio-design and organic Art Nouveau: growth as a form-making principle. The message is natural and contemporary — a piece for an interior with character.

The height, the density of the colony and the number of glowing pores are all made to order.

## LC0419 — Decorative composition

A composition about a bow. Broad mirrored bands are tied into loose loops and knots that hover in the air — a gesture from the world of wrapping and gifts, enlarged to the size of a chandelier and stripped of its occasion.

The surface is broken into thousands of triangular facets, so the composition does not reflect, it multiplies: points of light hide in the bends and are multiplied without end.

By day the piece gathers all the light of a space and returns it as a salute of highlights — it works with not one lamp switched on, living only on what is around it. In the evening the sources in the bends add a glow of their own, and the facets begin to fire sharp sparks in every direction.

**Where it works.** Above a table, in a window display, in an entrance hall, in a boutique. The composition depends on its surroundings: it is placed where there is something to reflect and where directed light will strengthen the facets.

**Style.** Hollywood glamour and contemporary Art Deco: shine as a subject in its own right. The message is festive — a piece for a space that wants an effect.

The number of bands, their widths and the choreography of the knot are all made to order.

## LC0418 — Light composition

A composition about a rising vortex. Crescent ribbons wind around a vertical axis like petals caught in a current — the movement is directed upward rather than down, which for a suspended composition is unnatural and therefore noticeable.

White and dusty blue are intermixed, the surface glossy; a chrome axis rules the vertical right through.

By day it is fresh white-and-blue graphic work on chrome — a light, airy piece read through the silhouette of the crescents. In the evening points of light in the bends lift the gloss from inside the stream, and the vortex begins to read as a direction: the glow runs upward, as though the current were accelerating.

**Where it works.** Light interiors — staircases, bay windows, double-height living rooms, spa areas. The vertical requires height: the vortex is built upward and does not read in a low room.

**Style.** Scandinavian minimalism and contemporary sculpture: a cold register, pure movement. The message is fresh and light — a piece for an interior in a cool palette.

The height of the vortex, the number of crescents and the balance of white to blue are all made to order.

## LC0417 — Light composition

A composition about an inverted city. Plates with a cellular honeycomb texture descend in tiers, and the composition reads as a stalactite cave — architecture grown from the top down, with no foundation.

Three finishes lead three parts: whiteness for purity and structure, transparency for depth, gold for warmth and accent. The white upper tier melts into clear, the gold burns at the core.

By day it is a cellular mass read through texture and the transition of tones — a dense, almost architectural piece. In the evening the light lives between the plates and inside the cells: the honeycomb flares as a net, the clear tiers recede into depth, the gold goes white-hot at the centre. A cave of treasure built with a jeweller's precision.

**Where it works.** Generous rooms with high ceilings — lobbies, atriums, double-height living rooms, banqueting halls. Tiering only exists where there is height to spare.

**Style.** Art Deco and natural architecture: the cave as a formal motif. The message is rich — a piece for a space that has to astonish.

The tiering, the share of gold and the dimensions are all made to order.

## LC0416 — Light composition

A composition about a branch under the weight of a harvest. The sprigs bend into crescents — not for beauty but because they carry a load: the form follows the weight of the clusters.

The crystals are amber and clear intermixed — large grains and shards of ice in one bunch, the warm next to the cold.

By day it is a precious branch: the faceted clusters catch daylight and scatter it, a piece read through brilliance. In the evening points at the joints ignite the crystals from within — the amber ones fill with warmth, the clear ones flare cold, and the clusters begin to wink at one another. A harvest that glows.

**Where it works.** Above a long table, in a dining room, in a banqueting hall, above a bar counter. The crescent bend gives different heights above the table — the composition adapts to the zone.

**Style.** Art Deco and formal classicism: the cluster as a symbol of abundance. The message is generous and festive — a piece for the table people celebrate at.

The length of the branch, the mix of crystals and the proportion of amber are all made to order.

## LC0415 — Light composition

A composition about an invisible chain. Clear loops catch on one another like links — the structure holds by its own linkages, and where metal should be there is emptiness.

The collars are bronze in tone with glowing segments inside them; the cloud is horizontal. The dark finish gives the composition graphic quality and weight.

By day it is strict engineering graphic work: clear contours on dark nodes, a piece read through lines and overlays. In the evening light runs along the tubes from the collars and traces every loop with a radiant contour — the chain appears entire while remaining transparent.

**Where it works.** Studies, meeting rooms, lofts, reception areas. Strict graphic work suits working spaces that do not need a decorative accent.

**Style.** Industrial minimalism and brutal opulence: structure as an aesthetic. The message is rational — a piece for an interior that values how a thing is made.

The number of loops, the dimensions of the cloud and the finish of the collars are all made to order.

## LC0414 — Light composition

The same loop stood upright and rendered in gold. The cascade is gathered into a heart, the loops linking into a living mail coat — the same logic of the invisible chain, but now it streams rather than hangs.

The change of orientation and finish changes the character of the piece: the horizontal bronze version reads as engineering, the vertical gold one as ceremony.

By day it is a gold cascade with clear contours — a piece read through its linkages and the highlights on the collars. In the evening the glowing segments ignite the contours and the cascade streams as a waterfall of clear links: the form disappears and only downward movement remains.

**Where it works.** Staircases and double-height spaces: the composition reads from any floor. The heart form requires a frontal view along the axis of the opening.

**Style.** Art Deco and jewelled Art Nouveau: the link as a unit of luxury. The message is formal — a piece for a representative vertical.

The height of the cascade, the number of loops and the density are all made to order.

## LC0413 — Light composition

A composition about a flowering branch above a path. The vine reaches out horizontally and the flowers look down — exactly as a real magnolia branch leans over a garden path. The piece does not depict a tree, it reproduces a situation.

White flowers with large lifelike petals sit on gold branches; a source is hidden at the heart of each. White and gold — the brand's classic in its most botanical reading.

By day it is a branch in bloom: large petals read through light and shade, the gold only holding the structure. In the evening the light in the cores makes the petals translucent from inside and the white stops being flat — the flowering turns out to be full.

**Where it works.** Dining rooms, living rooms, lobbies, restaurant halls. The horizontal vine is calculated for the length of the table.

**Style.** Art Nouveau and romantic classicism: the magnolia as a signature motif. The message is tender and formal — a piece for a light interior.

The length of the vine, the number of flowers and the density of the flowering are all made to order.

## LC0412 — Light composition

A composition about a constellation. The stars are scattered along the branches rather than set on a grid: the branches gather them into a figure the way imagination gathers random points of sky into a drawing. The arrangement is free — the constellation can run horizontally, wrap a corner, descend as a cascade.

Three-rayed elements with a core of light at the centre sit on dark bronze branches curving through space.

By day it is dark graphic work with clear accents — a piece read through the lines of the branches and the silhouettes of the stars. In the evening the dark finish of the branches goes into shadow and only the stars' own lights remain, hanging in emptiness: the drawing of the constellation appears entire.

**Where it works.** Living rooms, lobbies, stairwells, private studies. The free arrangement allows the composition to be assembled for a specific room geometry.

**Style.** Art Deco and contemporary sculpture: the constellation as a motif. The message is poetic — a piece for an interior that loves stories.

The number of stars, the configuration of the branches and the dimensions are all made to order.

## LC0411 — Light composition

A composition about mass. Hundreds of identical capsules — streamlined like grains, caught by a gold band — fold into a wave field with a deep relief. No single element matters here: only quantity works.

The grain form was chosen for its neutrality: it refers to nothing in particular and lets the mass read as a surface rather than as a set of objects.

By day it is a breathing relief beneath the ceiling: identical forms at differing heights read through light and shade, a field with not one accent. In the evening the light burns in the band and fills both halves of every grain with a soft radiance — the surface comes alive entire. A harvest of light suspended above a space.

**Where it works.** Generous rooms — lobbies, atriums, conference halls, retail galleries. The field is designed for an area: the composition covers a ceiling rather than occupying a point.

**Style.** Parametric design and contemporary minimalism: an array instead of an object. The message is architectural — a piece that is a surface rather than a luminaire.

The number of grains, the shape of the wave and the area of the field are all made to order.
