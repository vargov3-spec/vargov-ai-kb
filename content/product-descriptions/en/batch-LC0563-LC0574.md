## LC0574 — Light composition

A composition about nature never repeating itself. That principle is built into the piece itself: every rod is like the section of a mineral, with its own veining, streaks, depth and direction of texture. Two identical elements do not physically exist.

The rods are gathered into black collars and hang as a dense field with a live lower contour. The collars hold the rhythm like a bass line under a melody.

By day it is a collection of sections: close up the piece is examined like a mineralogical case, from a distance it reads as a dark field with an even rhythm. In the evening the light comes on from within and the stone catches fire — warm gold and blue and green seams surface from the depth while the black collars stay hollows. A glowing mountain range.

**Where it works.** Dining rooms, lobbies, bars, studies, gallery spaces. The texture is visible close up, so the composition is not hung higher than necessary.

**Style.** Natural aesthetics and brutal opulence: the mineral as a gem. The message is collectorly — a piece for someone who values substance.

The make-up of the field, the lengths of the rods and the selection of textures are all made to order.

## LC0571 — Light composition

A composition about a molecule. Amber bubbles join polished spheres into clusters — bonds, chains, points of attraction: an enlarged fragment of matter, alive and a little chaotic.

The piece has both scientific precision and artistic freedom: the elements look random and assemble into a considered formula.

By day it is warm volumes with cold chrome nodes — a piece read through structure and reflections. In the evening light comes on inside the amber volumes, the mirrored inner surface turns every bubble into a small furnace with warm reflections, and the chrome between them stays cold: the molecule breathes on contrast.

**Where it works.** Living rooms, lobbies, meeting rooms, showrooms, lofts. The clusters spread through a volume — the composition is designed for a specific space.

**Style.** Scientific aesthetics and mid-century: the structure of matter as a form. The message is intellectual — a piece for an interior with an idea.

The number of clusters, their make-up and their spread through the space are all made to order.

## LC0570 — Light composition

A composition about rain under wind. Here the rain does not fall straight down but at a slant: the elements hang with a slight diagonal offset, and the gold tubes bend into drawn-out loops — the trajectories of drops ruled through the air.

The contour fixes an instant of movement, and inside every loop is a clear drop twisted by its flight.

By day it is gold graphic work with clear drops inside — a piece read through trajectories and the diagonal. In the evening segments of light on the tubes lift the drop along its whole length and it shimmers as though catching the last ray before a storm: an arrested downpour.

**Where it works.** As a horizontal stream above a table or as a dense storm cloud in an entrance hall — the format adapts, the sensation stays. The direction of the wind is set at assembly.

**Style.** Art Deco and natural Art Nouveau: rain as a classic motif of movement. The message is dynamic — a piece for an interior that does not stand still.

The direction of the stream, its density and the number of drops are all made to order.

## LC0569 — Table art object

A composition about an object of the inner circle. The piece lives on a table, a console or a shelf, at arm's length — where the texture is visible and the character can be heard. This is not an accent for a room but a companion.

The folds radiate from the centre in uneven rays — the skirt of a jellyfish or a gramophone horn compressed at the moment of sound. In the smoky version the surface is deep and mirrored; in the version with gold inclusions, thousands of warm particles shimmer in the mass.

By day the object changes constantly with the angle of view and with whatever is happening around it: every facet catches a highlight of its own. In the evening light leaves the core and spreads through the folds from within — the relief appears entire and the object becomes a glowing flower.

**Where it works.** A desk, a console in a hallway, a shelf in a living room, a bedside table. The compact format requires no ceiling space and no wiring.

**Style.** Art Deco and marine modern: the horn as a form. The message is intimate — a piece for a personal space.

The execution, the finish of the core and the calibre are all made to order.

## LC0568 — Light composition

A composition about a bouquet that will never go over. Every flower is open in its own direction — the moment of flowering fixed at its best phase.

The composition is built on a contrast of textures: the milky surface gives softness and an even glow, the clear blue gives depth and highlights, the gold spheres at the cores give precision and rhythm.

By day it is blue with milk on gold: everything reassembles as you walk around — open bowls, the ribs of the folds, flashes off the spheres. In the evening the milky ones glow evenly, the blue ones let light pass right through and fill with depth, and the spheres reflect their neighbours: the bouquet begins to shift colour from within.

**Where it works.** Dining rooms, living rooms, spa areas, reception rooms, wedding halls. Bowls facing in different directions mean it is viewed in the round: the composition is placed at the centre of a zone.

**Style.** Art Nouveau and marine modern: the horn as a flower. The message is fresh and festive — a piece for a light interior.

The number of flowers, the balance of blue to milk and the scale are all made to order.

## LC0567 — Wall light

A composition about a line written in one movement. A vertical loop of polished gold holds two clear drops inside it — the contour sets the geometry, the drop breaks it. The object lives in that balance.

In the mass of the drops there is a scattering of air bubbles.

By day it is a gold line with clear forms inside: a piece read through contour and bubbles against the light, graphic and light. In the evening segments of light lift the drops from above and below, they catch fire from within and the bubbles flare as a constellation — the loop stays dark and what is inside it glows.

**Where it works.** Singly beside a mirror or a bedhead, in a pair flanking a portal, in a rhythmic series along a corridor or a staircase. The vertical accent takes little wall space.

**Style.** Art Deco and contemporary classic: the loop and the drop as a pair. The message is elegant — a piece for an interior with poise.

The finish of the contour, the character of the drops and the height are all made to order.

## LC0566 — Light composition

A composition about ascent. The cascade narrows downward and the piece reads as a current slowly rising, drawing the light up behind it — the movement runs against expectation.

Cold chrome and warm amber work off each other: the contour rules strict verticals and arcs, the drops fill them with a dense glow. Each is twisted its own way — there are no two identical movements in a current.

By day it is chrome loops with amber drops inside: a piece read through the graphic of the contour and the colour of the drops. In the evening the drops fill with honey, the chrome goes into shadow, and what remains is a single stream of warm points rising from the bottom up.

**Where it works.** Vertical spaces — stairwells, double-height entrance halls, atriums, where upward movement can be shown at full height.

**Style.** Contemporary Art Deco and sculptural modernism: the rising current as subject. The message is optimistic — a piece for a vertical that leads the eye upward.

The height of the cascade, the number of elements and the ratio of chrome to amber are all made to order.

## LC0565 — Wall light

A composition about a branch growing up a wall. The flowers open in different directions, the trumpet stems fly apart in a free graphic — the vertical reads as a plant rather than as a composition.

Among the white flowers there is one smoky and clear: a shadow in the bouquet that gives it depth. Without it the branch would be flat.

By day it is fine plasticity and chrome graphic work: white flowers with a live uneven edge read as anemones or poppies, and the smoky one works as a hollow. In the evening a soft light leaves the mirrored hemispheres at the cores and settles as haloes around every corolla — warm islands of light appear on the wall.

**Where it works.** Wall piers, walls beside a bedhead, corridors, stair flights, hallways. The vertical branch sits on a narrow surface where a wide wall light would not fit.

**Style.** Art Nouveau and contemporary classic: the branch as a classic wall motif. The message is alive — a piece for an interior that loves botany.

The number of flowers, the inclusion of smoky accents and the height are all made to order.

## LC0564 — Wall light — sculptural composition

A composition about living matter. A band of mirrored gold with ragged edges is stitched with cells — the skeleton of a sea sponge; inside the cells sit milky bubbles, like air caught in rock. It seems the structure is still growing on the wall.

The light is its breathing: it burns inside and comes out through the openings.

By day it is a gold relief with milky inserts: a piece read through cellular structure and a ragged contour, tactile and complex. In the evening light leaves through the openings as warm patches and sharp gold highlights — the coral begins to glow by its pores.

**Where it works.** Singly as a concentrated art accent or in a series — and then the coral spreads across the wall as a colony. Entrance halls, living rooms, spa areas, reception rooms.

**Style.** Bio-design and marine modern: the sponge as a form with no author. The message is natural and tactile — a piece for an interior with character.

The size, the configuration of the cells and the number of elements in a series are all made to order.

## LC0563 — Wall light — sculptural composition

A composition about a pen drawing. The light travels along the edges of the folds and gathers into a shimmer of contour: the silhouette of the wall light is drawn in light rather than flooded with it.

Two corrugated smoky fans meet on a chrome sphere — the point of attraction for the whole composition; gold sparks are scattered through the mass of the fans.

By day the undulating relief makes the surface alive: with every step the reflections reassemble, the gold inclusions flare and fade, and the depth of the smoky tone opens anew. In the evening the edges of the folds light up and the piece becomes a contour drawing on the wall — the volume goes, the line remains.

**Where it works.** Hallways, living rooms, bedrooms, corridors. In a paired version two wall lights enter into a dialogue and set one rhythm on a wall — no longer a luminaire but a wall sculpture.

**Style.** Art Deco and contemporary sculpture: the fold and the sphere as the entire vocabulary. The message is expressive — a piece for a wall that needs an accent.

The execution of the fans, their number and the composition on the wall are all made to order.
