## LC0400 — Light composition

A composition about the last drops. The cascade is gathered into a heart: a dense core at the centre, sparse elements at the edges and solitary spindles hanging lower than the rest — like the last drops of a retreating rain. It is exactly those strays that make the form alive.

Faceted lozenges with sharp noses repeat the finials of old balusters; between them rise fine gold candles. The classic formula of a formal chandelier is translated into a contemporary language: no tinsel, only facets, light and a precise line.

By day it is a crystalline mass — dense from one position, an airy scattering from another; a piece read through faceting and highlights. In the evening every spindle catches the light of the candle beside it and flares from within with a cold brilliance — the cascade acquires a depth invisible by day.

**Where it works.** Formal spaces — entrance halls, dining rooms, stairwells, banqueting halls. The heart form requires a view from the front and from below: the composition is hung on the axis of the room.

**Style.** Contemporary classic with an Art Deco note: a quotation from a formal chandelier, cleared of ornament. The message is representative — a piece for an interior that greets a guest.

The number of spindles, the depth of the cascade and the density of the candles are all made to order.

## LC0399 — Light composition

A composition about a bonfire drawn out along the ceiling. Every element is twisted helically: a white gloss face on one side, polished gold on the other. The light hides in the bend itself, and either the white flank or the gold reverse lights up — depending on where you look from.

The field is horizontal, dense, with a sagging centre. The alternation of white and gold creates a shimmer even in a motionless composition.

By day it is graphic work: white and gold bands read through the light and shade of the bends, a dense and dressy piece. In the evening the composition begins to seem alive, crackling — the light runs about the flanks, and no two viewpoints give the same drawing. A bonfire drawn out above the table.

**Where it works.** A long table, a bar counter, a lobby — anywhere that needs a warm horizontal accent. The light goes down and to the side: the composition lights the table and the faces.

**Style.** Contemporary Art Deco and Hollywood glamour: white with gold, no geometry. The message is hospitable — a piece for a table people sit at for a long time.

The number of elements, the balance of white to gold and the length of the field are all made to order.

## LC0398 — Light composition

A composition about a city seen from above. The mass reads as blocks of differing height, narrow alleys between them, a light in every window. The lower edge is not levelled — it steps downward like the terrain the city was built on.

Inside every block lives a flowing texture, and above it rises a fine candle of light: the light enters from the top and passes right through the block.

By day it is an architectural model under the ceiling: pink-cognac blocks read through volume and inner drawing. In the evening the facets light up amber and the mass turns into a city seen at night from a plane. A second execution is clear instead of smoky: the same architecture, but cold, glacial, almost technical.

**Where it works.** Lobbies, meeting rooms, long dining rooms, reception desks. The stepped underside gives different heights above a zone — the composition is designed for a specific plan.

**Style.** Urban modernism and brutal opulence: the city as an ornament. The message is contemporary — a piece for an interior with an urban agenda.

The number of blocks, the depth of tone and the dimensions of the mass are all made to order.

## LC0397 — Light composition

A composition about a dragonfly's wing. The finest longitudinal hatching on every leaf is not decor but structure: on a real wing these are the veins that hold the transparent film. The author uses that literally — in the evening the light travels along exactly that hatching.

The branches interlace, the feathers point in different directions, the density grows toward the centre: the composition reads as a thicket rather than a bouquet.

By day, from a distance, it is a pink cloud; up close a collection of separate feathers, each at its own angle, lilac-pink intermixed with smoky and clear. In the evening light from the joints lifts the feathers from below, the hatching flares, and every leaf comes to resemble a dragonfly's wing.

**Where it works.** Living rooms, boudoirs, bedrooms, reception areas, boutiques. The dusty pink register is rare in light objects and needs neutral surroundings so as not to argue with the finishes.

**Style.** Art Nouveau and romantic modern: the insect and the plant as classic motifs. The message is feminine and expensive at once — a piece for an interior with fine taste.

The length of the vine, the palette and the density of the thicket are all made to order.

## LC0396 — Light composition

A composition about a single movement. A square plate is pressed in at the centre and turned up at the corners — a figure folded with one push, with no pattern and no second step. The simplicity of the device is its strength.

The cloud is very flat in silhouette and deep in layers: the plates hang at different heights, and as you move through the room it constantly reassembles itself.

By day it is a field of folded figures — clear intermixed with smoky amber, read through folds and overlays. In the evening light burns beneath each plate and flows down the folds, and the field shimmers at two temperatures at once, cold and warm. Origami that has caught fire.

**Where it works.** Lobbies, meeting rooms, dining rooms, panoramic living rooms. The flat silhouette suits rooms of medium height, where a bulky composition would be heavy.

**Style.** Japanese minimalism and contemporary design: the fold instead of the form. The message is light and intellectual — a piece for an interior that likes simple solutions.

The area of the cloud, the number of plates and the balance of clear to amber are all made to order.

## LC0395 — Light composition

A composition about a night sky. Large stars of the first magnitude, a fine gold scattering between them, thinned edges and a dense core — the distribution is taken from a real sky, where the bright is always scarce and the small is plentiful.

**Style.** Contemporary classic and minimalism: the star as the purest of signs. The message is ceremonial — a piece for a space that has to impress.

## LC0385 — Light composition

By day it is clear sections on gold spokes: pure texture, no colour, a piece for those who value optics over decor. In the evening light enters along the spoke and spreads through the grooves, turning every disc into a glowing spiral. The composition starts to rotate before your eyes.

**Where it works.** Living rooms, dining rooms, lobbies, meeting rooms. The moiré reads in movement: the composition is placed where people walk past.

**Style.** Optical art and contemporary minimalism: a pattern without ornament. The message is intellectual — a piece for an interior that loves precision.

The number of discs, the tiering and the dimensions are all made to order.

## LC0382 — Light composition

A composition about geometry arguing with ornament. The same baroque pendant is set in dialogue with flat rectangles of polished gold: strict facets against shaped transparent contours. The character of the piece is born of that argument — classical and sharply contemporary at once.

Clear and mirrored alternate through the cascade, overlapping one another; the form narrows downward into a wedge.

**Style.** Postmodernism in the best sense: a classical quotation next to pure geometry. The message is bold — a piece for an interior that knows how to join eras.

## LC0381 — Light composition

A composition about armour. The leaves hang in dense tiers, overlapping like plumage or like plates of armour, and the cone narrows downward: the form reads now as a folded wing, now as a fir cone, now as a suit of armour. The ambiguity works toward the image of protection — everything listed covers something of value.

Graphite-grey and matte outside, polished gold within. The most dramatic contrast in the brand's catalogue.

By day it is a dark sculptural mass — a blind, heavy piece that absorbs light. In the evening the light hidden under the feathers reflects off the gold reverse and only a warm radiance from the depth escapes: a dark exterior, a glowing interior. Armour with a fire under it.

**Where it works.** Interiors with character — dark walls, wood, warm metal, subdued light. Against a pale ground the contrast stops working: the composition needs a dark context.

**Style.** Dark luxe and brutal modernism: graphite with gold. The message is masculine and strong — a piece for an interior that does not flirt.

The number of feathers, the depth of the cone and the finish are all made to order.
