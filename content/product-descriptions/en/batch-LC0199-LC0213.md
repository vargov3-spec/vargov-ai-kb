# Product card texts — batch 34: LC0199–LC0213 (15 items)

Approved format: artistic image, day-to-evening shift, "Where it works", "Style", closing line on customisation. Element materials are never named. The batch is almost entirely floor lamps; LC0204, LC0203, LC0202 and LC0201 are suspended compositions.

---

## LC0213 — Floor lamp

Velvet below, thorns above. The contrast is set hard: an opaque matte base across which light does not slide but settles in bands, and a bristling luminous crown where every rib catches a beam.

Spiral ribbing winds around the body through its full height — a vertical that rotates slightly while standing still. Three tiers of the crown descend in steps, repeating that movement from above.

By day the lamp reads as a sculptural vessel with a large crown — dark, dense, almost stone-like. In the evening the light passes through the crown in vertical hatching and lights the neck: the violet fills and deepens, while the crown above it flares into sparks.

**Where it works.** Living rooms, bedrooms, studies, private reception rooms — wherever light is needed without a ceiling fixing.

**Style.** Art Deco and contemporary opulence: deep colours, velvet, mirrors, brass fittings. The message is a jewel on a plinth — a composition about stone and faceting, set one upon the other.

The height, the tone of the base, the number of tiers and the calibre of the crown are all made to order.

## LC0212 — Floor lamp

A structure held together by a difference in weight. Below, a heavy dark block with a white vein; above, a weightless fringe — and between them a stem that all but disappears in the dark.

The branches are uneven, with runs and swellings: an icicle grown in layers, or a fragment of coral. The fringe is set at varying lengths — shorter at the outside, deeper toward the centre — so the lower edge comes out alive.

By day it is a sculpture on a stone plinth — a clear crown, a dark base, the pure graphics of the stem. In the evening the branches glow along their whole length and most brightly at their tips: the fringe looks as though it is thawing, about to start dripping.

**Where it works.** Living rooms, halls, private studies, hotel rest zones, lounges, reception rooms.

**Style.** Nordic modernism and brutalist luxury: stone, dark timber, a cold palette, large volumes. The message is the thaw — a composition about the moment winter begins to give way.

The height, the crown diameter, the branch lengths and the stone of the base are all made to order.

## LC0211 — Floor lamp

A cluster filling with light. The petals are crushed as though squeezed in a fist — the form was not calculated but obtained, and that is what makes it convincing.

Clear and mirrored are mixed without system: the cone shimmers in patches — cool transparency here, a warm flare there. The stem is deliberately reduced to a minimum so the shade appears to hover.

By day it is a sculptural object on a stem — light, almost invisible against a pale ground. In the evening the light catches between the layers, the source is lost, and the cone becomes a cluster filled to the brim.

**Where it works.** Living rooms, bedrooms, lounges, private reception rooms, hotel rooms, studies.

**Style.** Contemporary classicism and soft modernism: pale walls, rounded furniture, brass. The message is ripening — a composition about something filling up rather than something shining.

The height, the cone diameter, the number of petals and the ratio of clear to mirrored are all made to order.

## LC0210 — Floor lamp

A bush lit from within. The branches are uneven, knotted, dividing — a form taken from a coral colony or from driftwood tumbled by the sea.

The light hides inside the fringe and emerges only through the gaps, in narrow warm slots. The grain of the finish will not let a beam slide across it: the light breaks up, and the crown shimmers instead of gleaming.

By day it is a metal sculpture on a stem — warm, dense, richly textured. In the evening the cool stem recedes into darkness and only the glowing bush remains, grown out of nothing.

**Where it works.** Living rooms, halls, lounges, private studies, showrooms, restaurant areas.

**Style.** Eco-luxury and organic modernism: natural stone, timber, a subdued palette. The message is growth — a composition about a form that branches by laws of its own.

The height, the crown diameter, the branch lengths and the finish are all made to order.

## LC0209 — Floor lamp

Frost settled on rock. Below, stone with violet, ochre and golden veining — the most colourful element in the whole structure; above, a colourless mass working purely through refraction.

The light passes from pendant to pendant, refracting at each: from outside it is impossible to tell where the source is — the cone itself glows. Onto the stone the light falls from above and lights the veining, so it fills with colour.

By day it is a stone column with an icy cap — heavy below, clear above. In the evening the cap lights up from within and the stone answers from below: two glows moving toward each other.

**Where it works.** Living rooms, bedrooms, private reception rooms, rest zones, studies, hotel rooms.

**Style.** Alpine luxury and contemporary classicism: stone, timber, large windows, a cold palette. The message is a mountain winter — a composition about frost on rock on the first sunlit morning.

The height, the shade diameter, the set of pendants and the stone of the base are all made to order.

## LC0208 — Floor lamp

The same cone, but set on stone — and a different object because of it. Instead of a light disc beneath the shade there is now a heavy wedge, and the lamp becomes substantial, almost architectural.

The difference in mass is the device: stone below, air above, a slender stem between them. The shade meanwhile stays as it was — airy, layered, full of gaps.

By day it is a stone column with a cluster on top — a contrast of weight read instantly. In the evening the clear elements transmit the light, the mirrored ones reflect it, and the stone below picks up the reflection until the white vein within it begins to glow.

**Where it works.** Living rooms, halls, private studies, reception rooms, lobbies, showrooms.

**Style.** Brutalist luxury and contemporary classicism: unworked stone, dark timber, brass. The message is support — a composition about lightness needing weight.

The height, the shade diameter, the ratio of clear to mirrored and the stone of the base are all made to order.

## LC0207 — Floor lamp

An icy bush on a stone plinth. The crown looks accreted rather than manufactured: uneven branches with runs, descending in layers of varying length.

The scale shows in the photograph with a model: the lamp stands a head above waist height, the crown reaching the shoulder. This is not a side light but an object in its own right, holding the zone around it.

By day it is a sculpture — a clear crown on pale stone with grey veining, pure geometry. In the evening the light travels down into the branches from above, the tips flare brighter than the rest, and the bush gives soft diffuse light without a single direct highlight.

**Where it works.** Living rooms, bedrooms, halls, private rest zones, showrooms, boutique hotel lobbies.

**Style.** Nordic minimalism and Alpine luxury: pale stone, timber, spare detailing, plenty of air. The message is clarity — a composition about winter without a single superfluous colour.

The height, the crown diameter, the branch lengths and the stone of the base are all made to order.

## LC0206 — Floor lamp

A ribbon that changes colour as it turns. Every element is twisted a half-turn on its axis: white gloss on one side, gold on the other — and the crown never looks the same twice.

The lower edge is ragged: individual ribbons descend deeper than the rest. The base is a dark wedge with a bright white vein along its face, and between it and the crown there is almost nothing.

By day it is a sculpture in twisted metal — dense, rhythmic, with the interplay of two finishes. In the evening the light falls on the gold faces and reflects in warm patches while the white ones stay cool: a vortex of sparks hangs above black stone.

**Where it works.** Living rooms, bedrooms, reception rooms, lounges, studies, hotel rooms.

**Style.** Contemporary Art Deco and graphic modernism: contrasting combinations, black with gold, mirrors. The message is duality — a composition about a thing with two sides, both of them true.

The height, the crown diameter, the number of ribbons and the stone of the base are all made to order.

## LC0205 — Floor lamp

The same crown on a light base — and the object changes character. You can move it with one hand; it does not bind a zone to itself the way the stone versions do.

The light emerges reflected off the gold faces: no direct source is visible from any position. For a lamp standing at the eye level of a seated person, that matters.

By day it is a decorative sculpture on a stem — mobile, almost weightless. In the evening it gives a soft warm patch around a chair: not illumination for a room, but a circle in which people sit.

**Where it works.** Living rooms, bedrooms, studies, zones beside a chair or sofa, hotel rooms, reading corners.

**Style.** Contemporary classicism and comfortable minimalism: pale walls, textiles, timber. The message is personal space — a composition about light for one person.

The height, the crown diameter, the number of ribbons and the base finish are all made to order.

## LC0204 — Light composition

A ridge of ice floes forced to the surface. The shards stand vertically in a dense array, slightly turned toward one another — the way ice rises into pressure ridges when a field is squeezed.

The surface is uneven, as if split; some elements are colourless, others richly turquoise, and there are transitions where colour is drawn into the clear body as an uneven patch. Brightness wanders from element to element because of the differing thickness.

By day it is a section through a glacier — cold, faceted, with internal shadows. In the evening the light runs from above along the whole plane, the clear elements flare white at their bases, the turquoise fill entirely: an aurora brought down to the table.

**Where it works.** An elongated horizontal format — for long dining tables, bar counters, meeting rooms, reception areas, restaurant floors.

**Style.** Nordic minimalism and contemporary luxury: concrete, pale timber, a cold palette, panoramic windows. The message is austere beauty — a composition about ice that does not melt.

The length, the number of elements, the share of turquoise and the drop heights are all made to order.

## LC0203 — Light composition

A meadow with fewer flowers in it than it seems. There are not many buds, but hundreds of small beads between them — the density is created not by the flowers but by what has not yet bloomed.

The light is distributed in patches: some hearts burn, others stay dark. That is how a real meadow looks, with the sun breaking through unevenly — evenness here would be a falsehood.

By day it is a botanical study — fine, pale, almost weightless. In the evening the meadow rises to the ceiling and lights up from within: the flowers do not all come on at once, and the field begins to shimmer in patches.

**Where it works.** For living rooms with high ceilings, stairwells, lobbies, winter gardens, wedding venues.

**Style.** Romantic modernism and eco-classicism: pale walls, living greenery, natural fabrics. The message is the first bloom — a composition about early spring, when not everything is flowering yet.

The overall diameter, the number of flowers, the ratio of pearlescent to gold and the density of the buds are all made to order.

## LC0202 — Light composition

A wall of rain caught in a single frame. The rods stand in a dense array, the lower edge wavy from the difference in heights — a downpour in which every thread is visible.

The light runs down the rod like a lightguide: the rod itself glows faintly while the drop at its end flares. The form of the drop is taken from a real one — at the moment of release, when it has left the surface but is not yet falling.

By day the composition reads graphically, as pure hatching against a pale ceiling. In the evening the rods vanish altogether and a row of bright points remains, suspended in the air: rain with the lines erased and only the drops left.

**Where it works.** For large spaces: lobbies, atria, stairwells, double-height living rooms, banquet halls.

**Style.** Contemporary minimalism and high-tech: clean planes, transparency, an absence of decoration. The message is reduction — a composition about how one drop is enough to stand for rain.

The overall size, the rod lengths, the calibre of the drops and the tone are all made to order.

## LC0201 — Light composition

A pod in motion, not a set of figures. The dolphins run in waves, some higher, some lower, all turned the same way — you read direction and speed.

The form is reduced to a pure silhouette, without detail: only the line of movement works. The light falls on the polished flanks and rolls out along the body — a glowing rib flares on every figure from nose to tail.

By day it is graphics against a dark ground — silhouettes, rhythm, pure line. In the evening not one source is visible directly: only the metal glows, and the pod moves through a body of water lit from the surface.

**Where it works.** For generous volumes: atria, lobbies, pools and spas, panoramic living rooms, children's and family zones.

**Style.** Marine modernism and contemporary minimalism: white walls, water, pale timber. The message is the joy of movement — a composition about animals that swim not out of need but because they can.

The length, the number of figures, the direction of the pod and the finish are all made to order.

## LC0200 — Floor lamp

A column shedding feathers. The column is dense at the middle and thinned at its ends: below, individual feathers break away from the mass and hang on their own, as though they had only just fallen.

The light runs along the axis and spreads into the grooves of the vane: every feather glows in strokes rather than as a patch. The amber ones fill from within, the clear ones give highlights — one structure, two behaviours.

By day the lamp reads as a clear sculpture at human height — all but invisible, only contours and flares along the ribs. In the evening the spiral lights up from bottom to top, and the column becomes a shaft of light that is losing feathers.

**Where it works.** Living rooms, halls, private reception rooms, showrooms, restaurant areas, lobbies.

**Style.** Art Nouveau and contemporary opulence: flowing lines, gilding, natural textures. The message is moulting — a composition about plumage renewing itself while the bird stays.

The column height, the number of feathers and the ratio of amber to clear are all made to order.

## LC0199 — Floor lamp

A weave that reads differently from every side. A white ribbon runs inward, a gold rod comes out, the next ribbon covers it again — volume is built by alternation rather than by thickness.

The lower half is left empty: bare rods only. All the mass and all the light gather at the top, at the eye level of a standing person, while the lower part works as a leg.

By day it is a sculptural column with rich plasticity — you can see how one and the same ribbon is white at one edge and shows a gold lining at the other. In the evening the light emerges through the cells of the lattice, and the column begins to glow from within, evenly, without a single direct point.

**Where it works.** Living rooms, halls, reception rooms, lounges, showrooms, hotel lobbies.

**Style.** Contemporary Art Deco and Eastern luxury: brass, deep tones, rugs, carving. The message is craft — a composition about the maker's hand, legible in every interlacing.

The column height, the density of the weave, the finish of the rods and the colour of the ribbons are all made to order.
