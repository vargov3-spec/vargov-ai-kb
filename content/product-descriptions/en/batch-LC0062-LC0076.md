# Product card texts — batch 43: LC0062–LC0076 (15 items)

Approved format: artistic image, day-to-evening shift, "Where it works", "Style", closing line on customisation. Element materials are never named. LC0074–LC0067 are wall lights and mirrors in sculptural frames; LC0079 elsewhere in the range is an art object.

## LC0076 — Light composition

A composition about a single line. There is no set of elements here — there is one continuous strip, twisted and crushed into a loose knot: it folds over, crosses itself and returns, never repeating a previous bend. The piece reads whole and at once, as a single movement.

The surface is polished, with facets and dents left by working hot metal: light does not slide evenly across it but breaks into dozens of highlights, and every bend acquires its own line of light.

By day it is a metal sculpture — warm gold with greenish shifts in the shadows, plastic and heavy. In the evening light wakes deep inside the knot: it escapes through the gaps between the coils, lighting the inner faces of the strip, and the knot begins to glow without a single visible lamp. A ribbon heated from within.

**Where it works.** Living rooms, dining rooms, lobbies, reception rooms, zones above a long table. The knot has no principal view: the piece is placed where people walk around it.

**Style.** Contemporary sculpture and brutalist luxury: one gesture instead of a composition. The message is artistic — a piece that exists as an object of art.

The length of the strip, the pattern of the knot, the metal finish and the drop height are all made to order.

## LC0075 — Light composition

A composition about a chain that holds nothing. Large irregular links are threaded one through another, each crumpled and twisted, none repeating its neighbour. Together they sag freely and twist about their own axis — a chain stripped of its function and left as form.

Traces of forming remain on the surface — dents and waves — and they read as drawing, not as flaw. The piece has no principal view: from the side and from below it is two different compositions.

By day it is warm gold with greenish and coppery shifts in the shadows, a sculpture working through the plasticity of the link. In the evening the light emerges along the inner rim of each ring and traces it with a glowing line; because the rings pass through one another and face different ways, those lines cross and layer. An interlacing of luminous loops.

**Where it works.** An elongated horizontal format — long dining tables, bar counters, meeting rooms, lobbies. The sag sets a lower limit: allow clearance beneath.

**Style.** Brutalist luxury and contemporary Art Deco: the link as a unit of opulence. The message is strong and direct — a piece for an interior with character.

The length of the chain, the number of links, their calibres and the metal finish are all made to order.

## LC0074 — Wall light

A composition about the pearl in the shell. A shell grows in annual accretions — several nested layers with wavy edges, spiralling toward an off-centre core, with swellings and folds. There is no regularity, because growth knows none.

The finish is mirrored, and every layer reflects its neighbour: an endless exchange of highlights builds up in the depth of the shell, something no matte form could produce.

By day the object reads as a sculptural shell on the wall — polished gold with cool blue-green reflections along the rims, giving a pearlescent effect. In the evening a matte pearl lights up at the centre: it illuminates all the surrounding layers from within, the gold around it grows hot, and the outer coils stay subdued. The light never strikes the eye — it is always reflected.

**Where it works.** Entrance halls, corridors, bedrooms at the bedhead, bathrooms, private living rooms, spas. Reflected light without direct glare suits positions beside a mirror or a bed.

**Style.** Art Nouveau and marine modernism: the shell as a classic motif of preciousness. The message is intimate — a piece for a private space.

The dimensions, the depth of the relief, the metal finish and the calibre of the pearl are all made to order.

## LC0073 — Wall light

The same shell set upright. The layers no longer run in circles but converge upward toward a pointed apex, with the pearl shifted into the upper third: the form becomes teardrop-like, with a clear direction. The folds are larger and softer — a few broad waves instead of close accretions.

Direction changes the piece fundamentally. The round version is static; this one reaches upward.

By day the object reads as a mussel shell stood on end: polished gold with cool pearlescent reflections along the rims, pure sculpture. In the evening the light from the matte pearl runs down the folds and spreads across the full height — the lower part stays in shadow and works as form, the upper part burns. A drop of light in a metal setting.

**Where it works.** Entrance halls, narrow corridors, bedrooms, stair flights, private reception rooms. The vertical format goes where there is no width: in piers and along flights.

**Style.** Art Nouveau and organic modernism: a natural form with direction. The message is aspiring — a piece for a narrow space that needs height.

The dimensions, the depth of the relief, the metal finish and the position of the pearl are all made to order.

## LC0072 — Wall light-ring

A composition about a ring that burns only at its edge. The band is broad, its surface wavy, crushed and twisted along its whole length; the inner opening is not a circle but a living contour with swellings and lobes. There is no correct geometry anywhere.

A small matte sphere sits in the upper part, slightly off centre — and it is the only source. Its light runs along the inner rim, tracing the opening with a hot line, while the outer surface stays reflective.

By day it is a metal ring-sculpture: each wave carries its own elongated band of highlight, and as you walk past they shift — the object looks mobile. In the evening the outer part falls into shadow, and what remains is a hoop burning along its inner edge — the glowing contour of an emptiness.

**Where it works.** Entrance halls, corridors, bedrooms, private living rooms, stair flights, reception areas. The light is directed into the ring and onto the wall — the fitting works as an accent rather than as illumination.

**Style.** Contemporary sculpture and organic modernism: a ring without a single straight line. The message is spare — a piece for a wall that needs one object only.

The ring diameter, the width of the band, the metal finish and the calibre of the sphere are all made to order.

## LC0071 — Wall light-ring

The same solution in hard plasticity. Instead of soft waves — large planes meeting at angles, and an inner opening with sharp projections and hollows. The form is closer to a nugget or a fragment of ore than to an organic fold.

The device is identical but the result is different: on the flat facets the highlights are broad and calm, on the breaks they flare sharply, and the object reads more graphically than the round version.

By day it is metal melted and set again — polished gold with cool reflections, heavy and angular. In the evening the light runs along the inner rim and lights the ragged contour: you see not the shape of the object but the outline of the hole in it. On angular geometry the device works more dramatically than on soft — an opening with fire behind it.

**Where it works.** Entrance halls, private studies, bars, lounges, gallery walls. The hard plasticity needs a dark or neutral backdrop.

**Style.** Brutalist luxury and contemporary art: the fracture instead of the line. The message is hard — a piece for an interior that does not soften.

The dimensions, the character of the geometry, the metal finish and the lighting scheme are all made to order.

## LC0070 — Backlit mirror

A composition about the mark of the hammer. Uneven dents, grooves and swellings run across the whole frame — as though the metal had been struck while still soft. The pattern is irregular, without a single repeat: handwork is shown as it is, not smoothed away.

The chief difference from the other mirrors in the line is the backlight. It is hidden in a ring recess between frame and mirror and emerges as a narrow band.

By day the frame works through texture: pale antiqued silver with gold wear and dark patina in the hollows, a matte surface with visible grain. In the evening the light comes from within and grazes the relief — every dent takes a shadow, the frame gains volume, and the mirror itself sits inside a glowing ring. The convex plate meanwhile draws the room into a compact frame with strong distortion at the edges.

**Where it works.** Entrance halls, corridors, bedrooms, bathrooms, dressing rooms. The backlight makes the mirror functional for the zone, but the convexity distorts the reflection — it is not a mirror for getting ready.

**Style.** Brutalist luxury and hand forging: the marks of work as decoration. The message is honest and craft-driven — a piece that shows how it was made.

The diameter, the character of the forging, the metal finish and the brightness of the backlight are all made to order.

## LC0069 — Mirror

A composition about a whirlpool. The folds run in coils around an off-centre opening, welling over one another and narrowing toward the middle: a funnel, fabric twisted into a rope, a section through a shell — the form reads several ways at once and none of them to the end.

The surface is mirrored, so every coil reflects its neighbour and the frame acquires its own depth, independent of the reflection in the plate. The piece turns out to be dimensional twice over.

By day it is polished gold with cool blue-green reflections deep in the folds and wear along the crests — the frame lives on the reflections of the room. In the evening a small matte sphere lights up in the lower fold: it gives a soft glow from below and grazes the whole spiral, bringing out the relief. Reflection and frame together pull the eye into a single point.

**Where it works.** Entrance halls, living rooms, boudoirs, private studies, gallery walls. The asymmetry needs a clear wall — the piece is not hung in a row.

**Style.** Baroque plasticity in a contemporary reading: movement set in metal. The message is dramatic — a piece for an interior that likes strong forms.

The diameter, the depth of the spiral, the metal finish and the position of the sphere are all made to order.

## LC0068 — Mirror

A composition about a mirror that is barely there. The opening is very small and set deep into the frame: almost the entire area is given to relief, and only enough reflective surface remains for a reflection to register. The proportion is inverted — here the frame is the subject and the mirror is secondary.

The relief is built from soft broad folds spiralling toward the centre in uneven coils: they well over one another, break, and lift into a sharp crest in places. Crumpled fabric, a rose, a cabbage leaf — the ambiguity is deliberate.

By day the object works through light and shade rather than reflection: warm bronze with a matte surface, bluish veining in the shadows, bright gold wear on the crests, very little shine. In the evening, under directional light, the folds throw deep shadows and the frame becomes almost a relief sculpture, while the deeply recessed convex plate reads as a look down a tunnel — distant, compressed, barely real.

**Where it works.** Entrance halls, bedrooms, living rooms, corridors, gallery walls — wherever a sculptural object is wanted rather than a functional mirror. It was never conceived as a working mirror.

**Style.** Organic modernism and wabi-sabi: a subdued palette, irregularity as a virtue. The message is quiet — a piece for an interior that avoids shine.

The diameter, the depth of the folds, the size of the opening and the finish are all made to order.

## LC0067 — Wall light-ring

A composition about the point everything gathers toward. The sphere here is polished rather than matte: it gives no light of its own but collects and redirects the glow running along the inner rim of the ring. Source and accent are separated — one thing shines, another is looked at.

The band is broad and crushed into soft waves, the circle wandering slightly along its contour, the inner opening alive with swellings. The inner surface is polished with particular care: it is the reflector.

By day it is a metal hoop with a sphere — polished gold with cool blue-green reflections along the crests of the folds. In the evening what remains is a ring of light with one bright point in the upper left. The opening stays empty: the wall is visible through it, and that is part of the intent — the piece frames a void.

**Where it works.** Entrance halls, corridors, private living rooms, stair flights, reception areas, showrooms. An empty opening means the wall beneath the fitting is part of the composition: its texture and colour matter.

**Style.** Contemporary sculpture and minimalism: a frame around nothing. The message is conceptual — a piece for an interior that values the idea.

The ring diameter, the width of the band, the metal finish and the calibre of the sphere are all made to order.

## LC0066 — Mirror

A composition about dark water. Concentric waves of deep black with a mirrored gloss spread from an off-centre point, welling over one another — rings from a stone thrown into water at night.

The finish is wholly glossy, without a single matte area: the frame reflects everything around it and all but disappears in the dark, leaving only bands of highlight. That sets it apart from the gold versions in principle — here it is not colour that works but pure reflection.

By day the object reads graphically, black against the wall; the convex plate with its warm golden cast draws the room into a compact frame, and against the black waves that reflection looks like a window cut into a dark surface — the only warm patch in the whole object. In the evening the frame all but dissolves, and only the glowing circle of the reflection remains, hanging on its own.

**Where it works.** Interiors in a dark palette — entrance halls, private studies, bars, living rooms, gallery walls. Against a pale wall the disappearing act does not work.

**Style.** Dark luxury and contemporary minimalism: black gloss as the principal language. The message is austere — a piece for an interior that does not flirt.

The diameter, the depth of the waves, the finish and the size of the sphere are all made to order.

## LC0065 — Light composition

A composition about a city. Arches, windows, portals, rectangular plates — some with rounded tops, some stepped, some flat: the vocabulary of architecture broken down into signs. Each element is turned at its own angle, and from a distance the mass reads as a city skyline with roofs, spires and windows receding into depth.

The array is dense: hundreds of plates at varied levels, overlapping, level at the top and deeply sagging below.

By day it is graphics made of mirrored silhouettes — flat, sharp, read through outline. In the evening the mirrored plates reflect the light and pass it to one another: an endless exchange of highlights builds inside the composition, the source is impossible to locate, and every arch reflects its neighbour. A panorama with lit windows — a city reflected in itself.

**Where it works.** An elongated horizontal format — long dining tables, lobbies, meeting rooms, reception areas. The skyline reads from a distance: the piece is designed for a view from the entrance, not from directly beneath.

**Style.** Art Deco and urban modernism: architecture as ornament. The message is metropolitan — a piece for an interior that loves the city.

The length, the number of elements, the set of forms and the metal finish are all made to order.

## LC0064 — Light composition

A composition about a horn with a crystal. The element is double: a cone rolled into a bell with an open mouth, and a faceted cube at its wide end. It reads both as a horn with a mouthpiece and as a flower with a crystal for its heart — a form from the world of machinery and a form from the world of minerals in one object.

The elements hang at varied angles: some mouth up, some down, some sideways. The bowl is shallow, and in places the elements interlock into clusters.

By day it is a cluster of gold flowers with icy hearts: the bells work through reflection, the cubes through refraction. In the evening the light leaves the bell for the crystal, which gathers it, refracts it on the facets and flares from within, while the cone remains a reflector — lit, but not luminous itself. The orientations vary, so the glowing crystals face every direction.

**Where it works.** Living rooms with high ceilings, dining rooms, lobbies, banquet halls. The absence of a principal view means the piece is placed at the centre of a zone, not against a wall.

**Style.** Art Deco and industrial luxury: a mechanical form with a precious filling. The message is ceremonial — a piece for a formal space.

The overall size, the number of elements, the calibres of the crystals and the gilding finish are all made to order.

## LC0063 — Light composition

A composition about the fruit and its medium. Large pomegranates with faceted sides and a dimple on top are rare and heavy; around them falls a dense vertical rain of small drops on clear threads, even and uniform. The whole composition rests on that contrast: a few large events within a uniform medium.

The form of the fruit is taken almost literally. The tone is deep scarlet with gold runs, the surface mirrored: the fruits reflect and glow from within at the same time.

By day it is an orchard with ripened fruit — red against clear, read through colour and highlight. In the evening the light passes through the body and fills it to ruby, while the rain picks up the reflection and scatters it as pinpoint sparks through the whole volume. The source cannot be found — both the fruit and the medium around it glow. Rubies suspended in a cloud of sparks.

**Where it works.** Generous rooms — lobbies, double-height living rooms, banquet and wedding halls, restaurants. The rain sets the vertical envelope: allow for height.

**Style.** Grand classicism and Eastern opulence: the fruit as a symbol of abundance. The message is festive and generous — a piece for a space where guests are received.

The overall size, the number of fruits, the density of the rain and the depth of the scarlet tone are all made to order.

## LC0062 — Light composition

A composition about freezing rain. The rods are overgrown with small crystals along their whole length — uneven accretions, thicker in places, sparser in others. This is how a branch looks the morning after rain has frozen in flight: the crust builds not evenly but wherever it managed to.

The rods hang at varied angles — almost horizontal, oblique, crosswise — and intersect to form a three-dimensional lattice with no centre and no symmetry. A thicket of bare branches caught by frost.

By day it is an interlacing of branches — graphic, cold, living on the highlights along the crystals. In the evening every accretion lights its own point, and an uneven glowing line runs along each branch: where the crystals are denser, it is brighter. The polished ends flare as separate hard highlights. A thicket covered in luminous frost.

**Where it works.** Generous rooms — lobbies, double-height living rooms, stairwells, winter gardens, restaurants. A lattice without a centre is built for the specific volume and has no principal face.

**Style.** Nordic modernism and organic aesthetics: winter as motif. The message is cool and clean — a piece for an interior in a cold palette.

The overall size, the number of branches, the density of the crystals and the metal finish are all made to order.
