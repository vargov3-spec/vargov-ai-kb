## LC0584 — Light composition

A composition about a turn. Every element reads as a maple seed or as a swift in flight: a contour of polished golden tube with a clear faceted wing stretched inside it. Seed and bird have one thing in common — both turn rather than fly straight.

The light here is not gathered into points: it lives in the wings themselves. The flock crosses a space in a wide arc.

By day the composition reassembles from different angles: now a dense shoal, now a thinned scattering of solitary wings. In the evening the facets break the rays, the gold adds warm reflections, and the whole flock shimmers like a current in a shaft of sunlight — movement appears with not one moving part.

**Where it works.** Extended spaces — above a long table, along a staircase, in an entrance hall with a high ceiling: anywhere there is room for flight. The trajectory is designed for a specific volume.

**Style.** Art Nouveau and contemporary sculpture: flight as a classic motif. The message is dynamic — a piece for a space people move through.

The trajectory of the flock, the number of elements and the spread are all made to order.

## LC0583 — Wall light

One element of the flock taken in close-up. The contour seems to hold the light inside a given silhouette and keep it from spreading: an unbroken line of gold encloses a clear faceted spindle.

The piece is reduced to a minimum — and the precision of the lines reads more clearly for it than it does in the large composition.

By day, switched off, the wall light remains a jeweller's object on the wall: pure form, faceting, nothing surplus. In the evening the facets gather the rays into a vertical line, the spindle becomes a glowing core, and the gold lights up as a warm contour — the light turns out to be caught in a setting.

**Where it works.** Solo beside a bedhead or a mirror, in a pair flanking a portal, in a rhythmic series along a corridor. It also works as a companion to the large LC0584 composition, continuing its plasticity on the wall.

**Style.** Art Deco and jewelled Art Nouveau: the setting as a device. The message is spare — a piece for a wall for which one detail is enough.

The size, the finish of the contour and the character of the faceting are all made to order.

## LC0582 — Room divider

A composition about a border that does not cut. The divider does not split a space, it separates it into layers: air and light pass through, silhouettes behind it soften, zones are divided without walls.

Vertical polished strings are strung with white lenses: the module repeats, and from the repetition a graphic is born — light, transparent, almost musical.

By day light from the window plays in the lenses as highlights, and the divider lives on reflections while staying almost transparent. In the evening it is lit and becomes a curtain of light — the border appears exactly enough to be seen.

**Where it works.** Between a living room and a dining room, a bedroom and a dressing room, in hotel lobbies and restaurants. The transparency gives zoning where a solid wall would be surplus.

**Style.** Contemporary minimalism and Art Deco: the module and repetition instead of ornament. The message is calm and architectural — a piece that is a background rather than an accent.

The height, the width, the density and the rhythm of the modules are all made to order, to the opening.

## LC0581 — Light composition

A composition about a downpour at sunset. The opal surface glows softly from within, without glare: the light seems dissolved in the mass rather than hidden inside it.

Every drop is an independent vertical line with a chrome tip; together they gather into a cloud. The chrome adds graphic quality — cold vertical strokes in a warm haze.

By day it is a pink-and-pearl stream with silver tops: a piece read through the rhythm of the verticals and the softness of the colour. In the evening the opal fills from within with an even glow, the chrome stays cold, and the composition becomes an arrested downpour lit by a sunset.

**Where it works.** A dense low stream above a table, a tall thinned cloud in an entrance hall, a cascade in a stairwell — the format is adjusted to the mood of the space.

**Style.** Romantic modern and contemporary classic: rain in a warm register. The message is tender — a piece for an interior with a soft palette.

The number of drops, the differences in height and the tone are all made to order.

## LC0580 — Light composition

A composition about a branch lying along the wind. The tubes reach out in horizontal waves, and on them, like buds, sit crystals — the light is drawn out along rather than gathered at a point.

The branches cross at differing heights, forming a light canopy: it takes up almost no volume and gathers the whole space around it.

By day it is the graphic of branches with coloured buds — smoky blue in the gold version, deep green in the chrome one; the bubbled structure reads against the light. In the evening a source lights inside every crystal, the bubbles flare as a constellation, and the bud glows from within — the branch comes into flower in light.

**Where it works.** Above a long table, a bar counter, a reception desk — wherever a horizontal is needed. The canopy steals no height and suits medium ceilings.

**Style.** Art Nouveau and natural modern: the branch as a classic motif. The message is alive and spring-like — a piece for an interior that loves botany.

The number of branches, their curves, the finish and the colour of the crystals are all made to order.

## LC0579 — Light composition — object sculpture

A composition about a landscape suspended in air. Crumpled plates of mirrored gold read as a molten flow, white matte elements with live openings as bone or coral, and between them faceted crystals hover. Three geologies in one volume.

Every texture leads its own part: the gold gathers and multiplies reflections, the matte whiteness kills them, the crystals scatter sharp highlights.

By day the piece holds a space even switched off — the layered drama of textures reads anew from every angle. In the evening fine drops of light between the elements come on, the gold goes white-hot with reflections, and the white stays cold: the space turns into a gallery for a single work.

**Where it works.** Lobbies, atriums, double-height living rooms, gallery spaces, showrooms. This is no longer a luminaire in the domestic sense — an object sculpture that needs room.

**Style.** Contemporary art and brutal opulence: texture instead of form. The message is artistic — a work rather than a furnishing.

The make-up, the scale and the balance of textures are made to order, as a single example.

## LC0578 — Light composition

A composition about cloth that has caught a gust. Vertical sheets bend into free folds, and light from within makes a rigid form look soft — the contradiction the whole piece rests on.

This is a system from which anything can be assembled: a spare accent of five panels or a light architecture the full height of an atrium. There is no single correct shape — there is a logic of growth, as in natural structures.

By day it is a deep burgundy-chocolate beside mirrored gold: a dark register, rare in the world of light, read through folds and reflections. In the evening light comes out of the depth of the folds, slides along the curves and lifts the neighbouring sheets — not white gloss but depth: wine, chocolate, old gold.

**Where it works.** Atriums, lobbies, restaurants, private living rooms, stairwells. The dark register requires either a large volume or dark surroundings.

**Style.** Dark luxe and contemporary sculpture: drapery in a noble register. The message is adult and deep — a piece for an interior with a mood.

The make-up and the scale are made to order, to the ceiling height and the rhythm of the interior.

## LC0577 — Light composition

A composition about how a finish changes the voice of a form. The brand's familiar flagship curl is rendered in a dense glossy finish: the silhouette has become sharper, the form more composed, the plasticity more expressive. In the clear execution this element sounded light and airy; in the opaque white one it sounds substantial and sculptural. One character, two languages.

A fine gold capsule beside every element does not interfere with the form, it emphasises its rhythm.

By day the flock flies in a broad wedge, the gloss catching the reflections of its neighbours, and the composition reads as one movement — dense at the centre, thinned at the edges. In the evening the capsules draw warm verticals between the elements, and the wedge is stitched through with light.

**Where it works.** Living rooms, lobbies, dining rooms, stairwells. The wedge has a direction: the composition is oriented along the route from the entrance.

**Style.** Contemporary sculpture and Art Deco: the curl as a signature. The message is expressive — a piece for an interior that wants plasticity.

The number of elements, the spread of the flock and the ratio of mass to light are all made to order.

## LC0576 — Light composition

A composition about a sky mixed with a forest. Soft lumps — white and mossy green — read as fragments of a cumulus sky and as fragments of a canopy at the same time. The form is deliberately alive: no sharp corners, no geometry, as though it had grown by itself.

The white ones give purity and air, the green ones nature and calm; some versions add mirrored gold details, like flecks of sun in foliage.

By day it is a calm mass with not one straight line — a piece read through plasticity and the alternation of colour. In the evening light comes out from under the clouds as soft warm patches, lifting the neighbouring elements, and the cloud acquires depth: what burns is not the surface but the gaps.

**Where it works.** Bedrooms, living rooms, spa areas, restaurants. It gathers into a dense low cloud or scatters across a ceiling in occasional groups — the format adapts to the room.

**Style.** Bio-design and natural minimalism: cloud and moss as paired motifs. The message is calm and natural — a piece for an interior that soothes.

The size and density of the cloud and the balance of white to green are all made to order.

## LC0575 — Wall light compositions

A composition about a principle from which combinations grow. Every module is self-sufficient — one panel works as a wall light. But the real story begins in the assembly: the panels fall into a rhythm, with offsets and pauses, and as in origami one principle gives rise to endless variants.

Rectangular panels with a radial relief read as sheets of paper folded into a fan; at the centre of each is a gold mirrored window.

By day the relief reads through light and shade while the mirrored windows gather the room — the wall works as graphic art. In the evening warm light leaves the windows, the rays of the relief radiate from it, and every panel glows like a small sun behind a blind.

**Where they work.** A strict horizontal above a console, a chequerboard order in a stairwell, a continuous light panel in a living room. The layout is chosen for a specific wall.

**Style.** Art Deco and modular design: the panel as a unit of composition. The message is architectural — a piece that works with a wall rather than hanging on it.

The number of panels, the layout and the scale are all made to order.
