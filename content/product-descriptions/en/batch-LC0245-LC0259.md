# Product card texts — batch 31: LC0245–LC0259 (15 items)

Approved format: artistic image, day-to-evening shift, "Where it works", "Style", closing line on customisation. Element materials are never named. The batch includes several items atypical for the catalogue — bio-fireplaces, a park sculpture, mannequin art objects — described according to their actual purpose.

## LC0259 — Light composition

Metal caught in the act of flowing. The form has run and crumpled — it looks not made but happened, as though it had been stopped a second before it flowed further.

Some drops are opaque and mirrored, others glow from within. Because of that the cluster burns in every other element: the light here is selective rather than general, and the composition reads as a branch on which half the berries have ripened.

By day it is a gathering of mercury bubbles with a cool blue-steel shift. In the evening gold kindles inside them — a warm patch amid cold metal, like fire seen through ice.

**Where it works.** A horizontal format for long tables, generous living rooms, lobbies, restaurants, private studies.

**Style.** High-tech and contemporary opulence: polished surfaces, transparency, a dark palette. The message is a controlled element — a composition about a material that was once liquid and remembers it.

The number of drops, the ratio of mirrored to luminous and the span of the branches are all made to order.

## LC0258 — Light composition

A mosaic that does not hang down. The composition spreads beneath the ceiling as a flat layer — it does not occupy volume, it marks out a plane.

The pearlescence shifts from blue-violet into green, like a beetle's wing or a mussel shell: the colour is not fixed and changes with the angle of view. The light hides behind the elements and emerges at their edges, tracing every drop with a contour.

By day it is a scatter of pearls on dark velvet — dense, quiet, almost jewel-like. In the evening the contours light up and the mosaic becomes a star chart in which every pearl is ringed by its own orbit.

**Where it works.** For ceilings of medium height where compactness matters: bedrooms, studies, lift lobbies, dressing rooms, corridors.

**Style.** Soft Art Deco and contemporary classicism: pearlescence, mirrors, subdued tones. The message is preciousness in small compass — the composition needs no height and works where a chandelier is impossible.

The overall diameter, the number of drops and the tone of the pearlescence are all made to order.

## LC0257 — Light composition

Black outside, sun within. The whole object is built on a single device: the sphere stays dark and matte on the outside while the light lives deep inside and emerges only through the bells.

The petals are rolled into funnels and form a tangle with deep pockets. The light reflects off the gold inner surfaces and bursts out in narrow warm beams — it does not diffuse, it strikes.

By day it is a sculpture in crumpled metal — dense, opaque, absorbing. In the evening it is a spent coal that still holds its heat: blackness outside, a blast furnace within.

**Where it works.** A compact format for bedrooms, studies, zones above a round table, private living rooms, bars.

**Style.** Brutalist luxury and dark modernism: graphite walls, leather, timber, subdued light. The message is hidden force — the composition shows nothing on the outside and holds everything within.

The sphere diameter, the number of petals and the gilding finish are all made to order.

## LC0256 — Light composition

Pleating that works as venation. The relief radiates from the centre, and when light passes through the thin wall the folds emerge like the veins of a real leaf.

The edges are all uneven and slightly rolled: the composition looks grown rather than stamped out. The discs hang in layers, overlapping — the way water lilies lie when there is not quite enough room on the water.

By day it is a field of white leaves, calm and almost graphic. In the evening it is a pool lit from beneath the water: the light comes from below, from where a real pond has nothing but silt.

**Where it works.** For large horizontal spaces: lobbies, panoramic living rooms, dining rooms, spas, meeting rooms.

**Style.** Eco-modernism and Japanese minimalism: pale timber, paper, water, emptiness as a material. The message is stillness — a composition about water without movement and about time that has stopped.

The number of discs, their calibres and the area of the composition are all made to order.

## LC0255 — Light composition

A horn with light pouring out of it. The form is taken from a wind instrument: a bell rolled from a sheet, its rim crumpled differently on every element.

The contrast decides everything: a cold blue back and a hot gold interior with a hammered texture. The light goes downward as a warm patch while the top stays blue and opaque — the object shows two temperatures at once.

By day it is a group of metal flowers — blue, dense, sculptural. In the evening it is a rank of horns pouring gold: sound you cannot hear but can see.

**Where it works.** For long tables, bar counters, generous halls, restaurants, lobbies, meeting rooms.

**Style.** Industrial luxury and contemporary modernism: dark walls, metal, large planes. The message is force and warmth — cold armour with fire hidden behind it.

The number of bells, the tone of the blue and the gilding finish are all made to order.

## LC0254 — Light composition

The same water lily, turned invisible. The discs are so transparent that they read only through relief and highlights along their rims — the form exists, the surface does not.

Matte white spheres are scattered between them — pearls, or roe caught in the foliage. The light passes through several layers in a row, refracting and diffusing: the source is lost and a radiance without an address remains.

By day it is a cloud of clear leaves with pearl inclusions, barely distinguishable against a pale ground. In the evening it is a luminous pool whose bottom cannot be made out: the light comes from everywhere and from nowhere.

**Where it works.** For formal spaces with high ceilings: atria, lobbies, double-height living rooms, banquet and wedding halls.

**Style.** Contemporary classicism and romantic minimalism: pale walls, plenty of air, light fabrics. The message is transparency — the composition is vast in volume and weightless in presence.

The number of discs, the share of pearls and the depth of the layers are all made to order.

## LC0253 — Art object

Presence without a face. The figure is reduced to silhouette, pose and drapery — there are no hands and no features, and because of that the object stops being a portrait and becomes a sign.

The drapery is rigid yet looks fluid: you can see the tension at the hip, the sag at the feet, the turn of the shoulder. The gold ornament along the hem is applied with blackening and reads in relief — the one detail permitted to speak.

By day it is a sculpture of pure plasticity — dark, quiet, demanding to be walked around. Under directional light the folds gain depth and the figure seems to take a step.

Three viewpoints are shown: the object is made for viewing in the round and has no reverse.

**Where it works.** A boutique hall, a private living room, a showroom, a gallery space, a hotel lobby, a reception room.

**Style.** Eastern luxury and contemporary art: deep colours, minimal furniture, large planes. The message is mystery — a piece for an interior that does not explain itself at once.

The height, the colour of the drapery and the ornament pattern are all made to order.

## LC0252 — Backlit decorative panel

A window where there is no window. The panel solves a problem a picture cannot: it gives not an image but a condition — the view into a conservatory early in the morning.

The effect rests on blur: the near leaves read sharply, the far ones dissolve into a luminous haze. The matte face removes definition, and the greenery stops being an object and becomes atmosphere.

By day the panel works as a decorative inset in the wall — calm, almost unnoticed. In the evening the backlight brings it forward, and a windowless room acquires a window into a winter garden.

**Where it works.** For interiors without windows: corridors, dressing rooms, washrooms, meeting rooms, lift lobbies, basement areas.

**Style.** Eco-modernism and biophilic design: natural textures, greenery, a pale palette. The message is air — the composition expands a room without touching its walls.

The dimensions of the case, the planting and the colour temperature of the backlight are all made to order.

## LC0251 — Backlit decorative panel

The same device taken to drama. Here the light does not diffuse but strikes in patches, and the leaves read as shadows against it — charcoal silhouettes on a blazing ground.

This is a tropical evening at a precise moment: the sun already behind the foliage but not yet down. A state that lasts ten minutes in nature stays switched on here for as long as you want it.

By day the panel looks restrained, almost graphic — a dark rectangle with a drawing on it. In the evening it becomes the principal source of mood: the room takes its temperature from a single wall.

**Where it works.** For lounges, bars, private studies, bedrooms in a dark palette, restaurants, smoking rooms.

**Style.** Dark modernism and colonial luxury: timber, leather, deep tones, subdued light. The message is a tropical evening — a composition about heat on its way out.

The panel dimensions, the density of the foliage and the colour of the backlight are all made to order.

## LC0250 — Park sculpture

A form that refuses to be named. A shell, a sail, a petal, a wave — the object reads as all of these and as none in particular, and that ambiguity is deliberate.

The polish is taken to a mirror, so the sculpture changes with the sky: silver in the morning, gold at sunset, almost gone at night. It has no permanent appearance — the weather supplies it.

A mirrored sphere in the lower bowl gathers everything around it into a single globe: the park, the sky, the viewer. By day the object reflects the world; in the evening it dissolves into it.

**Where it works.** Parks, the grounds of private residences, entrance groups, embankments, forecourts of hotels and offices.

**Style.** Contemporary sculpture and landscape minimalism: gravel, clipped greenery, the clean geometry of a setting. The message is a dialogue with the sky — a piece that works not on its own but together with the light of the day.

The height, the surface finish and the material of the plinth are all made to order.

## LC0249 — Bio-fireplace

A hearth in the middle of a cliff. The upper part hangs above the fire — an opaque mass suspended so that a gap remains between it and the hearth, through which the flame is visible from every side.

The texture is weathered: vertical cracks, chips, patches of moss. The object looks like a fragment of a cliff face lifted out of a landscape and set down in a room together with the fire that burned at its foot.

By day it is a sculptural column — heavy, stony, silent. In the evening the hearth is lit and the composition becomes what it was conceived as: a campfire beneath an overhanging rock, the oldest interior there is.

**Where it works.** Living rooms with high ceilings, lobbies, spas, terraces, winter gardens, restaurant floors.

**Style.** Brutalist luxury and organic modernism: unworked stone, timber, large volumes. The message is the primal — fire and rock, the things people gathered around before there were houses.

The dimensions, the character of the rock and the fuel type are all made to order.

## LC0248 — Bio-fireplace

Two elements in one vertical. Damp greenery behind a matte pane above, an open flame below — water and fire set one above the other with no intermediary.

The flame reflects in the pane of the case and lights the leaves from beneath, so the greenery looks warmed through. Conservatory and hearth do not merely coexist, they work for each other: without the fire the greenery would be nothing but a display case.

By day the object reads as a decorative case of plants — calm, green, humid. In the evening the fire becomes the protagonist and the conservatory turns into its backdrop: the tropics, lit from below.

**Where it works.** Living rooms, lounges, restaurants, winter gardens, spas, hotel lobbies.

**Style.** Eco-luxury and tropical modernism: living plants, natural stone, dark timber. The message is joining the unjoinable — a composition about opposites holding each other up.

The dimensions, the contents of the case and the burner type are all made to order.

## LC0247 — Art object

A find lifted out of a dig. The figure is wrapped in bands carrying signs — pictograms, symbols, something like writing that cannot be read and makes you want to.

The plasticity rests on contrast: a heavy dense top and an almost weightless base, where the figure balances on a small stand. It stands as though it were about to rock — and does not.

By day it is an archaeological object: faded stone, pale signs on dark, a dry texture. Under directional light the signs gain contrast and begin to read as an inscription that means something.

Three viewpoints are shown: the pattern of signs differs on every side and walking around is essential.

**Where it works.** Halls, gallery spaces, private living rooms, showrooms, libraries, studies.

**Style.** Ethnic and contemporary art: unworked textures, blank walls, pinpoint lighting. The message is antiquity — a piece for someone who values the question more than the answer.

The height, the colour and the set of symbols are all made to order.

## LC0246 — Wall composition

A moon risen over a field. The sphere is set off centre, and around it the elements grow smaller and denser — as though drawn in by the gravity of a body heavier than any of them.

The main mass is matte white and rough; gold grains are scattered through it. The light emerges from beneath the sphere and spreads across the white field, emphasising the relief with a grazing beam.

By day it is a biological specimen under magnification — a colony, spores, something growing out of a plane. In the evening it is a moon over a plain: the same relief, read as a landscape rather than as a microcosm.

**Where it works.** Feature walls in a hall, a bedroom, a private study, a meeting room, a gallery space.

**Style.** Contemporary art and organic minimalism: white walls, an absence of ornament, sculptural furniture. The message is a change of scale — one and the same form reads through a microscope and through a telescope alike.

The diameter of the circle, the density of the elements and the share of gilding are all made to order.

## LC0245 — Wall composition

A panel that failed to hold its contents. The shoal has crossed the boundary of the circle and swims across the bare wall — the composition loses its closure deliberately, and that is its principal gesture.

Below, a mass of cones grows out of the plane like the tubes of marine worms or the mouths of volcanoes; at the centre a gold sphere around which they thicken. The shoal breaks away from that mass and leaves — movement from below upward, out of the dense into the empty.

By day it is a section of reef raised onto a wall: texture, relief, runs of colour. In the evening the glow emphasises the relief, the fish begin to throw highlights, and the shoal seems to accelerate.

**Where it works.** Feature walls in generous rooms: a hall, a stair landing, a private living room, a lobby, a restaurant.

**Style.** Art Nouveau and organic modernism: natural textures, a subdued palette, large planes. The message is escape — a composition about how living things will not stay inside a frame.

The panel diameter, the number of fish and the palette are all made to order.
