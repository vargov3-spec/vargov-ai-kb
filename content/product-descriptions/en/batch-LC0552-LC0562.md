## LC0562 — Pop-art floor lamp

A composition about repetition as rhythm. Every module is a separate graphic brushstroke; together they give repetition, symmetry and contrast. The light here is part of the visual rhythm rather than a function.

The column is assembled from "grain" modules in glossy enamel: in the white version, snowy purity in a chrome setting; in the gold one, a warm shine.

By day the gloss reflects the surroundings and gives the piece a futurist edge: the floor lamp reads as a sculpture living on borrowed light. In the evening a soft glow leaves the core of every module and balances the audacity of the form — the column turns warm while staying graphic.

**Where it works.** Solo as a sculpture beside an armchair, or in a pair framing a sofa group with a symmetrical rhythm. The piece speaks loudly — it needs an interior ready for that.

**Style.** Pop art and retro-futurism: the module instead of the ornament, gloss instead of texture. The message is audacious — a piece for an interior with character.

The height, the finish and the number of modules are all made to order.

## LC0561 — Wall art object

A composition about a chase. Half the petals glow, half are faceted polished chrome, and around the circle they chase one another: light after shine, shine after light. The wall is set in motion.

The spiral is twisted so that the eye finds no beginning: the circle has no point of entry.

By day the object lives on sculptural plasticity: the chrome gathers the interior into shards of reflection while the pale petals hold the purity. In the evening a second life begins — a warm glow around the mirrored cores turns the composition into an interior jewel.

**Where it works.** Accent walls — entrance halls, living rooms, reception rooms, reception desks. Objects like this do not fill a space, they shape it: they set a rhythm and gather attention.

**Style.** Contemporary Art Deco and kinetic art: rotation as subject. The message is expressive — a piece that starts the conversation about an interior.

The diameter of the vortex, the number of petals and the balance of light to chrome are all made to order.

## LC0560 — Floor lamp — vertical light sculpture

A composition about a shoot that has broken through a column. The stems set a strict vertical rhythm, and a clear twisted form appears inside it — like movement arrested where it has no business being.

The proportions are calibrated on growth: the drawn-out vertical emphasises the upward movement, and placing the forms at different levels creates an inner rhythm.

By day it is a bundle of gold stems with clear sails at the top: the bubbles in the mass read against the light, a strict and light piece. In the evening light burns at the heart of every sail, the bubbles flare as a constellation, and the clear mass throws live highlights onto the tubes — the strict column begins to breathe.

**Where it works.** The corners of a living room, the zone beside a window, wall piers, reception rooms. The vertical takes little floor area and works both as a source and as a sculpture.

**Style.** Organic Art Nouveau and architectural minimalism: growth within order. The message is alive — a piece for an interior that loves contrast.

The height, the finish of the stems and the number of clear elements are all made to order.

## LC0559 — Floor lamp — vertical light sculpture

A composition about a flame that does not burn out. A cold base and a hot top — the whole object is built on that contrast: a technical, almost architectural base and a living organic crown.

A bundle of polished rods rises as a strict column, and at the top amber tongues with bubbles open out, gathered into a single ascending form.

By day it is chrome and amber: a strict vertical with a warm cap, a piece read through the contrast of two temperatures. In the evening the light inside is reflected over and over between the tongues, and the flame burns steadily, without one movement — a fire from which time has been taken away.

**Where it works.** Solo beside an armchair, in a pair or a trio — and then the floor lamps become an avenue of fires: a rhythm for an entrance hall, a staircase or a winter garden.

**Style.** Industrial luxury and organic Art Nouveau: engineering below, nature above. The message is strong — a piece for an interior with character.

The height, the finish of the rods and the tone of the flame are all made to order.

## LC0558 — Floor lamp — vertical light sculpture

A composition about water arrested mid-fall. The contour holds a strict geometry — the tubes rise in a bundle and finish in smooth arches; the drops inside break it softly and naturally.

Order and its breaking work as a pair: without the arches the drops would be random, without the drops the arches would be empty.

By day it is gold arches with clear twisted drops: the bubbles read against the light, a rhythmic and composed piece. In the evening light passes through the drop and fills it with an inner radiance, while highlights run down the tubes toward the base — the light quite literally flows down the structure.

**Where it works.** Living rooms, bedrooms, reception rooms, studies. It takes little space and gathers a whole room around it. Related to the LC0567 wall light — together they give one language of light for walls and floor.

**Style.** Art Deco and contemporary classic: the arch and the drop as a classic pairing. The message is elegant — a piece for an interior with poise.

The height, the number of drops and the finish of the tubes are all made to order.

## LC0556 — Light composition

A composition with a plot. Out of a wave a flock of birds flies — the moment when the sea lets the sky go. The piece has a direction and a breath: this is one of the brand's most cinematic compositions.

Turquoise ribbons woven of the finest tubes curve into the crests of waves or into mountain ridges; points of warm light are scattered along their edges.

By day, from different angles, you read now waves, now wings, now a mountain range — the piece changes its plot along with the viewer. In the evening the edges light up warm, the blue birds go into shadow, and an aurora over water remains: what glows is the boundary, not the mass.

**Where it works.** Architectural scale — atriums, lobbies, stair spaces several floors high, panoramic shopfronts. The composition is designed for a specific volume.

**Style.** Contemporary sculptural installation and marine modern: narrative as a genre. The message is epic — a piece for a building that wants a story.

The length of the wave, the number of birds and the palette are all made to order.

## LC0555 — Light composition

A composition about an enfilade. Every element works as an arch through which the next one is visible: the composition is built on depth and rhythm rather than on density. The gaze travels inward, as through a run of openings in an old city.

Warm vertical capsules strengthen the architectural association — windows lit at sunset.

By day it is a clear enfilade: the upward-reaching silhouettes read through overlay and depth, a light and all but weightless piece. In the evening light passes through the mass softly and diffusely, and the composition does not dazzle but sets a mood — an Eastern evening, shadow and gold.

**Where it works.** Living rooms, lobbies, dining rooms, reception rooms, restaurants. The depth reads from the side: the composition is hung at eye level rather than up under the ceiling.

**Style.** Eastern classicism and Art Deco: the arch as a decorative motif. The message is warm and atmospheric — a piece for an interior with an Eastern note.

The number of arches, the depth of the layers and the scale are all made to order.

## LC0554 — Light composition

A composition about a stroke of the brush. Fine drawn-out forms read as feathers or as the traces of quick movements — a gesture rather than an object. Each with its own curve and its own character.

Golden capsules sit on the elements as tips: they add rhythm, warmth and points for the eye to settle on.

By day it is a layered flock of clear strokes — a piece read through movement and with almost no mass. In the evening the gold tips light up, the clear forms stay invisible, and what remains in the air is a single dotted line of movement — swift, light, effortless.

**Where it works.** Above a table, in an entrance hall, along a staircase, in a meeting room. The layering requires a view from the side — the composition is not hung high.

**Style.** Calligraphic aesthetics and contemporary minimalism: the gesture instead of the form. The message is light and precise — a piece for an interior that values a line.

The number of elements, the spread of the flock and the density are all made to order.

## LC0553 — Light composition

A composition about a balance of opposites. The white glossy boat-shapes open with a soft glow from within; the chrome ones work by pure reflection, breaking an interior into highlights. Softness and cold shine gathered into one form.

The cloud is dense and low; light passes between the surfaces and is reflected back and forth in the facets.

By day the chrome elements gather the room while the white ones hold a quiet background: the cloud seems to reassemble slowly as you walk past. In the evening the white ones light from within while the chrome ones go on only reflecting — and the piece separates for good into two parts sounding together.

**Where it works.** Living rooms, dining rooms, meeting rooms, lobbies. The low cloud requires no great height and suits medium ceilings.

**Style.** Contemporary minimalism and Art Deco: two behaviours of light as a device. The message is restrained — a piece for an interior that loves a nuance.

The size and density of the cloud and the ratio of white to chrome are all made to order.

## LC0552 — Decorative composition

A composition about a rising current. The petals are arrested in free flight, each with its own curve, turn and height; together they gather into a spatial whirlpool where rhythm, pause and depth are what matter.

This composition needs no light source to be bright: white enamel gives the purity, gold on the inner side of some elements gives warmth and weight.

By day the vortex lives on daylight: the white gloss reflects it softly, the gold reverses flare in the depth as precious accents, and the composition reads from below, from the side, from the stairs — a new frame every time. In the evening, under directed light, the gold catches fire more sharply while the white stays calm — the vortex acquires a rhythm of temperature.

**Where it works.** Stairwells, double-height entrance halls, living rooms, reception rooms — wherever the composition is visible from several points. It requires no wiring but gains from directed light.

**Style.** Contemporary sculpture and Art Deco: movement instead of ornament. The message is light and formal — a piece for an interior that wants volume without weight.

The scale of the vortex, the number of petals and the share of gold ones are all made to order.
