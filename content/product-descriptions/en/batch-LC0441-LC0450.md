## LC0450 — Light composition

A composition about a fan. The pleated disc reads both as a scallop shell and as the cap of a mushroom — what they have in common is that the folds radiate from one point: a structural principle rather than an ornament.

The colour decides everything: ashen rose makes the piece warm and adult at once — a rare shade for light and a precious one for an interior.

By day it is a tilted field of dusty pink fans: the pleating reads through light and shade, the composition soft and almost monochrome. In the evening a warm light comes on at the heart of every disc, and the folds radiate from it as rays of shadow — the fan acquires a drawing of its own that is invisible by day.

**Where it works.** Living rooms, boudoirs, bedrooms, boutiques, reception areas. The dusty pink register asks for neutral surroundings so as not to argue with the finishes.

**Style.** Art Deco and marine Art Nouveau: the fan as a classic motif. The message is feminine and expensive — a piece for an interior with fine taste.

The number of fans, their calibres and their tilts are all made to order.

## LC0449 — Light composition

A composition about a curtain of scarves. Hundreds of ribbed "kerchiefs", bent at a free break, descend as a dense vertical volume — an almost textile gesture repeated at the scale of a building.

Gold capsules of light stitch the curtain right through: the sources are neither under the composition nor above it but inside it.

By day it is a waterfall of clear and smoky folds — a piece read through overlays and depth, pale and all but weightless. In the evening light runs along the ribbing of every kerchief as along strings, and the curtain sounds entire: hundreds of glowing strokes at differing depths.

**Where it works.** One of the brand's largest verticals — atriums, stair shafts, double-height lobbies several storeys tall. The composition is designed for a specific opening.

**Style.** Contemporary sculptural installation: a textile gesture in a rigid substance. The message is monumental and soft at once — a piece for a building that wants warmth.

The number of kerchiefs, the height of the curtain and the palette are all made to order.

## LC0448 — Light composition

A composition about a pirouette. The ribbed funnels read as pleated skirts in a dance — a form that exists only in movement: a stopped skirt does not hold its folds.

The clusters hang as a free group, in tiers on gold cylinders — a dance stopped halfway through a turn.

By day it is a pale group of funnels: the pleating catches daylight and gives a soft vertical hatching. In the evening light enters from above and spreads through the folds — every skirt glows from waist to hem, and the group begins to seem to be turning.

**Where it works.** Dining rooms, living rooms, reception rooms, banqueting halls. The free group is assembled for a specific plan: the composition can hang compactly or stretch along a table.

**Style.** Art Deco and the dance aesthetics of the twenties: movement as decor. The message is festive and light — a piece for an interior that loves joy.

The number of skirts, the tiering and the format of the group are all made to order.

## LC0447 — Light composition

A composition as gesture. One continuous ribbon woven of thousands of the finest tubes winds in free waves — a silk scarf thrown into flight, or an aurora tinted by sunset. A piece as long as a conversation, passionate and fluid.

Red on dark, light on red — pure drama, without one softening element.

By day it is a scarlet wave beneath the ceiling: a dense colour read through folds and amplitude, a loud and whole piece. In the evening points of warm light flare along the crests of the folds — they emphasise the relief and set the ribbon moving before your eyes: an aurora that has acquired a rhythm.

**Where it works.** Lobbies, restaurants, banqueting halls, showrooms, gallery spaces. Saturated red takes command of a room's palette — neutral surfaces are needed nearby.

**Style.** Contemporary sculptural installation and theatrical aesthetics: colour as the principal argument. The message is passionate — a piece for a space that wants to speak.

The length of the ribbon, the amplitude of the waves and the saturation of the red are all made to order.

## LC0446 — Light composition

A composition about a garland that stayed for good. The branch spreads above the table as one continuous line — a festive gesture stripped of its occasion and therefore fit for every day.

The leaves have a lifelike curve, as though they had just unfurled; between them, points of light burn in the branch.

By day it is white on polished gold — the clean graphic of a leaf, a dressy and light piece. In the evening the points lift the gloss from below and the leaves begin to throw warm highlights onto one another: the garland comes alive the way a real branch comes alive under a lamp.

**Where it works.** Dining rooms, living rooms, lobbies, restaurants, reception desks. The horizontal line is calculated for the length of the table or the counter.

**Style.** Art Nouveau and contemporary classic: white-and-gold botany as the signature formula. The message is festive and hospitable — a piece for the table people are received at.

The length of the branch, the density of the foliage and the number of lights are all made to order.

## LC0445 — Light composition

A composition about the spiral of life. The axis grows dozens of tendrils, each ending in a drop: the form reads both as a molecule and as a flower head turning toward the sky. The double reading — biological and botanical — works toward one meaning: growth along a spiral.

The tendrils curve in different directions, and rings of light on the axis ignite them tier by tier.

By day it is a gold vertical with clear drops at the tips — a piece read through lines and highlights, light and mobile. In the evening the rings light up tier by tier, the drops flare at the ends, and the spiral reads as growing: the light climbs it from the bottom up.

**Where it works.** Stairwells, bay windows, tall niches, double-height living rooms. The vertical takes little floor area and requires height.

**Style.** Art Nouveau and scientific aesthetics: the spiral as the universal form of life. The message is intellectual and alive — a piece for an interior with an idea.

The height of the spiral, the number of tiers and tendrils are all made to order.

## LC0444 — Light composition

A composition about a firework held in the act of opening. Bundles of tubes sit as stars on a branch — these are both fir boughs in hoarfrost and a firework caught at its best phase. Two readings, both wintry and both festive.

The composition is built without one surplus colour: white and gold, nothing more.

By day it is matte white stars on a gold branch — a piece read through the silhouette of the bundles and the rhythm of their placing. In the evening light in the cores ignites the tubes, each glowing with an even milky light, and the stars open fully. A winter celebration in warm gold.

**Where it works.** Dining rooms, living rooms, lobbies, restaurants. The horizontal branch lies along a table; soft milky light without glare is convenient where people eat.

**Style.** Contemporary classic and Scandinavian winter aesthetics. The message is ceremonial and joyful — a piece for an interior that loves a celebration.

The number of stars, their calibres and the length of the branch are all made to order.

## LC0443 — Light composition

A composition about an explosion in slow motion. Ribbon-strips — bent, twisted, alive — sit as dense crowns on rings descending in a vertical spiral. The piece reads as a flower flying apart frame by frame.

Every ribbon is bent separately: there are no two alike in a crown.

By day it is a vortex of white gloss on gold rings — the composition reads through the plasticity of the ribbons and their overlays, light and mobile. In the evening light stitches the rings with points and reflects in the gloss: the highlights leap from ribbon to ribbon and the vortex begins to turn.

**Where it works.** Staircases, entrance halls, double-height living rooms, atriums. The spiral reads as you climb — the composition is calculated for a viewer moving upward.

**Style.** Contemporary Art Deco and sculptural modernism: movement instead of ornament. The message is dynamic — a piece for a vertical that has to lead the eye.

The number of rings, the density of the ribbons and the height are all made to order.

## LC0442 — Light composition

A composition about a frost star on a window. Sharp faceted spindles are gathered into burrs — a prickly, radiant form, exactly what hoarfrost draws: from a point in every direction, without symmetry.

The burrs are linked by gold arcs into a horizontal garland: ice on gold, cold on warm.

By day it is clear spiky stars on gold — a piece read through facets and highlights, sharp and festive. In the evening light burns at the base of every spike and the burrs begin to glitter with hundreds of rays: the composition charges a space with energy without overloading it with colour.

**Where it works.** Dining rooms, living rooms, lobbies, banqueting halls. Faceted spikes give a great deal of glare — the composition is formal rather than working.

**Style.** Art Deco and winter classicism: the crystal as a motif of celebration. The message is bright — a piece for an interior that wants brilliance.

The number of burrs, the spread of the garland and the density of the spikes are all made to order.

## LC0441 — Light composition

A composition about a liana. The spiral grows from the top down, putting out large leaves — the way a plant reaches in a conservatory when it has nothing to catch on and can only hang.

The leaves with their lifelike turns face in different directions; the points of light sit on the spiral between them.

By day it is white and gold on a vertical axis — botany rendered in two finishes, a graphic and formal piece. In the evening the light lifts the underside of the leaves in a warm tone: the gloss catches fire from below while the spiral goes into shadow — what remains is a liana glowing from its reverse.

**Where it works.** Stairwells, bay windows, tall niches, winter gardens. The vertical requires height and reads as you walk around it.

**Style.** Art Nouveau and conservatory classicism: the liana as a motif. The message is alive and warm — a piece for an interior with greenery.

The height of the vine, the density of the foliage and the number of lights are all made to order.
