## LC0480 — Light composition

A composition about split ice. The radial frost texture on the triangular plates reads unambiguously: that is how cracks run out from a single blow — from a point, in rays, not to a plan.

At the centre of every plate is a mirrored disc with the light hidden behind it: the source is covered by its own reflector and is never seen.

By day it is a silver horizon beneath the ceiling: the texture reads as rays, the mirrored centres catch the room, and the composition is strict and festive at once. In the evening the rays run out along the grooves and every shard shines with a rose of light of its own — an aurora cut into facets.

**Where it works.** Lobbies, dining rooms, meeting rooms, showrooms, banqueting halls. The broad field is built wide: the composition covers a zone rather than occupying a point.

**Style.** Nordic Art Nouveau and Art Deco: ice as a formal motif. The message is ceremonial and cold — a piece for an interior in a silver register.

The number of shards, their calibres and the width of the field are all made to order.

## LC0479 — Light composition

A composition about lily of the valley at the scale of a chandelier. A small woodland flower is enlarged to the size of a formal piece — a device that changes the relationship: what used to be examined bending down now looks down from above.

The plasticity of the petals is alive: some flowers are open, others have barely unfurled — the bouquet breathes, because flowering is never synchronous. Gold pin fixings set fine accents.

By day it is a full white heart-shaped cascade: the flowers read through the plasticity of the petals, a bright and dressy piece. In the evening light burns in the bowl of every flower, the petals are lit from within, and the cascade glows a creamy warm white — bells whose cups have caught fire.

**Where it works.** Dining rooms, living rooms, stairwells, wedding and banqueting halls. The heart form requires a frontal view: the composition is hung on the axis of the room.

**Style.** Art Nouveau and romantic classicism: lily of the valley as a motif of spring. The message is tender and festive — a piece for a light interior.

The number of flowers, the shape of the cascade and the height are all made to order.

## LC0478 — Light composition

A composition about an inverted colonnade. Turned spindles of spheres, rings and cones descend as a dense forest — a balustrade taken out of a palace and hung from the ceiling. Architecture deprived of its support and turned into light.

This is not a chandelier in the classic sense but a light environment with optical depth: the rhythm of the details and the pure geometry hold a space on their own.

By day it is a gold and clear forest: the turned profiles read as silhouettes and highlights, a dense and formal piece. In the evening light lives in the clear segments and the gold ends — the composition shimmers with warm brilliance and faceted transparency, and the colonnade turns out to be glowing right through.

**Where it works.** Lobbies, atriums, banqueting and conference halls, double-height living rooms. The dense forest requires height and area: the composition covers a ceiling entire.

**Style.** Art Deco and classical architecture: the baluster as a decorative module. The message is monumental — a piece for a formal hall.

The number of spindles, the proportion of gold to clear segments and the dimensions are all made to order.

## LC0477 — Light composition

A composition about a stroke of the pen. Every curl is twisted its own way — as a hook, a loop, a comma: the gesture of a nib left in the air and set. Not one repeated line.

The composition joins baroque plasticity to contemporary minimalism: only form and the gold of the suspension points, no surplus detail.

By day it is a cluster of clear curls on gold capsules — a piece read through the plasticity of the bends and the highlights on the edges. In the evening light enters from above and runs along the curve: every curl glows along its edge, like a stroke made in luminous ink.

**Where it works.** Living rooms, dining rooms, lobbies, reception rooms. The hanging cluster sets the lower boundary — clearance is needed beneath the composition.

**Style.** Baroque retold in the language of minimalism: the curl without ornament. The message is expressive — a piece for an interior that needs a gesture.

The number of curls, their calibres and the shape of the cluster are all made to order.

## LC0476 — Light composition

A composition about a scattering of diamonds. Faceting of jewel precision is repeated hundreds of times: what usually exists as a single specimen kept in a safe is here poured out into the air.

The field is gathered into a wave: a deep centre, light edges. From a distance a diamond cloud, close up a collection of individual gems.

By day it is a clear field with gold tips: the facets catch daylight and scatter it, a piece read through brilliance. In the evening light enters through the gold and fills the faceting — every crystal flares from within, breaking the rays into rainbow sparks.

**Where it works.** Lobbies, banqueting halls, reception rooms, boutiques, dining rooms. The breaking light gives a great deal of glare: the composition is formal rather than working.

**Style.** Art Deco and jewel classicism: faceting as a subject in its own right. The message is rich — a piece for an interior that declares its level.

The number of crystals, their sizes and the shape of the cloud are all made to order.

## LC0475 — Decorative composition

A composition about a state rather than an object. There is no lighting here in the ordinary sense — there is light coming apart, weightlessness and quiet dynamics: the space around the composition turns into air.

Every feather is unique: its own curve, its own plasticity, its own moment of flight. The vortex is dense, circling.

By day it is a gust of air set in a body: clear fluid forms read through highlights and have almost no mass. In the evening points of light dissolved in the vortex flare along the edges — the mass itself stays invisible, only the edges glow, and the movement appears as contours.

**Where it works.** Living rooms, lobbies, spa areas, boutiques, gallery spaces. The composition creates atmosphere — separate functional light is needed with it.

**Style.** Contemporary sculptural installation: air as a material. The message is poetic — a piece for a space that wants a mood.

The number of feathers, the scale of the vortex and the density are all made to order.

## LC0474 — Light composition

A composition about a canopy and a flock at once. White feathers with a wavy, live plasticity are intermixed with dark green leaves — the shadow of foliage within plumage, and the composition reads two ways at once, choosing neither.

Warm points of light hide deep in the mass and lift the feathers from within.

By day it is a fresh white-and-green mass: the gloss multiplies daylight, the green gives depth, and the piece reads through the plasticity of individual elements. In the evening light from the depth lifts the feathers from inside, the green leaves stay dark, and the cloud acquires volume — what glows is not the surface but the interior.

**Where it works.** Living rooms, winter gardens, lobbies, restaurants, spa areas. The dense cloud requires height — pressed to the ceiling, it loses its depth.

**Style.** Natural Art Nouveau: plumage and foliage as paired motifs. The message is fresh and alive — a piece for an interior with greenery.

The number of feathers, the share of green and the dimensions of the cloud are all made to order.

## LC0473 — Decorative composition

A composition about an ornament torn loose. Carved acanthus leaves — the very ones that grow on Corinthian capitals — have been torn off by the wind and circle in a free vortex. Ornament separated from architecture and let go.

Every element is independent, yet together they form a complex, almost natural rhythm — asymmetric, alive, new every time you walk around it.

By day the gold leaves burn with reflected light even with no lamps on while the white ones hold purity and rhythm: the piece works on daylight alone. In the evening the mirrored gold picks up artificial light and flares sharply, the white diffuses it softly — the vortex acquires a rhythm of temperature on top of its rhythm of form.

**Where it works.** Living rooms, lobbies, reception rooms, stairwells, boutiques. The free vortex is designed for a specific volume and has no front.

**Style.** Baroque and contemporary sculpture: a classical ornament lifted out of its context. The message is bold — a piece for an interior that knows how to quote.

The number of leaves, the proportion of gold and the scale of the vortex are all made to order.

## LC0472 — Light composition

A composition about shoots. The ribbons are tied into loose loops and waves and gathered into a dense garden of vertical gestures — the movement here is directed upward, as in spring growth.

The green is metered like a seasoning: it enlivens the white mass and gives the composition a botanical mood without turning it into a plant.

By day it is a garden of glossy ribbons — creamy white with olive, read through the plasticity of the loops and their overlays. In the evening a warm point comes on at the top of every ribbon and runs down the gloss: the light descends the curl, repeating its line, and the garden begins to glow from the top down.

**Where it works.** Living rooms, dining rooms, lobbies, winter gardens, reception desks. Vertical gestures need height to spare — otherwise the composition flattens.

**Style.** Art Nouveau and natural modern: the shoot as a motif of growth. The message is fresh and spring-like — a piece for an interior that loves movement.

The number of curls, the balance of white to green and the dimensions are all made to order.

## LC0471 — Light composition

A composition about scales. Three-dimensional pyramids hang as a dense swarm, facet to facet — that is how scales lie: element against element, without gaps, with the planes all facing one way.

Milky facets in a gold setting: two substances divided by role — one glows, the other outlines.

By day it is a dense swarm of geometric forms: the gold frames read as graphic work, the milky facets through light and shade, a piece strict and warm at once. In the evening the milky facets glow evenly, the gold frames flare with reflections, and the swarm begins to shift like scales turning.

**Where it works.** Above a table, in an entrance hall, in a lift lobby, in a meeting room. The dense swarm requires no great height — the composition is compact vertically.

**Style.** Art Deco and contemporary minimalism: strict geometry in a warm register. The message is restrained and expensive — a piece for an interior that values order.

The number of elements, their calibres and the density of the swarm are all made to order.
