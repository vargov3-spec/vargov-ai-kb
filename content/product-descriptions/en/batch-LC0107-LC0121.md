# Product card texts — batch 40: LC0107–LC0121 (15 items)

Approved format: artistic image, day-to-evening shift, "Where it works", "Style", closing line on customisation. Element materials are never named. LC0121, LC0120, LC0116, LC0112, LC0111 and LC0110 are wall compositions and a mirror.

## LC0121 — Wall panel

A composition about a wreath coming apart. The main mass is laid in a dense overlapping ring, but on the right the feathers break away one after another: first in a group, then in pairs, then alone. The last of them — the palest, almost white — hang far from the wreath and act as the point the eye is led toward.

A closed form with one side opened always reads as a process. The author uses that: the wreath is not broken, it is flying away.

The palette is warm and autumnal — copper, burnt bronze, dark ochre, with gold and near-white feathers across the field. By day the panel reads through colour and gradient: the ribbed surface of close barbs catches the light in bands. In the evening the backlight emerges along the rims and traces every feather with a fine gold line — the mass gains volume, and the departing tail lights up separately from the ring.

**Where it works.** Feature walls — halls, stair landings, private living rooms, reception areas, gallery spaces. An open composition needs room to the right: the tail must run into empty space rather than into a corner.

**Style.** Art Deco and autumn classicism: the feather as a motif of opulence, movement as the subject. The message is poetic — a picture-piece for a wall that leads the eye.

The overall size, the number of feathers, the length of the departing tail and the palette are all made to order.

## LC0120 — Wall panel

The same wreath, but shedding downward. The direction is reversed: instead of wind carrying feathers sideways, weight and fall do the work. The stream separates below, thinning as it goes, and the last feathers hang alone far from the mass.

The palette is cool — subdued moss green, silver-grey, matte white, with gold inclusions around the ring. The transition between tones is gradual, with no boundaries.

By day the panel reads as graphics: the worked shafts and barbs give a continuously ribbed surface that catches the light in bands. In the evening the ring lights up along its contour while the falling feathers stay dark — and the movement becomes more legible than it is by daylight. Light here works as direction rather than decoration.

**Where it works.** Feature walls — halls, living rooms, stair landings, reception areas, private studies. The vertical shedding needs clearance below the ring.

**Style.** Nordic modernism and contemporary classicism: a cool palette, a natural motif. The message is restrained — a piece for an interior that dislikes volume.

The wreath diameter, the number of feathers, the length of the fall and the palette are all made to order.

## LC0119 — Light composition

A composition about two temperaments. Soft and sharp meet here: broad crumpled caps with wavy rims and long faceted spikes drawn downward. The author mixes them without system — cap, spike, cap again — and the composition keeps changing its mood.

The bowl is shallow, denser at the centre, sparser at the edges, with single spikes descending below. No element repeats its neighbour in form or in orientation.

By day the object is all but invisible: clear and colourless, it shows itself only in highlights, and in a bright room it is easy to miss. In the evening the difference between the forms comes fully into play — the caps gather the light and diffuse it into a broad soft patch, the spikes carry it their whole length and flare at the tip. A gathering of luminous forms suspended in emptiness.

**Where it works.** Living rooms with high ceilings, stairwells, lobbies, zones above a table. Transparency needs a dark or saturated backdrop: against a white ceiling the piece is lost.

**Style.** Contemporary classicism and minimalism: contrast of form instead of colour. The message is spare — a piece for an interior built on nuance.

The overall size, the number of elements, the ratio of caps to spikes and the suspension lengths are all made to order.

## LC0118 — Light composition

A composition about descent. The colour changes with height: clear and colourless above, drifting into smoky blue lower down, near-graphite at the very bottom. The transition is gradual, without boundaries — this is how light diminishes in a body of water, and it diminishes here in the same way.

Clusters of differing height and diameter form a dense diamond-shaped volume, broad at the middle and pointed at top and bottom. The whole mass reads as one body, not as a set of parts.

By day it is organ pipes suspended below the ceiling — vertical hatching, austere and resonant. In the evening the upper tiers glow bright and white while the lower ones absorb the light and stay cold: from below the composition looks like a cloud lit only at the top. An iceberg lit from its summit.

**Where it works.** Generous volumes — atria, lobbies, stairwells, double-height living rooms, banquet halls. The gradient exists only across a substantial vertical: in a low room it has no space to unfold.

**Style.** Contemporary minimalism and Nordic aesthetics: the vertical, the cold and monotony as devices. The message is monumental — architecture rather than decoration.

The overall size, the number of clusters, the element lengths and the depth of the tinting are all made to order.

## LC0117 — Light composition

A composition about the gills beneath a cap. The pattern radiating from the centre is taken from a mushroom — from the part of it people never look at: gills exist precisely not to be examined. The author turns the thing over and makes the underside the face.

The discs hang almost flat, in layers, densely — the caps overlap with almost no gaps, and from below the field reads as continuous. The composition is drawn out as a rectangle: not a cloud but a canopy.

By day it is a colony seen from beneath — a smoky grey-beige tone, the ribbing read through light and shade. In the evening a gold heart lights up at the centre of each disc, the light spreads along the rays, and every cap glows radially, from a bright centre to a subdued rim. Hundreds of radial glows give an even warm field of luminous rosettes.

**Where it works.** Generous rooms — lobbies, dining rooms, meeting rooms, panoramic living rooms, reception areas. A continuous field gives even light: the piece works as principal illumination, not as an accent.

**Style.** Organic modernism and mid-century: a natural form in a rectangular grid. The message is calm and warm — a piece for working and dining spaces.

The overall size, the number of discs, the calibres and the depth of the smoky tone are all made to order.

## LC0116 — Wall composition with mirror

A composition about a gaze from the depths. A large disc with dense radial pleating — hundreds of fine petals running from the centre to an uneven rim — holds a round mirror at its middle. The reflection is set inside a living form: whoever looks sees themselves inside an anemone.

Beside it two smaller satellite discs without mirrors, and around them crumpled "stones" with a mirrored glaze — some gold, some chrome. They scatter from the centre in uneven groups and give the whole composition movement.

By day the object works as a sculptural mirror with satellites: a deep tone running from charcoal grey to plum, coppery shifts along the petal rims, mirror and glaze catching the room. In the evening the backlight emerges along the contours of the discs, brings out the relief of the pleating and reflects in the glaze of the stones — a colony lit on the wall.

**Where it works.** Entrance halls, dressing rooms, bathrooms, boudoirs, private living rooms. The mirror is functional, so mounting height is set by the user, not by the composition.

**Style.** Marine Art Nouveau and contemporary decorative sculpture. The message is intimate — a piece for a space where people are alone with themselves.

The mirror diameter, the number of satellite discs, the number of stones and the glaze palette are all made to order.

## LC0115 — Light composition

A composition about something grown rather than assembled. The branches curve in a broad loop in the horizontal plane, divide, and on every stalk sits its own rosette at its own angle. The composition reads as something that developed by itself — not one position is repeated.

The rosettes, with fine radial pleating and a slightly rolled, uneven rim, differ a little from one another in calibre and inclination. The fibrous texture gives the material visible veining.

By day it is a field of paper flowers on a gold loop — bright, light, almost weightless. In the evening light from the nodes of the branches passes through the thin structure and the discs begin to glow from within, entire, with their veining showing; the gold shows through them as a warm thread. Not one direct source is visible — a cloud of luminous rosettes.

**Where it works.** Generous rooms — lobbies, double-height living rooms, dining rooms, banquet and wedding halls. The horizontal loop reads from above and from the side: the piece is hung over a table or a gathering zone.

**Style.** Art Nouveau and romantic classicism: flowering as the subject, gold as the setting. The message is festive and bright — a piece for a ceremonial space.

The overall diameter, the number of rosettes, the calibres and the finish of the branches are all made to order.

## LC0114 — Light composition

A composition about soft and hard. Rounded scaled buds — and straight rods set obliquely between them. Two forms with nothing in common: one grew, the other was drawn. The author sets them against each other on purpose.

The buds form a dense lower mass with a ragged upper contour, a few rising high above it. The rods, by contrast, pierce the upper zone at varied angles and work as hard graphics against the rounded forms.

By day it is a botanical study laid over a drafting grid: white matte cones and gold lines. In the evening the light lives in two places at once — it emerges from under the scales, lighting the buds from within in a warm milky tone, and runs as a line along the rods, turning them into glowing strokes. Diffuse and linear glow together: a field of fruit stitched through with gold.

**Where it works.** Living rooms with high ceilings, lobbies, stairwells, restaurant floors. The rods extend beyond the envelope of the main mass: allow clearance to the sides.

**Style.** Contemporary sculpture and Bauhaus graphics: nature against geometry. The message is intellectual — a piece for an interior that likes contradiction.

The overall size, the number of buds and the length and number of rods are all made to order.

## LC0113 — Light composition

A composition about the valve. In a scallop the fan pattern is not decoration but structure: the ribs hold the thin valve together. The author takes that logic — here the ribbing works too, and what it carries is light.

The elements are turned in different directions: some lie almost flat, others stand on edge. The bowl is shallow, denser at the centre and thinned at the edges.

By day it is a scatter of shells below the ceiling — white, matte, read through the light and shade across the fan of ribbing. In the evening the light from the gold heart spreads along the ribs toward the wavy rim, and the whole structure of the valve emerges against the light. The glowing centres face different ways, and the composition looks different from every point — a field of luminous fans.

**Where it works.** Generous rooms — lobbies, double-height living rooms, dining rooms, spas and pools. Because the elements face every direction the piece has no "front": it is placed where people walk around it.

**Style.** Marine Art Nouveau and Mediterranean classicism: the shell as one of the oldest decorative motifs. The message is light and coastal — a piece for a bright interior near water.

The overall size, the number of elements, the calibres and the tone of the glow are all made to order.

## LC0112 — Wall composition

A composition about mercury. Crumpled stones of irregular geometry, each with a pressed-in surface — as though fingers had been drawn across something soft — are covered in unbroken mirrored silver without a single matte area. The object has no appearance of its own: it shows only what is around it.

Stones of varying calibre are laid out in a free circle, denser at the centre and sparser toward the edge. No pattern and no order — a natural distribution, like shingle on a shore.

By day it is a scatter of mercury drops: the mirrored surface catches the light of the room and inverts it in the crumpled folds, so each stone acquires its own complex highlight that shifts at the slightest movement of the viewer. In the evening, under directional light, the composition becomes a gathering of mirrors throwing light across the wall — and the wall comes alive.

**Where it works.** Feature walls — halls, living rooms, stair landings, reception areas, dressing rooms. The composition depends on its surroundings: it is hung where there is something to reflect.

**Style.** Contemporary art and minimalism: an object with no image of its own. The message is conceptual — a piece for an interior that values the idea.

The area of the layout, the number of stones, the calibres and the character of the mirrored finish are all made to order.

## LC0111 — Wall composition

A composition about the nugget. The face is polished to a mirror while the sides and back drift into dark patina — each stone reads as an ingot lifted from the ground and burnished on one side only. This is not an ornament but a find: the piece shows both the gold and the rock it came out of.

The stones are laid out in an elongated horizontal group: large at the centre, small at the edges, with breaks and clusterings. The composition is deliberately asymmetrical — it has not been arranged, it has been poured out.

By day it is a scatter of nuggets: gold on crumpled folds gives complex warm highlights, while the dark patina around the edges outlines each stone and separates it from its neighbour. In the evening, under directional light, the composition flares as a whole; under diffuse light it stays calm and almost graphic. Gold found in the rock.

**Where it works.** Feature walls — halls, private living rooms, studies, reception areas, bars. The effect depends on directional light: allow for a track or spots.

**Style.** Brutalist luxury and contemporary art: the precious alongside the unworked. The message is direct and masculine — a piece for an interior with a strong character.

The area of the layout, the number of stones, the calibres and the gilding finish are all made to order.

## LC0110 — Wall composition

A composition about what is inside. The oyster is a thing whose entire value is hidden: a rough layered shell outside, a nacreous layer with speckling and marbling within. The author opens the valves and lays them out on the wall face outward.

Valves of varying calibre lie in a dense circle: large at the centre, small at the edges, all turned differently, so the pale insets face different ways and the composition reads as volumetric.

By day the piece rests on three contrasts: the dark rough shell, the pale smooth core and the fine gold line along the rim. In the evening the backlight emerges from behind the valves and traces the gold rim, while the nacreous insets reflect it softly, without glare. A colony lit from beneath.

**Where it works.** Feature walls — halls, private living rooms, spas, restaurants, reception areas. The circular layout needs a clear wall: neighbouring objects break the form.

**Style.** Marine Art Nouveau and natural opulence: the shell as a symbol of hidden value. The message is tactile — a piece you want to touch.

The area of the layout, the number of valves, the calibres and the palette are all made to order.

## LC0109 — Light composition

A composition about a scree slope. Large crumpled stones hang almost touching, at varied levels, and the mass reads as continuous and heavy — a rockfall suspended in the air a moment before it lands. The density here is not decorative but semantic: scree is never sparse.

Every stone is pressed and folded in its own way, none repeating its neighbour. The polish is unbroken, without matte areas, so the object works purely through reflection.

By day it is a cluster of gold boulders — heavy, rich, read through ragged highlights on the crumpled surfaces. In the evening the light emerges only in the gaps between the stones, strikes the gold flanks and reflects repeatedly, boulder to boulder: the mass fills from within and looks red-hot. Rock just taken out of the furnace.

**Where it works.** Living rooms with high ceilings, lobbies, private studies, bars, showrooms. A dense heavy mass needs height and clear surroundings — everything else nearby should stay silent.

**Style.** Brutalist luxury and contemporary Art Deco: a mass of gold instead of filigree. The message is direct and generous — a piece for an interior unembarrassed by force.

The overall size, the number of stones, the calibres and the gilding finish are all made to order.

## LC0108 — Light curtain composition

A composition about a rain of stones. The curtain is not regular: some strands descend deep, others break off high, and the spacing of the beads differs on each. The top is level, the lower edge ragged — this is how something heavier than water falls, at differing speeds.

The main mass is a subdued green, with colourless clear beads set among it. The calibres vary — from large spheres to fine crumbs — and that too is about falling: large things fall differently from small ones.

By day it is a green veil — dense, subdued, working through colour and reflection. In the evening the light runs down the strands from above and enters every bead: the facets break it and scatter it in sparks, the green ones fill with a deep tone, the colourless flare white. The light passes through several layers and loses its source — the whole volume glows. A rain of precious stones.

**Where it works.** Generous volumes — atria, lobbies, stairwells, banquet and wedding halls. The curtain is installed without interfering with the walls and covers tall openings easily.

**Style.** Art Deco and Eastern opulence: stones on a strand as a classic of formal decoration. The message is generous — a piece for a space that celebrates.

The overall size, the strand spacing, the palette of the beads and the calibres are all made to order.

## LC0107 — Light composition

A composition about a caught drop. Every inverted drop with its sharp tip hangs on a fine hook bent like a question mark — a thing that ought to be falling turns out to be suspended. The whole tension of the composition rests on that.

The upper zone is left empty: only the hatching of threads, with small beads hanging between them — gold and cool blue. The mass of drops gathers in the lower third, with a wavy lower edge.

By day the composition reads graphically — hatched threads and clear forms, almost a technical drawing. In the evening the light passes through the body of each drop and bursts out at the tip as a bright point, the gold hook above it picks up the reflection and outlines it from above, and the beads give scattered sparks between the main elements. Only the glowing drops remain, hanging from gold loops.

**Where it works.** An elongated horizontal format — long dining tables, bar counters, lobbies, meeting rooms. The light gathers into points below: the piece lights the table, not the room.

**Style.** Jewellery modernism and contemporary classicism: the suspension as a subject in its own right. The message is refined — a piece for a table where people sit close.

The length, the number of drops, the palette of the beads and the drop heights are all made to order.
