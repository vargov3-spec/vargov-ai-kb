## LC0016 — Light composition

A composition about the outline. A fine gold band runs along the rim of every petal, tracing the form and flaring at each bend. The device comes from jewellery: this is how cloisonné enamel is bounded so the colour cannot run. Here it holds nothing — it only draws.

The mass is gathered into a lens shape: wide at the centre, tapering to sharp points at both ends. The petals are layered, each turned differently; the upper half is given over to the hatching of the suspension threads, the whole mass sits below.

By day it is an armful of petals caught on a gold thread: milky white with a hot contour, a graphic piece. In the evening the white diffuses the light and glows evenly, without a single flare, while the gold rims intercept it and trace every petal with a burning line. The source is never visible — the mass itself glows, drawn out by hundreds of gold outlines. A cloud with burning edges.

**Where it works.** An elongated horizontal format — long dining tables, banquet and wedding halls, lobbies, meeting rooms. The soft glare-free light is comfortable directly above a table.

**Style.** Art Deco and jewellery-inflected modernism: the outline as the only ornament. The message is festive and restrained at once — a piece for an interior that celebrates quietly.

The length, the number of petals, the width of the gold rim and the drop heights are all made to order.

## LC0015 — Light composition

A composition about a body that is barely there. The moon jellyfish shows only its ribs: everything else is transparent enough to read as emptiness. The author takes that literally — a flat, rounded dome with fine radial ribbing along the edge and a short tail.

Hundreds of domes hang mixed with tiny bubble-beads at varied heights and tilts; some trail down on long threads with sparse beads, like tentacles. What you read is not an object but a medium.

By day the composition is all but invisible: a cool bluish cast, a cloud of small clear things that is easy to overlook. In the evening the light enters the ribbing and traces every dome with a glowing net of ribs, while the bubbles flash as small points. The room becomes a body of water full of jellyfish.

**Where it works.** Generous rooms — lobbies, pools and spas, panoramic living rooms, meeting rooms. Transparency needs a dark backdrop: against a white ceiling the piece disappears by day.

**Style.** Marine modernism with a scientific edge: biological accuracy without stylisation. The message is contemplative — a piece you look into.

The length, the number of elements, the density of the bubbles and the drop heights are all made to order.

## LC0014 — Light composition

A composition about a pond in the rain. The discs hang almost flat — leaves on water; the threads run to the ceiling in dense hatching — the downpour above them. Two images laid over one another: a rainstorm with leaves suspended inside it.

The pyramid is wide at the top and narrows below, where single discs drop far down on their own. Small faceted beads are scattered between them.

By day it is a pool with floating leaves: deep blue-violet mixed with colourless, read through colour and ribbing. In the evening light enters each disc at the rim and runs out along the ribs, so every one glows radially from centre to edge — the blue ones fill with dense ultramarine, the colourless flash white, and the beads break the light into sparks. Night water lit from beneath.

**Where it works.** Generous rooms with high ceilings — atria, lobbies, stairwells, pools and spas. The pyramidal build needs height.

**Style.** Art Nouveau and Japanese water aesthetics: the water lily as a classic motif. The message is contemplative and cool — a piece for interiors near water.

The overall size, the number of discs, the proportion of blue and the drop heights are all made to order.

## LC0013 — Light composition

A composition about wind. The mass runs from the upper left corner down to the right, thinning as it goes; the last feathers hang alone, far from the group. All of them are turned along that movement — you read a current, not an arrangement.

The feather is narrow, with a wavy edge and close longitudinal scoring. Warm amber-honey mixes with clear silver; a few feathers carry a red accent at the tip.

By day these are feathers caught in a current: a diagonal band read through colour and inclination. In the evening the light enters at the quill and spreads along the scoring — each feather glows in strokes, the amber ones fill with copper, the clear ones throw white sparks, the red tips flare as points. A flock crossing the room on the diagonal.

**Where it works.** Generous rooms and irregular geometry — stairwells, atria, curved halls, double-height living rooms. The diagonal is designed for the specific volume.

**Style.** Art Nouveau and natural romanticism: the feather as a classic motif of lightness. The message is poetic — a piece that fills a room with movement.

The length and pitch of the current, the number of feathers, the palette and the drop heights are all made to order.

## LC0012 — Light composition

The rods hang almost horizontally, layered and overlapping. The large irregular faceting gives each its own pattern of refraction; colourless ice mixes with warm gold.

By day it is an icy flock — a clear mass with gold inclusions, read through the shape of the wing. In the evening the faceting breaks the light and scatters it along each rod, so the layers glow in strokes: the gold sections fill with warmth, the icy ones flash white. At the fold of the wing, where the rods are densest, the glow is at its strongest — a wing assembled out of light.

**Where it works.** Generous rooms with high ceilings — atria, lobbies, stairwells, double-height living rooms. The wingspan needs width: the piece is designed for the specific volume.

**Style.** Contemporary sculptural installation and grand classicism. The message is monumental — a piece that sets the scale of a room.

## LC0011 — Wall composition

A composition about a cross-section. Concentric arcs around an off-centre point draw close, then open to reveal the ground — a tree ring, a fingerprint, ripples on water. The ambiguity is deliberate: what all three share is that the pattern cannot be repeated; it is always unique.

The form is broken open: the composition occupies not a full circle but a crescent with one side cut away. Because of that it never reads as a closed target — it reads as a fragment of something larger.

By day the panel works as metalwork graphics: polished bronze with gold and copper shifts, a mirrored surface in which each band reflects its neighbour, so an endless exchange of highlights builds up in the depth. In the evening the backlight emerges from behind the plates and grazes them at a sharp angle, bringing out the whole relief: a cross-section lit from within.

**Where it works.** Feature walls — halls, stair landings, private living rooms, reception areas, gallery spaces. The open form suits a long wall: the cut side runs out beyond the field of view.

**Style.** Art Deco and contemporary sculpture: the concentric pattern as a classic motif. The message is expressive — a picture-object for a wall that needs nothing else.

The overall size, the number of bands, the ring spacing and the metal finish are all made to order.

## LC0010 — Light composition

## LC0009 — Light composition

A composition about an organ. The tubes in each cluster are cut to different heights, so the upper contour is stepped — the same logic as organ pipes, where length determines pitch. Here length determines the depth at which the glowing point hangs.

The clusters hang at varied levels and almost touch — together they read both as an organ and as a stalagmite forest. The material is clear and colourless, with occasional bubbles inside.

By day it is a transparent forest — clear verticals read through highlights, the composition visible right through. In the evening the light enters from above and travels down each cylinder like a lightguide: the cylinder itself stays almost invisible, while the lower cut flares into a bright point. Hundreds of glowing circles hang at varied depths — rain stopped mid-fall.

**Where it works.** Generous rooms — lobbies, atria, stairwells, double-height living rooms, banquet halls. The multi-level arrangement reads from below and from the side: it needs volume.

**Style.** Contemporary minimalism and architectural classicism: the vertical as the only motif. The message is austere and ceremonial — a piece for a formal space.

The overall size, the number of clusters, the tube lengths and the drop heights are all made to order.

## LC0008 — Light composition

The stems carry the drops well beyond the main volume, and a fringe forms around the edges. Inside the stand the drops hang closer and lower.

By day it is a thicket in bloom: clear hatching and white spheres on curved arcs, read through silhouette. In the evening the light lives in two places — the rods carry it along their length and flare at the ends as points, while the drops glow from within in an even milky tone, without flare. A bristling hatch inside and soft glowing spheres around the edge: a swarm of fireflies around a clear column.

**Where it works.** Living rooms with high ceilings, stairwells, lobbies, atria. The fringe extends beyond the main envelope: allow clearance to the sides.

**Style.** Art Nouveau and organic modernism: a plant with a different character inside and out. The message is alive — a piece for an interior that loves natural forms.

The overall size, the number of stems, the calibre of the drops and the drop heights are all made to order.

## LC0007 — Light composition

The composition is drawn out on the diagonal: the leaves are turned at random, overlapping in places, elsewhere breaking away alone far from the group. The upper edge is ragged.

By day it is an armful of leaves caught in flight: clear mixed with amber, the composition read right through, with real depth. In the evening the clear ones pass the light and flare along the net of veins, while the amber ones fill with copper from within. A crown whose venation is alight.

**Where it works.** Generous rooms — lobbies, atria, double-height living rooms, winter gardens. Transparency gives depth when seen from the side: the piece is not hung tight to the ceiling.

**Style.** Art Nouveau and botanical modernism: the leaf as a classic motif. The message is natural and warm — a piece for an interior with greenery.

The overall size, the number of leaves, the share of amber and the drop heights are all made to order.

## LC0005 — Light composition

A composition about plumage. The plates within each row are inclined identically, while the rows themselves rise, fall, twist and straighten again — together they give the effect of a feather or a crest laid down by the wind. The form changes completely with the viewpoint: the piece has no principal face.

There are hundreds of plates, each fixed at its own angle. Uniformity within the row and freedom between rows — the whole plasticity rests on that.

By day it is a surface built from scales: white ribbing read through light and shadow, calm and dense. In the evening the light grazes the plates at a sharp angle and brings out the ribbing — some rows light up entirely, others stay in shadow, and a wave of light and shade runs the whole length. There are no hard points: only grazing light. A wing across which light runs.

**Where it works.** Large spaces — atria, lobbies, conference halls, retail galleries, double-height living rooms. Grazing light means the piece gives little general illumination and works as an architectural element.

**Style.** Parametric design and contemporary architecture: the array rather than the object. The message is monumental — a surface-piece, not a luminaire.

The overall size, the wave pattern, the number of plates and the surface finish are all made to order.

## LC0004 — Light composition

**Style.** Grand classicism in contemporary execution: a cascade without a single faceted drop. The message is ceremonial — a piece for a space that receives guests.

## LC0002 — Light composition

A composition about three behaviours of one beam. White gloss, polished gold and clear are mixed without system, so at any point in the composition all three fall side by side. That is exactly what keeps the mass uneven: it never looks flat.

The bowl is deep, the mass dense below and thinned above, where single petals break away and hang alone among the hatching of threads with small point lights.

By day it is an armful of petals suspended below the ceiling: white, gold and clear intermixed, read through the plasticity of the crumpled edge. In the evening the white petals diffuse the light and glow evenly, the gold ones reflect and flare hot, the clear ones transmit it and throw icy sparks. A cloud in which light behaves differently at every point.

**Where it works.** Formal spaces — lobbies, double-height living rooms, banquet and wedding halls, atria. The deep bowl needs height: pressed to the ceiling it loses its volume.

**Style.** Contemporary Art Deco and Hollywood glamour: three finishes instead of ornament. The message is festive — a piece for an interior that loves shine.

The overall size, the number of petals, the ratio of the three finishes and the drop heights are all made to order.

## LC0001 — Light composition

A composition about a current. The arc rises at one end, sags in the middle and lifts again; the petals are turned along that line, in layers. Density grows toward the centre and falls toward the ends, where single elements break away and hang alone — a flock moving as one.

The surface is smooth, without texture of any kind: only form and finish are at work. White gloss and polished gold — two ways of meeting light, and nothing more.

By day it is the pure contrast of two surfaces: opaque gloss beside mirrored gold, read through silhouette and highlight. In the evening the gold petals reflect the light and flare into radiant stars, while the white ones diffuse it and stay soft patches. A current in which every second element burns.

**Where it works.** An elongated horizontal format — long dining tables, bar counters, lobbies, meeting rooms, reception areas. The curve of the arc is calculated for the length of the zone.

**Style.** Contemporary Art Deco and minimalism: two finishes, not one superfluous element. The message is representative — a piece for an interior that rests on proportion.

The length, the arc pattern, the number of petals and the ratio of white to gold are all made to order.
