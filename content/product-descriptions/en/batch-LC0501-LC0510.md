## LC0510 — Light composition

A composition about a double temperament. The wings are clear, the bodies gold: the cold anatomy of flight and a warm heart inside every bird. The source is hidden in the body and the light comes out through the mass of the wing.

Every wing is worked down to the individual feather — a detailed form rather than a silhouette.

By day it is a clear flock with gold bodies spread across a wide front: the composition reads through anatomy and the highlights on the feathers. In the evening the light comes out through the mass and the feathers catch an icy fire — the bodies stay warm points, the wings turn cold.

**Where it works.** A long table, a bar counter, a reception desk, a meeting room. The horizontal front covers a space the way a flock covers a field: the composition is oriented along the length of the zone.

**Style.** Art Nouveau and contemporary sculpture: the bird as a classic motif of flight. The message is alive and narrative — a piece that creates a scene.

The number of birds, the spread of the flock and the direction of flight are all made to order.

## LC0509 — Light composition

The same flock twisted into a vertical column. The birds rise in a spiral, like a current caught by a draught — and the composition fills a vertical with movement from the bottom up.

Every bird is unique in its turn and its tilt: the vortex does not repeat itself from any angle.

By day it is a clear spiral with gold bodies — a piece read through its twist and its silhouettes, alive as you walk around it. In the evening light shimmers in the feathers as the sun shimmers in the flight feathers of a real flock: some individuals flare, others stay shadow, and the current seems to be moving.

**Where it works.** Staircases and double-height spaces. The vortex is designed for a specific height and reads differently from every level.

**Style.** Art Nouveau and contemporary installation: the rising current as subject. The message is dynamic — a piece for a vertical that has to lead the eye upward.

The height of the vortex, the number of birds and the density of the spiral are all made to order.

## LC0508 — Decorative composition

A composition about the time of day. The gradient of the flock reads as the passage of a day: from daylight above to dusk below — from white through dusty rose to deep violet. The flock is not flying, it is darkening.

This is a decorative composition with no light of its own: it works through plasticity, colour and shadow. The matte surface kills highlights nobly, and the gold of the beaks and breasts sets the accents.

By day the gradient reads in full: colour is the piece's principal argument, and daylight shows it more precisely than any lighting. In the evening, under directed light, the matte surface gives deep soft shadows while the gold details flare as points — the flock acquires a volume invisible by day.

**Where it works.** Staircases, entrance halls, wall piers, living rooms — wherever a coloured vertical accent is needed without lighting technology. It requires no wiring but gains from directed light nearby.

**Style.** Contemporary sculpture and natural Art Nouveau: the gradient as subject. The message is poetic — a piece for an interior that loves colour.

The number of birds, the stretch of the gradient and the palette are all made to order.

## LC0507 — Decorative composition

A composition about flight in all its phases at once. The wings are open, folded, turned — one movement broken into frames and gathered into a single vertical.

Snow-white ones with gold bodies alternate with occasional graphite blue: gulls and petrels in one flock. The dark wings give depth and rhythm.

By day the white feather texture gathers light from the space and looks luminous with no lamps at all — the piece works on daylight alone. In the evening, under directed light, the texture gives a relief of light and shade while the dark wings drop into hollows: the flock becomes more contrasted than it is by day.

**Where it works.** Interiors where the lighting is already resolved and what is needed is volume, movement and character — living rooms, entrance halls, staircases, reception rooms. The composition requires no wiring.

**Style.** Marine modern and contemporary sculpture: the flock as graphic art in three dimensions. The message is alive — a piece for an interior that lacks movement.

The number of wings, the share of dark ones and the scale are all made to order.

## LC0506 — Sculptural composition

A composition about a feather cast in mercury. Mirrored crescents with the finest ribbed texture circle in a vortex around an invisible centre — the movement is set by the arrangement rather than by the form.

The mirrored surface gathers the interior and the light around it: the composition is always the colour of its own space — cold in the morning, warm in the evening. Gold finials place the full stops in the whirl.

By day the vortex lives on daylight: the crescents reflect the window, the walls, one another, and the drawing changes as the sun moves. In the evening warm lamplight tints the mirror and the same composition turns golden — the piece has no permanent colour, it takes one from the room.

**Where it works.** Contemporary interiors — above a staircase, in a dining room, on an accent wall, in an entrance hall. It fixes to a wall or a ceiling and works as graphic art in three dimensions.

**Style.** Contemporary sculpture and minimalism: the mirror as a device. The message is conceptual — a piece for an interior that values an idea.

The diameter of the vortex, the number of wings and the finish are all made to order.

## LC0505 — Light composition

A composition about a runway. The ribbing on a broad single wing turns it in the evening into a strip of light — the same marking that rules an airfield runway, only carried into the air.

The wings read as gliders or as rays: whole, without division into feathers. Beneath each is a gold keel-cone with light. The white ceiling rails are part of the aesthetic: the installation looks technological, like a light-aviation hangar.

By day it is an almost colourless flock: the wings read through ribbing and form, and the composition is very airy. In the evening light floods the wing from below, the ribbing drives it along, and the flock covers a space with an even warm radiance — a hundred runways overhead.

**Where it works.** Lobbies, conference halls, meeting rooms, retail galleries, panoramic living rooms. The even radiance makes the composition the main source of lighting.

**Style.** Industrial modernism and aviation aesthetics: engineering as beauty. The message is rational — a piece for a contemporary space.

The number of gliders, the area of the flock and the drop height are all made to order.

## LC0504 — Light composition

A composition about snow in headlights. The dense bubbled texture on V-shaped wings reads as frost on a window, and in the evening it gives the principal effect: the light breaks up in thousands of bubbles and shimmers like snow caught in a beam.

The column is vertical, and the body of every pair of wings carries a source that traces the contours in gold.

By day it is a column of ice: the texture reads against the light, the wings fold into a vertical rhythm, a cold and graphic piece. In the evening the texture takes the leading part — the light breaks up and shimmers over the whole height, and the column begins to tremble.

**Where it works.** Stairwells and narrow verticals. The composition takes no floor area but fills a height — ideal where there is no room for a bulky piece.

**Style.** Nordic Art Nouveau: the butterfly and the hoarfrost as paired motifs. The message is poetic and cool — a piece for an interior in a cold register.

The number of butterflies, the height of the column and the density are all made to order.

## LC0503 — Decorative composition

A composition about a migration over a northern sea. Birds of a smoky blue shade fly in a vertical stream — calmly and steadily, without drama: this is neither a flight nor a take-off but a long journey.

The muted blue-grey is a rare guest in light decor and precious for that reason: it ties the interior to the sky beyond the window. The gold breasts are the only warm accent.

By day the colour works at full strength: the smoky blue answers the sky, and the composition seems a continuation of the view. In the evening, under directed light, the matte surface gives deep shadows, the gold flares as points, and the stream becomes gathered and graphic.

**Where it works.** Bedrooms, studies, staircases, interiors in a Scandinavian or Japanese aesthetic. The composition is decorative — it works without lamps but gains from side light.

**Style.** Scandinavian minimalism and the Japanese school: the migration as a classic subject. The message is quiet — a piece for an interior that does not raise its voice.

The number of birds, the depth of the shade and the height of the stream are all made to order.

## LC0502 — Light composition

A composition as an event. This is not a luminaire but the central object of a space: giant wings opened to their full span, holding a hall single-handed.

Feather by feather, from the flight feathers to the down — the form is worked entire, with no generalisations. The mass with its air bubbles makes every feather alive.

By day it is a transparent sculpture: the bubbles read against the light, the feathers catch daylight along their edges, and the wings seem almost weightless. In the evening light flows along the edges from within, flares at the tips and runs down as needles of ice — the wings catch fire in gold while staying cold to the eye.

**Where it works.** Above a grand piano, in an atrium, above a formal staircase, in a double-height lobby. The composition requires complete openness: there should be no other accents near it.

**Style.** Art Nouveau and monumental sculpture: the wing as a symbol. The message is ceremonial, almost sacred — a piece for a space that has to astonish.

The span, the number of feathers and the colour temperature of the light are all made to order.

## LC0501 — Light composition

A composition about a shaving off a cutting tool. Springy rings and half-rings of ribbed ribbon repeat a form that arises by itself — as a by-product of work. The author makes it the main thing.

Every curl carries a gold capsule of light; the cloud is vertical and dense.

By day it is a vortex of ribbed coils: the composition reads through plasticity and hatching, alive and mobile. In the evening light runs along the spirals, slips off the coils and fills the space with dancing highlights — the cloud begins to turn.

**Where it works.** Stairwells, the zone above a round table, bay windows. The composition is flexible in mood: assembled densely it reads as baroque, thinned out as light calligraphy.

**Style.** Baroque and industrial minimalism in one piece: the curl without ornament. The message is expressive — a piece for an interior that needs character.

The number of curls, the height of the cloud and the density are all made to order.
