## LC0541 — Light composition

A composition about a fragility that must not be over-lit. The light here does not dominate, it accompanies: fine gold drop-luminaires are dissolved through the flock and underline the contours without destroying the lightness. The author deliberately dims the source so as not to kill the image.

The butterflies — clear and smoky, with gold accents on the bodies — hover as a cloud on fine suspensions.

By day the wings catch daylight and pass it on: reflections fly from facet to facet, and the swarm comes alive at any movement of air. In the evening the drops trace the contours in warmth while the wings themselves stay clear — what glows is not the butterflies but the lines between them.

**Where it works.** A dense swarm above a table, a sparse flock along a staircase, a vortex in an entrance hall — the size and the character of the flight are set at assembly. The composition adapts to any geometry.

**Style.** Art Nouveau and romantic modern: the butterfly as a classic motif of fragility. The message is light and poetic — a piece for an interior that does not want heaviness.

The number of butterflies, the palette of the wings and the shape of the cloud are all made to order.

## LC0540 — Light composition

A composition about frozen rain. The tubes descend in a dense cascade — jets caught by frost halfway down. The lower edge breathes: long and short alternate like the edge of melting ice.

Inside, a bubbled mass; outside, chrome tips: a cold, clean note held all the way through.

By day it is clear verticals with bubbles seen against the light — a piece read through rhythm and the ragged lower edge, strict and bright. In the evening thousands of bubbles flare along the whole length, and every tube turns into a column of radiance: the ice catches fire from within.

**Where it works.** Stairwells and the zone above a kitchen island, where verticals read at full height; contemporary spaces with panoramic windows and spare finishes. The cascade is designed for the height.

**Style.** Contemporary minimalism and high-tech: cold as an aesthetic position. The message is clean and technical — a piece for an interior with no embellishment.

The number of tubes, their lengths and the format of the cascade are all made to order.

## LC0538 — Light composition

A composition about a storm. Fan-shaped wings twist through space in swift spirals — the feathers of a firebird, or petals caught by the wind. The movement is taken to the limit: this is one of the brand's most temperamental pieces.

The colour works at the same limit: a deep ruby with not one softening shade.

By day it is red vortices with chrome cores — a dense colour read through the twist and the rod texture of the fans. In the evening capsules within ignite the fans with a deep garnet radiance and the space around is charged: the light does not illuminate, it heats.

**Where it works.** Interiors that need character — restaurants, bars, art spaces, living rooms with a bold palette. Saturated red takes command of a room: neutral surfaces are needed nearby.

**Style.** Contemporary sculptural installation and theatrical aesthetics: colour and movement as a statement. The message is passionate — a piece for a space that is not afraid to be loud.

The spread of the vortex, the number of fans and the saturation of the red are all made to order.

## LC0537 — Light composition

A composition about a trace on ice. The ribbons wind in smooth spirals — the crests of waves, untied bows, the arcs left by a skater: a form with no beginning and no end, which the eye follows without stopping.

The rods stay almost colourless: all the expression is in movement and light, not a gram of decor.

By day it is a clear streamer in the air — a piece read through trajectory and with almost no mass. In the evening gold capsules woven into the ribbons ignite them from within at warm intervals: the light comes not as an unbroken line but as a dotted one — like a trace that shows and then does not.

**Where it works.** Large spaces — atriums, double-height living rooms, lobbies where the streamer has somewhere to fly. The trajectory is designed for a specific volume.

**Style.** Contemporary sculptural installation: the line as the only material. The message is light and dynamic — a piece for a space that wants movement.

The length and trajectory of the ribbons and the number of coils and points of light are all made to order.

## LC0536 — Light composition

A composition about the beginning of autumn. Smoky and pale leaves alternate, giving the cloud a depth and a weather that a uniform mass never has: the sky in the first days of autumn looks exactly like this.

Large leaves with the finest vein texture fall as a broad cloud, each on its own cable, each at its own turn. Gold cones at the top read as stalks from which the leaf has only just come away.

By day the texture makes the surface alive: light runs along the veins and every leaf gets a pattern of its own. In the evening the veins light from within — the canopy glows by its veining rather than by its plane.

**Where it works.** As a cascade in a stairwell or as a broad wave above a long table. The composition is good at full extent: it needs either height or length.

**Style.** Art Nouveau and natural modern: the leaf as a classic motif. The message is warm and melancholy — a piece for an interior in a natural register.

The number of leaves, the balance of smoky to clear and the shape of the cloud are all made to order.

## LC0535 — Light composition

A composition about turning in different directions. Every element is twisted its own way, and the cloud seems alive, fluid, breathing — if the coils all ran the same way the piece would be mechanical.

Long spirals with a ribbed texture read as a curl of hair or as a shaving off a jeweller's bench. Clear and smoky intermixed add depth.

By day it is a cluster of twisted forms on gold cones: the composition reads through plasticity and the hatching of the ribbing. In the evening light enters from above and runs down the coils, flaring at every turn of the spiral — the curls light up along the spiral rather than all at once.

**Where it works.** Above a dining table, in a stairwell, above a reception desk — wherever the composition is seen from the side and from below. The hanging cluster sets the lower boundary.

**Style.** Baroque in a minimal rendering and jewellery aesthetics: the curl without ornament. The message is expressive — a piece for an interior that needs character.

The number of curls, their lengths and the palette are all made to order.

## LC0534 — Light composition

A composition in two scales. The architectural rigour of the blocks — and the weightless scattering of drops around it: the city and the rain work at the same time, and neither subordinates the other.

Blocks of green ribbed plates read as the quarters of a transparent city with warm windows inside; through them and around them falls a rain of thousands of faceted beads.

By day it is green volumes in a clear suspension — a piece read through quarters and ribbing, dense and graphic. In the evening light from the blocks reflects in the beads and the composition shimmers like a city on a rainy night: the windows burn and the rain blurs them.

**Where it works.** Tall spaces — atriums, lobbies, grand staircases. A large statement piece: the rain is built downward and the blocks need volume around them.

**Style.** Urban modernism and Art Deco: the city as a formal motif. The message is large-scale — a piece for a building rather than for a room.

The number of blocks, the density of the rain and the tone of the plates are all made to order.

## LC0533 — Light composition

A composition about sunflowers looking for the sun. The flowers face in different directions — the bouquet has no front and is beautiful from any position, because every flower looks where it needs to.

Dozens of petals radiate from a mirrored chrome disc with a hemisphere core: the chrome at the centres adds a contemporary edge to the warm amber.

By day it is honey-coloured blooms with mirrored centres: the bubbled mass reads against the light, a warm and generous piece. In the evening the light burns behind the disc and pushes out between the petals, tracing each with a fiery contour — the flower glows around its edges rather than from within.

**Where it works.** A dense crown above a round table or a scattering of flowers at differing heights in an entrance hall — the bouquet is assembled for the space. Having no front means it is viewed in the round.

**Style.** Art Nouveau and mid-century: the aster with a technical core. The message is warm and alive — a piece for an interior in a honey register.

The number of flowers, their diameters and the angle of tilt are all made to order.

## LC0532 — Light composition

A composition about the sun caught in the flesh. The glow sits right at the stalk of every fruit — where a real olive holds its stone. The light turns out to be inside, like a kernel.

Fruits ranging from soft lime to deep emerald hang in clusters among gold leaves twisted by the wind. The clusters gather loosely: a dense harvest at the centre, occasional drops at the edges.

By day it is the Mediterranean in pure form: gold foliage, green fruit, a bubbled mass against the light. In the evening a warm point comes on inside every fruit and the cluster fills — the green turns succulent and the leaves pick up the reflected glow.

**Where it works.** Above a dining table, in a winter garden, in a kitchen-dining room, in spaces that want the south. The loose cluster is assembled for a specific plan.

**Style.** Mediterranean classicism and Art Nouveau: the olive branch as the oldest of motifs. The message is warm and southern — a piece for an interior that loves the sun.

The number of fruits and leaves, the shades of green and the scale of the cluster are all made to order.

## LC0531 — Light composition

A composition about rain arrested in a body. Inside every faceted lozenge, gold sparks shimmer — drops caught at the moment of falling and left there for good.

Lozenges of differing size hang at differing heights: the swarm is deep and layered and reassembles as you walk around it, like a kaleidoscope.

By day it is smoky gold — a rare, adult register: the composition looks expensive and restrained at once, read through faceting and inner sparks. In the evening light burns inside every crystal and the facets hand it out to the space in sharp warm highlights — the swarm begins to throw light across the walls.

**Where it works.** Living rooms, dining rooms, studies, lobbies, reception rooms. The layered swarm requires height and reads as you walk around it.

**Style.** Art Deco and jewelled Art Nouveau: the smoky stone as a gem. The message is restrained and expensive — a piece for an interior with adult taste.

The number of crystals, their sizes and the density of the swarm are all made to order.
