## LC0289 — Light composition

A contour drawing carried out into the air. The metal traces the form the way a setting traces a stone, and the middle stays empty — the piece consists of a line and of what that line encloses.

The elements stand close together and slightly rotated relative to one another. A moiré arises from that: move along the composition and the contours overlap and part again, and the field begins to waver while staying perfectly still.

By day it is graphic work — fine gold lines hanging against a transparent ground. In the evening the light sets every contour alight along its edge, and the drawing turns into a bonfire in which only the outlines burn and there is no fire inside.

**Where it works.** Long spaces: above a table for ten or more, in a lobby, in a gallery, in a meeting room, in a banqueting hall.

**Style.** Contemporary minimalism and graphic Art Nouveau: white walls, clean lines, no small-scale decor. The message is understatement — the composition shows the boundary of the object and leaves the contents to the imagination.

The number of elements, the spread of the field and the finish of the contours are all made to order.

## LC0288 — Light composition

A bird simplified to two strokes. A forked tail, sharp wings — a silhouette in a dive with everything removed except recognisability.

The flock travels in echelons: some higher, some lower, a few broken away and heading off to the side. Point sources light the elements from the flank and inner currents surface in the mass — swirls invisible under direct light.

By day the composition reads graphically: dark silhouettes on a pale ceiling, almost a draft drawing. In the evening the green deepens to emerald and the flock is no longer flying through air but through water — the medium changes together with the light.

**Where it works.** A horizontal format for generous halls, double-height living rooms, lobbies, restaurants, winter gardens.

**Style.** Eco-modern and natural minimalism: natural textures, muted green in the finishes, plenty of air. The message is freedom of movement — the composition is about those who fly, not those who watch.

The number of birds, the echeloning of the flock and the tone are all made to order.

## LC0287 — Light composition

A form taken all the way to a sign. A body, two wings, not one detail — recognition rests on silhouette and tilt, and that turns out to be enough.

The gold is applied in points, only to the wingtips and the beaks: an accent within the drawing, not a finish. The light is hidden under each bird and lifts the wings from below, which leaves the edges half-transparent.

By day the flock reads as a sculptural group — white on white, pure plasticity. In the evening the birds glow from within and a wave of light travels the whole arc from end to end, the way movement travels through a real flock.

**Where it works.** Large horizontal spaces: lobbies, conference halls, panoramic living rooms, banqueting halls, wedding venues.

**Style.** Contemporary classic and minimalism: pale walls, simple forms, a minimum of colour. The message is clarity and peace — the composition reads as a symbol without turning into an allegory.

The number of birds, the span of the arc and the proportion of gilding are all made to order.

## LC0286 — Light composition

The same flock, but gathered before take-off. If the sparse version is about flight, this one is about mass: a cloud in which the individual bird no longer matters.

The light is distributed through the whole volume, and warm hollows open up in the depth where the gaze travels in layers. Gilded figures among the white ones work by reflection and set a rhythm, keeping the mass from going uniform.

By day it is a sculptural group with gold inclusions — dense, legible, almost monolithic. In the evening it becomes a glowing cloud in which the figures can be guessed at but not made out: the flock turns into light.

**Where it works.** Formal spaces with high ceilings: hotel lobbies, banqueting halls, double-height living rooms, atriums.

**Style.** Contemporary classic and grand luxury: mouldings, pale stone, gilded detail. The message is abundance — the composition speaks of generosity outright, without resorting to hints.

The number of birds, the density of the flock and the ratio of white to gold are all made to order.

## LC0285 — Light composition

Two different lights in one field. Some elements glow evenly and softly, others brightly and in points, with a highlight deep inside; the composition is built on that difference rather than on form.

Varying the drop height turns a flat field into a relief: waves, crests, hollows. The eye travels it as it would travel terrain, finding now a dense stretch, now a gap.

By day it is a garden of white flowers with amber inclusions — calm, almost monochrome. In the evening the matte pieces fade to a warm glow and the clear ones flare: the garden becomes a field of fireflies that have settled on the blooms.

**Where it works.** Long tables, lobbies, generous dining rooms, meeting rooms, restaurant halls.

**Style.** Contemporary classic with a botanical note: warm wood, brass, pale textiles. The message is hospitality — the composition reads as a table laid before anyone has laid it.

The number of elements, the ratio of matte to clear and the area of the field are all made to order.

## LC0284 — Light composition

A whirlwind coiled around a spiral staircase. The composition does not hang in the shaft, it passes through it — from the top floor to the bottom, accompanying whoever is coming down.

The light enters the end of each rod and runs downward: the result is rain falling along a spiral — a movement that does not occur in nature. Because of the helical geometry the piece reads differently from every floor: a funnel from below, a cascade from the side, an open rose from above.

By day it is an architectural structure — precise, engineered, with a legible frame. In the evening the frame disappears and what remains is a whirlwind of light with neither axis nor fixings in sight.

**Where it works.** Tall verticals only: stairwells, atriums, panoramic halls of several levels, double-height lobbies.

**Style.** Contemporary luxury and high-tech: open staircases, glazing, metal, large scale. The message is the vertical — the composition turns moving between floors into the main event of the house.

The height of the spiral, the number of turns and the density of the fringe are all made to order.

## LC0283 — Light composition

Foam that was not allowed to burst. Every element differs in size and is slightly irregular in form — like a real bubble, which has no two identical siblings.

The coating produces interference: the colour is not fixed, it shifts with the viewing angle, flowing from blue to green and pink. The composition has no permanent palette — it depends on where you are standing.

By day it is a cluster of soap foam suspended in the air, light and almost invisible. In the evening the shells glow across their whole surface, and the foam becomes a cloud of small moons with iridescent shifts.

**Where it works.** Interiors with a playful character: children's rooms, spas, pools, lounges, bathrooms, window displays.

**Style.** Soft futurism and optical design: smooth surfaces, a pale palette, no sharp corners. The message is lightness and play — the composition takes the seriousness out of a space without making it childish.

The number of bubbles, their calibres and the palette of the coating are all made to order.

## LC0282 — Light composition

A fold with gold hidden inside it. From the outside the form is restrained and white; inside there is a gilded cavity with the source: the piece shows its interior only to those who approach from the right side.

The composition runs as a diagonal stream, from a dense core to sparse edges. The elements overlap and depth accumulates in layers — as in a wave, where you see both the crest and what is beneath it.

By day it is a sculpture of rich plasticity, read through relief and shadow. In the evening it becomes a scattering of warm glowing pockets with darkness between them: the light here does not flood, it peers out.

**Where it works.** Long spaces: above a table, along a bar counter, in a gallery hall, in a restaurant, in a reception room.

**Style.** Contemporary classic and a softened baroque: pale walls, gilded detail, rounded furniture. The message is restrained luxury — the composition does not put its gold in the window, it hides it.

The number of elements, the depth of the stream and the gilding finish are all made to order.

## LC0281 — Light composition

A ring that outlines a space rather than filling it. The inner part is empty — and that emptiness works on equal terms with the material.

The petals are laid overlapping in several layers, like roof tiles, each turned with its gold reverse facing inward. The light collects in the cavity and comes out reflected, without a single direct beam: the source is invisible from anywhere.

By day it is a wreath — dense, white, with gold glimmers deep inside. In the evening it becomes a glowing hoop suspended in the air, in which not the surface burns but the interior.

**Where it works.** A ring format for round tables, rotundas, the central zone of a living room, private dining rooms, wedding venues.

**Style.** Contemporary classic and neo-baroque: symmetry, mouldings, gilding, formal proportions. The message is ceremony — the composition draws a circle inside which the main thing happens.

The diameter of the ring, the number of petals and the depth of the layering are all made to order.

## LC0280 — Light composition (Award Winning)

A sheet of paper caught in flight. The pleating reads as folds in cloth, and the form as something a draught picked up and would not let fall.

The light escapes only through the slots between the ribs, so the ribbon glows in fine longitudinal lines while staying dense and sculptural. This is not a glowing object but an object with light inside it — the difference is fundamental.

By day it is a giant sheet of pleated paper frozen halfway through its fall. In the evening it becomes a glowing wave with close hatching, as though a comb had been drawn along it.

The model holds an international award.

**Where it works.** A horizontal format for generous halls: dining rooms, lobbies, meeting rooms, galleries, showrooms.

**Style.** Minimalism and Japanese aesthetics: paper, wood, emptiness, no ornament. The message is simplicity of gesture — one form, one movement, nothing beyond.

The length of the ribbon, the character of the bends and the density of the ribbing are all made to order.

## LC0279 — Light composition (Award Winning)

The same ribbon in mirror — and the opposite character because of it. Where the matte version is soft and diffused, this one is sharp, flaring, cold.

The ribbing works as an optical grating: reflections break into hundreds of vertical strokes and the surface never shows a whole picture. The room is reflected in it taken apart into stripes.

By day it is a sculpture of mercury — heavy, fluid, gathering the interior into itself. In the evening the backlight surfaces through the slots and glowing stripes run along its whole length, as though the metal had cracked from inside.

The composition holds an international award.

**Where it works.** Interiors with strong architecture: concrete, glazing, stone, double height. Lobbies, atriums, galleries, offices, showrooms.

**Style.** High-tech and industrial luxury: large planes, exposed structure, monochrome. The message is precision of substance — a piece about metal being capable of flow.

The length, the depth of the bends and the finish are all made to order.

## LC0278 — Light composition

Icicles under an eave. The lower edge is ragged and the elements differ in length — the composition is deliberately not levelled, because levelled icicles do not exist.

Every spiral is its own: tight in one place, barely started in another. The light enters from above through the tip and fills the form entirely — the matte pieces glow evenly, the clear ones throw sharp highlights off the facets of the twist.

By day it is a collection of ice figures laid out through the air. In the evening it becomes a frozen waterfall lit from within: water that has stopped and goes on glowing.

**Where it works.** Stairwells, bay windows, double-height spaces, winter gardens, spa areas.

**Style.** Nordic minimalism and alpine luxury: pale wood, stone, large windows, a cold palette. The message is winter as a state — the composition brings the clarity of a frosty day rather than festive decor.

The number of elements, their lengths and the balance of clear to matte are all made to order.

## LC0277 — Light composition

Ice with whatever was in it frozen in place. Bubbles and swirls remain in the mass, so under light the piece looks like a genuine block rather than a coloured shape.

The white elements damp down the intensity of the colour and keep the composition from becoming decorative — they work as pauses, without which the aquamarine would sound insistent.

By day the object recalls a cluster of stalactites — dense, cold, mineral. In the evening it becomes a cave of ice flows lit from somewhere deep inside, where there should be no light at all.

**Where it works.** A vertical format: staircases, narrow double-height spaces, spa areas, pools, bathrooms.

**Style.** Nordic Art Nouveau and spa aesthetics: stone, water, subdued light, a cold palette. The message is coolness and depth — the composition lowers the temperature of a space and slows it down.

The number of spindles, the depth of tone and the height of the group are all made to order.

## LC0276 — Light composition

Resin with something caught in it for good. Gold foil lives in the mass, and when the light comes on it passes through, scattering warm sparks inside the body of the piece.

The white elements provide pauses and keep the composition from overheating on colour. Without them the honey tone would turn cloying — with them it reads as amber rather than syrup.

By day it is a cluster of honeycomb or drops of resin — dense, heavy to the eye, warm in colour. In the evening it becomes a swarm of glowing cocoons with something happening inside each one.

**Where it works.** Stairwells, dining rooms, living rooms in a warm palette, restaurants, wine rooms.

**Style.** Eco-luxury and warm Art Nouveau: wood, leather, natural fabrics, subdued light. The message is time preserved — the composition is about warmth being something you can put up in a jar.

The number of elements, the balance of honey to white and the dimensions are all made to order.

## LC0275 — Light composition

An ingot cast into a transparent shell. The gilding is applied from the inside, so the element stays clear on the outside and burns with metal within — the form shows its contents without opening.

The clear elements between the gilded ones work as air: without them the composition would become a solid mass, with them it breathes. The proportion matters more here than the count.

By day it is a collection of precious drops laid out through the air. In the evening it becomes a stream of molten gold arrested at the moment of pouring: a movement that never happened and is nonetheless visible.

**Where it works.** A horizontal format for long tables, generous halls, lobbies, banqueting rooms, restaurants.

**Style.** Art Deco and contemporary luxury: gilding, deep tones, symmetry, large scale. The message is value — the composition speaks of wealth through substance rather than ornament.

The number of elements, the share of gilded ones and the spread of the group are all made to order.
