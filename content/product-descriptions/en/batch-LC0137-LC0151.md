# Product card texts — batch 38: LC0137–LC0151 (15 items)

Approved format: artistic image, day-to-evening shift, "Where it works", "Style", closing line on customisation. Element materials are never named. LC0151, LC0150 and LC0148 are decorative screens, LC0149 is a bio-fireplace.

## LC0151 — Decorative screen

A composition about dried time. Real fern fronds are sealed inside the plates — a fine silhouette with every pinna drawn, held in the body for good. The device is old and botanical: plants were pressed between pages exactly this way, to keep the form once colour and scent had gone.

Triangular plates are set point down and laid in offset rows — the plane reads as scales or as roof tiling. The impressions are scattered across the field irregularly: a herbarium gathered without system, not an ornament.

By day the screen works as a translucent panel — smoky, almost clear in places, drifting into graphite in others, with internal marbling. The view behind it is subdued but not closed. In the evening light enters at the rim, the plates fill from within, and the ferns emerge as dark silhouettes against a glowing ground. A botanical archive lit from behind.

**Where it works.** Zoning — separating a living room from a dining room, a screen at an entrance, a panel in a spa, a backdrop behind reception, an inset in a restaurant wall. Translucency gives privacy without a solid wall.

**Style.** Botanical modernism and Art Nouveau: the plant as the principal subject, transparency as the means of holding it. The message is contemplative — a piece for an interior that loves detail.

The screen dimensions, the number of plates, the selection of botanical inserts and the depth of the smoky tone are all made to order.

## LC0150 — Decorative screen

A composition about a curtain. The lozenges are strung on fine vertical cords — the construction is closer to a bead curtain than to a wall: it hangs rather than stands. Offsets in both directions break the grid: there is no strict pitch, only rhythm.

Three kinds of lozenge — clear with internal marbling, smoky grey with a frosted texture, and dense amber. Each is faceted irregularly, so the facets catch the light differently and no two points in the field are alike.

By day the outline of whatever is behind the screen is visible through the gaps, but not the detail: the panel divides without cutting off. In the evening light runs down the gold cords and enters the facets at the fixing points — the clear ones give white flares, the smoky ones glow subdued, the amber ones fill with copper and become the brightest points. A curtain of lights hung on threads.

**Where it works.** Zoning — separating a living room from a kitchen, a screen in a lobby, a partition beside a staircase, a backdrop behind a bar counter, an inset in a window opening. A suspended structure goes where permanent fixing is impossible.

**Style.** Art Deco and jewellery modernism: faceting, thread, light at a point. The message is dressy — a piece for an interior where the screen should be an event.

The screen dimensions, the number of lozenges, the ratio of the three kinds and the layout pitch are all made to order.

## LC0149 — Bio-fireplace

A composition about rock above fire. Below, a living flame; above it a tall cylinder built from hundreds of overlapping flat plates, like masonry or tiling. A gap is left between bowl and cylinder: the fire is visible from every side and works not as decoration for the column but as its source.

No plate repeats its neighbour — runs, cavities, patches of differing density. The column looks laid by hand out of found material rather than assembled from parts: rock, not product.

By day it is a sculptural volume — warm amber, read through texture and reflection, calm and dense. In the evening the fire below lights the masonry from beneath, the light passes through the amber and emerges warm and diffuse. The column begins to glow like heated rock — stone with the heat still in it.

The same object exists in a rose-copper palette, where the tone shifts toward fired clay and terracotta.

**Where it works.** Living rooms with high ceilings, lobbies, spas, restaurants, winter gardens, terraces. A live flame needs ventilation and a clear perimeter — the object stands at the centre, not against a wall.

**Style.** Organic minimalism and the archaic: fire as the oldest centre of a dwelling. The message is primal and luxurious at once — a piece for a space people gather around.

The column dimensions, the plate palette, the burner type and the drop height are all made to order.

## LC0148 — Decorative screen

A composition about a break. The surface is uneven, with cavities and matte patches — not a product but a splinter of ice or a slab of rock salt. The author deliberately avoids smoothness: what is valued here is looking found rather than made.

Plates of differing size are laid in vertical bands offset in height — the masonry is uneven, with steps and gaps. Dark slots run between them, passing air and some light. The slim frame is visible but does not argue with the mass: it works as hard rhythm against ragged masonry.

By day the screen looks like a wall of ice — cold, white-grey, almost opaque, living on texture alone. In the evening light is fed in at the rim and spreads unevenly across each plate, emphasising the relief: thicker means duller, thinner means brighter. A glacier lit from within.

**Where it works.** Zoning — separating a living room from a dining room, a panel in spas and pool areas, a partition beside a staircase, a backdrop behind reception. A nearly opaque plane gives full privacy while still glowing.

**Style.** Nordic minimalism and organic architecture: cold, stone and irregularity as an aesthetic. The message is severe and clean — a piece for an interior without embellishment.

The screen dimensions, the number of plates, the depth of the relief and the lighting scheme are all made to order.

## LC0147 — Light composition

A composition about a nest. Hooks with turned tips interweave into a tangle — a mass with neither top nor bottom, only interlock. Between them, on separate threads, hang small faceted spheres that descend lower: drops running off the hooks.

Some elements are opaque and white, with a gold hollow inside; others are entirely clear, read only through highlights along the rim. One form in two readings: visible and nearly absent.

By day it is a tangle of clear horns — a pale sculptural mass in which the transparent elements all but dissolve. In the evening a chain builds itself: the light emerges reflected from the gold hollows, enters the clear hooks, which carry it onward along their curve, and the faceted spheres gather it and give it back as pinpoint flares. A nest with sparks falling out of it.

**Where it works.** Living rooms with high ceilings, stairwells, lobbies, zones above a round table. The trailing pendants set a lower limit — allow clearance beneath.

**Style.** Art Nouveau and jewellery modernism: a complex interweaving that works as a setting. The message is collector-minded — a piece for someone who looks closely.

The overall size, the number of elements, the ratio of white, gold and clear, and the pendant lengths are all made to order.

## LC0146 — Light composition

A composition about sparseness. What matters here is not what is present but what is absent: the elements lie almost flat, in layers at varied levels, barely overlapping. Between them, large voids. The flock is stretched through the whole body of water rather than packed into a shoal.

The form is a narrow feather with a wavy edge and a slight twist, repeated in three finishes: clear turquoise with internal marbling, opaque milky white, and gold with patina.

By day it is a flock suspended below the ceiling: sparse, light, read through individual silhouettes. In the evening one and the same beam behaves in three ways — the turquoise feathers transmit it and glow a cool green-blue, the white ones diffuse it softly, the gold ones reflect it sharply. Some elements burn, others merely gleam, others stay dark: a current lit from the surface.

**Where it works.** Generous volumes — atria, lobbies, pools and spas, double-height living rooms. Sparseness needs area: compressed, the composition turns into an ordinary chandelier.

**Style.** Marine modernism and contemporary sculpture: the movement of water as the principal motif. The message is calm and immersive — a piece for a space where people unwind.

The overall size, the number of elements, the ratio of the three finishes and the drop heights are all made to order.

## LC0145 — Light composition

A composition about monochrome. There is no gold here and no contrasting inset — only white, and all the work falls to form and light. This is deliberate self-restraint: by removing the palette, the author makes you look at the plasticity.

A broad bowl with a rolled edge, one side raised and the other lowered, the rim wavy — an opened flower or a shell curled in water. The elements face in different directions: some open their mouths downward, others show a smooth back.

By day it is a field of white flowers below the ceiling — a quiet, almost soundless mass read through light and shade. In the evening the light emerges through the open mouths in soft warm patches and lights the thin walls from within, so the white fills with cream. From outside the elements stay cool and opaque: what you see is not form but a net of glowing mouths. A pool lit from beneath the water.

**Where it works.** Generous rooms — lobbies, double-height living rooms, spas and pools, banquet and wedding halls. Monochrome works in any interior palette, so the composition is universal.

**Style.** Minimalism and organic modernism: form without decoration. The message is clean and ceremonial — a piece for a bright interior that does not want to argue with itself.

The overall size, the number of elements, the tone of the glow and the drop heights are all made to order.

## LC0144 — Light composition

A composition about a whirlwind. The cascade is wound into a spiral: it begins high as a narrow stream, widens at the middle and narrows again below, curving around an invisible axis. The form is taken not from an object but from a movement — this is how leaves fall in a rising current.

The elements are small, no larger than a palm, and read almost not at all on their own: only the mass works. The cables vanish into darkness and are invisible, so the petals appear to hang unsupported.

By day it is the trace left by a whirlwind — a drawing in the air, living on reflected light, all but imperceptible. In the evening the principal device takes over: not all the elements glow but one in ten. The gold backings give warm flares, the clear fragments cold sparks, and the rest of the mass stays dark. It is precisely that selectiveness that creates the sense of movement: a current of sparks descending between floors.

**Where it works.** A vertical format for tall openings — stairwells, atria, shafts between floors, double-height living rooms. The spiral is designed for the specific height and viewpoint.

**Style.** Contemporary sculptural installation: the composition as an event within a volume. The message is dynamic — a piece that makes people look up.

The height of the cascade, the pitch of the spiral, the number of elements and the share of gold are all made to order.

## LC0143 — Light composition

A composition about directed sound. The bell is a form from the world of sound, not light: a horn, a gramophone trumpet, a megaphone. The author uses its chief property — a bell does not diffuse, it aims.

Outside, the bodies are opaque, graphite and matte — in the dark they do not read at all. Inside, polished gold or mirrored silver. The elements are turned at random: some mouth down, some sideways, some almost up.

By day it is a cluster of metal flowers — a dark, dense, graphic mass below the ceiling. In the evening the bodies disappear and only the open mouths remain: the gold ones give a warm honeyed glow, the silver a cool white one, and each throws a narrow directed beam. A gathering of luminous funnels suspended in emptiness — a swarm of spotlights facing every way.

**Where it works.** Living rooms with high ceilings, lobbies, restaurants, stairwells, zones above a round table. Directed light lets you pick out specific points: a table, a bar counter, a picture.

**Style.** Industrial luxury and steampunk minimalism: a technical form presented as something precious. The message is bold — a piece for an interior with character.

The overall size, the number of bells, the ratio of gold to silver interiors and the colour of the outer surface are all made to order.

## LC0142 — Light composition

A composition about the stroke. Close longitudinal grooves run the whole length of the leaf, and that changes everything: the surface shifts in density as it turns, and light travels across it in lines rather than patches. The form acquires graphics of its own — the ribbing works like hatching in an etching.

The pyramid is dense toward the centre and thinned at the edges, with single leaves descending below. Bare cables with glowing points are set between them — they establish the volume and stop the mass congealing.

By day it is autumn foliage lifted to the ceiling: amber leaves mixed with clear ones, a warm mass living on the reflections along the grooves. In the evening the light enters at the stalk and spreads along the ribbing — every leaf glows in strokes, the amber ones fill with copper, the clear ones throw white sparks along their ribs. The sources are lost among the layers and the whole crown glows.

**Where it works.** Living rooms with high ceilings, stairwells, lobbies, double-height spaces. The dense pyramid needs height — otherwise the silhouette is lost.

**Style.** Art Nouveau and organic modernism: the leaf as a classic motif, the ribbing as the author's device. The message is warm and autumnal — a piece for an interior in a natural palette.

The overall size, the number of leaves, the share of amber and the drop heights are all made to order.

## LC0141 — Light composition

A composition about a colony. The sea anemone is a creature that does not move and does not look alive until it starts to glow. The author takes that form: a rounded body with a grainy core and dozens of tentacles radiating in every direction. Each is drawn out its own way and curved separately — there are no identical elements.

The composition is vertical, wound into a spiral. Some anemones break away from the mass and hang alone, with gaps between them: the colony looks grown rather than assembled.

By day the object is all but invisible — entirely clear, it shows itself only in highlights, a gathering of transparent creatures easy to overlook. In the evening the light enters the base of each tentacle and travels to its tip, flaring there as a point. From below the composition reads as a swarm of glowing stars with radiating rays — a colony lit in the depths.

**Where it works.** A vertical format — stairwells, atria, double-height living rooms, spas. Transparency needs a dark backdrop: against a pale wall the colony is lost by day and by night alike.

**Style.** Marine modernism and scientific aesthetics: a biological form reproduced precisely. The message is investigative — a piece for someone who loves nature unstylised.

The overall height, the number of elements, the length of the tentacles and the density of the colony are all made to order.

## LC0140 — Light composition

The same colony, but with something to hold on to. The anemones are set not on cables but on a tubular branch curved into a broad loop in the horizontal plane: the branch divides into short stalks, and on each sits a figure at its own angle.

The mass reads as grown on its support — the colony has covered the branch the way polyps cover a sunken tree. Density is uneven: in places the elements bunch into clusters, elsewhere the branch stays bare. That matters more than symmetry: growth is never even.

By day it is a coral colony — clear bodies on a gold line, graphic and light. In the evening two levels of glow appear at once: cold points at the tips of the tentacles, and the warm vein of the branch showing through the clear mass beneath them. A branch overgrown with luminous creatures.

**Where it works.** A horizontal format — long tables, bar counters, meeting rooms, spas, reception areas. The curved loop reads from above and from the side, so the piece is hung lower than usual.

**Style.** Marine Art Nouveau: a natural form on a natural support, without geometry. The message is organic — a piece for an interior that avoids straight lines.

The length of the branch, the number of elements, the density of the colony and the metal finish are all made to order.

## LC0139 — Light composition

A composition about a crown. The elements stand vertically around the perimeter, tips upward, and lean outward — the silhouette comes out exactly like a diadem or the cup of an opened flower. The form is old and unambiguous: a crown, a bud and a defensive palisade are all built this way.

Inside the bowl the elements are denser and shorter, along the rim taller and sparser — the mass grows from centre to perimeter, and the object reads as opening.

By day it is a clear sculpture: the thick walls work as lenses, and the composition catches daylight from the window and scatters it across the table. In the evening the light enters each hook from below, travels the curve and gathers in the drop-shaped finial, which flares into a gold core. Every layer is visible right through — the crown seems filled with light entirely.

**Where it works.** Living rooms with high ceilings, dining rooms, lobbies, banquet halls, zones above a round table. A round bowl asks for a round table beneath it — form answers form.

**Style.** Grand classicism in contemporary execution: a diadem without a single ornament. The message is ceremonial — a piece for a table where people are received.

The bowl diameter, the number of elements, the height of the hooks and the depth of the setting are all made to order.

## LC0138 — Light composition

A composition about interlock. The rings pass through one another and form couplings — the composition is held not by its suspensions but by its own connections. The form is deliberately irregular: every ring has its own geometry, closer to an oval here, to a triangle with rounded corners there. Not one true circle among them.

Straight gold crossbars are set between the rings — they work as graphic pauses and stop the mass turning into a jumble.

By day it is a sculpture of interlocked rings — a gold band, a clear inset with a grainy texture, the piece read through outline. In the evening the light travels the entire perimeter and flares on the grainy texture in fine sparks, while the band reflects it and traces every ring with a hot line. What remains is an interlacing of luminous loops suspended in emptiness.

**Where it works.** Living rooms with high ceilings, lobbies, stairwells, reception rooms. The couplings read in the round — the piece is designed to be walked around rather than viewed frontally.

**Style.** Jewellery modernism and contemporary sculpture: the link as the unit of composition. The message is spare and expensive — a piece for an interior that rests on one strong detail.

The overall size, the number of rings, the calibres and the gilding finish are all made to order.

## LC0137 — Light composition

A composition about a ripple. The line of elements rises and falls, forming two crests with a trough between them: on the crests the elements bunch closer, in the trough they thin out. The wave is stated twice — by the length of the rods vertically and by the density of the spacing horizontally.

Each element is a narrow twisted ribbon with a ragged edge, crushed freely, with no repeats. It hangs on its own rod with a point source at the end: the light arrives from above, from outside, not from within the form.

By day it is a ripple on water — amber ribbons beneath the silver hatching of the rods, a pattern that changes with every step. In the evening the light travels from the point straight into the body, the ribbon picks it up and carries it through all its folds, and a burning core appears inside every element. Hundreds of cores give an unbroken warm field, while the rods above them stay cold. A golden wave suspended above the table.

**Where it works.** An elongated horizontal format — long dining tables, bar counters, meeting rooms, lobbies. The wave pattern is calculated for the length of the table.

**Style.** Contemporary classicism and organic modernism: water as motif, light as material. The message is warm and hospitable — a piece for a table where people sit long.

The length, the wave pattern, the number of elements and the depth of the amber tone are all made to order.
