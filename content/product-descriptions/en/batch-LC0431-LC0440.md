## LC0440 — Light composition

A composition about a mercury icicle. A mirrored funnel draws out into the finest of tails — a form in which everything runs downward and ends in a point. The cluster is assembled points down, densely, without a single gap.

The composition is monochrome and hypnotic for it: only shine, light and the vertical. Not one colour, not one texture — a piece reduced to three qualities.

By day it is a mirrored mass that shows not itself but the room: the neighbouring stems reflect in one another and the drawing changes with every step. In the evening the light burns in the funnels and runs down the mirrored surface, breaking up in the reflections — the cluster begins to seem liquid.

**Where it works.** Contemporary interiors with high ceilings — lobbies, double-height living rooms, showrooms, boutiques. A mirror works where there is something to reflect: the composition needs surroundings.

**Style.** Contemporary minimalism and high-tech: pure shine without decor. The message is strict and technical — a piece for an interior of straight lines.

The number of icicles, their lengths and the density of the cluster are all made to order.

## LC0439 — Light composition

A composition about repetition. Hundreds of identical gestures differing only in height and tilt — the botany here is strict, almost architectural, and that is the strength of the piece: a forest reads through quantity rather than variety.

Every flower is drawn out into a tube: a green stem flowing into a white bowl, the light sitting in the cut itself. The direction is firm — downward.

By day it is a dense forest on invisible suspensions: a green gradient passing into white, a piece read through the rhythm of the verticals. In the evening every calla becomes a living spotlight — the light goes down in narrow cones, and a field of light pools appears beneath the composition.

**Where it works.** Lobbies, dining rooms, meeting rooms, restaurant halls. The downward light gives working illumination of a zone rather than only atmosphere.

**Style.** Art Nouveau and modernism: a botanical motif with architectural rigour. The message is clear and functional — a piece for the image and for the light alike.

The number of callas, the green gradient and the area of the forest are all made to order.

## LC0438 — Light composition

A composition about a winter forest. The two-colour formation reads directly: the white cones are trunks in snow, the dark green ones are conifer. The form is one; the colour turns it into a landscape.

The texture is alive, slightly crumpled, catching shadows like hand-cast paper. The cones stand points up in dense formation, with light burning in the cut of each.

By day it is graphic work in two colours: white and deep green read through texture and the play of light on a crumpled surface. In the evening light goes down from every cut — the composition delivers genuine working illumination, a rarity among art objects — and the forest turns into a field of lights.

**Where it works.** Dining rooms, kitchen islands, meeting rooms, restaurants, reception desks. The downward light lets the composition be used as the main lighting of a working zone.

**Style.** Scandinavian minimalism and natural Art Nouveau: the forest as a motif without stylisation. The message is warm and domestic — a piece for an interior that loves nature.

The number of cones, the balance of white to green and the format are all made to order.

## LC0437 — Wall light — sculptural art object

A composition about a surface grown by nature. The deep cellular texture reads as crystallisation or as cell structure — a drawing with no author. At the centre is a glossy sphere, a core, the only smooth detail in the whole piece.

The contrast is set simply: chaos around, order in the middle.

By day the object works as a wall sculpture: the relief catches daylight, every cell gets a shadow of its own, and the bowl looks volumetric with not one lamp on. In the evening the core gives a soft glow that spreads across the relief and does not dazzle — the moon and a pearl in the white version, a Martian crater with a warm interior in the terracotta one.

**Where it works.** Hallways, corridors, bedrooms at the bedhead, bathrooms, private living rooms. The light is soft and reflected — the wall light does not dazzle and suits rest areas. It works switched off as well.

**Style.** Natural minimalism and bio-design: texture as the principal content. The message is tactile — a piece you want to stand close to.

The diameter, the colour of the bowl and the colour temperature of the light are all made to order.

## LC0436 — Light composition

A composition about a natural phenomenon. Every drop is a complete luminaire, yet on its own it means nothing: only mass turns a set of objects into rain.

The form is double: a mirrored tip flowing into a matte milky body. The flock is dense, narrowing toward the bottom.

By day it is silver and milk: the mirrored tips catch light, the matte bodies stay calm, and the composition reads through the difference between two surfaces. In the evening the milky bodies fill with an even glow while the tips go on reflecting — rain in which only the drops glow and the whole mass does not glitter.

**Where it works.** Living rooms, dining rooms, lobbies, stairwells. The monochrome does not argue with an interior — the composition crowns a space without dictating a palette to it.

**Style.** Contemporary classic and minimalism: silver with milk, nothing surplus. The message is calm and formal — a piece for an interior that chooses restraint.

The number of drops, the depth of the flock and the dimensions are all made to order.

## LC0435 — Light composition

A composition about a repeated gesture. One and the same form — a disc with soft concentric waves — is taken in different calibres, from a saucer to a tray. Rings on water arrested in an object, and then multiplied across scales.

The tiers overlap like the roofs of a pagoda: the East read in the language of modernism.

By day it is milky discs on mirrored funnels — a piece read through the silhouette of the tiers and the soft waves on the surface. In the evening light burns in the funnel and spreads across the waves of the disc, tracing every ring: the rings on water begin to glow from the centre out.

**Where it works.** Living rooms, dining rooms, lobbies, meeting rooms. The pyramidal assembly requires height — pressed to the ceiling, it loses its tiering.

**Style.** Oriental modernism and mid-century: a pagoda kept from the literal. The message is calm and Eastern — a piece for an interior that values rhythm.

The number of discs, the tiering and the dimensions are all made to order.

## LC0434 — Light composition

A composition about an element caught and made to shine. Ribbons of thousands of vertical rods twist into a giant spiral narrowing toward the bottom — the form of a funnel, which in nature means destruction and here means light.

The light is woven into the arcs rather than hung from them: the structure itself burns, every turn from within.

By day it is a metal funnel with dense hatching of rods — a piece read through lines and twist, strict and monumental. In the evening every turn lights up and the funnel begins to rotate in light: the spiral reads as movement although nothing moves.

**Where it works.** Atriums and grand staircases several floors high. One of the brand's largest verticals: the composition is designed for a specific shaft and reads differently from every level.

**Style.** Contemporary sculptural installation and Art Deco: the element as a formal motif. The message is monumental — a piece for a building rather than for a room.

The height of the funnel, the number of turns and the direction of the twist are all made to order.

## LC0433 — Light composition

A composition about a spinning top. A top exists only while it spins — here there is a swarm of them, stopped mid-spin, tilted in different directions the way real ones stop.

The tilts decide everything: the composition is full of movement with not one moving part. Highlights slide along the lenses with every step the viewer takes.

By day it is a swarm of clear lenses on gold axles — a piece read through faceting and reflections, alive as you walk around it. In the evening light enters through the axle and runs out across the concentric faceting in rings: every disc begins to glow from the centre outward, and the rotation becomes visible.

**Where it works.** Living rooms, dining rooms, lobbies, meeting rooms. The movement reads as the viewer moves: the composition is placed where people walk past.

**Style.** Optical art and contemporary classic: rotation as subject. The message is intellectual and playful — a piece for an interior that loves paradoxes.

The number of tops, their calibres and their tilts are all made to order.

## LC0432 — Light composition

A composition about an inverted organ. Ribbed columns with capitals descend in a V-shaped cascade — pipes taken out of the instrument and hung in the air. The form has kept its solemnity and lost its support.

Light fills the ribbing along the whole length: every column sings with its own light, and together they give a chord rather than a unison.

By day it is strict vertical graphic work: cylinders with gold capitals, read through rhythm and ribbing. In the evening the ribbing fills with light over its full height and the cascade turns cathedral-like — the piece gives a space its vertical and its rhythm the way music gives it tempo.

**Where it works.** Lobbies, atriums, banqueting and conference halls, double-height living rooms. The V-shaped cascade requires height and a frontal view.

**Style.** Art Deco and cathedral classicism: the organ as a symbol of solemnity. The message is monumental — a piece for a formal hall.

The number of columns, their lengths and the depth of the cascade are all made to order.

## LC0431 — Framed light composition

A composition about a banking turn. The dynamics are built into the structure: the lines of the frame draw the movement and the elements pick it up. The branches curve through space, and on them sit flocks of wings caught mid-turn.

The points of light stand on the branches rather than in the figures: the flock is lit from within and the source is never read directly.

By day it is a gold line with clear and white wings — a piece read through the graphic of the frame and the silhouettes of the birds. In the evening the light lifts the flock from below and from inside, the branches go into shadow, and only the wings remain, suspended in their turn. There are executions in contrasting black-and-white enamel — more graphic and more severe.

**Where it works.** Living rooms, lobbies, stairwells, restaurant halls. The frame is designed for a specific geometry: the composition can wrap a corner, run along a wall, descend as a cascade.

**Style.** Art Nouveau and contemporary sculpture: flight as a classic motif. The message is alive and poetic — a piece for an interior that loves movement.

The number of birds, the configuration of the branches and the palette are all made to order.
