## LC0061 — Floor light-sculpture

A composition about growth. Segments with rounded sides are stacked one on another, each throwing off short offshoots — the form reads at once as coral, as a spine and as a cactus. The ambiguity is deliberate: the author takes the general principle of growth rather than any single species.

The glaze runs unevenly, with drips, and the object looks grown rather than cast. Openings of varying calibre are cut through the whole body — from fist-sized down to small points — and their inner surfaces are gold.

By day it is a dense sculpture: warm white with a cream cast or deep blue with a grey undertone, opaque and matte. In the evening the light emerges from the openings in narrow warm beams while the column itself stays dark — the same device as a coal: cold shell, burning core. The base is reduced to a minimum, so the mass seems to stand of its own accord.

A second finish exists, faced in preserved moss, where a living green texture replaces the glaze.

**Where it works.** Halls, living rooms, spas, showrooms, reception rooms, restaurant interiors. Fragmentary light gives atmosphere rather than illumination: a second source is needed nearby.

**Style.** Biodesign and organic modernism: growth as the form-giving principle. The message is natural and contemporary — a piece for an interior with character.

The height, the number of segments, the glaze colour and the number and calibre of the openings are all made to order.

## LC0060 — Light composition

A composition about a colony. Every element differs from its neighbour — three offshoots here, five there, a wider mouth on one, a narrower one on another. This is not variety for its own sake but accuracy: a colony consists of individuals, not of parts.

The pyramid is dense, the elements almost touching, thicker at the centre and thinned at the edges. From below the mass reads as continuous, like a real growth on a rock.

By day it is a limestone colony — white, matte, read through relief and the shadows between the offshoots. In the evening the light emerges through the open mouths as a warm milky patch and shows through the thin walls from inside, so the white fills with cream while the outer surfaces stay cool and opaque. The mouths face every direction — a reef lit from within.

**Where it works.** Generous rooms — lobbies, pools and spas, double-height living rooms, winter gardens. The dense pyramid needs height; the soft glare-free light suits positions above water.

**Style.** Marine modernism and organic minimalism: a biological form without stylisation. The message is calm — a piece for an interior where people rest.

The overall size, the number of elements, the tone of the glaze and the drop heights are all made to order.

## LC0059 — Light composition

A composition about a thrown handful. The rods hang at wildly varied angles — horizontal, oblique, crosswise — and intersect to form a lattice with no centre and no symmetry. A handful of matches thrown into the air and frozen: there is no order, because there was none at the moment of the throw.

Some rods bunch into tight clusters, others hang alone far from the mass. The cables vanish into darkness and are barely visible.

By day it is a drawing carried into space: polished lines read graphically, with occasional flares on the faceted heads. In the evening the light enters each rod at its upper end and travels to the sphere — which gathers it, breaks it on the facets and flares into a point, while the rod itself stays a cold stroke. Only the glowing heads are visible: a swarm of sparks suspended in the air.

**Where it works.** Living rooms with high ceilings, stairwells, lobbies, meeting rooms. The lattice builds in the round and has no principal face — it is designed for the specific room.

**Style.** Contemporary minimalism and the graphic school: line and point, nothing more. The message is intellectual — a piece for an interior that rests on an idea.

The overall size, the number of rods, the metal finish and the calibre of the heads are all made to order.

## LC0058 — Light composition

A composition about a paper cone. An elongated cone with an open mouth below and a sharp apex above, thin walls, a wavy fire-polished rim — a rolled cone or a bindweed flower. The simplest of forms, and that is its strength: it is recognised instantly and needs no explanation.

The cones hang at several levels, slightly turned and overlapping, with a wavy lower edge. The upper zone stays empty — only the hatching of cables.

By day the composition is all but invisible: clear and colourless, it reads through highlights along the wavy rims alone. In the evening the light travels from the apex down to the mouth, reflecting off the walls, and a glowing column appears within the body; it escapes through the open base. Dozens of luminous funnels hang in the air with not a single lamp in sight.

**Where it works.** Living rooms, dining rooms, lobbies, meeting rooms, zones above a long table. The light is directed downward through the mouth — the piece lights the table, not the room.

**Style.** Scandinavian minimalism and organic modernism: the simplest form without decoration. The message is light — a piece for an interior that refuses weight.

The overall size, the number of cones, the suspension lengths and the calibres are all made to order.

## LC0057 — Light composition

A composition about the difference between finishes. The wings themselves give no light here — the sources stand between them, on gold holders. The wings only receive the light, and the whole piece is built on how differently they do it: the opaque white ones diffuse it into a soft patch, the mirrored ones reflect and pass it onward to their neighbours.

The form is a curved wing with a sharp tip and a deep hollow. The bowl is dense, the assembly spiral, the wings turned in every direction and overlapping in places.

By day it is a flock stilled below the ceiling: white mixed with mirror, read through silhouette and reflection. In the evening one half of the composition is always calm and the other sparkling, and as you walk around they change places — a swarm that burns in every other wing.

**Where it works.** Living rooms with high ceilings, lobbies, stairwells, banquet halls. The spiral assembly asks to be seen in the round: the piece is not placed against a wall.

**Style.** Contemporary Art Deco and glamorous minimalism: two behaviours of a single beam. The message is festive — a piece for a formal space.

The overall size, the number of elements and the ratio of white to mirror are all made to order.

## LC0056 — Light composition

The same form, stripped of contrast. The wings are entirely clear, and giving up the second finish changes the piece fundamentally: instead of the interplay of white and mirror, what remains is pure optics.

The wings hang at random and are laid in a spiral, with the sources standing between them. The body of each wing works as a lens: a glowing core appears in the hollow and a bright thread runs along the rim.

By day the composition is all but invisible and shows itself only in highlights — a clear mass that is easy to overlook. In the evening every layer is visible at once, the front one and everything behind it: the piece reads right through, with real depth, and never looks solid. A cloud of lights, each in its own setting.

**Where it works.** Living rooms with high ceilings, dining rooms, lobbies, stairwells. Transparency needs a dark backdrop: against a white ceiling the piece disappears by day.

**Style.** Contemporary classicism and minimalism: light as the only material. The message is clean — a piece for an interior that refuses colour.

The overall size, the number of wings, the suspension lengths and the colour temperature are all made to order.

## LC0055 — Light composition

A composition about a shoreline. Several different forms meet here — broad discs with a thick lens at the centre, curved white crescents, narrow curls, occasional gold bowls — and they are mixed without system. This is how the tideline lies: the unrelated ends up side by side.

The elements hang in layers, turned at random; the composition is drawn out horizontally with a ragged lower edge and a few pieces descending deeper than the rest.

By day it is a heap of shells — clear, white and gold intermixed, read through form. In the evening one and the same beam behaves in three ways: the clear discs gather it and give bright cores within their bodies, the white crescents diffuse it into soft patches, the gold bowls reflect it and flare hot. A shoreline lit from beneath.

**Where it works.** Generous rooms — lobbies, dining rooms, double-height living rooms, meeting rooms. The multi-level arrangement sets a lower limit: allow clearance beneath.

**Style.** Marine modernism and eclecticism in the best sense: the unrelated gathered on a single principle. The message is alive — a piece for an interior with no taste for uniformity.

The overall size, the number of elements, the ratio of finishes and the drop heights are all made to order.

## LC0054 — Light composition

A composition about prayer beads. The beads are strung several to a rod — larger above, smaller toward the bottom — and each strand ends in a drop. Diminishing calibre is an old device of rosaries and necklaces: it gives the strand direction and weight.

The array is dense, level at the top with a wavy lower edge. Above the composition runs a sparse net of bare rods with point lights, receding to the ceiling.

By day it is a row of hanging rosaries — a honey tone, large irregular faceting with planes of differing size, read through shine. In the evening the light enters the topmost bead and is passed down the whole strand from stone to stone, refracting on every facet: the amber fills from within, the composition gives a warm honey glow with no direct source at all, and the holders between the beads flare as sharp points. Drops of resin lit right through.

**Where it works.** An elongated horizontal format — long dining tables, bar counters, meeting rooms, reception areas. The wavy lower edge sets varying heights above the table.

**Style.** Art Deco and Eastern opulence: the strand of stones as a classic of formal decoration. The message is warm and rich — a piece for a table where people linger.

The length, the number of strands, the range of calibres and the depth of the honey tone are all made to order.

## LC0053 — Backlit wall panel

A composition about the fogged window of a greenhouse. The pane here is not perfectly clear but carries condensation and fine droplets on its inner face — and that is the key decision. The greenery is seen indistinctly, as through a morning conservatory window: neither a picture nor a plant, but a condition.

Inside the round case dense greenery is laid in layers — ferns, monstera, fronds of several kinds. The backlight is hidden around the inner perimeter and runs as a ring.

By day the panel works as a decorative inset of calm green: a circle of green on the wall, read through colour. In the evening the ring light strikes the plants from the side, the front leaves come into focus and the far ones dissolve into a luminous haze — and a round window into a winter garden opens in the wall.

**Where it works.** Interiors without windows — corridors, dressing rooms, washrooms, meeting rooms, lift lobbies, spas. The piece solves a specific problem: it gives a view where there is none.

**Style.** Biodesign and the Victorian conservatory tradition: the plant under a bell as a collector's object. The message is convivial — a piece for a room that needs bringing to life.

The case diameter, the plant selection, the planting density and the colour temperature of the backlight are all made to order.

## LC0052 — Light composition

A composition about a greenhouse quarter. Rectangular cases and cylinders of differing heights stand in a dense array at varied levels and together read as a city skyline of towers and blocks — only with greenery instead of windows.

Sealed inside each are fern fronds, leaves and shoots; the walls carry condensation, so the plants are seen indistinctly, as through the side of a terrarium. The upper line of the composition is ragged — a city is never level.

By day it is a collection of herbaria under clear covers: transparent volumes through which everything is visible, the front row and what stands behind it. In the evening the light in the upper part of each case runs downward and lights the greenery from within: the leaves fill with acid green and the walls glow along their edges. A quarter of glasshouses lit all at once.

**Where it works.** Generous rooms — lobbies, meeting rooms, restaurants, winter gardens, showrooms. Transparency works when seen through: the piece is placed within the volume, not tight to the ceiling.

**Style.** Biodesign and urban modernism: nature set into a city grid. The message is contemporary — a piece for an interior with an agenda.

The overall size, the number of cases, the plant selection and the drop heights are all made to order.

## LC0051 — Light composition

A composition about a summer night in a vineyard. The roles here are strictly divided: the foliage gives form and shadow, the fireflies give light. The leaves stay subdued and reflect only what reaches them from below — exactly as real foliage does in the dark.

The leaf form carries drawn lobes and venation, each one slightly crumpled and rolled at the edge. Small winged figures are set among them, unevenly distributed.

By day it is a vine thicket lifted to the ceiling — subdued green patina mixed with warm bronze and gold wear. In the evening every figure gives its own bright point with a radiant halo, while the foliage around it stays dark and is only lit from beneath. A vineyard full of fireflies.

**Where it works.** Generous rooms — lobbies, winter gardens, double-height living rooms, restaurant floors, terraces. Sparse point sources give atmospheric rather than working light.

**Style.** Art Nouveau and natural romanticism: the grapevine as a classic motif. The message is lyrical — a piece that brings back a memory of summer.

The overall size, the number of leaves, the number of fireflies and the palette of the patina are all made to order.

## LC0050 — Light composition

The same botany without the fireflies and at a larger calibre. There are fewer leaves, they are markedly bigger, and each one reads on its own — instead of a mass you get a herbarium, where the specimen matters.

The composition is sparse: wide gaps between the leaves, visible cables, the whole mass looking light. The leaves are turned at random, some flat, some on edge.

By day it is a herbarium suspended in the air: a matte surface with worked venation, green patina beside warm bronze. In the evening the light falls on the leaves from below and from the side — the patina absorbs it and gives a subdued glow, the bronze edges flare sharply and trace each leaf with a hot line, and the veins read as relief. A crown lit from beneath.

**Where it works.** An elongated horizontal format — long dining tables, meeting rooms, reception areas, winter gardens. The sparseness gives little light: the piece is an accent, not a main source.

**Style.** Botanical modernism and mid-century: the plant as a graphic object. The message is restrained and natural — a piece for a warm palette.

The length, the number of leaves, the palette of the patina and the drop heights are all made to order.

## LC0049 — Light composition

A composition about a vein of amber. The stones are irregular and tumbled, with faceted sides and dents — not one repeats its neighbour, the surface smooth and mirrored in places, welling up in others. The tone is honey-amber, almost colourless here and dense there: resin never has an even colour.

The cloud is dense, level at the top with a ragged lower edge — a few stones descend deeper than the rest. Enough air is left between the elements to see the depth.

By day it is a scatter of amber — warm, translucent, living on the refraction of daylight. In the evening the light passes through several layers, gathering temperature, and emerges already dense with honey; each stone works as a lens for its neighbour, and the source is impossible to locate. A vein glowing inside rock.

**Where it works.** An elongated horizontal format — long dining tables, bar counters, lobbies, meeting rooms. The layering is seen from the side and below: the piece is hung fairly low.

**Style.** Organic minimalism and the Baltic amber tradition: the stone exactly as it is. The message is warm and collector-minded — a piece chosen by feel rather than from a catalogue.

The length, the number of elements, the calibres and the depth of the honey tone are all made to order.

## LC0048 — Light composition

A composition about tension released. Each plate sags in an arc between two fixing points — the form fabric takes when you stop pulling it. The edges lift into sharp corners here and hang in triangular points there, so the line is ragged rather than smooth.

The panels run in two tiers, one beneath the other, close together, forming two undulating bands. The texture is large and scaled — worked hide or bark.

By day it is a row of stretched panels: the scaling catches daylight and gives a soft play of light and shade across the whole plane. In the evening the light runs along the upper edge and grazes the texture at a sharp angle, bringing out the entire relief: the surface comes alive and every scale gets its own shadow. Almost nothing escapes outward — only the grazing light works, and the composition reads as a garland lit along its top.

**Where it works.** An elongated horizontal format — long dining tables, meeting rooms, lobbies, restaurant floors. Grazing light means the piece gives little general illumination and works as an accent.

**Style.** Brutalist luxury and contemporary sculpture: texture instead of shine. The message is tactile — a piece whose chief quality is its surface.

The length, the number of panels, the texture and the number of tiers are all made to order.

## LC0047 — Light composition

A composition about a bird drawn in a single stroke. The simple tick — two blades diverging at an angle, with uneven edges — is how a child draws a bird and how a bird is seen at a distance. The author takes the most economical of signs and multiplies it into a flock.

The pyramid is dense, thicker toward the centre and the base, thinned at the edges and above. Over the mass runs a net of bare cables with glowing points — the sky above the flock.

By day it is a flock sketched in strokes alone: clear mixed with polished gold, read through silhouette. In the evening the irregular facets refract the light and scatter it in white sparks, the gold ticks reflect it and flare warm, and because the array is dense the glow passes from element to element — the whole flock shimmers. A swarm seen against the light.

**Where it works.** Generous rooms — lobbies, atria, double-height living rooms, banquet halls. The pyramid needs height: pressed to the ceiling it loses its silhouette.

**Style.** Contemporary minimalism and the graphic school: the sign instead of the image. The message is light — a piece for an interior that likes things left unsaid.

The overall size, the number of elements, the share of gold and the drop heights are all made to order.
