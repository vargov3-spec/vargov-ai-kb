## LC0460 — Decorative composition

A composition about direction. This flock has no top and no front — only a direction of flight, and that is set at assembly. The composition has no orientation of its own: it takes one from the room.

Ribbed boat-shapes fly in a wide crescent, each at its own angle; clear ones are intermixed with olive green, the tips gilded.

By day it is light graphic work in the air: the ribbing reads as hatching and the composition has almost no mass. In the evening points of light are scattered through the flock and ignite the ribbing — the feathers flare as they do under a low sun, when the light travels along rather than from above.

**Where it works.** Living rooms, lobbies, meeting rooms, restaurant halls. The direction of flight is chosen along the route of movement from the entrance — the composition leads the eye.

**Style.** Natural Art Nouveau and contemporary sculpture: the feather as a motif of lightness. The message is alive — a piece for an interior that dislikes stasis.

The number of feathers, the balance of green to clear and the spread are all made to order.

## LC0459 — Decorative composition

A composition about laundry in the wind. Every leaf is bent twice — not once, as in a static form, but with a second fold of the kind only cloth gets when a current takes it. Hence the soft movement through the whole mass.

The matte texture kills highlights and makes the light velvet: white and smoky grey waves with occasional burgundy hang as a vertical forest.

By day it is a quiet mass of pale waves — a piece read through plasticity and soft chiaroscuro, without one hard highlight. In the evening gold rods glow between the leaves like candles in a garden while the leaves themselves stay matte — the sources are visible and the mass around them stays calm.

**Where it works.** Living rooms, bedrooms, lobbies, spa areas, meeting rooms. Velvet light without glare suits zones where people stay a while.

**Style.** Natural Art Nouveau and Japanese aesthetics: cloth and petal in one gesture. The message is quiet — a piece for an interior that does not raise its voice.

The number of leaves, the palette and the density of the forest are all made to order.

## LC0458 — Light composition

A composition about fur made of hoarfrost. Semicircular fans are built from a dense needled texture — what grows on branches over a frosty night: not smooth ice but a prickly pile.

The fans overlay each other in waves, like the scales or feathers of a giant bird; the arcs alternate gold and steel.

By day it is northern opulence: the needled mass catches daylight in thousands of points, a ceremonial and cold piece. In the evening light from under the arcs floods the needles, and every fan burns with a prickly radiance — hoarfrost that has caught fire from within.

**Where it works.** Lobbies, banqueting halls, reception rooms, showrooms. The wave assembly requires area: the composition is built wide rather than deep.

**Style.** Art Deco and Nordic Art Nouveau: fur and hoarfrost as motifs of luxury. The message is ceremonial — a piece for a formal space with a cold palette.

The number of fans, the balance of gold to steel and the dimensions are all made to order.

## LC0457 — Light composition

A composition about pebbles. The form is deliberately simple — a smooth stone with rounded sides and a live, uneven surface: something a river rolled rather than a craftsman turned. The author adds nothing to it.

The magic comes from the mass: a field of stones on fine needle suspensions shimmers like a shallow in the sun.

By day it is clear cobbles suspended in the air — a piece read through the irregularity of the form and its refractions, calm and quiet. In the evening light comes on at the crown of each and runs down through the mass: the stones fill from within, and the field begins to shift like water over a shallow.

**Where it works.** Spa areas, pools, living rooms, lobbies, meeting rooms. The field is designed for an area: the composition covers a ceiling rather than occupying a point.

**Style.** Natural minimalism and wabi-sabi: the simplest of forms, without intervention. The message is contemplative — a piece for an interior that values calm.

The number of stones, their calibres and the area of the field are all made to order.

## LC0456 — Light composition

A composition about a downpour under a spotlight. Narrow faceted lancets descend in a dense vertical cascade several storeys deep — hundreds of arrows with one direction and differing lengths.

The cathedral solemnity here comes not from decor but from scale and repetition.

By day it is a clear and smoky amber cascade: the facets catch daylight and scatter it, a piece read through brilliance and depth. In the evening light enters from above and runs down the facets — the cascade glitters entire, like rain caught in a beam.

**Where it works.** Staircases and atriums. A large formal piece: the composition is designed for a specific height and reads differently from every floor.

**Style.** Formal classicism in a contemporary execution: a cascade without a single conventional faceted pendant. The message is ceremonial — a piece for a building that greets.

The number of arrows, the height of the cascade and the palette are all made to order.

## LC0455 — Light composition

A composition about rain turned into jewellery. The light is hidden inside long stems and descends the shaft, filling the drop right to its tip: the source is not hung from the drop, it leads to it.

Differing tube lengths gather the composition into a three-dimensional cloud; the rhythm of gold verticals and clear drops makes the piece strict and jewel-like at once.

By day it is graphic work: gold lines with clear drops at their ends, a composition read through rhythm and with almost no mass. In the evening the light descends the stems and ignites in the drops — the stems stay dark and only the ends glow. Rain with burning drops.

**Where it works.** Living rooms, dining rooms, lobbies, meeting rooms. The differing stem lengths give three-dimensionality — the composition needs height to spare.

**Style.** Jewelled Art Nouveau and contemporary classic: the suspension as a detail in its own right. The message is graceful — a piece for an interior that loves precision.

The number of drops, the lengths of the stems and the format of the cloud are all made to order.

## LC0454 — Decorative composition

A composition about a meadow after rain. Discs with a radial texture and a live wavy edge read as mushroom caps or as angelica flower heads — the sort of thing that appears in a meadow suddenly and all at once.

The composition brings a clearing, a meadow, a forest into an interior — and does it without one literal leaf.

By day it is a cloud of milky and amber umbrellas: the radial texture reads through light and shade, the edge lives by its irregularity. In the evening the light hides under the caps and lifts the rays from within — the meadow lights up from below, as under a low sun.

**Where it works.** Living rooms, winter gardens, restaurants, lobbies, spa areas. The cloud is built wide: it needs area rather than height.

**Style.** Natural Art Nouveau and bio-design: a meadow instead of a bouquet. The message is alive and fresh — a piece for an interior that loves nature without stylisation.

The number of umbrellas, the balance of milky to amber and the dimensions are all made to order.

## LC0453 — Light composition

A composition about a leaf stalk. The gold capsule here is fixing and source at once: the rays of the texture radiate from it exactly as the veins radiate from the stalk of a real leaf. Structure and botany have coincided.

Clear, smoky and cognac leaves hang as a dense canopy at differing heights.

By day it is a canopy in three tones: the texture reads as rays, the layers of canopy overlaid and visible right through. In the evening the light enters the leaf from within and runs out along the rays — every leaf glows from stalk to edge, and the canopy burns like a tree at sunset.

**Where it works.** Living rooms, lobbies, winter gardens, restaurant halls. The layered canopy requires height — pressed to the ceiling, it loses its depth.

**Style.** Art Nouveau and botanical modern: the leaf as a classic motif. The message is warm and natural — a piece for an interior in a natural register.

The number of leaves, the palette and the shape of the canopy are all made to order.

## LC0452 — Light composition

A composition about a regatta in the dark. Triangular prisms hang from chrome yard-bars — the form quotes a sail outright and the bars read as spars: a fleet held still before the start.

Geometry and colour make the piece graphic: this is the most architectural of the brand's green series.

By day it is emerald triangles on chrome — the radial carving reads as hatching, the composition strict and clean. In the evening light comes from the bars and fills the sails, the facets flaring cold emerald: the fleet catches fire in green.

**Where it works.** Lobbies, meeting rooms, restaurants, showrooms, contemporary living rooms. The saturated green tints the air: neutral surfaces are needed nearby.

**Style.** Modernism and marine classicism: the sail as a geometric sign. The message is graphic and contemporary — a piece for an interior of straight lines.

The number of sails, the depth of the green tone and the spacing are all made to order.

## LC0451 — Light composition

A composition about a ring on water. The rays radiate from one point, and even a static form reads as movement — a drawing that always has a source and a direction.

The contour is deliberately imperfect: a live asymmetry makes the form natural rather than drafted. No disc repeats its neighbour.

By day it is clear cushions with a radial texture: the drawing reads against the light, and the uneven edge keeps the composition from turning mechanical. In the evening light from the gold capsules runs along the rays from centre to edge, and every disc begins to breathe — an impulse repeating over and over.

**Where it works.** Living rooms, dining rooms, meeting rooms, lobbies. The flat discs steal no height: the composition suits medium ceilings too.

**Style.** Optical art and natural minimalism: the wave as a pure form. The message is calm — a piece for an interior that likes the simple.

The number of discs, their calibres and the density of the cloud are all made to order.
