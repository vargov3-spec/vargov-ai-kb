## LC0430 — Light composition

A composition about a wave that has passed through an organ. A strict vertical of elements and a free curve of suspension — the whole piece rests on that contrast: the tubes hang plumb while the carrying branches bend in waves, and the field of pipes looks disturbed.

The surface of the tubes is alive, run down — like a candle that has already burned.

By day it is a milky field on dark branches: the composition reads through the rhythm of the verticals and the relief of the suspension. In the evening light fills the tubes from top to bottom in a warm gradient — from peach to milk — and each begins to look like a candle lit from inside. An organ whose pipes have caught fire.

**Where it works.** Dining rooms, lobbies, meeting rooms, restaurant halls. The undulating suspension gives different heights above a zone: the composition is designed for a specific plan.

**Style.** Organic Art Nouveau and contemporary classic: the candle as the oldest image of light. The message is warm — a piece for an interior where people gather.

The number of tubes, the curve of the branches and the dimensions are all made to order.

## LC0429 — Light composition

A composition about a seed. A smooth drop — a form in which everything is compressed and nothing is shown; on some elements there is the finest engraving of patterns, like carving on a shell. The difference between smooth and carved gives the composition a second plane that is visible only close up.

The wave is broad and the suspensions invisible: the mass reads as hovering.

By day it is a quiet pastel — white and turquoise, smooth matte, the rhythm of the drops. The composition does not argue with an interior, it gives it room to breathe. In the evening points of light hidden in individual seeds shine downward in warm beams: not all of them burn, only the chosen ones, and the field acquires accents it did not have by day.

**Where it works.** Bedrooms, living rooms, spa areas, meeting rooms, reception rooms. The meditative rhythm and soft light suit zones where people stay a while.

**Style.** Scandinavian minimalism and natural Art Nouveau: pastel and a smooth form. The message is calm — a piece for an interior that calms.

The number of seeds, the balance of white to turquoise and the shape of the wave are all made to order.

## LC0428 — Light composition

A composition about weight pretending to be vapour. Every dome is crumpled its own way, like a cloud sculpted by wind — and it looks weightless although it is a dense glossy volume. The play of weight against weightlessness is the piece's principal trick.

The flock hovers at differing heights, white intermixed with smoky blue.

By day it is glossy clouds beneath the ceiling: the crumpled surfaces catch light and give uneven highlights, and the composition reads through plasticity. In the evening a soft light comes on inside the domes and leaves the folds as a warm radiance — the dense mass turns into vapour for good.

**Where it works.** Living rooms, bedrooms, children's rooms, spa areas, lobbies. A multi-level flock requires height — pressed to the ceiling, it loses its volume.

**Style.** Contemporary minimalism and pop aesthetics: the cloud as a form, without irony. The message is light and friendly — a piece for an interior with no pretension.

The number of clouds, the palette and the area of the flock are all made to order.

## LC0427 — Light composition

A composition about passion and restraint. The white flowers burn, the burgundy ones keep silent: the light lives only in the pale bowls while the dark ones hold colour and depth. One composition speaks in two voices at once.

The petals are crumpled, uneven, alive; the cluster is assembled on a vertical gold axis. Red and white on gold is a classic operatic register.

By day it is a dense cluster of cream and burgundy on gold — a saturated piece read through colour and the plasticity of the petals. In the evening the white bowls fill with warm light and the burgundy ones go into deep shadow: the composition turns dramatic, like a stage where not everyone is lit.

**Where it works.** Staircases, tall niches, bay windows, private living rooms. The vertical format takes little floor area and requires height.

**Style.** Art Nouveau and operatic classicism: the poppy as a symbol of passion and oblivion. The message is dramatic — a piece for an interior with character.

The number of flowers, the balance of burgundy to white and the height are all made to order.

## LC0426 — Light composition

A composition about lace. Openwork discs with honeycomb perforation read as a frost pattern or a hand-made doily — but are executed with machine precision. Joining airiness to precision is the device: a cloud from a distance, a jeweller's work up close.

At the centres of the discs, flowers open with light in them; the bowl is wide, the discs white intermixed with gold.

By day it is a field of lace: the perforation reads as graphic work, light from the window passes through it and draws a second composition on the ceiling. In the evening the light leaves through the perforation as hundreds of highlights — a small cloud of points appears around every bowl, and the lace turns out to be glowing.

**Where it works.** Living rooms, dining rooms, boudoirs, wedding halls, boutiques. The perforation gives patterned shadows — quiet surfaces are needed nearby so the drawing can be seen.

**Style.** Art Nouveau and jewelled Art Nouveau: lace as a classic decorative motif. The message is dressy and feminine — a piece for an interior that loves detail.

The number of discs, the balance of white to gold and the dimensions of the bowl are all made to order.

## LC0425 — Light composition

A composition about handwriting. Hundreds of commas written by one hand, and not one of them the same: each is curved by its own movement — a rise, a turn, a fall. A form on the edge of calligraphy, where what matters is not the letter but the pressure.

The flock is dense, glossy white intermixed with matte gold.

By day it is calligraphy in the air: white and gold read through curves and overlays, a formal piece without heaviness. In the evening warm points of light are scattered deep inside the flock — they light the elements from within the mass, and the handwriting appears in layers: the near lines sharp, the far ones blurred.

**Where it works.** Living rooms, dining rooms, lobbies, reception rooms, reception desks. The dense flock reads from a distance: the composition is calculated for the view from the entrance.

**Style.** Contemporary Art Deco and calligraphic aesthetics: the gesture instead of the form. The message is light and graceful — a piece for an interior that values a line.

The number of elements, the proportion of gold and the spread of the flock are all made to order.

## LC0424 — Light composition

A composition about kelp in a column of light. The ribbons wind around a vertical frame — they do not wrap it decoratively, they reach upward, as weed reaches for the surface. The vertical is full of movement: the ribbon seems to be still growing.

Sparks are scattered through the mass of the amber ribbons — the suspension you find in water lit from above.

By day it is a warm amber spiral on cold chrome: a contrast of two temperatures read through highlights. In the evening light travels down the ribbons, igniting the sparks in the mass and the gloss of the bends — the light descends into the depth as it does in real water.

**Where it works.** Staircases, bay windows, tall wall piers, atriums. The vertical takes little floor area and works where there is no room for a bulky composition.

**Style.** Marine Art Nouveau: the weed as a natural motif without stylisation. The message is warm and organic — a piece for an interior that avoids straight lines.

The height, the number of ribbons and the saturation of the amber tone are all made to order.

## LC0423 — Light composition

A composition about heat under ash. The light burns only inside the clear stones while the red ones merely reflect it: the ruby mass does not glow on its own, it is heated by its neighbours. That is how coals smoulder — the light comes not from everywhere but from inside the heap.

Hundreds of faceted stones are gathered into a crescent bowl on the finest of cables: a piece of jewellery at the scale of an interior, treasure poured out into the air.

By day it is a scattering of deep ruby and clear — a piece read through faceting and colour, dense and rich. In the evening the clear crystals flare, the red ones pick up their light, and the scattering begins to shimmer unevenly. The red is metered out by the light — the composition is deep rather than loud.

**Where it works.** Dining rooms, private living rooms, bars, restaurants, reception rooms. The saturated colour asks for restrained surroundings.

**Style.** Art Deco and Eastern opulence: a scattering of gemstones as a motif. The message is rich and warm — a piece for an interior that is not afraid of colour.

The number of stones, the proportion of ruby to clear and the dimensions are all made to order.

## LC0422 — Light composition

A composition about a flare. Curved tusks break out in every direction from a mirrored core — the sun at the moment of a prominence, a dandelion enlarged to a chandelier, a firework that will not go out. Three readings of one form, and all three about a release of energy.

There are bubbles in the mass of the tusks, some clear, some smoky. The core is mirrored — it gathers and returns everything around it.

By day it is an explosion caught in a freeze frame: clear rays and a mirrored centre, a piece living off reflections and changing with every step. In the evening every tusk is lit from its base — the rays run along the curves to the points, and the flare turns out to be lasting. The composition holds a room single-handed.

**Where it works.** Lobbies, atriums, double-height living rooms, showrooms. The spread in every direction requires free volume: nothing should be near the composition.

**Style.** Art Deco and cosmic modernism: the explosion as a form. The message is powerful — a piece for a space that has to astonish.

The diameter, the number of tusks and the palette are all made to order.

## LC0421 — Light sculpture

A composition about coral grown in air. The tees link into an extended structure hovering horizontally, and the differing orientation of the elements makes it alive and fluid — growth never goes in one direction.

The light lives inside and comes out through the openings — it is aimed nowhere in particular, it breathes out of the pores in soft warm patches.

By day it is a white glossy colony: a piece read through relief, voids and inner passages, complex when examined close up. In the evening the pores light up, the light is distributed unevenly, and the structure turns into a living organism for good.

**Where it works.** Lobbies, restaurants, spa areas, showrooms, gallery spaces. The horizontal extension requires length — the composition is built along rather than deep.

**Style.** Bio-design and organic Art Nouveau: the colony as a form with no author. The message is natural and contemporary — a piece for an interior with an agenda.

The length of the colony, the density of the linking and the number of glowing modules are all made to order.
