## LC0031 — Light composition

A composition about a line folded into a loop. A long pin bent into a half-circle at the end reads both as a paper clip and as the tubing of a wind instrument. What they share is a path travelled by something invisible — paper in one case, air in the other. Here that path carries light.

The loops hang vertically, layered, threaded through one another and overlapping, level at the top and deeply sagging below. Because everything is clear, you see straight through.

By day it is an interlacing of clear tubes with gold insets — graphics read through outline. In the evening the light enters a loop and runs its whole length like a lightguide, rounding the bend and bursting out at the cut end as a bright point; the gold loops reflect and give warm bands. What you read is not form but a net of glowing lines nested inside one another.

**Where it works.** Living rooms with high ceilings, dining rooms, lobbies, meeting rooms. Transparency in depth works when seen from the side — the piece is not pushed up against the ceiling.

**Style.** Contemporary minimalism and industrial graphics: the line instead of the volume. The message is spare — a piece for an interior that rests on structure.

The overall size, the number of loops and the ratio of clear to gold are all made to order.

## LC0030 — Light composition

A composition about a crumpled sheet. Each plate has been crushed and smoothed out again — creases, folds and torn corners preserved entirely. The author fixes the moment when the sheet has already been spoiled but not yet thrown away.

The plates stand vertically, slightly turned, laid in layers. The top is ragged — individual sheets rise above the main mass — and the bottom is wavy; the array is deliberately uneven.

By day it is a heap of sheets thrown into the air: mirrored gold mixed with matte white, a random pattern of highlights. In the evening the crumpled gold reflects the light unevenly, in bands along the folds, flaring sharply, while the white diffuses it softly and holds a calm ground. The composition reads as an alternation of hot and cold rather than a single mass.

**Where it works.** An elongated horizontal format — long dining tables, lobbies, meeting rooms, reception areas. The ragged upper edge needs clearance to the ceiling.

**Style.** Contemporary art and minimalism: chance as form. The message is bold — a piece for an interior with no taste for the smooth.

The length, the number of plates and the ratio of gold to white are all made to order.

## LC0029 — Light composition

A composition about a scarf picked up at its centre. The material is suspended from a single point and falls freely in folds — a form that cannot be designed, only allowed. Each element settles its own way: two folds here, four there, a corner lifted somewhere else.

The longitudinal ribbing reads clearly and serves the image: this is how ribbed fabric hangs. The elements sit on fine rods with conical holders above and gather in a dense group at varied levels, with a wavy lower edge.

By day it is fabric frozen in the act of falling: a warm smoky tone, translucent, with clear hatching and its own shadows inside the folds. In the evening the light enters from above and runs down along the ribbing — every fold glows in strokes and flares more brightly at the breaks. A row of glowing scarves.

**Where it works.** Living rooms with high ceilings, dining rooms, lobbies, banquet halls. The folds read from the side: the piece is hung at eye level rather than overhead.

**Style.** Contemporary sculpture and Baroque drapery in a minimalist reading. The message is expressive — a piece for a space that needs a gesture.

The overall size, the number of elements, the pattern of the folds and the depth of the smoky tone are all made to order.

## LC0028 — Light composition

A composition about the spindle. A rod with a thickening at its middle is among the oldest tools there is, and its form has not changed in thousands of years because there is nothing in it to change. The author takes it as it stands and adds only what the spindle never had: light.

Some rods carry two thickenings, some one — the rhythm is uneven. The composition is vertical, the rods hanging in a dense group at several levels.

By day it is a set of clear instruments — precise, cool forms with gold collars and finials. In the evening light enters from above and below and travels along the rod to meet in the middle: the transverse scoring breaks it into close horizontal strokes, while the thickened disc gathers it and flares into a bright ring. A double graphic — fine hatching along the rod and a bright point at the swell.

**Where it works.** Living rooms with high ceilings, stairwells, lobbies, meeting rooms. The vertical group builds downward: allow for height.

**Style.** Industrial minimalism and craft aesthetics: the tool as form. The message is rational — a piece for an interior that values how things are made.

The overall size, the number of rods, the number of swells and the finish of the collars are all made to order.

## LC0027 — Light composition

A composition about the candle. The form reads unambiguously, with no stylisation at all: a cylinder in a gold sleeve. The author does not hide the reference — on the contrary, it is carried through to the end, giving every "candle" its own gradient from bright to dark, exactly as a real one burns hot at the top and stays cool below.

The cylinders hang closely at several levels, level at the top and ragged below. The calibres are identical, only the lengths differ: the candles have burnt down unequally.

By day it is a set of gold sleeves — a dense row read through metal and the matte white beneath it. In the evening the light runs down the cylinder: the matte section diffuses it and glows evenly, the clear lower part transmits it and almost fades out, while the transverse scoring breaks the glow into horizontal strokes. A row of burning candles suspended in the air.

**Where it works.** Living rooms, dining rooms, stairwells, lobbies, meeting rooms. The downward light lifts the surface beneath it — the piece works above a table.

**Style.** Contemporary classicism: the candle without the candelabrum. The message is warm and ceremonial — a piece for a table where people gather.

The overall size, the number of candles, the cylinder lengths and the finish of the sleeves are all made to order.

## LC0026 — Light composition

A composition about brushwood. The rods hang almost horizontally at varied angles and cross one another, forming a three-dimensional lattice — the way gathered deadwood lies, where no branch repeats another's direction.

The layers run one beneath the other through the full height of the opening, twisting into a spiral. Density is uneven: in places the rods bunch together, elsewhere they hang alone.

By day it is brushwood suspended in the air: clear lines with gold collars, read graphically. In the evening light enters at the collar and runs both ways, flaring at the ends as points. Hundreds of horizontal glowing strokes at varied heights give the effect of a pencil drawing built in space — not a mass but a net of lines descending between floors.

**Where it works.** A vertical format — stairwells, atria, shafts between floors, double-height living rooms. The spiral is designed for the specific height and route of ascent.

**Style.** Contemporary minimalism and the graphic school: the stroke as the only element. The message is intellectual — a piece for a space that thinks.

The overall height, the number of rods, the pitch of the spiral and the finish of the collars are all made to order.

## LC0025 — Light composition

A composition about balance. A horizontal rod passing straight through a disc, with collars at both ends — a scale beam, a propeller, a spindle laid on its side. A form from the world of mechanics, which always has a fulcrum and two symmetrical sides.

The spiral descends, thinning downward — the last elements hang alone. Every one is turned differently, so the composition has no principal face.

By day it is a set of clear mechanisms — precise forms with gold details, read through highlights. In the evening the disc gathers the light and flares along its rim, while the ends give directed points with radiant haloes; because the elements are turned at random, the glowing points face every direction. From each floor the piece looks different — a swarm of beams descending in a spiral.

**Where it works.** A vertical format — stairwells, atria, shafts between floors, double-height living rooms. It is designed to be seen from several levels at once.

**Style.** Industrial minimalism and precision mechanics as an aesthetic. The message is rational and contemporary — a piece for an interior with an engineer's taste.

The overall height, the number of elements, the pitch of the spiral and the finish of the collars are all made to order.

## LC0024 — Light composition

A composition about what was broken and set again. Inside every medallion lies a net of cracks and shards compressed into a single layer: ice split by frost and then caught back together. Destruction is not depicted here, it is recorded.

The discs stand on edge, gathered into one dense row — a crest, or a rank of shields. The calibres vary and the tilts differ slightly, so the row never looks machined.

By day it is a rank of icy plates in a fine gold setting: the white net inside reads as graphics, the setting draws the outline of every disc. In the evening the light enters at the rim and travels inward along the cracks like lightguides — each crack flares along its own line, the whole disc glows with a net of white veins, and the setting rings it with a warm contour. Medallions with frozen lightning inside.

**Where it works.** An elongated horizontal format — long dining tables, bar counters, meeting rooms, reception areas. Discs on edge read end-on: the piece is oriented along the line of sight.

**Style.** Contemporary Art Deco and natural aesthetics: the fracture as a drawing. The message is dramatic — a piece for an interior that likes a strong detail.

The length, the number of discs, the calibres and the finish of the settings are all made to order.

## LC0023 — Floor lamp

A composition about an inverted crown. The bowl opens upward and tapers below to a sharp point: a cone standing on its tip — a crown set on the head, or a flower head caught by the stem. The form of authority, turned inside out.

Hundreds of small elements hang from a fine hoop — clear faceted drops and gold petals, mixed without system. The base is a cone of black stone with a bright white vein, and between it and the shade runs a slender stem reduced to a minimum. The contrast of heavy stone below and shimmering mass above holds the whole structure.

By day it is a sculptural object: a shimmering mass on black stone, expensive and dense. In the evening the light from deep in the bowl refracts in the drops and scatters into sparks, while the petals reflect it in warm patches. Not one source is visible directly — the mass itself glows, and the stone below picks up the reflection.

**Where it works.** Living rooms, halls, reception rooms, showrooms, restaurant floors. The light goes upward and sideways — the lamp gives volumetric illumination, not only an accent.

**Style.** Art Deco and Hollywood glamour: stone, gold, shimmer. The message is representative — a piece that states the level of an interior.

The height, the bowl diameter, the number of elements and the stone of the base are all made to order.

## LC0022 — Decorative screen-curtain

A composition about a surface that answers. Every scale rotates freely on its own axis, and so the wall is alive: it responds to moving air and to anyone passing by. This is not decoration but behaviour — the screen can never be caught looking the same twice.

The palette is deliberately uneven: plates of differing tones are mixed, so there is always a ripple in the surface. It exists in two versions — warm, running from bronze and brass to dark copper, and cool, in silver, pewter and steel.

By day the screen works as a textured surface with a soft metallic sheen: the ripple is visible but calm. In the evening the backlight in the frame edges grazes the scales at a sharp angle, and wherever a plate has turned toward the light a bright point flares. The pattern of highlights never repeats — the wall shifts like water, or like fish scales.

**Where it works.** Zoning and wall treatment — lobbies, restaurants and bars, reception areas, stage backdrops, showrooms, photo zones. The movement needs a gap: the screen is not fixed flat against a wall.

**Style.** Contemporary Art Deco and kinetic art: movement as a material. The message is striking — a piece for a space that must be remembered.

The screen dimensions, the palette and calibre of the scales and the lighting scheme are all made to order.

## LC0021 — Light composition

A composition about backlight. A real shoal seen from below looks exactly like this: some fish flare, others stay silhouettes, and it depends not on them but on the angle. The author reproduces the effect through finishes — scoring on the silver bodies breaks the light into a shimmer along the whole flank, while polished gold reflects it sharply.

Every figure faces the same way: what you read is the movement of a shoal, not a set of objects. Density is uneven — thicker at the centre, with strays at the edges.

By day it is a shoal below the ceiling: gold mixed with softly shimmering silver, read through silhouette. In the evening some fish glow and others stay shadow — a shoal running just beneath the surface of the water.

**Where it works.** Generous rooms — lobbies, atria, pools and spas, panoramic living rooms, restaurants. The direction of the shoal sets an axis: the piece is oriented along the flow of people.

**Style.** Marine modernism and contemporary installation: natural behaviour reproduced precisely. The message is narrative — a piece that makes a scene.

The overall size, the number of fish and the ratio of gold to silver are all made to order.

## LC0020 — Light composition

A composition about what draws them in. The light is hidden inside the tangle, the butterflies sit outside — the piece literally shows the reason they are here: a swarm come to the flame. Cause and effect are shown at once.

The rods are woven into a sphere with no order at all, crossing and jutting outward to form an open cage. The butterflies have settled unevenly: clustered in one place, leaving the cage bare in another.

By day it is a gold tangle covered with white figures — dense, sculptural, read through silhouette. In the evening the light escapes from within through every gap between the rods, lighting the butterflies from below and behind; the wings turn translucent and fill with milky white, and the rods flare into lines. From outside you see a sphere whose surface does not glow — its core does.

**Where it works.** Living rooms with high ceilings, stairwells, lobbies, wedding and banquet halls. The sphere reads from every side — it is hung in the volume, not against a wall.

**Style.** Art Nouveau and romantic classicism: the moth drawn to light as an eternal subject. The message is poetic — a piece with a story inside it.

The sphere diameter, the number of butterflies, the density of the cage and the metal finish are all made to order.

## LC0019 — Wall panel

A composition about a field seen from above. The mass is packed element to element without a single gap — this is how a flowering field looks from height, where individual plants merge into unbroken colour. Closed buds, half-open cups and small berry spheres are mixed together: flowering is shown in every phase at once.

White butterflies are set across the field — there are few of them, they are larger than the rest and they work as pale accents in the dark mass. Gold beads are scattered between the elements.

By day the panel reads as a botanical specimen under magnification: deep violet-blue with a glossy glaze, dense and saturated. In the evening the backlight emerges as points between the elements — the glaze reflects it, the beads flare, the butterflies glow softly, and the surface begins to shimmer without losing the density of its colour. A field at dusk full of fireflies.

**Where it works.** Feature walls — halls, living rooms, bedrooms, private studies, reception areas. The dense dark mass needs a clear wall and directional ambient light.

**Style.** Botanical modernism and jewellery miniature: a field assembled by hand. The message is contemplative — a piece people come close to.

The panel diameter, the glaze palette, the number of butterflies and the frame finish are all made to order.

## LC0018 — Light composition

The array is stepped, level at the top and deeply sagging below; the silhouette recalls terraces. The frames stand vertically, layered and overlapping.

By day it is a set of empty frames — mirrored gold mixed with clear ones read only at their edges. In the evening the gold reflects the light and fills from within, the clear frames transmit it and flare along their rims, and the nesting multiplies the glow. A system of luminous openings receding into depth — an enfilade with lit windows.

**Where it works.** An elongated horizontal format — long dining tables, lobbies, meeting rooms, reception rooms. The perspective reads from the side: the piece is designed for a view along its length.

**Style.** Art Deco and architectural minimalism: the opening as a decorative motif. The message is representative — a piece for a formal space.

The length, the number of frames, the calibres and the ratio of gold to clear are all made to order.

## LC0017 — Light composition

A composition about a wingbeat. The mass is gathered into a lens — broad at the centre, tapering to sharp points at both ends; the petals are turned along the axis, in layers. You read an open fan, or a wing at the top of its span: a form that exists for a single instant.

Each petal is narrow and pointed, twisted on its axis, each with a slightly different bend. Crimson-purple is mixed with polished gold.

By day it is an open fan: deep wine colour with a mirrored sheen beside gold, dense and ceremonial. In the evening the purple transmits the light and fills from within to wine, while the gold reflects it and flares hot — the glow comes out uneven, cool sitting next to burning, and the composition shimmers. A firebird's feather stretched above a table.

**Where it works.** An elongated horizontal format — long dining tables, bar counters, reception rooms, restaurant floors. Saturated colour needs a calm setting.

**Style.** Art Deco and Eastern opulence: purple with gold as a classic pairing. The message is vivid and regal — a piece for an interior unafraid of colour.

The length, the number of petals, the depth of the purple tone and the share of gold are all made to order.
