## LC0520 — Light composition

A composition about a face and a reverse. Outside the petals are deep graphite, inside milky white: the dark contour holds the form, the pale interior fills it. That one device makes the sphere volumetric with no relief at all.

The flower heads are built from dozens of narrow petals and hang as a cluster, turned in different directions as on a real bush.

By day it is graphite spheres with glimpses of white in the depth — a dense piece read through silhouette and contrast. In the evening the light comes out of the core between the petals, and every flower becomes a soft lantern with a petal filter: dark outside, burning within.

**Where it works.** Above a table, in a bedroom, in a private living room; in large spaces it gathers into whole flowerbeds. The dark outer side asks for a pale background so the silhouette can read.

**Style.** Dark luxe and natural Art Nouveau: the chrysanthemum in a graphite register. The message is intimate and evening-bound — a piece for an interior with a mood.

The number of flower heads, their calibres and the balance of graphite to white are all made to order.

## LC0519 — Light composition

A composition about an inverted city. Faceted obelisks descend in dense formation — the towers of a Gothic city turned spires down. The formation reads as ceremonial, almost musical: an organ assembled out of light.

The double temperature is the composition's signature move: light enters through the gold pin and runs out along the facets, the clear mass flares cold while the finial stays warm. Gold and ice argue and balance each other.

By day it is a formation of clear spires with gold tops — a piece read through facets and rhythm, strict and formal. In the evening the cold and the warm separate for good: the mass fills with ice, the pins burn gold, and the city glows in two colours at once.

**Where it works.** Formal verticals — staircases, atriums, entrance groups, double-height lobbies. The formation is designed for a specific height.

**Style.** Gothic in a contemporary reading and Art Deco: the spire as a symbol of aspiration. The message is ceremonial — a piece for a building that greets.

The number of spires, their lengths and the proportion of gold are all made to order.

## LC0518 — Light composition

A composition about a rising seam. The eye reads a movement from the bottom up: a large nugget below, and above it a scattering of shards rising on invisible threads — a flare stopped at the moment of its ascent.

The nugget is built as a geode in reverse: a mirrored gold core inside a clear shell, with the light coming from the middle.

By day it is a faceted stone with a gold interior and a scattering of amber shards above it — a piece read through faceting and vertical movement. In the evening the core burns with a dense warm light, the shards shimmer with reflections, and the whole seam begins to rise: the light thins toward the top the way it thins through a body of water.

**Where it works.** Stairwells and narrow tall niches — where a vertical is cramped. The composition is built upward and takes little floor area.

**Style.** Natural aesthetics and Art Deco: the mineral as a formal motif. The message is rich and warm — a piece for an interior in a gold register.

The height of the ascent, the number of shards and the scale of the core are all made to order.

## LC0517 — Light composition

A composition about sparks over a coal. Below, a large core of deep red; above it a plume of crystals rises — what flies up at the moment a white-hot thing is struck.

The red here is dramatic rather than decorative: the composition works on emotion and keeps a space under tension. Smoky mirrored crystals damp and set off the heat — without them it would be unbearable.

By day it is a ruby core with a plume of faceted shards — a dense colour read through faceting and depth. In the evening the core lights from within like heat in rock, while the smoky crystals above it only reflect it and stay dark: one point burns and everything else merely catches the glow.

**Where it works.** Interiors with a bold palette — bars, wine rooms, private cinemas, living rooms with dark walls. Against a pale ground the drama is lost.

**Style.** Dark luxe and theatrical aesthetics: colour as emotion. The message is passionate — a piece for a space with character.

The size of the core, the length of the plume and the proportion of red are all made to order.

## LC0516 — Light composition

A composition about an inverted canopy. Gold trunks with forked joints carry a harvest: white petal-fruits and clear faceted drops. The tree grows downward, and the fruit hangs where the roots would usually be.

Three textures are gathered into one volume: matte whiteness gives softness and weight, faceting gives brilliance and transparency, gold gives structure. The canopy opens in layers as you walk around it.

By day it is a dense rich mass with not one surplus colour — a piece read through the overlay of three surfaces. In the evening the faceted drops light from within and scatter the light, the white petals diffuse it softly, and the gold picks it up and traces the structure: abundance without gaudiness.

**Where it works.** Dining rooms, living rooms, lobbies, banqueting halls. The dense canopy requires height — pressed to the ceiling, it loses its layers.

**Style.** Art Deco and formal classicism: the harvest as a symbol of plenty. The message is generous — a piece for an interior that receives.

The scale of the canopy and the number of petals and drops are all made to order.

## LC0515 — Light composition

A composition about two layers. Outside, a full flower head of dozens of curved petals — from honey to cognac; inside, a white branching frame, a bone coral that glows through the amber. The piece shows the flower and its skeleton at once.

The spherical form has no front: the composition is equally beautiful from any side.

By day it is an amber sphere with a white frame showing through between the petals — a piece read through colour and structure. In the evening points in the core light up, the frame comes through as a silhouette against the amber, and the light turns honeyed — the interior around it becomes evening-like and comfortable.

**Where it works.** The centre of a space — above a round table, in a rotunda, in a bay window, in an entrance hall. Having no front means it is viewed in the round.

**Style.** Art Nouveau and natural modern: the dahlia as a motif of abundance. The message is warm — a piece for an interior in a honey register.

The diameter of the sphere, the density of the petals and the depth of the amber tone are all made to order.

## LC0514 — Light composition

A composition about a rain of its own. Every parasol is tilted its own way, as though catching something of its own — and the swarm stops being a formation: every cap has a task of its own.

The form is simple: a fine stem, a wide pleated skirt, a mirrored surface.

By day the monochrome and the mirroring make the composition graphic — it reflects an interior and dissolves into it, leaving only contours. In the evening light burns under the skirts, the pleating takes it apart into silver rays, and all that remains of the swarm is a set of glowing coronets.

**Where it works.** Contemporary spaces — concrete, panoramic glazing, minimalist living rooms and lobbies. A mirror works where there is something to reflect.

**Style.** Contemporary minimalism and high-tech: the mushroom as pure geometry. The message is technical — a piece for an interior of straight lines.

The number of parasols, their diameters and their angles of tilt are all made to order.

## LC0513 — Light composition

A composition about swirled syrup. The bubbled mass in the green-and-amber "rings" reads as a viscous liquid with bubbles caught in it — movement stopped halfway.

Every element is threaded on a gold funnel axis and resembles a spinning top frozen mid-turn. The swarm hangs at differing heights, narrowing downward: the sense of rotation survives in complete stillness.

By day it is warm amber volumes with bubbles seen against the light — a piece read through form and inner drawing, dense and comfortable. In the evening light burns in the axis and floods the volume from within: the bubbles flare like sparks in swirled syrup, and the tops begin to turn.

**Where it works.** Spaces with a retro-futurist character — bar counters, kitchen islands, studies, lounges. The dense swarm requires no great height.

**Style.** Retro-futurism and mid-century: a sixties form without quotation. The message is playful and warm — a piece for an interior with humour.

The number of tops, their calibres and the depth of tone are all made to order.

## LC0512 — Light composition

A composition about a piece of jewellery at the scale of an interior. Large faceted links are joined by gold bushings into live vertical chains — literally a jewel enlarged to the size of a chandelier.

The palette runs as a gradation: from pale lavender to deep amethyst, and the chains chime with one another in shades.

By day it is faceted lilac links catching daylight and scattering it across the walls — a piece read through colour and faceting. In the evening light enters the link through the bushing and leaves the facets in lilac flashes: the chain lights from within, link by link.

**Where it works.** Boudoirs, dressing rooms, boutiques, private living rooms. The saturated colour asks for neutral surroundings; lilac highlights tint the walls — the background should be quiet.

**Style.** Art Deco and jewelled Art Nouveau: the gemstone chain as a classic of adornment. The message is bold and feminine — a piece for an interior that is not afraid of colour.

The length of the chains, the number of links and the palette are all made to order.

## LC0511 — Light composition

A composition about an object let go. A table lamp is a thing whose name says everything: it stands on a table. The author takes that silhouette, multiplies it and lifts it into the air. The move is ironic and therefore instantly recognisable.

Dozens of small "lamps" hang as a dense cloud, each with its own tilt and height — as though they had only just been released and had not yet fallen into line.

By day it is recognisable silhouettes beneath the ceiling: ribbed skirts on fine gold stems, a piece read through form and repetition. In the evening light burns in the stem and floods the skirt, turning each into a glowing pleated bell — a swarm of table lamps with no table.

**Where it works.** Above a long table, in cafés and bar spaces, in lofts, in meeting rooms — wherever a joke is welcome. The dense swarm gives even light along the whole length of a zone.

**Style.** Postmodernism and contemporary design: the quotation of an object instead of an ornament. The message is witty — a piece for an interior with a sense of humour.

The number of "lamps", their sizes and the density of the swarm are all made to order.
