## LC0551 — Light composition

A composition about a moment that changes along with the viewer. From different angles the piece reads differently: now a current, now a flowering, now a firework stopped at its peak. No one reading is the principal one — the author leaves them all.

Clear relief fans with a fine inner texture are gathered into a layered cascade; at the core of each is a glossy gold sphere, a point of jewel precision.

By day the composition reads close up through the texture of the relief and from a distance through the overall movement of the cascade: two scales of perception in one piece. In the evening light slides along the folds, breaks up in the relief and gathers as highlights on the gold — every fan becomes a separate impulse of light, and together they give a living crystalline structure.

**Where it works.** Living rooms, dining rooms, lobbies, stairwells. The layered cascade requires height and reads from a distance.

**Style.** Art Deco and contemporary sculpture: the fold and the sphere as the entire vocabulary. The message is formal — a piece for an interior that greets.

The shape of the cascade, the number of fans and the height are all made to order.

## LC0550 — Light composition

A composition about an inverted flowerbed. Greenery above, flowering at the tips: the garden is hung roots up, and the leaves reach downward like shoots through water.

Every orchid is a small glowing jewel; the green tier adds depth and sets off the whiteness.

By day it is a graceful botanical installation: green leaves above white flowers, a piece read through its two tiers and their contrast. In the evening the light lifts the petals carefully from within without destroying the fragility of the form, while the green tier stays dark — only the lower edge glows, like a flowering rim.

**Where it works.** Above a table, in a stairwell, in a winter garden, in a reception room. The two-tier assembly requires height: without it the tiers merge.

**Style.** Art Nouveau and botanical modern: the orchid as a motif of refinement. The message is refined — a piece for an interior with fine taste.

The number of flowers and leaves and the height and shape of the garden are all made to order.

## LC0549 — Light composition

A composition about a form between three things. The matte mantle reads as a calla flower, as a sail and as the wing of a ray — the author does not specify, because what they have in common matters more than the differences: all three glide.

Every element is curved its own way: the shoal lives, dives, turns. Gold winding stems rule the composition with a fine live graphic, like weed in a current.

By day it is white mantles on gold lines — a piece read through the plasticity of the curves and the graphic of the stems. In the evening the points at the tips of the stems light up, the matte whiteness glows evenly and without glare, and the gold goes into shadow: what remains are glowing figures gliding through the dark.

**Where it works.** Above a table, in a stairwell, in a double-height entrance hall. The composition has no principal angle — it is beautiful from everywhere and is placed where people walk around it.

**Style.** Marine modern and Art Nouveau: gliding as subject. The message is calm and plastic — a piece for an interior that loves a line.

The number of figures, the trajectory of the shoal and the scale are all made to order.

## LC0548 — Light composition

A composition about lace turned by the sea, and about what grows through it. White plates with an organic perforation hold the structure while cascades of ruby elements push through them — the piece shows two natures at once: the bony and the living.

The contrast of white against saturated red makes the composition ceremonial and faintly mysterious.

By day it is white lace with red clusters: the bubbled mass of the ruby elements reads against the light, the perforation as graphic work. In the evening the light passes softly through the clusters, igniting them a deep red, and seeps through the perforation as warm points — the white becomes a sieve, the red a core.

**Where it works.** Spaces with character — wine rooms, restaurants, living rooms with a dark palette, bars. Saturated red asks for quiet surroundings.

**Style.** Marine Art Nouveau and dark luxe: coral and ruby as a pair. The message is dramatic — a piece for an interior with a mood.

The ratio of plates to clusters, the depth of the red and the scale are all made to order.

## LC0547 — Light composition

A composition about a reef raised from the sea floor. Two kinds of element form a colony: openwork blue-violet nets, like the fans of gorgonians, and white textured cones with a cellular surface, like colonies of polyps. Between them, cold points of light — plankton.

The composition is built on the contrast of dense against clear: the nets let the gaze pass right through, the cones hold the volume. From every angle the reef reassembles, opening new gaps and shadows.

By day it is a marine palette — ultramarine, lilac, white: a piece fresh and deep at once, read through openwork and texture. In the evening the points between the elements shimmer as suspension, the nets show through and the cones stay dark — the colony turns out to be at depth.

**Where it works.** Spas, pools, bathrooms, living rooms in a marine register, children's rooms. The openwork structure steals no light and suits shaded zones.

**Style.** Marine modern and bio-design: the reef as a form with no author. The message is natural — a piece for an interior beside water.

The composition of the reef, the balance of the colours and the size of the colony are all made to order.

## LC0546 — Light composition

A composition about suspension. This is not a chandelier in the usual sense but a light environment: the movement of air, an accumulation of particles, a suspension that has caught the sunset. Hundreds of crumpled leaves hover as a dense swarm, with gold drops flaring among them here and there.

The composition constantly changes in perception: as the viewer moves the swarm reassembles, and at different brightnesses it changes density.

By day the smoky and amber elements read as a mass without detail: a dense warm cloud living off reflections. In the evening the occasional gold drops light up between them and the swarm acquires depth — what glows is not the elements but the gaps.

**Where it works.** Where there is time to look — above a long table, in a lounge, in a meeting room with a high ceiling. The dense swarm requires volume.

**Style.** Contemporary sculptural installation: an environment instead of an object. The message is contemplative — a piece for a space where people linger.

The density of the swarm, the palette and the dimensions are all made to order.

## LC0545 — Light composition

A composition about a bouquet of living and man-made flowers at once. Two characters of corolla — milky white and smoky clear — are gathered into one garland, and the difference between them is not immediately visible: the composition breathes on that contrast.

At the core of every flower is a mirrored sphere and a soft light.

By day the white corollas read through matte plasticity while the clear ones play on highlights and depth: an airy, almost weightless piece. In the evening the white ones glow evenly and softly, the clear ones let light pass right through and flare — one and the same flower behaves in two ways. The moment of flowering, extended for good.

**Where it works.** Living rooms, dining rooms, bedrooms, wedding halls, reception rooms. The garland is calculated for the length of the zone and hung at a level where the contrast between the two corollas is visible.

**Style.** Art Nouveau and romantic classicism: flowering as subject. The message is tender — a piece for a light interior.

The composition of the garland, the balance of white to smoky and the scale are all made to order.

## LC0544 — Light composition

A composition as a collection. Inside the large elements are gold ammonites in relief: ancient spirals of time enlarged by the faceting as by a lens. The piece is examined like the display case of a mineralogical museum assembled by a jeweller.

The faceting works as optics: it refracts, magnifies and animates the gold relief — the ammonites seem volumetric and almost in motion.

By day it is a cloud of faceted boulders with gold spirals inside — a piece read through refractions, and every element looks different depending on the angle. In the evening light circulates inside the facets, flaring on the ridges, and the ammonites emerge from the depth — time lit from inside the stone.

**Where it works.** Living rooms, studies, lobbies, reception rooms, gallery spaces. The composition is examined close up: it is hung no higher than necessary.

**Style.** Jewelled Art Nouveau and natural aesthetics: the fossil as a gem. The message is collectorly — a piece for someone who values detail.

The number of elements, the number of ammonites and the scale of the cloud are all made to order.

## LC0543 — Wall art object

A composition about a fracture. Two black relief wings — the texture of split rock or the bark of an ancient tree, with gold veining along the edges of the breaks — open up the plane of a wall. Between them a strict vertical line of light: energy held inside the mass.

The object works with shadow and with pause: what matters here is not the wings but the gap between them.

By day it is a graphic sculpture on the wall: the black texture reads as relief, the gold veining traces the breaks. In the evening the gap lights up and the object becomes a glowing fracture with warmth seeping out of it — the mass stays dark and only the crack glows.

**Where it works.** Spare interiors — hallways, studies, bedrooms with character, gallery walls. The dark texture asks for a pale background so the silhouette can read.

**Style.** Brutal opulence and minimalism: the fracture as a form. The message is strong and restrained — a piece for an interior for which one detail is enough.

The size, the finish of the wings and the colour temperature of the light are all made to order.

## LC0542 — Light composition

A composition about light where the sun does not reach. Elongated cocoons with a cellular surface have grown in darkness: not one repeats another, as in nature. Between them, clear bubbles rise on cables.

The glow is calm and deep — the way organisms glow far down: not brightly but evenly.

By day it is white cellular forms with a clear suspension between them — a piece read through texture and the variety of the silhouettes. In the evening light passes through the half-transparent shell as a soft inner radiance, the bubbles catch it and rise as a glowing chain — a depth in which life itself is what glows.

**Where it works.** Bedrooms, spas, bathrooms, rest areas — places where light should calm. The soft glow without glare suits long stays.

**Style.** Bio-design and marine modern: bioluminescence as a motif. The message is contemplative and quiet — a piece for an interior that immerses.

The number of cocoons, the density of the bubbles and the dimensions are all made to order.
