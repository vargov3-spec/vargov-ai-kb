## LC0500 — Light composition

A composition about a game stopped on the winning move. Cubes assemble into a structure with setbacks, cantilevers and towers — a construction set with no final appearance: the composition is built like a building and can take any number of storeys and any configuration.

Between the clear cubes, gold modules with light are set in — they are part of the structure rather than a pendant attached to it.

By day it is transparent architecture: the undulating lens surface distorts everything behind it, and the structure reads through setbacks and refractions. In the evening the gold modules light up inside the construction and the light is multiplied in the facets of the neighbouring cubes — the building glows from within, storey by storey.

**Where it works.** Contemporary interiors — lofts, lobbies, meeting rooms, showrooms, offices. The strict geometry needs straight lines around it; the configuration is chosen for a specific room.

**Style.** Bauhaus and architectural minimalism: the module instead of the ornament. The message is rational — a piece for a space that values structure.

The number of cubes, the proportion of gold modules and the shape of the assembly are all made to order.

## LC0499 — Light composition

A composition about a knot. The knot is an ancient sign of connection and of infinity; here it is turned into sculpture and repeated by the dozen, each with its own plasticity and its own bend. Tying the same knot twice is impossible.

The cloud is dense, on gold needle pins: it reads whole from a distance and opens into the plasticity of each knot close up.

By day it is a white cloud of free loops — a piece read through curves and overlays, bright and mobile. In the evening a soft light comes on inside the loops: it slides along the gloss and floods the core of every knot with warmth — what glows is not the mass but the middle of each linkage.

**Where it works.** Living rooms, dining rooms, lobbies, reception rooms, wedding halls. The dense cloud requires height — pressed to the ceiling, it loses its plasticity.

**Style.** Contemporary sculpture and Art Deco: the knot as a symbol. The message is expressive — a piece for an interior that needs a gesture.

The number of knots, the density of the cloud and the scale are all made to order.

## LC0498 — Light composition

A composition about a formula written in the air. Hexagonal honeycomb frames are linked into a horizontal chain — a structure out of a chemistry textbook carried into space. At the nodes of the lattice, white loop-petals open: strict geometry and living organics work off each other.

That contrast is exactly what carries the piece: the lattice sets the order, the petals break it.

By day it is gold graphic work with white accents — the chain reads through the lines of the cells, an elongated and strict piece. In the evening light comes on inside the petals, the cells go into shadow, and a row of glowing points remains, threaded on an invisible formula.

**Where it works.** Dining tables, bar counters, reception desks — long spaces that need holding with one continuous line. The chain is calculated for the length of the zone.

**Style.** Bauhaus and natural Art Nouveau: the lattice against the plant. The message is intellectual — a piece for an interior that loves a contradiction.

The length of the chain, the number of cells and the density of the petals are all made to order.

## LC0497 — Light composition

A composition about a branch found in a forest. The composition spreads horizontally, repeating the curve of a real branch — not straightened and not symmetrical, but as it grew.

Points of warm light hide in the thick of the leaves like berries: they have to be spotted, they are not put on show.

By day it is white with occasional gold on the cold shine of the branches — a fresh, spring note, a piece read through the silhouettes of the leaves. In the evening the garland glows through the foliage like a window through a canopy: the sources are invisible, and the light pushes through in patches between the leaves.

**Where it works.** Dining rooms, living rooms, winter gardens, restaurant halls, reception desks. The horizontal garland is calculated for the length of the table.

**Style.** Art Nouveau and Scandinavian minimalism: the branch as a motif without stylisation. The message is fresh and spring-like — a piece for a light interior.

The length of the garland, the density of the foliage and the share of gold are all made to order.

## LC0496 — Light composition

A composition about the body of water. This is the brand's most numerous shoal: hundreds of figures circle in a giant ring, filling a space the way a real shoal fills water — as a dense current with thinnings and thickenings.

Every fish is simple, but the magic comes from the mass: on its own an element means nothing.

By day it is a ring of silhouettes beneath the ceiling: the composition reads through form and density, alive as you walk around it. In the evening light shimmers on hundreds of gold backs, the cables go into the dark, and the whole ring begins to swim slowly — movement appears with not one moving part.

**Where it works.** Large spaces — atriums, lobbies, restaurants with high ceilings, double-height living rooms. The ring reads only from above and from a distance.

**Style.** Marine modern and contemporary installation: the shoal as a natural phenomenon. The message is narrative — a piece that turns a room into a stage.

The number of fish, the diameter of the ring and the density of the shoal are all made to order.

## LC0495 — Sculptural composition

A composition about reflected space. Rounded mirrored boulders crumple like liquid metal and reflect an interior as a circular panorama — the piece does not show itself, it multiplies everything around it.

Light here is an accent rather than the main thing: gold points flare in the folds of every lens.

By day the composition lives exclusively on borrowed light: every lens gives its own distorted panorama of the room, and the drawing changes with every step. In the evening the gold points in the folds light up and the mirrored field acquires lights of its own — the reflection gets an anchor.

**Where it works.** Accent walls and ceilings — entrance halls, living rooms, lobbies, reception desks, showrooms. The composition depends on its surroundings: it is placed where there is something to reflect.

**Style.** Contemporary art and minimalism: an object with no image of its own. The message is conceptual — a piece for an interior that values an idea.

The number of lenses, their calibres and their layout across a surface are all made to order.

## LC0494 — Light composition

A composition about a leaf fall twisted by wind. The funnel narrows downward, the elements circling densely — the form of a vortex that has drawn fallen leaves into it.

The light hides in the gold: points of warm radiance are scattered through the vortex like sparks in a fire of leaves. Clear green among the whites works as a fresh accent.

By day it is a white funnel with gold tips and occasional green — a piece read through its twist and silhouettes, light and mobile. In the evening the points light up inside the vortex and it begins to turn before your eyes: the light runs down the spiral from the top.

**Where it works.** Stairwells and double-height halls. The funnel form requires height — the composition is built downward and reads from several levels.

**Style.** Natural Art Nouveau and contemporary sculpture: the vortex as a form of movement. The message is alive and spring-like — a piece for a space with character.

The number of petals, the share of green and the height of the vortex are all made to order.

## LC0493 — Light composition

A composition about a stamen made of pure light. A gold needle with a glowing filament passes through every bud — the light burns along the whole axis, exactly where a real flower has its stamen. Structure and botany have coincided.

The cluster is dense, matte white intermixed with clear amber; needle pins above the buds stitch the composition with a gold rhythm.

By day it is a cluster of buds running from cream to honey — a piece read through the form of the drop and the transition of tones. In the evening the white ones give a matte, calm glow and the amber ones a deep, warm one, and the cluster shifts along its whole height while the gold axes show through it as vertical threads.

**Where it works.** Living rooms, dining rooms, stairwells, reception rooms. The dense cluster is built downward: clearance is needed beneath the composition.

**Style.** Art Nouveau and jewelled Art Nouveau: a flower with a metal core. The message is warm and expensive — a piece for an interior in a gold register.

The number of buds, the balance of white to amber and the shape of the cluster are all made to order.

## LC0492 — Wall composition

A composition about a vine on a column. The branches climb the wall from bottom to top, and the composition turns a plane into a flowering relief — a gardener's device: a plant is trained up a support so that the wall stops being a wall.

Along the branches grow glossy domes ranging from the size of a palm to the size of a saucer, and between them clusters of clear bubbles and curls.

By day it is a painterly relief in a powdery pink register — tender and rare for light decor; the composition reads through the silhouette of the vine and the calibres of the domes. In the evening a warm light comes on inside the domes, the bubbles pick it up and scatter it in sparks, and the vine begins to flower in light.

**Where it works.** Accent walls — entrance halls, living rooms, boudoirs, bedrooms, boutiques. The vertical vine sits on a pier or runs alongside a stair flight.

**Style.** Art Nouveau and romantic modern: the vine as a classic wall motif. The message is feminine and tender — a piece for an interior with a soft palette.

The height of the vine, the number of domes and the density of the scattering are all made to order.

## LC0491 — Light composition

A composition about rigging. A ship's tackle is translated into the language of luxury: some columns keep a genuine rope texture in a warm flax tone, others are twisted and faceted with light inside. Coarse fibre next to faceted radiance, craft next to jewellery.

The contrast decides everything, and it is underlined by one detail: the ends of all the columns are caught identically in polished gold.

By day it is a dense bundle of ropes: the flax texture reads tactilely, the faceted columns through highlights, an honest and heavy piece. In the evening the glowing ropes light the corded ones and the whole bundle smoulders like rigging at sunset — the light comes from inside the bundle rather than from outside it.

**Where it works.** Lobbies, restaurants, bars, lounges, interiors on a marine theme. The dense bundle is built downward: the composition needs height to spare.

**Style.** Brutal opulence and marine classicism: tackle as a decorative motif. The message is honest and artisan — a piece for an interior with character.

The number of ropes, the ratio of textured to faceted and the lengths are all made to order.
