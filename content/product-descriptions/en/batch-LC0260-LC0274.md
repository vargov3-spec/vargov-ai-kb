## LC0274 — Light composition

A nugget given a transparent shell. The gold lives inside the mass — it creases, catches the light and glows through from within, while staying out of reach.

No two elements are alike: each has its own twist and its own inner pattern of foil. The composition alternates fully gilded pieces with half-clear ones, and because of that the mass reads as a volume with deep hollows between the layers.

By day it is a collection of nuggets lifted into the air and laid out on invisible shelves. In the evening the light ignites the foil from inside and the group begins to shimmer red-gold — gold that burns rather than glints.

**Where it works.** Stairwells, bay windows, living rooms with high ceilings, private studies, wine rooms.

**Style.** Art Deco and contemporary luxury: deep tones, dark wood, gilded detail. The message is treasure — a piece about value hidden inside and visible only against the light.

The number of elements, the proportion of gilding and the height of the group are all made to order.

## LC0273 — Light composition

A frost pattern seen against the light. The disc glows not by its surface but by its drawing: the light enters at the centre and runs out along the relief, igniting every facet separately.

The discs hang at different angles: some face the viewer and burn whole, others are seen edge-on and all but vanish. The composition shows itself and hides by turns, depending on which door you came through.

By day it is a collection of rosettes read through relief and shadow. In the evening it becomes a constellation of glowing circles where half the stars face you and half look away.

**Where it works.** Generous horizontal spaces: lobbies, dining rooms, meeting rooms, reception rooms, galleries.

**Style.** Contemporary classic with a softened Art Deco accent: pale walls, mirrors, symmetry. The message is winter clarity — the composition brings the cold graphic of a frosted window indoors.

The number of discs, their calibres and the density of the composition are all made to order.

## LC0272 — Light composition

The same pattern, but with the structure left open. The bar is not hidden — it cuts the circle in half and becomes part of the drawing rather than a service detail.

The discs overlap, and the light passes through several layers in a row. The relief is laid over itself and a moiré appears — a moving pattern that exists in no single element.

By day it is a bundle of flowers on gold stems, dense and sculptural. In the evening it becomes a cascade of glowing rosettes with every facet alight and a ripple running between the layers.

**Where it works.** Stairwells and double-height spaces where the cascade is seen from two levels at once, and halls with a mezzanine.

**Style.** Art Deco and grand classicism: gilding, mouldings, large scale. The message is generosity of form — the composition economises on neither mass nor shine.

The number of discs, the depth of the cascade and the finish of the bars are all made to order.

## LC0271 — Light composition

A backwater under the moon. The glow is silvery and cold — the same form in warm light would read as decorative; here it stays graphic.

The plates lie flat, slightly tilted, like leaves on the surface of still water. The composition is flat in silhouette and deep in layers: the elements hang at different levels and the eye passes straight through them.

By day it is a scattering of shells laid out beneath the ceiling. In the evening it becomes a backwater lit by a cold moon, where both the surface and what lies under it are visible.

**Where it works.** Large flat ceilings: lobbies, conference halls, spa areas, pools, meeting rooms.

**Style.** Minimalism and Nordic modern: a cold palette, stone, smooth surfaces, subdued light. The message is stillness — the composition does not warm the room, it calms it.

The number of plates, their calibres and the area covered are all made to order.

## LC0270 — Light composition

An armful of branches thrown into the air and caught before it reached the floor. The angles are accidental, the crossings uncalculated — the whole piece is about disorder being more precise than composition.

Inside each tube a thread of light runs, and the bubbles in the mass flare along its full length. The stick turns into a glowing rod; the joints hold the structure together and double as accents.

By day it is strict graphic work: intersecting straight lines, nothing surplus, almost a draft drawing. In the evening the lines catch fire and begin to live on their own — the structure disappears and what remains is a sketch of light hanging in the air.

**Where it works.** Contemporary interiors: lofts, offices, minimalist living rooms, co-working spaces, showrooms.

**Style.** Industrial minimalism and constructivism: concrete, exposed services, monochrome. The message is freedom of gesture — the composition looks accidental and is alive for exactly that reason.

The number of tubes, their lengths and the density of the crossings are all made to order.

## LC0269 — Backlit wall mirror

A sun that has a reflection. A crown of petals radiates from the centre, and in the middle there is a mirror: the object is a luminary and a window at once.

Each petal reads as a volume thanks to the darkened recesses, and the whole crown shimmers at the slightest movement of the viewer. The backlight is hidden behind the frame and escapes along the contour, drawing a halo on the wall.

By day it is a classic sunburst mirror in a formal frame — a piece you notice first and look into second. In the evening the halo turns it into a glowing disc and the reflection recedes: the mirror stops being a mirror.

**Where it works.** Entrance halls, dressing rooms, accent walls in a living room, boudoirs, reception rooms.

**Style.** Art Deco and neo-baroque: gilding, symmetry, mouldings, deep tones. The message is ceremony — the object greets whoever comes in and sets the tone for the whole house.

The diameter, the number of rows of petals and the gilding finish are all made to order.

## LC0268 — Decorative panel

A fabric that does not bend. The petals are laid in dense overlapping rows and the surface reads as embroidered cloth, though it will not give by a millimetre.

At the centre the pattern parts — dark rounded fragments emerge through the gold, like pebbles laid bare on the bed of a stream. It is the only event on the whole plane, and it keeps the composition from monotony.

By day the panel looks like a relief of gold cloth — calm, almost a background. Under directed light the relief comes alive: shadows begin to travel across it, and the motionless surface starts to move.

**Where it works.** An accent wall: an entrance hall, behind a bedhead, in a meeting room, in a showroom, in a private study.

**Style.** Contemporary luxury and Eastern opulence: deep colours, velvet, an abundance of metal. The message is the tactile — the panel asks to be touched, and that is its main action.

The dimensions of the panel, the density of the petals and the pattern of the openings are all made to order.

## LC0267 — Light composition

A field suspended in weightlessness. There is a great deal of air between the elements — the composition does not press down with mass, it is distributed through the volume so that the gaze passes right through.

The light is hidden in only some of the flowers; the rest pick it up and pass it on. There are therefore many times fewer glowing points than elements, and the composition shimmers unevenly, like a real field at dusk.

By day the object is almost transparent and reads only through highlights — it is easy to miss. In the evening the key flowers light up and the clear ones flare with borrowed light: the result is a starfield with warm thickenings.

**Where it works.** Very large spaces: atriums, lobbies, panoramic double-height halls, banqueting venues.

**Style.** Romantic classicism and light Art Nouveau: pale walls, plenty of air, a minimum of heavy furniture. The message is weightlessness — the composition occupies an enormous volume without creating any weight.

The number of flowers, the area of the field and the proportion of lit elements are all made to order.

## LC0266 — Decorative screen

A thicket translated into the vertical. Stalks with swollen joints reproduce bamboo down to the rings at the nodes — a replica taken to botanical accuracy.

Gaps remain between the stalks: the screen lets through both light and sight, yet it still divides. It does not cut the space off; it turns the passage between zones into a walk through a grove.

By day it is a thicket set in the transparent — a graphic of verticals with a honeyed cast. In the evening light from below makes the stalks glow along their full height, and the grove becomes a wall of light with a rhythm.

**Where it works.** Zoning: between a living room and a dining room, in a spa, in a restaurant, in a private area, beside a staircase, in a hotel lobby.

**Style.** Eastern minimalism and eco-modern: natural textures, wood, stone, a muted palette. The message is a natural border — dividing space with something that grows rather than something that is built.

The dimensions of the panel, the number of stalks and the tone are all made to order.

## LC0265 — Light composition

An object with no appearance of its own. The mirrored drops reflect the surroundings and each other — the composition consists entirely of what is around it.

The light is hidden underneath and aimed downward: a narrow beam escapes while the surface stays mirrored. The piece both shines and disappears — it works without showing itself.

By day it is a gathering of mercury beneath the ceiling, in which the room is reflected taken apart into fragments. In the evening dark mirrors remain above and points of light burn below: the object vanishes, the light stays.

**Where it works.** Contemporary interiors with clean architecture: concrete, glazing, minimalism. Lofts, offices, galleries, showrooms, meeting rooms.

**Style.** High-tech and radical minimalism: no ornament, large planes, monochrome. The message is self-effacement — the composition gives up an appearance of its own in favour of the space.

The number of drops, their calibres and the density of the group are all made to order.

## LC0264 — Light composition

A shoal held still in the current. Every wing is curved in its own way: some folded almost in half, others barely bent — phases of a single movement laid out through the air.

The light spreads along the concave surface from inside: from below you see a glowing underside, from above the plate stays dark. The composition shows itself only to whoever stands beneath it — from above it barely exists.

By day it is a shoal mid-turn — smoky pink, iridescent, with inner currents running through the mass. In the evening it becomes a cloud of glowing wings at different heights with darkness falling away between them.

**Where it works.** Generous halls with high ceilings: lobbies, double-height living rooms, restaurants, spas, pools.

**Style.** Soft Art Nouveau and resort luxury: a pastel palette, pale fabrics, natural stone. The message is fluidity — the composition is about slow movement through a dense medium.

The number of elements, the spread of the shoal and the tone are all made to order.

## LC0263 — Light composition

A form wrapped around emptiness. The loops are coiled around nothing — what holds the object together is inside it and does not exist.

The cocoons hang in a dense sphere, and through the near ones you see the far ones: repeated refraction creates a depth in which the gaze gets lost without finding a bottom. The light is hidden inside individual elements and reflected in all the rest.

By day it is a cluster of bubbles or spawn — clear, almost indiscernible against a pale background. In the evening it becomes a glowing tangle whose source cannot be located: the whole sphere burns at once.

**Where it works.** Central positions: above a round table, in a rotunda, in a double-height living room, in an entrance hall, in a reception room.

**Style.** Contemporary minimalism and optical design: white walls, clean forms, no ornament. The message is birth — the composition is about what happens inside, before it becomes visible.

The diameter of the sphere, the number of cocoons and the density of the assembly are all made to order.

## LC0262 — Light composition

Pebbles from a river shallow, lifted into the air. The form is worn down to complete simplicity: not one corner, not one detail, only a smooth volume.

The light floods the stone entirely and a bright rim appears along its edge. Varying the drop height turns the field into a relief: waves, shallows, depths — a map of the riverbed carried up to the ceiling.

By day it is a scattering of amber set in mid-air, warm and heavy to the eye. In the evening every element burns with a fire of its own, and the field becomes a shallow in which the sun got tangled and cannot get out.

**Where it works.** Large horizontal spaces: lobbies, long tables, panoramic living rooms, restaurants, winter gardens.

**Style.** Eco-modern and warm minimalism: wood, linen, natural stone, a soft palette. The message is simplicity — the composition makes do with a single form and proves that it is enough.

The number of elements, the area of the field and the depth of tone are all made to order.

## LC0261 — Decorative screen

Chainmail woven out of light. Links are joined into chains, chains into a fabric, and the lattice comes out alive because every link is slightly irregular.

Three executions change the character entirely: clear — light, almost invisible; milky — soft and diffusing; white-and-gold — formal, with a metallic shine. One structure, three different rooms.

By day the screen reads as chainmail — dense in its drawing, transparent in fact. In the evening, lit from the side, the links act as lenses and scatter hundreds of oval highlights across the wall: the lattice stops being a border and becomes a projector.

**Where it works.** Zoning and decorative screens: entrance halls, restaurants, showrooms, private areas, hotel lobbies, window displays.

**Style.** Contemporary classic with a softened Art Deco accent: mirrors, brass, velvet, subdued light. The message is shelter without isolation — a border you can see straight through.

The dimensions of the panel, the execution of the links and the density of the weave are all made to order.

## LC0260 — Light composition

A score for three voices. The clear rings carry light along the arc and flare at the breaks, the gilded ones reflect and work as accents, the matte ones glow evenly — three behaviours of one beam in one composition.

The rings are open and hang in a vertical stream, catching at each other by eye but never touching. A gap always remains between them: the piece is about connection that requires no contact.

By day it is a scattering of bracelets laid out through the air. In the evening it becomes a stream of glowing arcs twisted into a spiral, where every arc burns in its own way.

**Where it works.** A vertical format for stairwells, double-height spaces, bay windows, tall entrance halls.

**Style.** Contemporary luxury and soft Art Nouveau: rounded forms, brass, pale walls. The message is consonance — three different characters gathered into one piece without argument.

The number of rings, the ratio between the executions and the height of the stream are all made to order.
