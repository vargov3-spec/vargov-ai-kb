## LC0244 — Wall panel

A panel that failed to hold its contents. The central gesture is that the fish do not fit inside the frame: part of the shoal has already crossed the edge and is swimming across the bare wall.

The ground works as a seabed lit through water: the colours run into one another in uneven patches, the surface rough and streaked. Above it the shoal moves diagonally, set on rods of differing height — a flat panel turns out to be three-dimensional.

By day it is a fragment of reef shot close up: texture, relief, a subdued palette. In the evening the ground recedes into darkness and only the silver of the scales remains — night water in which you see nothing but what moves.

**Where it works.** Feature walls in halls, on stair landings, in private living rooms, restaurants, lobbies.

**Style.** Art Nouveau and organic modernism: natural textures, subdued tones, large planes. The message is freedom — a composition about how living things do not stay within the boundaries assigned to them.

The panel dimensions, the number of fish, the palette of the ground and the direction of the shoal are all made to order.

## LC0243 — Light composition

One form at a distance, a hundred forms from two steps away. From afar it is a dense sphere; come closer and it breaks into separate elements, each at its own angle.

The light comes not from the caps but from the stems: the tubes glow along their whole length, and the composition becomes a tangle of luminous lines with discs laid over it. The caps reflect that light and invert it — hot from below, cool from above.

By day it is a sculpture read through mass and silhouette. In the evening it is a mycelium lifted out of the earth and hung from the ceiling: what grows in darkness, carried into the light and still glowing on its own.

**Where it works.** For living rooms, stairwells, zones above a round table, restaurants, lounges.

**Style.** Organic modernism and eco-luxury: natural textures, a warm palette, timber. The message is hidden life — a composition about what happens beneath the surface and usually goes unseen.

The sphere diameter, the number of elements, the finish of the caps and the length of the stems are all made to order.

By day it is a frozen explosion of paper — graphics, shadow, volume. In the evening the pages light up and the falling becomes luminous: a blizzard in which every snowflake is a page that has been read.

**Where it works.** Libraries, studies, lobbies of educational institutions, book-lined staircases in private houses, stairwells, atria.

**Style.** Intellectual classicism and the contemporary library: dark timber, leather, tall shelving. The message is enlightenment — a composition about content mattering more than the medium.

The number of volumes, the density of the page blizzard and the height of the fall are all made to order.

## LC0241 — Light composition

Tiling on a tower, scales on a tail. The elements are set overlapping and offset — a construction invented independently by nature and by builders, each for their own purpose.

Two finishes give two different objects from one geometry: in warm gold the column reads as heavy, cast, almost jewel-like; in cool silver as fish-like, mobile, aquatic. The choice here determines the whole scene around it.

By day it is a sculptural column — dense, rhythmic, architectural. In the evening the light creeps from top to bottom through the gaps between the layers, and the column becomes a shaft assembled out of burning plates.

**Where it works.** For stairwells, narrow tall openings, entrance groups, zones beside panoramic glazing, piers.

**Style.** Art Deco and contemporary opulence: vertical divisions, metal, symmetry. The message is armour — a composition about protection that is beautiful precisely because it is functional.

The column height, the number of scales, the finish and the overlap pitch are all made to order.

## LC0240 — Ceiling composition

A vortex pressed to the ceiling. The tiers are offset from one another around the circle, and the mass winds into a spiral, forming a shallow funnel — movement with no height at all.

The light enters the rods at their ends and bursts out at the tip as a bright point: from below the composition reads as a swarm of luminous needles converging on a centre. A warm base above and icy tips below give a temperature difference within a single object.

By day it is a decorative rosette on the ceiling — flat, calm, almost unnoticed. In the evening the spiral switches on and a funnel appears overhead: the room gets a whirlpool where usually nothing happens at all.

**Where it works.** A compact surface-mounted format for rooms with low ceilings, where a full chandelier is impossible: bedrooms, studies, dressing rooms, corridors, lift lobbies.

**Style.** Contemporary classicism and soft Art Deco: pale walls, gilded fittings, symmetry. The message is opulence in tight quarters — the composition brings ceremony where there is no height.

The disc diameter, the number of tiers, the rod lengths and the gilding finish are all made to order.

## LC0239 — Light composition

A column with no boundary. Density is distributed unevenly: the mass is thick at the middle, thinner toward the edges, with individual fragments hanging on their own — the object does not end, it dissolves into the air.

The shards are small, with torn edges: this is not a cut stone but a splinter of ice, with internal cracks and matte patches. Perfection here would be a mistake.

By day it is a frozen waterfall — heavy, cold, stopped. In the evening the light runs down the shards from above, refracting on every facet, and the waterfall becomes a shaft of light with neither beginning nor visible source.

**Where it works.** A narrow vertical format for stairwells, shafts between floors, piers beside tall windows, bay windows.

**Style.** Nordic minimalism and Alpine luxury: stone, pale timber, a cold palette, panoramic windows. The message is water stopped by frost — a composition about force held in a pause.

The column height, the diameter, the density of the shards and the calibre of the fragments are all made to order.

## LC0235 — Light composition

By day the composition barely reads: only highlights and contours against a pale ground. In the evening it becomes a cloud of warm lights, each set into an invisible mount.

**Where it works.** An elongated horizontal format — for long dining tables, bar counters, meeting rooms, generous halls, restaurants.

**Style.** Contemporary minimalism and optical design: white walls, clean lines, an absence of ornament. The message is light without a luminaire — the composition gives all attention to the effect and removes the object.

## LC0229 — Light composition

From a distance the composition reads as a single shoal moving as one; close up it breaks into separate bodies, each with its own marking. Scale decides what you see — the shoal or its members.

By day the object is restrained and almost graphic: cool silhouettes against a pale ground. In the evening the blue comes on at full strength, the light passes through several layers and emerges as a cold radiance — the room goes underwater entirely.

**Where it works.** For generous spaces with high ceilings: atria, lobbies, pools, spas, double-height living rooms, restaurant floors.

**Style.** Marine modernism and contemporary minimalism: white walls, water, natural stone, panoramic windows. The message is depth — the composition changes not the lighting of a room but its medium.
