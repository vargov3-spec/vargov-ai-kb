## LC0183 — Light composition

A composition about cold flame. An elongated lozenge twisted on its axis is a form you cannot draw at rest: the half-turn makes the eye complete the movement upward. The author sets the elements in a dense array, slightly turned toward one another, and the row reads as the crest of a wave or as tongues of fire rising all at once.

Clear, turquoise and opaque white elements are mixed with no system whatever. It is precisely the absence of system that keeps the mass alive: there is an array, but it has not been drawn up.

By day it is a ridge of ice — cold, sharp, with hard highlights on the twisted faces. In the evening the row begins to shimmer: the clear ones flare at the rim, the turquoise fill with colour entire, the white stay soft patches. Brightness wanders from element to element, and the flame turns out to be made of ice.

**Where it works.** An elongated horizontal format — long dining tables, bar counters, meeting rooms, reception areas. It is viewed along its length, so what it needs is extent, not height.

**Style.** Contemporary minimalism with a Murano note: clean geometry, colour as the only decoration. The message is restrained and design-led — a piece for an interior that rests on form.

The length, the number of elements, the ratio of clear, turquoise and white, and the drop heights are all made to order.

## LC0182 — Floor lamp

A composition about ripeness. The ear of grain is a form with a season: it exists only at the end of summer. The author takes a pointed leaf with two short offshoots at its base and repeats it hundreds of times, tips upward — the result is a sheaf raised on a gold stem.

The rows are offset from one another by half a step, so the column reads as plaited rather than stacked. The lower half is left bare: the mass gathers at the top, at eye level, like grain on a stalk.

By day it is pure form — white gloss on gold, a sculptural ear of grain with no visible source of light. In the evening the light emerges from deep in the column through the gaps between the leaves, lights the rods from within, and the vertical fills with a warm glow while staying white on the outside. A sheaf with the sun hidden inside it.

**Where it works.** Living rooms, bedrooms, reception rooms, lounges, showrooms. The soft diffuse glow without direct glare suits rest areas and bedrooms.

**Style.** Organic modernism and rustic luxury: a natural motif presented without folklore. The message is warm and domestic — a piece for an interior that speaks of plenty rather than status.

The column height, the number of elements, the tone of the glow and the finish of the rods are all made to order.

## LC0181 — Floor lamp

The same ear of grain, read in winter. The geometry is kept but everything else is inverted: the leaves thin toward their tips to full transparency, the offshoots are longer and finer, the rods chromed instead of gold. The palette moves into cold — silver, white, icy highlights.

One and the same form in a warm and a cold reading gives two different objects. Here the silhouette comes out open-work and the object loses weight: a sheaf from which density has gone.

By day it is an ice sculpture — the rims glow along their contour even without a source, because they catch daylight on their edges. In the evening the light passes through the body and makes every leaf glow entire, from base to tip, not only through the gaps. The column becomes dimensional right through: you see both the front layer and what stands behind it.

**Where it works.** Living rooms, bedrooms, spas, reception rooms, interiors in a cold palette. Transparency needs a dark or neutral backdrop — against a white wall the piece loses its outline.

**Style.** Scandinavian minimalism and Nordic modernism: cold as an aesthetic position. The message is clear and ascetic — a piece for an interior without excess.

The column height, the number of elements, the degree of transparency and the finish of the rods are all made to order.

## LC0180 — Floor lamp

A composition about a support that disappears. The frame here is graphite-black and matte — deliberately made invisible, so that when lit the gold sails seem to hang in emptiness. The author plays with what the piece shows and what it conceals: switched off, it honestly displays its structure; switched on, it denies it.

Every element is bowed like a sail, with sharp ends and a deep hollow, and none repeats its neighbour. This is the pure plasticity of a leaf, fixed at the moment of bending.

By day the object reads as a metal sculpture — gold on graphite, heavy and composed. In the evening the black frame falls entirely into shadow, the light strikes the gold hollows and reflects outward in hot patches. What remains is a column of gold sparks, resting on nothing.

**Where it works.** A dark palette — living rooms in deep tones, bars, private studies, showrooms, restaurant interiors. The vanishing-support trick works only against a dark ground.

**Style.** Contemporary Art Deco and dark luxury: black with gold, drama instead of ornament. The message is theatrical — a piece designed for effect.

The column height, the number of elements, the gilding finish and the colour of the rods are all made to order.

## LC0179 — Light composition

A composition about the outline. These leaves have no body — only a closed thickened rim, with emptiness or the thinnest membrane inside. The author keeps the boundary of the form and removes its content: the leaf is reduced to its own contour.

The leaves hang flat, in layers, turned at random; through the upper ones you see the lower. Some are turquoise, some carry a milky haze, and the colour is drawn into the body unevenly, in bands — traces of hand-drawing are visible and nowhere concealed.

By day it is a flock of leaves lifted by wind: the mass is transparent right through, and the composition reads as light to the point of weightlessness. In the evening the principal device engages — the light runs along the thickened contour like a lightguide and traces every leaf with a glowing line. The turquoise fill with colour, the clear ones give a white flare. In the dark only the outlines remain.

**Where it works.** Generous rooms — lobbies, panoramic living rooms, dining rooms, meeting rooms. The layering reads at a distance; in a tight room the contours merge.

**Style.** Art Nouveau in a contemporary idiom: a natural motif reduced to line. The message is graphic — a piece for an interior that loves drawing.

The overall size, the number of leaves, the share of turquoise and the drop heights are all made to order.

## LC0178 — Light composition

A composition about a frozen downpour. Hundreds of needles point downward, gathered into a dense shallow bowl — thicker at the middle, sparser at the edges. This is rain with its time taken away: the drops have already drawn out in falling but have never touched the floor.

The element is double: a gold cone tip-up above, a clear spike below on a base textured like broken ice. A spindle, an icicle grown under a metal cap — the form reads several ways at once.

By day it is a gold field: from below only the cones are visible, and the composition looks metallic and dense. In the evening the light passes from the gold into the clear spike, which carries it the whole length and flares at the point, while the icy base scatters part of it sideways. The field of needles becomes a downpour caught an instant before it lands.

**Where it works.** Generous rooms with high ceilings — lobbies, atria, double-height living rooms, banquet halls. It is seen from below, so height matters more than area.

**Style.** Grand classicism in contemporary execution: the same logic as a large cascade chandelier, but without a single faceted drop. The message is ceremonial — a piece for a space that makes an impression.

The overall size, the number of elements, the length of the spikes and the gilding finish are all made to order.

## LC0177 — Light composition

A composition about the trace of wind. There is no cloud here and no symmetry — there is a line: the leaves run in a broad arc that winds into a spiral, and density falls from the centre toward the ends. Single elements remain on the tails, strays from the current.

The leaf form is narrow, with sharp ends and a twist on its axis, the rims thickened. Some are clear, some carry a gold backing that gives a warm mirrored interior — and that determines how each element will behave with light.

By day it is a trace left by wind: a drawing in the air, read through highlights. In the evening the leaves begin to burn selectively — the clear ones transmit and diffuse, the gold ones reflect and flare, and as you walk around the composition the brightly burning elements change, because they depend on the angle. The cables vanish into darkness and the leaves seem to hang unsupported. A current of sparks curving around the room.

**Where it works.** Large volumes and irregular geometry — stairwells, atria, curved halls, double-height living rooms. The arc is designed for the specific volume: it does not fit into a space, it runs through it.

**Style.** Contemporary sculptural installation: the composition as an architectural gesture. The message is authorial — a piece that makes a space recognisable.

The length and pattern of the arc, the number of leaves, the share of gold and the drop heights are all made to order.

## LC0176 — Light composition

A composition about the time that tumbles things smooth. These stones have no facets and no correct geometry — only rounded flanks and a matte surface, as though the sea had carried them for a long while. The author deliberately refuses cutting: what is valued here is not the work of a maker but the work of the elements.

The stones hang individually, gathered into a broad shallow bowl. Density is uneven: they mass together at the centre and thin to a scatter at the edges. No array at all — a natural distribution, like shingle on a shore.

By day it is a scatter of sea stones suspended in the air: cobalt blue beside cloudy white, the palette of a northern coast. In the evening the glow comes from within — the matte surface does not transmit light but glows as a soft subdued patch: the blues deepen to ultramarine, the whites to opal. Not a single hard highlight. A night sea lit from beneath.

**Where it works.** Generous rooms — lobbies, spas and pools, panoramic living rooms, dining rooms. Even diffuse light without glare is comfortable where people spend time beneath it.

**Style.** Organic minimalism and Nordic aesthetics: imperfection as a virtue. The message is contemplative — a piece for an interior that calms.

The overall size, the number of stones, the ratio of blue to white and the calibres are all made to order.

## LC0175 — Light composition

A composition about a shell with light sounding inside it. A broad bowl with a rolled rim — a section like a shell or a petal curled by heat. Copper-rose and calm outside, mirrored within: one and the same form behaves in two different ways depending on which side you look at it from.

The cluster is vertical, broad above and tapering below. The elements are turned at random: some show a mirrored interior, some a clear back.

By day it is a cluster of rose forms — warm, quiet, working through reflection. In the evening light kindles inside each bowl, takes on copper and emerges through the open edge as a narrow hot patch. The mouths face different ways, and the composition reads differently from every point — a swarm of copper lights with no principal view.

**Where it works.** A vertical format — stairwells, atria, double-height living rooms, shafts between floors. It is designed to be seen from several levels at once.

**Style.** Warm Art Deco and contemporary classicism: copper and rose gold rather than yellow. The message is intimate and rich — a piece for an interior with a warm palette.

The cluster height, the number of elements, the depth of the copper tone and the finish of the inner surface are all made to order.

## LC0174 — Light composition

A composition about what is left. The fragments are crushed and smoothed out again: folds, creases and torn edges are all preserved. The author does not assemble a form, he records a consequence. The object must look like what remains after something, not like something carefully put together.

The cloud is broad, the fragments at varied levels, turned at random, with no array and no symmetry. Large pieces with polished gold alternate with pearlescent ones, and small scraps are scattered between them.

By day it is scattered foil: uneven highlights, a random pattern, nothing predictable. In the evening the light flares as stars between the fragments — the crumpled gold reflects it unevenly, in bands along the folds, while the pearlescent diffuses it softly. No evenness at all, only patches and flares. A field of shards, each with its own highlight.

**Where it works.** Generous rooms with high ceilings — lobbies, double-height living rooms, stairwells, gallery spaces. The cloud needs air around it: pressed to the ceiling, it loses its depth.

**Style.** Contemporary art and brutalist luxury: the aesthetics of destruction, executed expensively. The message is bold — a piece for an interior unafraid of being uncomfortable.

The overall size, the number of fragments and the ratio of gold to pearlescent are all made to order.

## LC0173 — Light composition

A composition about the moment of stopping. A spinning top exists only while it spins; a stationary top falls over. The author catches it in an impossible state: motionless and upright at once, suspended in the air.

The form is built from three parts: a flattened disc with a broken-ice texture at the middle, a short cone above, a long sharp spike below. The elements hang on gold tubes of varying length, with empty tubes between them — they work as hatching and give the composition depth, hollows and gatherings.

By day it is a set of clear instruments — precise, cool, almost laboratory forms. In the evening every element gives two different glows at once: the icy texture of the disc scatters light in fine sparks, while the smooth spike carries it onward and lights up at the very tip. A swarm of tops glowing in the dark.

**Where it works.** Living rooms with high ceilings, stairwells, lobbies, zones above a round table. The multi-level arrangement needs height — the composition builds in depth, not in width.

**Style.** Contemporary classicism and precise minimalism: geometry instead of decoration. The message is intellectual — a piece for someone who values the idea over the effect.

The overall size, the number of elements, the suspension lengths and the finish of the tubes are all made to order.

## LC0172 — Light composition

A composition about a flower made of a single leaf. The calla is a rare case in botany: its "flower" is one leaf wrapped around itself. The author takes precisely that peculiarity: a funnel with a sharp tip below, the inner spiral clearly visible and not concealed.

The composition is drawn out horizontally: the flowers hang on gold tubes of varying length, with empty tubes set between them to establish the rhythm. The elements are turned at random — some show an open mouth, others a smooth back.

By day it is a field of clear flowers, read through rims and highlights; the thick fire-polished edges give a sharp contour. In the evening the light travels the spiral of the funnel, reflecting off the walls, and bursts out at the tip as a bright point, while the body works as a lens — a gold core of its own appears inside every flower. Not a single direct source is visible: a swarm of luminous funnels above the table.

**Where it works.** An elongated horizontal format — long dining tables, bar counters, meeting rooms, reception areas. The light is gathered into points, so the composition gives directed rather than general illumination.

**Style.** Art Nouveau and contemporary classicism: a botanical motif in pure clear execution. The message is elegant — a piece for an interior that holds its form.

The length, the number of flowers, the suspension lengths and the finish of the tubes are all made to order.

## LC0170 — Light composition

A composition about the passage from dawn to dusk. White and gold are not mixed here but drawn apart to the ends: the white boats gather on one side, the gold on the other, and in the middle they blend. The arc reads not as a pattern but as a timeline — a cold field warming toward gold.

The element is a narrow boat with high sharp prows and a deep hollow. The arc rises from one side, the elements lying in tiers and layers with a gradual gain in height.

By day the gradient reads calmly: white all but dissolves against a pale ground while gold holds the accent. In the evening the difference intensifies — the white diffuses the light into a soft even glow, the polished gold reflects it sharply and throws highlights. The transition becomes more legible than it is by daylight: the composition literally heats up toward one edge.

**Where it works.** Generous rooms with irregular geometry — stairwells, curved halls, atria, double-height living rooms. The rise of the arc is designed for the specific volume.

**Style.** Contemporary classicism with an Art Deco accent: white with gold, movement as the principal motif. The message is formal — a piece for a space where the first impression matters.

The length and rise of the arc, the number of elements and the point of transition from white to gold are all made to order.

## LC0169 — Light composition

A composition about the setting. The petal here is placed inside a second, clear form: an opaque grey-white core surrounded by an amber shell, with an air gap between them and a visible boundary along the rim. The device is a jeweller's — this is how a stone is set into a collet so it never touches the metal.

The form is elongated, pointed at both ends, with a slight twist. The composition is a dense shallow bowl: the elements are laid in layers, denser at the centre and sparser at the edges, with single petals descending below.

By day it is a cluster of layered petals — the double structure is visible along the contour and every element reads separately. In the evening the light divides between the layers: the amber shell flares along its contour as a warm line, the grey-white core stays a calm matte patch. The mass never merges into a continuous glow — it is a multitude of outlined forms, a swarm of luminous cocoons.

**Where it works.** Living rooms with high ceilings, lobbies, stair landings, zones above a round table. The double structure is visible close to — the composition is made to be examined.

**Style.** Jewellery modernism and contemporary classicism: the setting as a device rather than a finish. The message is collector-minded — a piece for someone who looks at detail.

The overall size, the number of elements, the tone of the core and the depth of the amber are all made to order.

## LC0167 — Light composition

A composition about a ribbon of fire. Boats with sharp raised prows stand close and overlap, so the line reads as a continuous mass rather than a row of separate objects. The lower edge is wavy, the upper level — together they give the silhouette of a ribbon stretched below the ceiling.

Gold and pearl-white elements are mixed without system and turned at random: some show a burning interior, others a cool back. The rhythm is set not by spacing but by those orientations.

By day it is a ridge of shells — dense, metallic, read through silhouette. In the evening the hollows begin to work: the gold turns light into hot patches, the pearlescent diffuses it into a soft glow. Because the mass is dense the source never reads — what glows is the ribbon itself, a band of fire stretched above the table.

**Where it works.** An elongated horizontal format — long dining tables, bar counters, meeting rooms, reception areas. A continuous mass gives more light than a sparse composition of the same size.

**Style.** Contemporary Art Deco: white with gold, a streamlined form, rhythm instead of ornament. The message is representative — a piece for a table where people are received.

The length of the ribbon, the number of elements, the ratio of gold to pearlescent and the drop heights are all made to order.
