# Product texts — batch 25: LC0336–LC0350 (15 articles)

English version of the reference batch — the approved format is fixed here.

**Structure of a text:** artistic image and the author's intent (associations, message and device rather than a retelling of the photograph) → the shift from day to evening → **Where it works.** → **Style.** → a closing line with made-to-order parameters.

The materials of the elements are not named.

---

## LC0350 — Light composition

A composition with a plot — the rare case of an object that does not decorate a space but tells a story.

The birds are held in different phases of movement: one is diving, another hovers, a third is only approaching. The flowers wait. Between them is that instant before contact for which the whole thing was assembled.

The dusty lilac was not chosen for prettiness. The muted colour pushes the blossom into the background and brings the birds forward; in the evening the roles reverse — the flowers light up from within and become the stage while the birds go to silhouette. One object, two performances.

By day it is fine botanical graphic work: dusty lilac blossom and gold birds read as silhouettes. In the evening the stage turns over — the flowers fill with light from inside, the birds go into shadow, and the object becomes a theatre with the stage lit.

**Where it works.** The vertical format was made for stairwells and double-height living rooms: the stage has to be seen whole, top to bottom. Also good in a bay window, a winter garden, a generous bedroom.

**Style.** Contemporary classic and neoclassicism — pale walls, mouldings, soft furniture in pastel tones. The message is romantic: a piece for someone who wants a plot in the house, not simply a source of light.

The height of the cascade, the number of flowers and hummingbirds and the tone of the blossom are all made to order.

## LC0349 — Light composition

A flock with no leader. Every bird is generalised to the limit — two wings and a body, not one surplus detail — and precisely for that reason the eye sees movement rather than figures.

The composition is built by the laws of a real flock: a dense core, thinned edges, individual birds that have broken ahead. The formation never repeats itself, and a shimmer arises out of that — the gaze reads flight where nothing moves.

The intent is concision: the less that is said about a bird, the more is left to the viewer. This is a composition as hint, not as illustration.

By day the flock reads as graphic work on the ceiling — generalised silhouettes, not one detail. In the evening the light reveals it in layers: the near birds flare, the far ones stay shadow, and the flight acquires a depth it does not have by day.

**Where it works.** The horizontal format is ideal above a long dining table, in a lobby, in a meeting room, over a reception desk — anywhere that needs span without mass.

**Style.** Contemporary minimalism and Scandinavian restraint: white walls, pale wood, panoramic glazing. The message is freedom — the flock reads as open sea beyond the window, even when the window gives onto a city.

The number of birds, the span of the arc and the balance of white to clear are all made to order.

## LC0348 — Light composition

An object about a shell being a house built along a spiral. The form is coiled so that it has neither a beginning nor an end: the gaze is drawn inside and finds no floor.

The light is hidden deep in the whorl and comes out not straight but in a circle, drawing the curve. The result is not a luminaire but a find — something raised from the sea bed that goes on glowing by itself.

The birds between the shells keep the composition from the literal: sea and air in one scene — a device that stops the object becoming a seaside corner in the souvenir sense.

By day it is a sculptural find — a spiral read through plasticity and the highlights along the whorl. In the evening the light comes out in a circle, drawing the curve, and the shell begins to glow of its own accord, like something raised from the sea bed that will not go out.

**Where it works.** A dense vertical format — for staircases, bay windows, tall entrance halls, spa areas and poolside spaces where the theme of water sounds natural.

**Style.** Mediterranean classicism and resort luxury: white walls, natural stone, pale textiles. The message is calm — the composition brings the sense of a coastline into a house without a single literal shell on a shelf.

The number of shells and birds and the depth of the cascade are all made to order.

## LC0347 — Light composition

A composition about a fragility that turns out to be stronger than stone. A dragonfly lives one season, but its silhouette has not changed in two hundred million years — that paradox became the intent of the object.

The wings hold their transparency to the limit: light does not bounce off them, it goes through, developing the drawing of the veins the way film is developed. The bodies stay dense and dark — they do not glow, they give the wings their support.

The vertical is assembled without symmetry: each figure is turned its own way, and the swarm reads not as a formation but as an instant — what came before and what comes after is left outside the frame.

By day it is graphic work — the fine drawing of veins on a transparent wing, the bodies read as dark strokes. In the evening the wings light up against the light while the bodies stay blind: the swarm glows by its wings, suspended in the dark.

**Where it works.** The narrow vertical format suits stair flights, wall piers, zones beside a window, studies and private libraries — wherever the object is looked at closely and for a long time.

**Style.** Art Nouveau: the composition comes from the same aesthetic as Tiffany's stained glass and Lalique's jewelled brooches. The message is collectorly — a piece for someone who values a naturalistic detail and is willing to study it.

The number of dragonflies, the height of the column and the finish of the bodies are all made to order.

## LC0346 — Light composition

Water frozen together with everything that was in it. Inside each element there is a suspension: bubbles, sparks, grains, as though the sea had frozen before it could let go of the plankton.

The form is reduced to a minimum — a smooth cylinder, a smooth lens, not one gesture. All the work is given to the depth: the blue elements damp the light and hold shadow, the clear ones multiply it, and the field comes out layered, with hollows and flares.

The intent here is not in the form but in the state: the composition does not depict water, it reproduces the sensation of looking through a body of it.

By day it is a clear mass with a suspension inside — a piece read through depth and layers, calm and cold. In the evening the blue elements damp the light and hold shadow, the clear ones multiply it, and the field comes alive with hollows and flares: a look through water at dusk.

**Where it works.** A format for large horizontal spaces: above a long table, in a lobby, in a panoramic living room, in a meeting room, in a spa and beside a pool.

**Style.** Contemporary minimalism and Nordic Art Nouveau: concrete, glazing, dark wood, muted tones. The message is depth and quiet — the composition works like a window into water; it calms rather than entertains.

The number of elements, the balance of blue to clear and the area of the field are all made to order.

## LC0345 — Light composition

The man-made pretending to have been found. The crystals are split irregularly, the facets meet at random angles, and the object looks lifted out of rock rather than assembled in a workshop.

Light enters at the bases and turns every facet into a prism: some crystals burn from within, others only reflect their neighbours. A depth arises between them that exists in no single element.

That is the device: the composition is built from repeating modules and reads as a one-of-a-kind mineral. Order pretends to be chance — deliberately and all the way through.

By day it is rock: the split facets catch daylight unevenly and the cluster looks lifted out of the ground. In the evening light enters at the bases and every facet becomes a prism — some crystals burn from within, others only reflect their neighbours, and a depth opens inside the mass.

**Where it works.** The vertical cluster asks for height: a staircase, an atrium, a double-height entrance hall, a study with double light.

**Style.** Contemporary luxury and brutal opulence: rough stone, dark metal, leather, coarse texture. The message is the primordial — the piece speaks of the force of nature in the language of precise work.

The number of crystals, their calibres and the finish of the branches are all made to order.

## LC0344 — Light composition

A branch that was not cut but grown again. The leaves are twisted and turned out of step with each other — there is no garland rhythm here, there is the disorder of a real canopy.

Half the foliage transmits light and half reflects it. Because of that the canopy shimmers the way a tree shimmers in the sun: some leaves burn against the light, others stay dark, and the drawing changes with every step.

The composition is deliberately horizontal: it does not descend on the viewer, it reaches out above them — like a branch over a garden path that you want to stand under.

By day the canopy shimmers the way a tree shimmers in the sun: some leaves burn against the light, others stay dark, and the drawing changes with every step. In the evening the light comes from inside the branch, the clear leaves fill and the gold ones flare — the branch lights up every other leaf.

**Where it works.** A linear format for a long dining table, a bar counter, a kitchen island, a reception desk, an elongated meeting room.

**Style.** Contemporary classic with an Eastern note: warm wood, brass fittings, quiet walls. The message is hospitality — a branch above a table reads as the sign of a table laid and a guest invited.

The length of the branch, the number of leaves and the balance of clear to gold are all made to order.

## LC0343 — Light composition (Award Winning)

A pond lifted to the ceiling. The bowls open upward rather than down — and that inverts the usual logic: the light here does not pour onto the table, it lies in the leaves like water after rain.

The green mass works as a filter: light passes through it and tints the air beneath the composition a soft grass tone. The white bowls keep the colour from going monotonous — they hold the pause.

The composition holds an international award. What won it was the honesty of the device: form and optics here do not argue, they do one job.

By day the bowls read green against the light — water lying in leaves after rain. In the evening the green mass works as a filter: light passes through it and tints the air beneath the composition a grass tone, while the white bowls remain a pause in the colour.

**Where it works.** Above a round or oval table, in a winter garden, in a spa area, in a living room facing a garden — where the green outside will pick up the green of the composition.

**Style.** Art Nouveau and eco-modern: the line of nature, no geometry at all. The message is living water — the composition brings in the sense of a pond, damp coolness and slow time.

The number of bowls, the balance of green to white and the dimensions are all made to order.

## LC0342 — Light composition

There is no natural form here — only pure structure. The object is built like a thought: a horizontal, a vertical, a node, a pause.

The light lives inside the lines and never comes out as a patch — it rules the space, leaving air and shadow between the rods. By day the frame reads; in the evening only a glowing drawing remains, suspended with no support.

The intent is built on a contradiction: a rigid orthogonal grid filled with a texture that resists it — sparks inside the rods make the strict geometry alive.

**Where it works.** Contemporary spaces with high ceilings: lofts, open-plan offices, atriums, galleries, showrooms, meeting rooms.

**Style.** High-tech, constructivism, industrial minimalism: concrete, metal, exposed services. The message is intellectual: this is a structure rather than an ornament, and it declares the rationality of its owner.

The dimensions of the lattice, the density of the rods and the finish of the capsules are all made to order.

## LC0341 — Light composition (Award Winning)

A flower caught at the moment of opening. The petals are not fully unfurled — the movement is stopped a little before it finished, and the whole life of the object is in that.

Blue and white alternate in layers, so the sphere looks solid even switched off. The light is hidden in the core and pushes out between the petals, lifting their edges — what glows is not the surface but the gaps.

The composition received an international award. It is built on a rare balance: a recognisable natural form with not one naturalistic detail.

By day the sphere looks solid even switched off — the blue and white layers alternate and give light and shade. In the evening the light from the core pushes out between the petals and lifts their edges: what glows is not the surface but the gaps, and the flower turns out to be outlined in light.

**Where it works.** Above a round table, in a bedroom over the bed, in an entrance hall, in a dressing room — wherever a whole silhouette and soft shadowless light matter.

**Style.** Contemporary classic and soft Art Nouveau: rounded furniture, velvet, pale walls. The message is flowering — a note of spring in an interior, kept strict in form.

The diameter of the sphere, the number of petals and the palette are all made to order.

## LC0340 — Floor lamp (Award Winning)

The same flower, brought down to human height. The bloom is tilted slightly — like a real one reaching for the light — and the floor lamp stops being an object and becomes company beside an armchair.

The proportions rest on a risk: a heavy head, the finest of stems, a substantial base. The object looks fragile while remaining perfectly stable — a deliberate illusion, and everything was calculated for it.

The light goes up and to the side, leaving the floor in shadow. This is not general lighting but personal lighting — a circle you sit in.

By day it is a sculpture on the finest of stems: the tilted bloom reads as a silhouette, the base carries the weight. In the evening the light goes up and to the side, leaving the floor in shadow — one lit circle appears around the armchair, personal rather than general.

**Where it works.** Beside an armchair in a living room, at the head of a bed, in the corner of a study, in a hotel lounge. Works solo and in a pair flanking a sofa — in two colours it reads at once as a pair of different flowers.

**Style.** Contemporary classic and feminine Art Nouveau: a soft line, nothing technical. The message is intimacy — the floor lamp does not light the room, it makes one comfortable corner.

The height, the colour of the bloom and the finish of the base are all made to order.

## LC0339 — Light composition (Award Winning)

White on white — the hardest task in product design: with the colour taken away, form has nothing left to hide behind.

Here it holds. You can see how the thickness of a petal changes from base to tip, how it catches light with its gloss, how the double tier gives volume without a single hint of colour.

The glow shows through at the edges and the flower becomes half-transparent — not a glowing object but an object that light passes through. The difference is fundamental, and the whole piece rests on it.

By day you can see how the thickness of a petal changes from base to tip and how it catches light with its gloss: the double tier gives volume without a single hint of colour. In the evening the glow shows through at the edges and the flower becomes half-transparent — not a glowing object but an object that light passes through.

**Where it works.** The most universal version of the series: above a table, in a bedroom, in a hallway, in a meeting room, in a study — anywhere that needs an accent without colour.

**Style.** Suits almost any interior — from strict minimalism to classicism with mouldings. The message is restrained perfection — the piece declares taste through quiet rather than through brightness.

The diameter, the number of petals and the colour temperature of the light are all made to order.

Rain with neither a beginning nor an end. Dense gold at the top, clearer and clearer toward the bottom — the composition dissolves into the air instead of ending at an edge.

Inside every thread a chain of lights runs, and no thread repeats the rhythm of its neighbour. From a distance it is one continuous radiance; close up you see that it is assembled from thousands of separate sparks, each in its place.

The model holds an international award. The intent is simple and strong for that reason: the object does not depict rain, it occupies all the space that rain would occupy.

## LC0337 — Light composition

Three spheres threaded vertically — and three different views of one object: from the ground floor you see the lowest sphere, from the first the middle one, from above the whole row at once.

The structure is openwork throughout: through the weave you see both the neighbouring sphere and the space beyond it. The volume here is declared but not occupied — a form consisting mostly of air.

The intent is in the vertical. The composition does not hang above the viewer, it accompanies them on the way up, opening in parts — light tied to movement.

By day the structure is openwork throughout: through the weave you see both the neighbouring sphere and the space beyond it — the volume is declared but not occupied. In the evening light is born inside the spheres and comes out through the weave as a net of rays — three glowing cocoons threaded on a vertical.

**Where it works.** Ideal for a stairwell: the spheres read from different floors in turn. Also good in a narrow double-height entrance hall and in a shaft between floors.

**Style.** Contemporary Art Nouveau and soft high-tech: open staircases, glass balustrades, spare finishes. The message is upward movement — three steps of light leading the eye along a vertical.

The number of spheres, their diameters and the density of the weave are all made to order.

## LC0336 — Light composition

A form impossible to repeat twice. The lines do not follow the rules of a sphere, they fall as thread falls — freely, overlapping, with hollows and thickenings.

This is a composition about the hand-made: behind the apparent chaos stands exact calculation, but the eye reads not calculation but a nest, a cocoon, a ball of yarn — something wound rather than drawn.

The light is born at the knots of the weave and scatters in hundreds of points. The honey tone takes away the cold, and the sphere begins to seem warm to the touch — an illusion the whole object rests on.

By day it is a tangle of overlapping lines with hollows and thickenings: behind the apparent chaos stands calculation, but the eye reads a nest rather than a drawing. In the evening the light is born at the knots of the weave and scatters in hundreds of points, the honey tone takes away the cold — the sphere begins to seem warm to the touch.

**Where it works.** A spherical form has no front: it works in the centre of a space, above a round table, in a double-height living room, in an entrance hall, in a restaurant.

**Style.** Eco-modern and comfortable minimalism: wood, linen, rattan, warm walls. The message is domestic warmth — honey light and the form of a nest read as a promise of comfort.

The diameter of the sphere, the density of the weave and the tone of the threads are all made to order.
