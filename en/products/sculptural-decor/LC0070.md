## Description

A composition about the mark of the hammer. Uneven dents, grooves and swellings run across the whole frame — as though the metal had been struck while still soft. The pattern is irregular, without a single repeat: handwork is shown as it is, not smoothed away.

The chief difference from the other mirrors in the line is the backlight. It is hidden in a ring recess between frame and mirror and emerges as a narrow band.

By day the frame works through texture: pale antiqued silver with gold wear and dark patina in the hollows, a matte surface with visible grain. In the evening the light comes from within and grazes the relief — every dent takes a shadow, the frame gains volume, and the mirror itself sits inside a glowing ring. The convex plate meanwhile draws the room into a compact frame with strong distortion at the edges.

## Where it works

Entrance halls, corridors, bedrooms, bathrooms, dressing rooms. The backlight makes the mirror functional for the zone, but the convexity distorts the reflection — it is not a mirror for getting ready.

## Style

Brutalist luxury and hand forging: the marks of work as decoration. The message is honest and craft-driven — a piece that shows how it was made.

## Made to order

The diameter, the character of the forging, the metal finish and the brightness of the backlight are all made to order.
