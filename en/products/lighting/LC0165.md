## Description

A composition about the crack as ornament. Crazing is usually counted a defect: a net of fine cracks across a surface. Here it is rubbed with gold, and damage turns into drawing. The device is Japanese in spirit — kintsugi, where the break is not hidden but emphasised.

The branches are woven into a long horizontal band with side shoots. The leaves are turned at random: some show a white crazed back, others open a hot gold interior. Every lining is gilded in full.

By day it is a branch with whitened foliage — frost, old glaze, something time has touched. The crazing reads as graphics on white. In the evening the light strikes the gold linings and emerges reflected and warm; the white stays cool while the gold in the cracks catches the light and traces a glowing net across every leaf. A crown with gold burning between its leaves.

## Where it works

An elongated horizontal format — long tables, kitchen islands, meeting rooms, reception areas. The crazing is seen close to, so the piece is hung lower than usual.

## Style

Wabi-sabi and European classicism in one: the marks of time presented as value. The message is collector-minded — a piece for someone who values texture over shine.

## Made to order

The length of the branch, the number of leaves, the density of the crack pattern and the gilding finish are all made to order.
